Analysis of Oracle Corporation (ORCL) Based on 10-Q Report

Overview:
- Authorized Shares: 11,000 million common shares, indicating the maximum allowed but not necessarily issued.
- Balance Sheet Comparison: Analysis between November 30, 2025, and May 31, 2025.

Key Findings:

1. Assets:
   - Total Assets Increase: $36,623 million to $204,984 million, a significant growth.
   - Current Assets: Increased by ~$9.7 billion (40%), but specifics on cash and accounts receivable are missing.
   - Non-Current Assets: PPE increased by $24.7 billion, suggesting investment in physical assets; intangible assets decreased slightly.

2. Liabilities:
   - Total Liabilities Increase: ~$21.9 billion, with notable rises in borrowings ($14.7 billion) and operating lease liabilities.
   - Current Liabilities: Accounts payable up by over 50%, indicating increased credit sales; deferred revenues also rose.

3. Equity:
   - Common Stock & Capital Increase: ~$3.5 billion, possibly due to share buybacks improving EPS.
   - Accumulated Deficit Decrease: Positive sign of narrowing losses.

4. Concerns and Missing Data:
   - Cash and cash equivalents not mentioned in current assets, crucial for liquidity assessment.
   - No financial ratios provided (debt-to-equity, current ratio, ROE), essential for comprehensive analysis.

5. Strategic Considerations:
   - Share buybacks may have contributed to improved metrics but need context.
   - High PPE investment could boost future growth but increases depreciation expenses.
   - Decrease in intangible assets suggests possible operational streamlining or faster amortization.

6. Risks and Opportunities:
   - Growth in liabilities raises concerns about debt sustainability and interest expenses.
   - Deferred revenues indicate customer commitment but require monitoring for potential issues.

Conclusion:
While Oracle shows signs of growth with increased assets and equity, the significant rise in liabilities is concerning. The absence of key financial ratios and detailed cash information limits a complete assessment. Further analysis using additional financial data and context on asset investments and debt usage is recommended to fully evaluate Oracle's financial health.
