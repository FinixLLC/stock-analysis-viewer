## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through a four-party model connecting consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. The company is not a financial institution; it does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk. Instead, Visa earns revenue by providing transaction processing services—primarily authorization, clearing, and settlement—via its proprietary VisaNet network, which spans more than 200 countries. In fiscal 2025, Visa processed 258 billion transactions with a total payments and cash volume of $17 trillion. Its business model relies on an open partnership approach, offering modular capabilities through its "Visa as a Service" stack, which includes foundation network infrastructure, discrete services like risk management and authentication, and access layers via APIs and the Model Context Protocol (MCP) server. This structure allows Visa to serve traditional banks, fintechs, digital wallets, and governments, while also processing non-Visa-branded transactions by routing them to other networks.

Visa’s competitive moat is built on its massive scale and global connectivity, evidenced by nearly 5 billion payment credentials, over 175 million merchant locations, and connections to approximately 12 billion cards, bank accounts, and digital wallets. The company leverages its "network of networks" strategy to act as a single connection point for money movement, facilitating person-to-person, business-to-business, and government-to-consumer payments regardless of the originating network. Additionally, Visa differentiates itself through proprietary technology investments, including tokenization (with over 16 billion tokens provisioned), advanced fraud detection and risk management capabilities, and early adoption of artificial intelligence, such as the acquisition of Featurespace for real-time AI payments protection. The company also maintains strong brand recognition through high-profile sponsorships, including the International Olympic Committee and the FIFA World Cup, which it uses to showcase payment innovations.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, Visa aims to strengthen card-based payments and expand into non-card-based forms, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved spend such as cash, checks, and legacy ACH payments. Key initiatives include expanding "Tap to Everything" capabilities, enhancing token technology for agentic commerce, and improving cross-border performance. In CMS, the company is driving penetration in commercial payments and money movement. In VAS, Visa seeks to deepen client partnerships through innovative services. The company is also focused on extending its proprietary network to offer a single connection point for diverse endpoints and form factors, while continuing to invest in next-generation technologies like generative AI and stablecoins.

The most material risks flagged in the filing center on legal liabilities, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a U.S. litigation escrow account to address these claims. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025. This litigation is significant enough that it impacts the company’s capital structure, as the U.S. retrospective responsibility plan allows Visa to recover monetary liabilities through downward adjustments to the conversion rates of Class B common stock into Class A common stock. Additionally, the filing notes that GAAP operating expenses increased 30% year-over-year, primarily driven by higher litigation provisions and personnel expenses, highlighting the financial volatility associated with these legal matters.
