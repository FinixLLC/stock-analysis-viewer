## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QLYS_10-K_20260827.txt)

Qualys is a leading provider of a cloud-based platform that delivers integrated information technology (IT), security, and compliance solutions, operating primarily through a software-as-a-service (SaaS) model with renewable annual subscriptions. The company’s business model centers on the Qualys Enterprise TruRisk Platform, which allows customers to identify and manage IT and operational technology assets across on-premises, endpoint, cloud, container, and mobile environments. By leveraging a multi-tenant cloud infrastructure and shared core services, Qualys enables organizations to collect and analyze security data, discover and prioritize vulnerabilities, quantify cyber risk exposure, and automate remediation workflows. Revenue is generated primarily through subscription fees for access to various "Cloud Apps," with invoicing typically occurring at the start of the subscription term and recognized ratably over the period. In 2025, the company reported revenues of $669.1 million, a 10% increase from 2024, driven by both new customer acquisition and expansion from existing customers, with 76% of the revenue growth attributed to customers existing prior to 2024.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its integrated, single-platform approach that addresses the fragmentation of disparate security tools, offering a unified view of security and compliance posture for IT, security, and cloud teams. Key differentiators include the absence of hardware requirements for customers, real-time visibility via a web interface without plugins or VPNs, and seamless scalability. Qualys maintains one of the largest knowledge bases of vulnerability signatures in the industry, with security updates delivered in real-time. The platform’s architecture supports continuous visibility through physical and virtual sensors and cloud agents, enabling rapid deployment and lower total cost of ownership compared to traditional on-premise enterprise software. Additionally, the company offers a Private Cloud Platform (PCP) option for customers requiring on-premises or private cloud residency, further broadening its addressable market.

Management’s stated priorities focus on continuing to invest in research and development to improve existing solutions and develop new capabilities, as well as expanding sales and marketing efforts to support new solutions and drive growth. The company emphasizes the importance of maintaining a scalable, comprehensive, and end-to-end solution that helps customers escape the "tool-fragmentation dilemma." Management also highlights the need to continuously improve the platform’s ability to detect, measure, prioritize, and remediate cyber risk, particularly in response to the increasing complexity of IT infrastructures and the rapid adoption of cloud computing and serverless models. The company expects to continue expanding its shared cloud platform infrastructure and investing in customer support and operations teams to support business growth.

The filing flags several material risks, including the potential adverse impact of macroeconomic factors such as inflationary pressure, high interest rates, market volatility, and reduced customer spending on IT security. The company notes that uncertainty in the global economic environment could lead to extended sales cycles and reduced demand for its services. Additionally, the filing highlights risks related to the company’s reliance on a small number of large customers, as a significant portion of revenue is derived from the United States, and the potential for customers to reduce or eliminate their overall spending on IT security. The company also acknowledges the risks associated with its dependence on its cloud platform infrastructure and the need to continuously invest in technology to stay competitive. Furthermore, the filing notes that the company’s financial results are subject to the impact of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), which includes changes to domestic and international tax provisions that could affect its income tax provision and cash flows.
