## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GEV_10-K_20260906.txt)

GE Vernova is a global leader in the electric power industry, spun off from General Electric Company on April 2, 2024, and headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The company operates through three distinct segments—Power, Wind, and Electrification—designing, manufacturing, and servicing technologies that generate, transfer, convert, and store electricity. Its business model relies on a diversified portfolio of equipment and services, with a significant portion of revenue derived from servicing an installed base that generates approximately 25% of the world’s electricity. As of December 31, 2025, the company reported $150.2 billion in remaining performance obligations (RPO), reflecting strong demand for its solutions. The Power segment focuses on gas, steam, hydro, and nuclear technologies, including heavy-duty gas turbines and small modular reactors; the Wind segment provides onshore and offshore turbines and blades; and the Electrification segment offers grid solutions, power conversion, storage, and software to manage electricity from generation to consumption.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its ability to supply a broad array of advanced technologies for an intelligent, sustainable power system, which management identifies as a key differentiator. GE Vernova competes based on product performance, quality, branding, service, and price against major global rivals such as Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Power, Vestas, and ABB. A critical component of its competitive position is its extensive installed base, which generates recurring service revenue and allows the company to leverage long-term service agreements, such as the approximately 1,800 gas turbine units under such contracts with an average remaining life of ten years. Additionally, the company emphasizes its history of innovation and its capacity to reduce cycle times and ensure available capacity, which are cited as essential for remaining competitive in a market where demand is exceeding available capacity.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering global sustainability by enabling electrification and decarbonization, while simultaneously strengthening profitability and stockholder returns. Strategic initiatives include maintaining strong relationships with leading utilities and governments, servicing the existing installed base to drive cash flow, and improving margins through better underwriting and risk management. The company is actively streamlining its product portfolio to focus on core "workhorse" products to improve cost and quality, while using lean principles to enhance productivity. Capital allocation is focused on generating cash flow to invest in core businesses, targeted mergers and acquisitions—such as the announced $5.3 billion acquisition of the remaining stake in Prolec GE—and returning at least one-third of cash generation to stockholders. Furthermore, management prioritizes innovation in new technologies, including small modular nuclear reactors and direct air capture, to help customers navigate the energy transition.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the volatility of government policies and regulations that impact the energy sector. In the Wind segment, management notes that U.S. customers are dealing with policy uncertainty regarding tariffs and production tax credits, which has increased short-term demand volatility and led to a decrease in orders. Specifically, the U.S. Department of the Interior’s pause on leases for large-scale offshore wind projects has directly impacted the completion timeline of the Vineyard Wind project. In the Power segment, operating in emerging markets presents uncertainties in deal closures due to financing complexities. Across all segments, the company faces risks from global tariffs, which resulted in an estimated $250 million cost impact in 2025, as well as inflationary pressures and supply chain challenges. Additionally, the company highlights the need to adapt to evolving environmental regulations and the financial dynamics of securing capital for energy projects, noting that regulatory changes such as carbon pricing and renewable energy mandates can significantly impact the power generation landscape.
