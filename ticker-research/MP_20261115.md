## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MP_10-K_20260827.txt)

MP Materials Corp. is the largest producer of rare earth materials in the Western Hemisphere, operating a vertically integrated business model that spans the entire supply chain from mining to finished magnet manufacturing. The company’s operations are divided into two reportable segments: Materials and Magnetics. The Materials segment, centered on the Mountain Pass facility in California, handles upstream mining and midstream refining to produce rare earth oxides, primarily neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr), which are sold to customers in Japan, South Korea, and broader Asia. The Magnetics segment, based at the Independence Facility in Texas, converts these oxides into magnetic precursor products and neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) permanent magnets, with initial revenue generated from sales to General Motors and a long-term supply agreement with Apple Inc. A defining feature of the company’s current strategy is its shift away from selling raw concentrate to Chinese refiners; in July 2025, MP ceased all product sales to China to align with a new public-private partnership with the U.S. Department of War (DoW), which includes a price floor protection agreement for NdPr products and a guaranteed offtake for magnets produced at a future second facility, the 10X Facility.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its unique position as the only scaled, vertically integrated rare earth mining and processing site in North America. MP leverages the Mountain Pass ore body, described as one of the world’s largest and highest-grade resources, to achieve world-class production cost levels. This cost advantage is reinforced by a specific geological advantage in its processing method: the ability to remove lower-value cerium early in the separation process via oxidizing roasting, which significantly reduces the energy, reagents, and wastewater required to produce higher-value NdPr. Furthermore, the company’s strategic alignment with U.S. national security interests provides a distinct competitive edge, evidenced by the DoW’s commitment to guaranteeing at least $140 million in annual EBITDA for the 10X Facility and providing financing through preferred stock, warrants, and a $150 million promissory note. This government backing, combined with the scarcity of domestic alternatives, positions MP as the primary domestic source for critical materials in defense, electric mobility, and robotics.

Management’s stated priorities focus on executing a disciplined strategy to restore the full rare earth magnetics supply chain to the United States while maximizing long-term stockholder returns. Key operational goals include scaling the Independence Facility to 3,000 metric tons of magnets annually and constructing the 10X Facility to produce an additional 7,000 metric tons, thereby reaching a total domestic capacity of 10,000 metric tons per year. The company is also prioritizing the expansion of heavy rare earth element (HREE) refining capabilities at Mountain Pass, including the construction of a dedicated HREE Facility and the addition of samarium oxide separation, as well as the development of commercial-scale recycling lines for magnet scrap. Financially, management has focused on maintaining a strong balance sheet, holding $1.8 billion in cash and short-term investments as of December 31, 2025, to fund these capital-intensive expansions without excessive reliance on external debt, while also securing long-term demand through prepayments from Apple and offtake guarantees from the DoW.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the immediate financial impact of ceasing sales to China, which resulted in a 21% year-over-year revenue decline for the Materials segment in 2025. The company acknowledges that this strategic pivot has had a material negative impact on business results, cash flows, and liquidity in the short term, forcing it to stockpile unsold concentrate. Additionally, the company faces significant execution risk in its ambitious expansion plans, including the construction of the 10X Facility and the HREE Facility, which require substantial capital expenditure and precise commissioning timelines. There is also exposure to geopolitical and market volatility, as evidenced by the disruption caused by Chinese export controls and the subsequent U.S.-China trade deal, which has introduced uncertainty into the global rare earth market. Finally, the company relies heavily on a limited number of large customers for its downstream magnet sales, including General Motors, Apple, and the U.S. government, creating concentration risk in its revenue base.
