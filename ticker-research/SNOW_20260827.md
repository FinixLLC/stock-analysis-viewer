## Business Profile

Snowflake is a cloud computing company focused on delivering an AI-powered data platform designed to break down data silos and enable seamless access, sharing, and insights across organizations. Its business model centers around a customer-centric, consumption-based approach, where customers pay for the compute, storage, and data transfer resources they use on Snowflake's platform. The platform is built on a cloud-native architecture with three scalable layers—storage, compute, and cloud services—allowing it to handle diverse data types and scale efficiently across multiple public clouds.

Snowflake’s competitive moat stems from its ability to unify data into a single source of truth, enabling organizations to derive insights, apply AI, and share data securely. The company benefits from strong network effects as more customers adopt the platform, increasing the value of shared data and enhancing the ecosystem for all users. Snowflake also invests heavily in innovation, regularly introducing new features like Snowpark (a developer framework supporting multiple programming languages) and Snowflake Intelligence (for AI-driven data agents), which expand its capabilities across analytics, AI, and applications.

Management emphasizes growth strategies including platform innovation, customer acquisition, increasing usage among existing customers, global expansion, and ecosystem development. The company has a focus on industry-specific solutions, targeting sectors such as financial services, healthcare, and retail to provide tailored business solutions. Snowflake’s platform is used by 13,328 total customers as of January 31, 2026, with significant contributions from large enterprises, including 790 of the Forbes Global 2000.

Key risks flagged in the filing include macroeconomic challenges like inflation and high interest rates, which may impact customer spending and consumption patterns. Additionally, competition from legacy database products and big data offerings poses a threat to adoption rates. The company also faces risks related to regulatory compliance, cybersecurity threats, and the need to maintain its reputation as a trustworthy vendor.
