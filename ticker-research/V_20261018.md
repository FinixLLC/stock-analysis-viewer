## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce through its proprietary VisaNet network, which connects consumers, financial institutions, and merchants across more than 200 countries. The company’s business model is based on the "four-party model," where Visa provides transaction processing services—specifically authorization, clearing, and settlement—without acting as a financial institution, issuing cards, extending credit, or bearing credit risk. Revenue is generated through service fees, data processing fees, international transaction fees, and other value-added services, while the company explicitly notes that its fees are set independently from interchange reimbursement fees (IRFs) and merchant discount rates (MDRs). In fiscal 2025, Visa processed 258 billion transactions with a total payments and cash volume of $17 trillion, leveraging a network of nearly 14,500 financial institution clients and over 175 million merchant locations.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by approximately 5 billion payment credentials and connections to roughly 12 billion cards, bank accounts, and digital wallets. Visa differentiates itself through its "Visa as a Service" stack, which modularizes core capabilities like authentication and risk management into reusable components accessible via APIs and its Model Context Protocol (MCP) server, allowing partners to integrate Visa’s infrastructure into their own products. Additionally, the company cites its leadership in advanced technologies, including the early adoption of AI, generative AI, stablecoins, and agentic commerce, as well as its proprietary tokenization technology (Visa Token Service), which has provisioned over 16 billion tokens to enhance security and consumer experience.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, the strategy involves strengthening card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved consumer spend. Key enablers include expanding "Tap to Everything" use cases, such as Tap to Phone and Tap to P2P, and enhancing cross-border capabilities. In CMS, the focus is on driving penetration in commercial payments and money movement for businesses and governments. Furthermore, management is prioritizing the integration of next-generation technologies, such as generative AI and agentic commerce, to innovate within the payment technology sector.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are related to legal and regulatory matters, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a U.S. litigation escrow account to address claims associated with this litigation. The filing notes that the estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025, down from $49.6 billion in 2023, but emphasizes that these estimates do not include all potential claims and that the litigation provision can vary significantly based on facts and circumstances. Additionally, the filing highlights that GAAP operating expenses increased 30% year-over-year, primarily driven by higher litigation provisions and personnel expenses, indicating that legal liabilities and associated costs remain a significant factor affecting financial results.
