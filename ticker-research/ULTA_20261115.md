## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to bridge the traditional separation between prestige, mass, and salon product channels. The company’s business model centers on a "specialty retail concept" that offers a broad assortment of approximately 30,000 products from around 600 brands across all price points, complemented by in-store salon services and an omnichannel digital presence. Ulta targets "beauty enthusiasts," a demographic it estimates at 140 million in the U.S., leveraging a network of over 1,500 U.S. stores—typically located in high-traffic power strip centers—and international operations through its subsidiary Space NK in the U.K. and Ireland, a joint venture in Mexico, and a franchise in the Middle East. The company’s revenue is driven by retail merchandise sales, salon services, and e-commerce, with a significant portion of sales derived from its loyalty program members, who accounted for approximately 95% of total sales in fiscal 2025.

The company’s competitive moat is built on four key differentiators: a differentiated assortment that spans entry-level to luxury brands; a convenient omnichannel footprint that integrates physical stores with digital platforms and diverse fulfillment options (such as buy-online-pickup-in-store and same-day delivery); a best-in-class loyalty program (Ulta Beauty Rewards) with over 46 million members that provides deep consumer data for personalization; and a focus on human connection through knowledgeable associates and welcoming store environments. Ulta also maintains exclusivity advantages through its private label brand, Ulta Beauty Collection, and exclusive third-party brands, which collectively represented approximately 11% of net sales in fiscal 2025. Additionally, the company is expanding into adjacent growth areas such as wellness products and an online marketplace (UB Marketplace) to broaden its addressable market.

Management’s stated priorities are outlined in the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan unveiled in 2025, which focuses on three strategic pillars. First, the company aims to drive core business growth through operational excellence, best-in-class store execution, digital acceleration, and enhanced marketing and personalization. Second, it seeks to scale new, accretive businesses by accelerating its focus on wellness, expanding its e-commerce presence via UB Marketplace, growing its international footprint, and enhancing its retail media network, Ulta Beauty Media. Third, the company intends to align its foundation for future success by optimizing ways of working, streamlining its cost structure, and cultivating an associate-centered culture. Management emphasizes that long-term growth depends on executing these priorities to maintain market share leadership in the beauty and wellness categories.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on macroeconomic pressures and operational execution. The company notes that persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures have impacted consumer spending habits and could negatively affect sales growth and profitability. It also highlights the risk of elevated interest rates, which could increase the cost of debt, as evidenced by the shift from net interest income in fiscal 2024 to net interest expense in fiscal 2025 due to increased borrowings. Operational risks include the dependence on comparable sales, which have fluctuated in the past and are sensitive to general economic conditions, merchandise strategy, and marketing effectiveness. Additionally, the company faces risks related to its ability to respond to changing consumer preferences, competition from alternative distribution channels, and the costs associated with opening new stores, which can negatively impact cost of sales and require significant capital investment (approximately $2.4 million per store). The filing also notes that the company’s financial performance is dependent on its ability to manage inventory shrink, supply chain costs, and the deleverage of fixed expenses as it expands its store footprint.
