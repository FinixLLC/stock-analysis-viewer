## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (HUM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Humana Inc. operates as a vertically integrated healthcare company headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, serving approximately 15 million members in medical benefit plans and 4.7 million in specialty products. The company’s business model is anchored in two reportable segments: Insurance and CenterWell. The Insurance segment generates the vast majority of revenue, with 83% of total premiums and services revenue derived from federal government contracts, primarily through Medicare Advantage (MA) and stand-alone prescription drug plans (PDP). Individual Medicare Advantage is the dominant product, accounting for 70.3% of consolidated premiums and services revenue, while state-based contracts, including Medicaid and long-term support services, represent 11.2%. The CenterWell segment provides adjacent services such as pharmacy solutions, primary care, and home solutions, which are designed to enhance member health and reduce costs; these services are sold both to Humana’s own insurance members and to third parties. A core strategic element is the "integrated care delivery model," which combines quality care, high member engagement, and data analytics to transition providers from fee-for-service to value-based arrangements. As of late 2025, 68% of individual MA members were in value-based relationships under this model.

Humana’s competitive moat appears to be built on its scale in the Medicare market, having participated in the program for over 30 years with a national presence in all 50 states, and its vertical integration through CenterWell. The company cites its geographically diverse membership base as a factor that provides greater ability to expand provider networks and obtain favorable contract terms. Additionally, the integration of pharmacy, primary care, and data analytics allows Humana to manage costs and improve outcomes more effectively than pure-play insurers. However, the filing notes that the company recently exited its Employer Group Commercial Medical Products business, indicating that not all segments are strategically viable or competitive for the long term.

Management’s stated priorities focus on "putting health first" for teammates, customers, and the company, with a specific strategic goal of offering affordable healthcare combined with a positive consumer experience in growing markets. To achieve this, management is executing a substantial multi-year transformation program to re-align the cost structure, operating model, and technology footprint with evolving market conditions. This includes driving cost savings and productivity through workforce optimization and asset impairments, as well as continuing to invest in the integrated care delivery model to improve health outcomes and affordability. The company also emphasizes the importance of engaging members in clinical programs and assisting providers in transitioning to value-based care.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to regulatory and macroeconomic pressures. Humana is heavily dependent on federal government contracts, with 83% of revenue coming from these sources, making it vulnerable to changes in Medicare Advantage and Part D regulations, including risk-adjustment model changes and reimbursement rate adjustments. The company explicitly notes that it is subject to new laws and regulations, such as the Inflation Reduction Act, which have altered benefit designs and cost-sharing structures. Additionally, the filing highlights risks associated with the company’s reliance on providers for accurate coding under the CMS risk-adjustment model, as well as the potential for regulatory reviews comparing Medicare Advantage profitability to non-Medicare businesses. The multi-year transformation program itself carries risks, evidenced by significant charges for severance, asset impairments, and external consulting spend recorded in 2023, 2024, and 2025, with expectations for additional charges. Finally, the company faces competitive pressures and the challenge of sustaining profitability in a rapidly changing healthcare landscape, as reflected in its decision to exit certain unprofitable plans and counties in its individual Medicare Advantage business.
