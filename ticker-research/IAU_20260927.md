## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (IAU_10-K_20260906.txt)

The iShares Gold Trust (IAU) is a passive, grantor trust formed under New York law that holds physical gold bullion on behalf of investors, with each share representing a fractional undivided beneficial interest in the Trust’s net assets. The Trust’s business model is strictly limited to issuing shares in exchange for gold deposited by Authorized Participants, selling gold to cover expenses, and redeeming shares in exchange for gold; it does not actively manage its assets, lend its gold, or engage in trading to profit from price movements. The Trust seeks to reflect the performance of the price of gold before expenses, offering a cost-effective alternative to direct physical gold ownership by eliminating the complexities of assay, transportation, and warehousing for individual investors. The Trust’s net asset value grew significantly from approximately $33 billion at the end of 2024 to $68.4 billion at the end of 2025, driven primarily by a 65% increase in the price of gold and a rise in outstanding shares from 668.65 million to 842.85 million.

The Trust’s competitive position is derived from its status as a large, liquid, and accessible vehicle for gold exposure, listed on NYSE Arca and backed by physical gold held by JPMorgan Chase Bank N.A. as custodian. The filing does not explicitly define a traditional "moat" such as proprietary technology or exclusive contracts, but it highlights the Trust’s scale and the efficiency of its structure, which disperses the high costs of physical gold storage among all holders. The Trust relies on the arbitrage mechanism between the secondary market trading price and the net asset value (NAV) to mitigate premiums or discounts, a process facilitated by the creation and redemption of Baskets by Authorized Participants. The Trust’s primary operational advantage is its simplicity and low cost, with the Sponsor assuming most administrative expenses, leaving the Sponsor’s fee as the only ordinary recurring expense.

Management, represented by the Sponsor (iShares Delaware Trust Sponsor LLC, a subsidiary of BlackRock), does not state specific strategic priorities beyond the Trust’s objective to reflect the performance of the price of gold. The Sponsor’s role is primarily administrative and financial, involving the assumption of Trust expenses such as trustee fees, custodian fees, listing fees, and audit costs in exchange for a fee accrued daily at an annualized rate of 0.25% of the Trust’s net asset value. The Sponsor also maintains the Trust’s website and ensures compliance with SEC reporting requirements. There is no indication of active management strategies or growth initiatives beyond the passive accumulation of gold and the issuance of shares.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to the Trust’s structure and the nature of its assets. The Trust is not registered as an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940, meaning investors do not have the regulatory protections afforded to shareholders of registered investment companies, such as limits on transactions with affiliates or prohibitions on suspending redemptions. Additionally, the Trust is not a commodity pool under the Commodity Exchange Act, so investors lack the specific disclosures and protections associated with registered commodity pools. The Trust’s value is directly tied to the price of gold, which is subject to market volatility, and the NAV may fluctuate due to changes in gold prices, the number of outstanding shares, and the Sponsor’s fees. The Trust also faces risks related to the custody of its gold, including the potential for loss or damage, although the Custodian is required to maintain insurance and is liable for losses resulting from negligence, fraud, or willful default. The filing notes that the Trust’s liquidity is dependent on its ability to sell gold to cover expenses, and there are no other sources of liquidity.
