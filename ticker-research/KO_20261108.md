## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KO_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Coca-Cola Company operates as a global "total beverage company," selling products bearing its trademarks in over 200 countries and territories. Its business model is bifurcated into concentrate operations and finished product operations. The concentrate segment, which generated 59% of net operating revenues in 2025, involves selling beverage bases, syrups, and certain finished beverages to independent bottling partners who then mix, package, and distribute the final products. The finished product segment, accounting for the remaining 41% of revenues, consists primarily of consolidated bottling and distribution operations where the company directly sells finished beverages to retailers, distributors, or wholesalers. While concentrate operations drive the majority of unit volume (85%), finished product operations generate higher revenues but lower gross margins. The company’s portfolio includes major sparkling soft drink brands like Coca-Cola, Sprite, and Fanta, as well as categories for water, sports, coffee, tea, juice, and emerging beverages.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its extensive global distribution network, which includes both independent bottling partners and consolidated operations, allowing it to reach consumers through a vast array of retail channels, including fountain retailers and direct-to-consumer stores like Costa. Management attributes its success to the ability to connect with consumers by providing a wide variety of beverage options that meet diverse desires, needs, and lifestyles, supported by a portfolio of world-leading nonalcoholic sparkling soft drink brands. The scale of this network is significant, with beverages bearing the company’s trademarks accounting for 2.2 billion of the estimated 65 billion servings of all beverages consumed worldwide daily.

Management has outlined five specific growth pillars to support future value creation: shaping a portfolio of loved brands, transforming the marketing and innovation agenda, optimizing the Coca-Cola ecosystem, building talent and capabilities, and enhancing the company’s "license to win." To address evolving consumer preferences and health-related concerns, management prioritizes offering an expanded portfolio of reduced-, low-, and no-calorie options, providing transparent nutrition information, and investing in research for new noncaloric sweeteners. Additionally, the company is focusing on adapting to the digital marketplace by increasing investments in e-commerce, data analytics, and digital marketing to maintain brand relevance and loyalty.

The filing identifies several material risks, with a particular emphasis on regulatory and compliance challenges. The company faces significant exposure to evolving laws regarding carbon emissions, plastic waste, water scarcity, and wastewater discharge, which could increase compliance costs, capital expenditures, and production costs. There are also substantial risks related to data privacy and cybersecurity, including the potential for service interruptions, data misappropriation, and reputational damage from attacks on the company’s or its bottling partners’ information systems. Financial risks include exposure to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, given that $28.8 billion of 2025 revenues were derived from outside the United States, as well as interest rate volatility affecting debt costs and credit ratings. The company also flags the risk of impairment charges on noncurrent assets, such as trademarks and goodwill, and the potential for significant losses due to counterparty default in financial instruments or banking relationships. Finally, the filing notes ongoing challenges related to obesity and health-related concerns, shifting consumer demographics, and intense competition from both global and regional beverage companies in a rapidly changing digital landscape.
