## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QLYS_10-K_20260827.txt)

Qualys operates as a leading provider of a cloud-based platform that delivers integrated information technology (IT), security, and compliance solutions, primarily through its Enterprise TruRisk Platform. The company’s business model is centered on a software-as-a-service (SaaS) approach, generating revenue from renewable annual subscriptions that allow customers to access a suite of over 20 "Cloud Apps" via a unified web interface. These solutions enable organizations to identify and manage IT and operational technology assets across on-premises, endpoint, cloud, container, and mobile environments, while simultaneously discovering vulnerabilities, quantifying cyber risk, and remediating issues. Qualys differentiates itself by offering a single, multi-tenant cloud infrastructure that eliminates the need for customers to purchase or manage hardware, providing real-time visibility and seamless scaling. The company serves over 10,000 customers globally, including a majority of the Forbes Global 100, with revenue reaching $669.1 million in 2025.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its integrated, single-platform architecture that addresses the fragmentation of disparate security tools, allowing for a unified view of security and compliance posture. Qualys leverages a shared, extensible core services architecture and a highly scalable multi-tenant cloud infrastructure, which supports continuous visibility through physical and virtual sensors and cloud agents. A key differentiator is the company’s possession of one of the largest knowledge bases of vulnerability signatures in the industry, with security updates delivered in real-time. Additionally, the platform’s ability to correlate large volumes of data using Elasticsearch clusters and its TruRisk scoring capabilities, which quantify risk in financial terms based on severity, exploitability, and business context, provide a comprehensive risk management framework that is difficult for point-solution competitors to replicate.

Management’s stated priorities focus on continuing to invest in research and development to improve existing solutions and develop new capabilities, as well as expanding sales and marketing efforts to support new solutions and drive demand. The company emphasizes the importance of maintaining its global shared cloud platforms and private cloud offerings to support customer growth. Furthermore, management highlights the need to continuously improve the platform to address the increasing complexities of IT infrastructures, including the rapid adoption of cloud computing, containers, and serverless models. The company also notes its intent to continue investing in its people and professional services to support growth and compliance with legal and regulatory requirements.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the macroeconomic environment, including inflationary pressure, high interest rates, and geopolitical conflicts, which could lead to reduced customer spending on IT security and extended sales cycles. The company also notes risks associated with its reliance on a small number of large customers, as a significant portion of revenue is derived from the United States, and the potential for customers to reduce or eliminate their overall spending. Additionally, the filing highlights the risk that new sales personnel may not become productive immediately, potentially affecting future operating results, and the uncertainty surrounding the impact of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) on its tax provision and financial results.
