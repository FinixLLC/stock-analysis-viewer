## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CVS_10-K_20260906.txt)

CVS Health operates as an integrated health solutions company that connects care across four reportable segments: Health Care Benefits, Health Services, Pharmacy Consumer Wellness, and Corporate/Other. The company’s business model relies on a vertically integrated ecosystem that combines insurance, pharmacy benefits management (PBM), and direct care delivery. As of December 31, 2025, the company served approximately 37 million people through its Aetna insurance operations, which offer commercial, Medicare, and Medicaid plans. Simultaneously, its CVS Caremark PBM manages benefits for approximately 87 million plan members, while its Pharmacy Consumer Wellness segment operates roughly 9,000 retail locations and its Health Services segment delivers care through over 1,000 clinics, virtual platforms, and home-based services. This integration allows CVS to leverage data and scale to lower costs, improve health outcomes, and expand into personalized, technology-driven care delivery.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from this unique combination of assets, which creates a "world of health" around each consumer. By controlling the payer (Aetna), the pharmacy benefit manager (CVS Caremark), and the provider (clinics and retail pharmacies), CVS can coordinate care more effectively than fragmented competitors. The filing highlights that this integrated model enables the company to harness enterprise capabilities, such as a nationwide provider network of approximately 2.0 million participating providers and advanced information technology platforms, to deliver seamless experiences and better health outcomes. Additionally, the company cites its status as a leading provider in specific high-value niches, such as standalone Medicare Part D prescription drug plans and value-based primary care through subsidiaries like Oak Street Health and Signify Health, as key differentiators.

Management’s stated priorities focus on simplifying health care experiences, improving engagement, lowering costs, and delivering better health outcomes. The company aims to be "America’s most trusted health care company" by transforming consumer experiences and becoming the partner of choice for employers and individuals. Strategic execution involves best-in-class operational performance, harnessing innovation—particularly in artificial intelligence to automate and augment operational capabilities—and maintaining capital stewardship. The company is also actively expanding into personalized, technology-driven care delivery and health services to create new sources of value.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and economic pressures. The company faces significant exposure to changes in government policy and funding for Medicare and Medicaid programs, which account for a substantial portion of its revenues. There is also increasing legislative and regulatory activity seeking to regulate PBM activities, which could adversely affect the company’s ability to conduct business on commercially reasonable terms and standardize services across state lines. Operationally, the company notes that elevated medical utilization levels are expected to persist, potentially pressuring margins in both the Health Care Benefits and Health Services segments. Furthermore, the company faces economic risks from inflation, tariffs, and supply chain disruptions, as well as competitive pressure from value-oriented retailers that may drive lower front-store sales. Finally, the filing highlights specific financial risks related to litigation, including legacy litigation charges and opioid litigation, as well as the impact of a $5.7 billion goodwill impairment charge related to the Health Care Delivery reporting unit.
