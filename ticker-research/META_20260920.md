## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (META_10-K_20260906.txt)

Meta operates as a technology company with a dual-segment structure, comprising the Family of Apps (FoA) and Reality Labs (RL). The FoA segment, which generates substantially all of the company’s revenue, consists of social and messaging platforms including Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, and Threads. The primary business model for this segment is the sale of advertising placements to marketers, who use self-service tools or Meta’s global sales force to reach users across these applications and third-party sites. In 2025, FoA generated $198.76 billion in revenue, driven by a 12% increase in ad impressions and a 9% increase in average price per ad. The second segment, Reality Labs, focuses on developing virtual reality (VR) hardware such as Meta Quest, augmented reality (AR) initiatives like the Orion prototype and Meta Ray-Ban Display, and AI glasses. RL generates revenue from hardware, software, and content sales but operates at a significant loss, with 2025 operating losses of $19.19 billion, as it invests heavily in long-term research and development for what management describes as the next computing platform.

Meta’s competitive moat is built on its massive user base, which reached 3.58 billion daily active people (DAP) in December 2025, and its proprietary data infrastructure. The company leverages this scale to power an AI-driven discovery engine that recommends content and targets ads, creating a feedback loop that enhances user engagement and advertiser effectiveness. Meta also maintains a technological edge through its investment in frontier AI models, including the open-sourcing of Llama foundation models, and its development of proprietary hardware and neural interfaces. The company’s ability to integrate AI across its ecosystem—from content ranking to ad delivery—allows it to offer advertisers sophisticated targeting and measurement tools, although this capability is increasingly constrained by external regulatory and platform changes.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 focus on several key investment areas: artificial intelligence, Reels and the discovery engine, wearables, monetization of products and services, youth engagement, platform integrity, and infrastructure capacity. The company emphasizes a mission to build "personal superintelligence" and is directing significant capital toward AI initiatives that power content recommendation, advertising tools, and new generative AI experiences. Additionally, management is prioritizing the transition from 2D screens to immersive experiences, with approximately 70% of Reality Labs operating expenses expected to be allocated to wearables initiatives in 2026. The company also highlights the importance of operating efficiently while investing in these long-term opportunities, noting that 82% of total costs and expenses in 2025 were recognized in the FoA segment.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and competitive pressures that impact advertising revenue. Meta explicitly states that its advertising revenue is adversely affected by limitations on ad targeting and measurement tools resulting from changes in the regulatory environment, such as the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the Digital Markets Act, and U.S. state privacy laws. These regulations have forced Meta to shift its legal basis for behavioral advertising in Europe from "legitimate interests" to "consent" and to offer less personalized ad options, which reduces ad effectiveness. Furthermore, product changes by mobile operating system providers like Apple and Google have limited the collection of user data signals, negatively impacting the budgets marketers are willing to commit to Meta. The company also flags competition from other products and services, particularly among younger demographics, and notes that Reels, while growing in usage, monetizes at a lower rate than Feed and Stories. Additionally, Meta faces risks from macroeconomic conditions, geopolitical uncertainty, and the substantial ongoing losses in the Reality Labs segment, which depend on the profitability of the FoA segment for support.
