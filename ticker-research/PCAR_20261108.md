## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PCAR_10-K_20260827.txt)

PACCAR operates as a global technology company with a diversified business model spanning three primary segments: Truck, Parts, and Financial Services. The Truck segment involves the design and manufacture of light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial vehicles, marketed under the Kenworth and Peterbilt nameplates in North America, DAF in Europe, and a mix of Kenworth and DAF in Mexico, Australia, and South America. The Parts segment distributes aftermarket components for these vehicles, while the Financial Services segment generates revenue by financing or leasing PACCAR products across four continents and 26 countries. This integrated structure allows the company to capture value across the entire vehicle lifecycle, from initial sale through aftermarket support and financing.

The company’s competitive position is supported by its status as a market leader with significant brand recognition and a rigorous credit application process that underpins its financial services portfolio. PACCAR maintains a strong balance sheet and has demonstrated consistent profitability, earning net income for 87 consecutive years. Its manufacturing footprint is strategically optimized for local production; for instance, its North American factories are positioned to operate efficiently under new Section 232 truck tariffs by producing trucks locally for the U.S., Canada, and Mexico. Additionally, the company holds a substantial market share in key regions, including approximately 30% in the U.S. and Canadian heavy-duty truck market and over 13% in the European over-16-tonne segment.

Management’s stated priorities focus on maintaining high-quality standards, investing in innovation, and navigating current economic headwinds. The company is embedding artificial intelligence across its business to drive innovation and performance, while continuing to invest in next-generation clean diesel and alternative powertrains, autonomous driving technologies, and connected vehicle services. PACCAR is also actively managing the impact of import tariffs by leveraging local manufacturing capabilities and is investing in infrastructure, such as new robotic paint facilities and parts distribution centers, to enhance operational efficiency and customer delivery.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the negative impact of U.S. import tariffs and trade policies on order intake and profit margins, which has already reduced truck revenues and increased costs. The company faces exposure to changes in tariff policy, including pending Supreme Court rulings on the International Emergency Economic Power Acts (IEEPA), as well as shifts in emissions regulations, such as EPA NOx limits. Furthermore, PACCAR is subject to cyclical economic risks; a decline in freight transportation conditions or a weaker economy could lead to increased past-due accounts, truck repossessions, and credit losses within its Financial Services segment, while also suppressing new business volume and average earning assets.
