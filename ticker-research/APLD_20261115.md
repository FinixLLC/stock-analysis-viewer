## Business Profile

Applied Digital Corporation (APLD) operates as a U.S.-based designer, developer, and operator of high-performance data centers and colocation services, focusing on artificial intelligence (AI), high-performance computing (HPC), and blockchain workloads. The company’s business model is structured around two core segments: the HPC Hosting Business, which involves building and leasing purpose-built, high-density GPU infrastructure to hyperscalers and AI developers, and the Data Center Hosting Business, which provides energized infrastructure to crypto mining customers. In fiscal year 2026, APLD generated $385.3 million in revenue from its HPC segment and $154.4 million from its data center hosting segment. Additionally, the company completed the separation of its cloud services business into ChronoScale Corporation, a Nasdaq-listed entity in which APLD retains approximately 97% ownership, allowing APLD to focus its strategic resources on its core data center operations.

APLD’s competitive moat appears to be derived from its ability to secure long-term, large-scale infrastructure contracts with investment-grade hyperscalers and specialized AI providers, such as CoreWeave. The company has contracted for 400 MW of capacity at its Polaris Forge 1 campus, 200 MW at Polaris Forge 2, and 300 MW at Delta Forge 1, with initial operations anticipated between 2026 and 2027. This pipeline of committed capacity, combined with the specialized nature of high-density GPU architecture and the significant capital barriers to entry in constructing such facilities, positions APLD as a critical infrastructure provider for the AI industry. The company also leverages its existing operational footprint in North Dakota, where it operates 106 MW and 180 MW facilities at full capacity for crypto mining, providing a stable revenue base while it scales its HPC operations.

Management’s stated priorities, as inferred from the filing, center on the rapid construction and deployment of new HPC data centers to meet the growing demand for AI and HPC workloads. The company has actively pursued capital markets to fund this expansion, including issuing Series G Convertible Preferred Stock with increasing commitment amounts and floor prices, and entering into at-the-market sales agreements. APLD has also undertaken targeted reorganizations of its subsidiaries, such as APLD ComputeCo and APLD ComputeCo 2, to facilitate the issuance of senior notes (2030 9.250% Notes and 2031 6.750% Notes), indicating a strategic focus on optimizing its capital structure to support its aggressive growth trajectory. Furthermore, the appointment of Jason Zhang as President in January 2026 suggests a focus on strengthening executive leadership to manage this complex expansion.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant legal proceedings and the inherent uncertainties of large-scale construction and customer concentration. APLD is currently facing a securities class action lawsuit (McConnell v. Applied Digital Corporation) and a derivative action (Weich v. Cummins), both alleging that the company overstated the profitability of its Data Center Hosting Business and its ability to transition into a low-cost cloud services provider, as well as issues regarding board independence. While the derivative action was dismissed without prejudice, the securities lawsuit is pending a motion to dismiss, and the company notes that an adverse outcome could be material to its results of operations. Additionally, the company’s heavy reliance on a small number of large customers for its HPC segment, such as CoreWeave and unnamed hyperscalers, presents a concentration risk. The filing also highlights the risks associated with the company’s substantial debt and equity issuances, as well as the operational complexities of managing multiple large-scale construction projects simultaneously.
