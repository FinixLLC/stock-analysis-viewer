## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

Snowflake operates as a cloud-native data platform provider, delivering its "AI Data Cloud" through a consumption-based business model where customers pay for compute, storage, and data transfer resources rather than fixed licenses. The company’s architecture consists of three independently scalable layers—storage, compute, and cloud services—hosted across three major public clouds and 53 regional deployments. This structure allows organizations to consolidate structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data into a single source of truth, enabling analytics, data engineering, AI application development, and secure data sharing without moving underlying data. Snowflake generates the substantial majority of its revenue from these usage fees, with customers typically entering into capacity arrangements of one to four years or on-demand monthly contracts. As of January 31, 2026, the company reported $4.7 billion in revenue, a 29% year-over-year increase, and served 13,328 customers, including 790 Forbes Global 2000 companies that contributed approximately 43% of total revenue.

The company’s competitive moat is primarily driven by powerful network effects inherent in its AI Data Cloud ecosystem. As more customers, partners, data providers, and consumers join the platform, the value of data exchange and collaboration increases for all users, creating a sticky environment where data silos are broken down. This is reinforced by high switching costs and deep integration, evidenced by a net revenue retention rate of 125% as of January 31, 2026, indicating that existing customers significantly expand their usage over time. Additionally, Snowflake leverages a multi-cloud strategy to reduce customer reliance on any single public cloud provider, while its security, governance, and compliance features serve as critical barriers for regulated industries such as financial services and the public sector.

Management’s stated priorities focus on advancing the AI Data Cloud through continuous innovation, particularly in AI and machine learning capabilities like Snowflake Intelligence and Snowpark. The company is actively investing in research and development to strengthen its technical lead, expand its global footprint in EMEA, APJ, and Latin America, and drive growth by acquiring new large enterprise customers while simultaneously increasing consumption among existing ones. Snowflake is also prioritizing the expansion of its partner network and the Snowflake Marketplace to foster a broader ecosystem of data providers and consumers. Recent strategic acquisitions, including Crunchy Data and Observe, reflect a commitment to integrating PostgreSQL technology and AI-powered observability into its core platform.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the dependence on market adoption of its platform and AI technologies, which is often hindered by customers' existing investments in legacy systems and the need for internal compliance reviews. Macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, high interest rates, and geopolitical conflicts, pose a risk as customers rationalize budgets, shorten contract durations, and optimize consumption to manage cash flow. Furthermore, the company faces risks related to the efficiency of its platform; improvements in storage compression and compute optimization may allow customers to accomplish the same workloads with fewer resources, potentially lowering revenue if not offset by new workloads. Finally, Snowflake highlights the competitive dynamics in its target markets and the challenges of maintaining security and reputation in the face of cybersecurity threats as significant factors that could impact its ability to attract and retain customers.
