## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CMG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Chipotle Mexican Grill operates a large-scale restaurant chain, owning 3,938 U.S. locations and 104 international company-owned restaurants as of December 31, 2025, alongside 14 partner-operated international sites. The company’s business model is centered on a "Food with Integrity" proposition, serving fresh, responsibly sourced ingredients without artificial colors, flavors, or preservatives, while maintaining a fast-casual service speed. Revenue is generated primarily from food and beverage sales, with digital channels representing a significant and growing portion of the mix, reaching 36.7% of food and beverage revenue in 2025. The company manages its U.S. operations through 11 regions but reports as a single segment, relying on a supply chain of multiple regional distribution centers and carefully selected suppliers to maintain quality and mitigate pricing volatility.

Chipotle’s competitive moat is built on its brand reputation for high-quality, wholesome food at reasonable prices, distinct from competitors who may offer similar value propositions but lack the same scale or specific sourcing standards. The company differentiates itself through its "Responsibly Raised" meat standards and "responsibly grown" produce, supported by a robust food safety infrastructure that includes an independent Food Safety Advisory Council and strict supplier compliance. Additionally, Chipotle leverages a strong digital ecosystem, including its app, Chipotlane drive-through format, and Rewards Program, to drive customer engagement and convenience. The brand’s purpose-driven identity, focused on cultivating a better world and investing in employee development—evidenced by high internal promotion rates and extensive benefits like tuition assistance—further distinguishes it in a highly competitive fast-casual market where taste, price, and brand reputation are key differentiators.

Management’s stated priorities are encapsulated in their "Recipe for Growth" strategy, which focuses on five key areas: protecting the core business through operational and culinary excellence; evolving brand messaging and menu innovation to drive demand; modernizing the business model with technology, including AI and a relaunched Rewards Program; expanding global reach through both company-owned and partner-operated models; and cultivating top talent. A specific operational priority is the expansion of the Chipotlane format, with management expecting approximately 80% of new company-owned restaurants in 2026 to include this drive-through capability. Financially, management prioritizes capital allocation toward new restaurant construction, with 2026 capital expenditures expected to reach $834.1 million, while also maintaining a significant share repurchase program, with $1.7 billion remaining available as of year-end 2025.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the competitive intensity of the restaurant industry, where Chipotle competes with national and regional chains as well as food delivery services on taste, price, and convenience. Supply chain vulnerabilities are also highlighted, noting that certain key ingredients are purchased from a small number of suppliers, creating potential exposure to pricing volatility and supply shortages, although the company actively diversifies suppliers to mitigate this. Regulatory and environmental compliance costs are noted as increasing, though management asserts these have not had a material effect on results. Additionally, the company faces risks associated with labor costs, which increased as a percentage of revenue in 2025 due to wage inflation and lower sales volumes, and the impact of tariffs, which are estimated to add approximately 15 basis points to food, beverage, and packaging costs on an ongoing basis. Seasonality also poses a risk, with historically lower sales in the first and fourth quarters due to weather and holiday patterns.
