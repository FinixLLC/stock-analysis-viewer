## Business Profile

Source: FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-26), fetched live from SEC EDGAR and read directly (Item 1 Business and risk-factor sections).

What the company does: Applied Optoelectronics is a vertically integrated provider of fiber-optic networking products across four end markets — internet data center, CATV, telecom, and FTTH (fiber-to-the-home). It designs and manufactures products at varying levels of integration, from laser chips and components up through complete turn-key equipment, starting from proprietary laser fabrication (using both Molecular Beam Epitaxy and Metal Organic Chemical Vapor Deposition processes — the company says it is unique in combining both at high volume).

Segment/product mix (FY2025): CATV (53.8% of revenue) remains the largest market and AOI's most established business (headend, node, distribution equipment, and its own "Quantum Bandwidth" branded products sold directly to MSOs since 2023). Internet data center (42.9% of revenue) is the fastest-growing business, supplying optical transceivers for hyperscale AI datacenter buildouts (800Gbps and above, moving toward 1.6Tbps).

Manufacturing footprint: Three sites — Sugar Land, Texas (laser chip fabrication, the company's only laser-chip site); Taipei, Taiwan (optical components, transceivers, CATV outdoor equipment); Ningbo, China (lower-cost labor-intensive components/transceivers). The company explicitly frames its U.S. production capacity as a competitive advantage, both for customers wanting domestic sourcing and — per recent news — for positioning ahead of a possible U.S. regulatory ban on Chinese optical transceiver imports.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: proprietary laser fabrication technology (dual MBE/MOCVD process said to be difficult and time-consuming for competitors to replicate), vertically integrated and highly automated manufacturing (advantage in scaling production rapidly), and an industry-leading position in CATV where it serves a majority of the largest CATV equipment manufacturers.

Risks the company itself flags as most material:
1. Customer concentration — explicitly the largest flagged risk. Two customers (Digicomm, Microsoft) accounted for ~82% of FY2025 revenue combined; the loss of either would materially and adversely affect results. No long-term contracts — the company relies on recurring purchase orders.
2. Supply chain / limited-source dependency — depends on a limited number of suppliers for certain raw materials and components, particularly for custom parts.
3. Geographic manufacturing concentration — facilities split across the U.S., China, and Taiwan, exposing the company to trade-policy and tariff risk on multiple fronts (both as importer of certain inputs and as an exporter from China/Taiwan-based facilities).
4. Semiconductor-cycle demand volatility tied to hyperscale capex cycles.
