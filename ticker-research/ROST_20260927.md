## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ROST_10-K_20260906.txt)

Ross Stores, Inc. operates as a value-oriented off-price retailer through two distinct banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model centers on purchasing broad-based assortments of apparel, accessories, and home goods from third-party sources at discounted prices, leveraging a "packaway" inventory strategy where merchandise is stored in warehouses and released to stores based on seasonality and product mix to maximize margin opportunities. As of fiscal 2025, the company operated 2,267 stores across the United States and Puerto Rico, generating $22.75 billion in sales with an operating margin of 11.9%. The firm maintains a strong balance sheet, evidenced by its ability to repay $950 million in senior notes over the past two years, maintain a $1.3 billion undrawn revolving credit facility, and return significant capital to shareholders through dividends and a newly approved $2.55 billion stock repurchase program.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its ability to deliver "compelling values" and broad brand assortments, which management states resonates with customers who prioritize value and convenience. This positioning allows Ross to drive sustainable, profitable growth even in a macroeconomic environment characterized by inflation and geopolitical uncertainty. The scale of its operations, including a network of eight distribution centers and a large buying organization, supports its ability to source diverse merchandise and maintain efficient distribution, although the company notes that distribution costs can deleverage during periods of rapid expansion, such as the opening of its eighth distribution center in Arizona.

Management’s stated priorities for the current period focus on three key initiatives: enhancing merchandising by delivering broad assortments timely with more brands at compelling values; advancing marketing efforts to strengthen customer awareness and engagement; and making meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. Looking ahead to fiscal 2026, the company plans to accelerate store growth by opening approximately 110 new stores, including a reacceleration of dd’s DISCOUNTS expansion, and expects to invest approximately $1.1 billion in capital expenditures to support supply chain infrastructure, store improvements, and information technology systems.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily stemming from the broader macroeconomic environment, including tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions. Specifically, the company reported an estimated unfavorable tariff-related impact of approximately $0.16 per share in fiscal 2025, which contributed to a 20-basis point decrease in merchandise margin. Additionally, the company faces ongoing legal and regulatory proceedings, including class-action lawsuits in California alleging wage and hour law violations, as well as a lawsuit against insurance companies regarding pandemic-related business interruption claims. While management believes these legal matters will not have a material adverse effect on financial condition, they remain subject to significant uncertainties.
