## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LLY_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eli Lilly and Company operates as a single-segment human pharmaceutical business that discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets innovative medicines across four primary therapeutic areas: cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The company’s business model relies on a continuous cycle of discovering or acquiring new molecular entities to replace products that lose intellectual property protection or face competition from generics and biosimilars. Lilly manufactures its products in the U.S., Europe, and Asia, distributing them to approximately 90 countries through a mix of direct sales forces, third-party distributors, and its own digital platform, LillyDirect. In 2025, the company generated $65.2 billion in revenue, with 56% of total revenue derived from its incretin-based products Mounjaro and Zepbound, which are central to its current growth strategy.

Lilly’s competitive moat is built on its deep pipeline of differentiated biologics and small molecules, particularly in the rapidly evolving cardiometabolic space, where it holds a significant market position with tirzepatide. The company emphasizes that its long-term success depends on its ability to innovate at the pace of global scientific advancement and to access innovation through strategic partnerships, such as those with Boehringer Ingelheim, Incyte, and Innovent Biologics. However, the filing explicitly notes that the company faces intensifying competition globally, including from China, where competitors are expanding R&D capabilities and licensing products to multinationals. Additionally, Lilly faces structural threats from generic and biosimilar entry, which can rapidly erode revenue for non-biologic products, and from the emergence of counterfeit or mass-compounded incretins, which the company states may impact patient safety and create market risks through consumer confusion and price erosion.

Management’s stated priorities focus on sustaining growth in the cardiometabolic health segment while managing the complex regulatory and pricing landscape. Key initiatives include expanding manufacturing capacity to meet anticipated demand for current and prospective incretin products, advancing the clinical pipeline for next-generation candidates like orforglipron and retatrutide, and navigating new U.S. government pricing agreements. In November 2025, Lilly announced preliminary voluntary agreements with the U.S. government to lower Medicaid prices and launch new medicines with balanced pricing across developed nations, aiming to expand access for Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries. The company also prioritizes maintaining its competitive position as the therapeutic landscape evolves, recognizing that the durability of its cardiometabolic offerings depends on delivering further innovations that provide sufficient value to sustain growth momentum.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the uncertainty surrounding the definitive terms of the U.S. government pricing agreements, which may result in unfavorable pricing concessions that fail to be offset by expanded access revenues. Lilly also highlights significant exposure to the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which mandates government-set prices for certain drugs, with Jardiance, Trulicity, and Verzenio already selected for future price caps. Other critical risks include the high failure rate inherent in pharmaceutical R&D, the potential for supply disruptions or cost increases due to tariffs and trade restrictions, and regulatory compliance issues related to Good Manufacturing Practices, quality assurance, and safety signals. The company further notes that private payers and pharmacy benefit managers continue to exert significant pressure on pricing and reimbursement, while litigation and investigations related to the 340B program, insulin access, and product safety pose additional financial and reputational risks.
