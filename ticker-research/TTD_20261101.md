## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically providing a self-service platform that empowers ad buyers—including advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers—to create, manage, and optimize digital advertising campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on independence and objectivity, focusing exclusively on the buy side of the digital advertising ecosystem to avoid the conflicts of interest associated with competitors who also sell inventory. TTD generates revenue primarily through platform fees, which are generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform, as well as through value-added services and data offerings. Clients enter into ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic orders, a structure that supports a highly scalable model and has resulted in a customer retention rate exceeding 95% for over a decade. The platform leverages artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities, such as its "Koa" AI co-pilot, and a rich ecosystem of inventory, publisher, and data partner integrations to facilitate programmatic buying, including in connected television (CTV), display, audio, and native formats.

TTD’s competitive moat is derived from its buy-side focus, which allows it to build long-term, trusting relationships with clients who provide proprietary first-party data to the platform. This data-driven approach, combined with strict protocols for data ingestion, enables precise targeting and closed-loop measurement that TTD argues is superior to competitors who lack objectivity. The company emphasizes its "expressiveness," a bid-factor-based architecture that allows for highly granular campaign targeting, and its open platform approach, which utilizes enterprise APIs to allow clients to customize functionality and integrate third-party tools. Furthermore, TTD differentiates itself through transparency, offering real-time reporting and channel-agnostic access to premium omnichannel inventory, including direct relationships with major publishers and supply-side partners. The company believes this combination of data depth, AI-driven optimization, and lack of inherent conflicts of interest creates a durable advantage in a market where advertisers are increasingly selective about their technology partners.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients’ digital advertising spend, particularly as budgets shift toward programmatic channels, and expanding the client base by targeting the mid-market and international markets, specifically in Europe and Asia. The company is heavily focused on continuing innovation in technology, data, and measurement, with a specific emphasis on incorporating AI to automate campaign execution and simplify data buying through features like "Audience Unlimited." TTD also prioritizes the expansion of its omnichannel reach, particularly in CTV and other video formats, and aims to drive the adoption of third-party and first-party data, including retail data, to enhance campaign performance. The company anticipates that operating expenses will continue to increase as it invests in platform operations, hosting capabilities, AI development, and sales and marketing teams to support these growth initiatives, believing that its operating leverage will allow it to support long-term growth profitably.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the company’s dependence on the growth of the programmatic advertising market and its ability to retain and expand spend from existing clients in a highly competitive environment. TTD notes that future revenue growth depends on its ability to differentiate its platform’s value against competitors who may offer artificially low prices enabled by conflicts of interest. The company also highlights risks associated with macroeconomic uncertainty, including changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments, which could decrease demand for advertising and disrupt supply chains. Additionally, TTD identifies the rising costs of hosting infrastructure components and the need to manage increased costs associated with developing and hosting its AI-rich platform as potential impacts on profitability. The filing also points to the risks of seasonal fluctuations in revenue, the challenges of expanding in international markets where adoption is still developing, and the general uncertainty surrounding the duration and impact of global economic disruptions on its operational and financial performance.
