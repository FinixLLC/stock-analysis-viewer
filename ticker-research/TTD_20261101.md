## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically functioning as a demand-side platform (DSP) that empowers advertising buyers to create, manage, and optimize digital campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on a self-service platform augmented by enterprise APIs, allowing clients to customize functionality. TTD generates revenue primarily by charging a platform fee, which is generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform, alongside fees for value-added services and data. Clients, which include advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers, enter into ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic insertion orders, a structure that supports a highly scalable model and has yielded a customer retention rate exceeding 95% for over a decade.

TTD’s competitive moat is rooted in its independence and objectivity, as it focuses exclusively on the buy side of the advertising ecosystem, avoiding the conflicts of interest associated with competitors who also sell owned inventory. This alignment with buyers fosters trust, enabling clients to ingest proprietary first-party data and utilize TTD’s rich third-party data marketplace for precise targeting and measurement. The company further differentiates itself through its "expressiveness," a bid-factor-based architecture that allows for highly granular campaign customization, and its open platform approach, which integrates a broad ecosystem of inventory, publishers, and data partners. Additionally, TTD leverages AI capabilities, such as its "Koa" co-pilot and predictive models, to automate optimization and provide real-time insights, creating a data flywheel where increased usage improves the platform's predictive accuracy.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients' digital advertising spend, particularly as budgets shift toward programmatic channels, while simultaneously growing the client base, including expanding into the mid-market. The company is prioritizing global expansion, with specific focus on markets in Europe and Asia, and is heavily investing in the development and incorporation of AI into its platform to enhance automation and decisioning. Other key strategic goals include expanding access to omnichannel inventory, such as connected television (CTV), audio, and retail media, and continuing to innovate in data usage, measurement, and targeting capabilities to maintain a competitive edge in a fragmented digital landscape.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily the dependence on the continued growth of the programmatic advertising market and the ability to retain existing client spend in a highly competitive environment. TTD notes that future revenue growth relies on differentiating its value against competitors who may offer artificially low prices enabled by inherent conflicts of interest. The company also highlights risks related to macroeconomic uncertainty, including changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments, which could decrease demand for advertising services. Additionally, TTD identifies the rising costs of hosting infrastructure and computing power required to support its AI-rich platform as a challenge to profitability, alongside the risks associated with expanding into international markets where adoption may vary. Finally, the filing notes that the company’s operating expenses are expected to increase as it invests in technology, sales, and infrastructure, which may impact near-term profitability.
