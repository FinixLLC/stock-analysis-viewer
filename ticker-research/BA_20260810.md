## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-01-30), read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text. A locally cached file existed at `company_profile/financial_reports/BA_10-K_20260708.txt`, but on inspection it turned out to be a short (8-line) raw XBRL data stub rather than the readable 10-K prose — so the live filing was fetched directly instead.

What the company does: Boeing is one of the world's major aerospace firms, organized into three reportable segments:
- Commercial Airplanes (BCA) — develops, produces, and markets commercial jet aircraft; current production family includes the 737 narrow-body and 767/777/787 wide-body models, with continued development on the 777X program and 737-7/737-10 derivatives.
- Defense, Space & Security (BDS) — research, development, production, and modification of manned/unmanned military aircraft and weapons systems (fighter/trainer aircraft, rotorcraft/tilt-rotor, tanker aircraft), strategic defense/intelligence systems, and satellite systems (government, commercial, and space exploration).
- Global Services (BGS) — supports commercial and defense customers worldwide with supply chain/logistics management, engineering, maintenance/modifications, upgrades/conversions, spare parts, training systems, technical documentation, and digital solutions/analytics.

Human capital / labor relations — a significant, currently-material operational factor: As of Dec 31, 2025, Boeing's total workforce was ~182,000 (14% outside the U.S.), including ~72,000 union members across 32 independent agreements with nine unions in the U.S. plus 18 international employee representative bodies. The 10-K explicitly discloses two major, materially damaging strikes in the recent past: IAM District 751 (representing 30,000+ manufacturing employees, mostly in Washington state) struck for 53 days in 2024, halting production of most commercial aircraft and some defense products; IAM District 837 (~3,200 employees at St. Louis-area sites) struck for 101 days in 2025, disrupting programs including F/A-18, F-15, T-7A, MQ-25, and Weapons. The company explicitly warns it "may experience additional work stoppages in the future" that could materially affect the business — several union agreements have near-term expiration dates (SPEEA Professional/Technical agreements expiring January/October 2026, UAW Local 952 expiring January 2026), meaning labor-relations risk is not a resolved, historical issue but an ongoing, recurring one.

Moat, as described/implied by the company: Boeing operates in a global commercial aircraft duopoly (with Airbus) for large jetliners, and holds a long-standing, entrenched position as a prime defense contractor with the U.S. government and allied militaries. Deep intellectual property portfolio and decades of engineering/manufacturing know-how across both commercial and defense platforms, though the company itself states it doesn't believe its business would be materially affected by any single patent's expiration — the moat is more about manufacturing scale, certification/regulatory relationships, and installed base/backlog than about any specific IP asset.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, "Risks Related to Our Business and Operations" listed first):
1. Heavy dependence on commercial airline industry cyclicality — demand for commercial aircraft driven by long-term passenger/cargo traffic trends, which are themselves sensitive to economic growth, political stability, airline profitability, aircraft financing availability, trade policy, geopolitics, fuel prices, inflation, terrorism, pandemics, and environmental regulation. Because Boeing's backlog spans years, any of these macro/industry shocks can affect deliveries over an extended period, and the company enters firm fixed-price contracts that expose it to cost-overrun risk if program costs exceed original estimates.
2. Labor relations / work stoppage risk — directly evidenced by the two major strikes disclosed above (2024 IAM District 751, 2025 IAM District 837), both of which materially and adversely affected production, revenue, and financial position; the company explicitly flags the possibility of further stoppages.
3. Dependence on supplier/subcontractor networks, energy availability, and a large workforce of engineers/technical personnel — the 10-K separately flags "highly dependent on the availability and quality of essential materials" and "dependent upon the ability of a large number of U.S. and non-U.S. suppliers" as distinct risk factors.
