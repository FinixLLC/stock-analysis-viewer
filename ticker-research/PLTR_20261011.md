## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that integrates data, decisions, and operations for government and commercial clients. Its business model relies on four principal platforms: Gotham, which serves defense and intelligence missions; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform acting as a central operating system for enterprises; Apollo, a continuous delivery platform that manages infrastructure across various environments; and AIP, a generative AI platform that connects large language models to organizational data and operations. The company generates revenue through long-term contracts, typically ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the contract term. In 2025, the company reported $4.5 billion in revenue, with 54% derived from government customers and 46% from commercial customers, while 74% of revenue came from the United States.

The company’s competitive moat is built on the "Palantir Ontology," a core architectural element that maps data to meaningful context and integrates data, logic, and actions into a decision-centric architecture. This vertically integrated approach allows users of varying technical skills to collaborate and build interconnected workflows, creating high switching costs and deep operational embedding. Management notes that the complexity of data environments, high installation costs, and long sales cycles associated with large-scale opportunities raise barriers to entry for competitors. Furthermore, Palantir targets complex, high-risk projects that smaller technology companies often avoid due to the risk of failure, leveraging its ability to deploy solutions in days rather than the months or years required for internal development.

Management’s stated priorities include expanding access to its platforms across a broader swath of the market in both commercial and government sectors. Key strategies involve developing "industry operating systems" to serve entire sectors, forming strategic partnerships with cloud providers and other entities (such as Fujitsu) to extend distribution, and utilizing AIP bootcamps to demonstrate value rapidly. The company also emphasizes growing its direct sales force and focusing on account-level management to optimize growth opportunities for each customer. Additionally, management intends to capture a greater share of U.S. federal government spending on software systems and continues to evaluate partnerships and investments in complementary technologies to expand its product offerings.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the public sector and macroeconomic conditions. A significant portion of revenue comes from government customers, which are subject to uncertainties regarding budgets, spending levels, timing, and regulatory or policy changes. Many contracts contain termination for convenience provisions, and the U.S. federal government is prohibited from exercising contract options more than one year in advance, creating uncertainty around future revenue. The company also highlights risks associated with geopolitical tensions, such as conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, which could disrupt operations or the global economy, although management states current operations in these regions are not material to financial results. Additionally, the company faces risks from foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations and general macroeconomic factors, including interest rates and trade relations, which may impact customer operations and demand.
