## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (WELL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Welltower Inc. is a real estate investment trust (REIT) and S&P 500 company that operates at the intersection of housing and hospitality, focusing on rental housing for aging seniors in the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada. The company’s business model is built on a portfolio of over 2,500 communities, predominantly consisting of seniors housing, wellness housing, and post-acute care facilities. Welltower structures its operations through an umbrella partnership REIT (UPREIT), with Welltower Inc. owning approximately 98.378% of Welltower OP LLC, which conducts the day-to-day business. The company generates revenue through three primary segments: Seniors Housing Operating, which accounts for the majority of revenue and involves managing properties with various levels of care (from independent living to Alzheimer’s/dementia care) often through third-party management partners; Triple-net, where properties are leased to operators under long-term master leases that shift operating costs and capital expenditure obligations to the tenant; and Outpatient Medical, which consists primarily of triple-net leased buildings affiliated with health systems. Additionally, Welltower deploys capital into construction, real estate loans, and investments in unconsolidated entities, seeking to diversify by property type, relationship, and geography while targeting affluent micro-markets with secular demand.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale, geographic diversification, and a disciplined capital allocation strategy powered by a proprietary Data Science platform and the "Welltower Business System." Welltower emphasizes its ability to partner with high-quality, aligned operators who demonstrate staying power across economic cycles, while investing at a compelling basis to provide a margin of safety. The structure of its Triple-net leases, particularly the use of integrated master leases, creates a bundling feature that mitigates risk by preventing tenants from cherry-picking high-performing properties or rejecting leases on a property-by-property basis in bankruptcy, thereby spreading risk across the entire leased group. Furthermore, the company focuses on protecting downside risk and avoiding permanent capital loss while pursuing long-term compounding of per-share earnings growth.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term compounding of per-share growth for existing investors through a disciplined approach to capital allocation. The company seeks to allocate capital with a singular focus on generating durable cash flow growth by investing in real estate within affluent micro-markets that benefit from secular demand. Key strategic objectives include partnering with aligned, high-quality operators, investing at a compelling basis to ensure a meaningful margin of safety, and structuring investments to protect downside risk. Welltower also aims to diversify its investment portfolio by property type, relationship, and geographic location, while monitoring investments through rigorous asset management processes that include reviewing financial statements, assessing partner creditworthiness, and ensuring covenant compliance.

The filing flags several material risks and accounting complexities. A significant portion of the Seniors Housing Operating segment relies on third-party management partners, with the top three partners (Care UK, Cogir Management Company, and Sunrise Senior Living) accounting for 36% of segment revenues in 2025, creating a dependency on their effective and efficient management. The Triple-net segment carries risks related to tenant creditworthiness and the potential exercise of purchase options, which could result in property dispositions for less than full market value. Additionally, the company faces credit risk in its loan portfolio, requiring quarterly evaluations of collectability and the maintenance of an allowance for credit losses, with specific risks identified for loans with deteriorated credit quality. There is also exposure to construction risks, including cost overruns and completion guarantees, as well as the complexities of valuing stock-based compensation, particularly for Executive Key Employee LTIP Unit Awards which require Monte Carlo valuation models and significant management judgment regarding fair value and service periods.
