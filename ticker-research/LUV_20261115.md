## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines operates as a major U.S. passenger carrier providing scheduled air transportation across the United States and near-international markets, utilizing a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft to serve 117 destinations. The company’s business model is built on a low-cost strategy that leverages a point-to-point route structure, high-frequency short-haul service, and a single-aircraft-type fleet to maximize asset utilization and minimize ground time. While historically distinct for its open seating and no-change-fee policies, Southwest is currently executing a significant transformation to align with broader industry standards. This includes the introduction of assigned and extra-legroom seating, a redesigned boarding process based on fare bundles and loyalty status, and the launch of bag fees for most fare products, effective May 2025. The company is also expanding its distribution channels by selling tickets through third-party platforms like Expedia and Google Flights, launching its first international airline partnerships, and introducing 24-hour operations with overnight "redeye" flights to increase aircraft utilization.

Southwest’s competitive moat is derived from its unique route network, which allows for frequent, convenient nonstop flights between secondary and downtown airports that are typically less congested than major hubs. This structure, combined with the operational efficiencies of a single Boeing 737 fleet, enables high employee productivity and lower unit costs. The company maintains a strong brand presence through its "Hospitality" reputation and is actively broadening its customer base through new marketing channels and international connectivity partnerships.

Management’s stated priorities center on elevating the customer experience, improving financial performance, and driving shareholder value through modernization initiatives. Key strategic actions include optimizing the network by shifting capacity from less profitable short-haul routes to longer-haul, higher-margin markets, and reducing service in underperforming cities like Atlanta and Fort Lauderdale while expanding in Nashville and San Diego. Operationally, management is focused on cost discipline, including a 15% reduction in corporate positions to create a leaner organization, improving fuel efficiency through fleet modernization, and enhancing operational reliability through digital turn-around processes. The company is also pursuing fleet flexibility by managing its Boeing order book and retiring older aircraft to support capital allocation strategies.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to cost volatility and labor. Fuel and oil expense remains the second largest operating cost category, and the company has terminated its fuel hedging program due to high premium costs, leaving it exposed to volatile market prices. Labor costs constitute the largest operating expense, accounting for approximately 47% of total operating expenses in 2025, with the company noting that ratified contracts and rate inflation have negatively impacted its cost structure. Additionally, the company faces risks associated with aircraft delivery delays from Boeing, which has necessitated amendments to its purchase agreements and reliance on confidential credits. The filing also highlights the uncertainty surrounding the FAA certification timeline for the Boeing 737-7, which impacts the company's fleet modernization schedule.
