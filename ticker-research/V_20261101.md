## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through a proprietary transaction processing network known as VisaNet. The company’s business model is based on the "four-party model," where it provides authorization, clearing, and settlement services among consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. Visa explicitly states it is not a financial institution; it does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk. Instead, it earns revenue through service fees, data processing fees, international transaction fees, and other value-added services, while its clients (financial institutions) handle the issuance of cards and the setting of merchant fees. In fiscal 2025, Visa processed 258 billion transactions with a total payments volume of $17 trillion, leveraging a network of nearly 14,500 financial institution clients and over 175 million merchant locations across more than 200 countries.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by its position as a leader in digital payments with nearly 5 billion payment credentials and connections to approximately 12 billion cards, bank accounts, and digital wallets. Visa differentiates itself through its "Visa as a Service" stack, which offers modular, componentized capabilities such as tokenization (Visa Token Service), risk management, and fraud detection that can be accessed via APIs. Additionally, the company maintains a "network of networks" strategy, acting as a single connection point for money movement regardless of the originating or receiving network, which unifies the complex payments ecosystem. Its brand strength is further reinforced by high-profile sponsorships, including the International Olympic Committee, the NFL, and the FIFA World Cup 2026.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, Visa aims to strengthen its impact in card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved consumer spend. Key initiatives include expanding "Tap to Everything" use cases, enhancing token technology for agentic commerce, and improving cross-border capabilities. In CMS, the company is driving penetration in commercial payments and money movement. Furthermore, management is investing in next-generation technologies, including generative AI, stablecoins, and the acquisition of real-time AI payments protection technology (Featurespace) to enhance fraud prevention.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are primarily legal and regulatory in nature, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made deposits of $875 million into a U.S. litigation escrow account to address claims associated with this litigation. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025. This litigation is significant enough that it impacts the company’s capital structure, as Visa may conduct successive exchange offers for Class B common stock based on the resolution of these claims. Additionally, the filing notes that GAAP operating expenses increased 30% year-over-year, primarily driven by higher litigation provisions and personnel expenses, highlighting the financial volatility associated with these legal matters.
