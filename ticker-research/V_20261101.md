## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through a four-party model connecting consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. The company is not a financial institution; it does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk. Instead, Visa earns revenue by providing transaction processing services—specifically authorization, clearing, and settlement—via its proprietary VisaNet network, which spans over 200 countries and territories. In fiscal 2025, Visa processed 258 billion transactions, generating $17 trillion in total payments and cash volume. Its business model is increasingly componentized through the "Visa as a Service" stack, which offers modular capabilities such as authentication, risk management, and fraud detection via APIs and structured data exchange, allowing clients to integrate these services into their own products.

Visa’s competitive moat is anchored in its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by nearly 5 billion payment credentials, approximately 14,500 financial institution clients, and acceptance at over 175 million merchant locations. The company leverages this infrastructure to offer a "network of networks" strategy, positioning itself as a single connection point for money movement regardless of the originating or completing network. This is supported by proprietary technologies such as Visa Token Service (VTS), which has provisioned over 16 billion tokens to enhance security and reduce fraud, and advanced risk management capabilities that differentiate Visa in cross-border travel and e-commerce. Additionally, Visa maintains a strong brand presence through high-profile sponsorships, including the International Olympic Committee, FIFA World Cup 2026, and Formula One teams, which it uses to showcase payment innovations and drive consumer preference.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three key areas: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, Visa aims to strengthen its impact in card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved spend such as cash, checks, and legacy ACH. Key initiatives include expanding "Tap to Everything" use cases, such as Tap to Phone and Tap to P2P, and enhancing token technology for agentic commerce. In CMS, the company is driving penetration in commercial payments and money movement. In VAS, Visa seeks to deepen client partnerships by delivering innovative services, including the integration of artificial intelligence models and the acquisition of Featurespace Limited for real-time AI payments protection. The company is also investing in next-generation technologies like generative AI, stablecoins, and agentic commerce.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant legal liabilities related to the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a U.S. litigation escrow account to address these claims. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025. This litigation is managed through a U.S. retrospective responsibility plan, which involves downward adjustments to the conversion rates of Class B common stock into Class A common stock. Additionally, the filing notes that GAAP operating expenses increased 30% year-over-year, primarily driven by higher litigation provisions and personnel expenses, although non-GAAP metrics exclude these items to reflect underlying operating performance.
