## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX is the leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer in the United States and worldwide, operating over 5,200 stores and six branded e-commerce sites. The company’s business model is built on an "opportunistic buying" strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise closer to the time of sale rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to offer a rapidly changing assortment of quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers. By maintaining lean inventory levels and utilizing flexible store layouts without permanent fixtures, TJX creates a "treasure hunt" shopping experience designed to encourage frequent customer visits and rapid inventory turnover. The company operates through four segments—Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International—along with the Sierra business, leveraging synergies across these chains to share best practices and utilize substantial global buying power with over 21,000 vendors.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its flexible business model, which includes specialized inventory management, logistics, and low-cost operations. TJX’s buyers have greater visibility into consumer trends and pricing when buying close to need, which helps reduce markdown exposure. The company maintains a low cost structure by focusing advertising on retail banners rather than individual products, designing stores without heavy fixture investments, and operating an efficient distribution network. Additionally, TJX’s status as a reliable, prompt payer that does not demand typical retail concessions such as advertising allowances or performance-based return privileges makes it an attractive channel for vendors, ensuring continued access to leading branded merchandise.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day and expanding the business through new store additions, which remains an important part of the global growth strategy. The company aims to maintain its pricing gap relative to traditional retailers and merchandise margins through various economic cycles. Recent financial highlights indicate a focus on operational efficiency, with a decrease in both cost of sales and SG&A ratios in fiscal 2026, and a commitment to returning capital to shareholders, having returned $4.3 billion through share repurchases and dividends in the last fiscal year.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to global economic conditions and tariffs. TJX notes that tariffs on imports from China and other countries have led to significant volatility in the global economy, creating uncertainty regarding their impact on direct imports, vendor pricing, and consumer demand. While the company is implementing measures to mitigate these pressures, the overall impact remains uncertain due to ongoing trade negotiations and legal developments, including a recent U.S. Supreme Court decision invalidating certain tariffs. Other risks include the impact of foreign currency exchange rates on operating results, the need to maintain sufficient quality merchandise in a competitive market, and the challenges associated with managing a large, global workforce, including those covered by collective bargaining agreements.
