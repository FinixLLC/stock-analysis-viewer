## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PFE_10-K_20260827.txt)

Pfizer Inc. operates as a research-based, global biopharmaceutical company that generates the majority of its revenue through the discovery, development, manufacture, marketing, and sale of biopharmaceutical products and vaccines. The company’s business model relies on a dual engine of internal research and development (R&D) to replenish its pipeline and business development activities, such as acquisitions and collaborations, to address critical unmet patient needs. In 2025, Pfizer managed its commercial operations through a single reportable segment, Biopharma, which includes the PC1 and Pfizer Ignite units, with a recent structural change in early 2026 creating a new Global Hospital and Biosimilars organization to handle off-patent branded and generic sterile injectables. The company emphasizes a science-driven approach to extend and improve lives, leveraging its global resources to deliver therapies while continuously evaluating collaborations with patients, physicians, and payors to support access and affordability.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its intellectual property portfolio and its ability to defend patent rights against infringement, although the filing notes that this moat is currently under pressure. Pfizer expects a significant reduction in revenue from patent-based or regulatory exclusivity expiries between 2026 and 2030, with the rate of reduction expected to accelerate over the next few years. Specifically, the company anticipates a $1.5 billion impact from these expiries in 2026 alone. To sustain its position, Pfizer relies on its R&D capabilities to translate advanced science into new medicines and vaccines, as well as to add value to existing products by improving their effectiveness, safety, and dosing profiles. The company also cites its strong pipeline and the ability to leverage platform technologies as key differentiators in a market driven by an aging global population and increasing demand for innovative treatments.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 focus on maximizing the value of key transactions, delivering on critical R&D milestones, investing to maximize post-2028 growth, and scaling artificial intelligence (AI) across the business. To support these strategic goals, Pfizer is pursuing digital enablement, including automation and data-driven decision-making, to enhance productivity and accelerate innovation. The company has also launched multi-year cost realignment and manufacturing optimization programs to align its cost base with longer-term revenue expectations, driven in part by enhanced digital capabilities and process simplification. Additionally, management highlights the importance of responsible capital deployment and the strategic use of business development to strengthen the company’s capabilities and portfolio.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the significant revenue erosion expected from patent expirations and the increasing pressure on drug pricing and access. Pfizer faces substantial regulatory and pricing constraints, including the implementation of the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) in the U.S., which has already negatively impacted 2025 revenues by approximately $1 billion due to Medicare Part D redesign changes. The company notes that CMS has selected several of its products, such as Eliquis, Ibrance, Xtandi, and Xeljanz, for the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program, with government-set maximum fair prices taking effect between 2026 and 2028. Furthermore, Pfizer faces ongoing challenges from generic and biosimilar competition, as well as the inherent risks associated with R&D, including the possibility that product candidates may fail to achieve desired clinical endpoints or receive regulatory approval. The company also flags the risk of intellectual property loss or invalidation and the potential for legal charges related to product liability, particularly concerning discontinued or divested products.
