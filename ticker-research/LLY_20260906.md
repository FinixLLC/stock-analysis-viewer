## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LLY_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eli Lilly and Company is a pharmaceutical company that discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets human pharmaceutical products globally. Its business model focuses on creating innovative medicines across therapeutic areas such as cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The company operates in approximately 90 countries, with manufacturing facilities in the U.S., Europe, and Asia. Key products include Basaglar, Humalog, Jardiance, Mounjaro, Trulicity, and Zepbound, which are used to treat conditions like diabetes, obesity, cancer, and autoimmune diseases.

Lilly's competitive moat is built on its ability to innovate and develop cost-effective, patient-centric medicines. The company emphasizes early market entry and rapid patient access, leveraging partnerships and collaborations to enhance its product portfolio. Lilly faces competition from branded, biosimilar, and generic products, as well as increasing global competition, particularly from emerging markets like China. To maintain its competitive edge, the company focuses on continuous innovation, strategic partnerships, and efficient commercialization strategies.

Management's priorities include expanding access to medicines through digital platforms like LillyDirect, managing pricing agreements with governments, and advancing its clinical pipeline. The company is investing in new molecular entities and indication extensions across various therapeutic areas, reflecting a commitment to addressing unmet patient needs.

Key risks flagged in the filing include regulatory challenges, such as government-set drug prices under the IRA, which could impact profitability. Additionally, Lilly faces risks from generic and biosimilar competition, particularly for its biologics, and potential disruptions from tariffs or trade restrictions. The company also highlights risks related to counterfeit and misbranded drugs, which could affect patient safety and market dynamics. Other material risks include uncertainties in drug development, pricing pressures from private payers, and litigation related to product safety and pricing policies.
