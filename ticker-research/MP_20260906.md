## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MP_10-K_20260827.txt)

MP Materials Corp. is the largest producer of rare earth materials in the Western Hemisphere, operating a vertically integrated supply chain from mining to magnet manufacturing. The company owns the Mountain Pass Rare Earth Mine and Processing Facility in California, the only large-scale rare earth site in North America, and the Independence Facility in Texas for magnet production. Its operations are divided into two segments: Materials (upstream and midstream) and Magnetics (downstream). The Materials segment produces rare earth oxides like neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr), while the Magnetics segment manufactures NdFeB permanent magnets, crucial for electric vehicles, wind turbines, and defense systems. MP Materials has a strategic partnership with the U.S. Department of War to build domestic magnet capacity, including a second facility (10X Facility) and expanded heavy rare earth refining. The company also collaborates with Apple and General Motors, ensuring long-term demand and supply chain security.

**Competitive Moat:**
MP Materials benefits from being the sole large-scale rare earth producer in North America, offering a secure and sustainable supply chain. Its vertically integrated operations reduce reliance on foreign sources, and its partnership with the U.S. Department of War provides financial support and market guarantees, enhancing its competitive edge.

**Management Priorities:**
The company prioritizes expanding domestic production capacity, reducing dependency on China, and securing long-term contracts with key customers like Apple and GM. It also focuses on sustainability through recycling capabilities at Mountain Pass and maintaining high environmental standards in operations.

**Material Risks:**
Key risks include market volatility due to geopolitical tensions, reliance on a few major customers, and the complexity of scaling new manufacturing facilities. Additionally, the company faces challenges from China's export controls and potential supply chain disruptions.
