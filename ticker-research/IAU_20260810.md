### What IAU holds and how it's structured
- Holdings: 100% physical gold bullion, held as allocated bars in secured vaults (custodian: JPMorgan Chase Bank, per standard IAU structure); a single "security" (gold) backs the trust — no derivatives, no leverage.
- Structure: grantor trust — each share represents a fractional, undivided interest in the trust's gold holdings. NAV is struck daily off the LBMA Gold Price PM benchmark. An inventory list of the specific gold bars is published by the issuer for transparency.
- Expense ratio: 0.25% — roughly half the ETF category median, and cheaper than SPDR's GLD (0.40%), though costlier than some newer, smaller gold-trust competitors (e.g., GLDM, SGOL, BAR) that have undercut IAU's fee since its launch.
- Not actively managed — the trust simply holds gold; “performance” is entirely a pass-through of spot gold minus the expense drag.

## Business Profile — Adapted (What the Fund Does)

Source: iShares product page / fact sheet (via WebSearch), plus PaoBull cache.

IAU's "business" is singular and mechanical: hold allocated physical gold bullion and let share price track spot gold (LBMA PM fix) minus a 0.25% annual expense drag, offering investors gold-price exposure without needing to buy, store, or insure physical metal themselves. There is no product mix, no management team executing strategy, and no competitive moat in the operating-company sense — the trust's only "competition" is other gold ETFs (GLD, SGOL, GLDM, BAR) differentiated almost entirely by expense ratio, share price per ounce (affecting minimum investment granularity), custodian, and liquidity/AUM. IAU sits in the middle of that field: cheaper than GLD, but not the cheapest option in the category, while offering better liquidity/AUM than most of the smaller undercutting competitors.
