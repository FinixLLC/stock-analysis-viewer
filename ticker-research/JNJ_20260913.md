## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (JNJ_10-K_20260906.txt)

Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) operates as a global healthcare company with operations spanning pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and consumer health products. The company is organized into two main business segments: Innovative Medicine, which focuses on therapeutic areas such as oncology, immunology, and cardiovascular diseases, and MedTech, which includes surgical, orthopedic, and vision-related products. JNJ's business model relies heavily on innovation, research and development (R&D), and partnerships to maintain a competitive edge in the healthcare industry.

JNJ's competitive moat is bolstered by its extensive portfolio of intellectual property, including patents and proprietary technologies, which are critical for maintaining exclusivity over key products. The company also benefits from strong brand recognition and established distribution networks, enabling it to reach a wide range of customers globally. Additionally, JNJ's ability to adapt to regulatory changes and maintain compliance with stringent healthcare regulations contributes to its competitive advantage.

Management has emphasized several priorities, including innovation in product development, cost management, and enhancing operational efficiency. The company is also focused on navigating the complexities of global healthcare markets, managing regulatory risks, and addressing challenges related to pricing and reimbursement. Furthermore, JNJ prioritizes sustainability initiatives and corporate responsibility, aiming to align its operations with broader societal goals.

The 10-K filing highlights several material risks facing the company. Regulatory scrutiny and compliance costs are significant concerns, as changes in laws and regulations can impact product approvals and market access. Product liability lawsuits, particularly related to talc-containing products like Johnson's Baby Powder, pose substantial financial and reputational risks. Intellectual property challenges, such as patent disputes and generic competition, threaten the exclusivity of key pharmaceutical products. Additionally, economic factors like inflation, currency fluctuations, and geopolitical instability can affect JNJ's global operations and profitability.

The company also faces risks related to product development delays, regulatory approvals, and commercialization challenges. The high cost and uncertainty associated with bringing new products to market mean that unsuccessful R&D efforts could negatively impact growth. Furthermore, the increasing use of artificial intelligence (AI) in business operations introduces new risks, including data privacy concerns and potential obsolescence of AI technologies.

JNJ is also exposed to financial risks such as changes in tax laws, which can affect its effective tax rate and deferred tax assets. The company operates in numerous countries, making it vulnerable to currency exchange fluctuations and economic instability in certain regions. Illegal importation of pharmaceuticals from lower-priced markets poses another threat to sales and profitability.

In summary, while JNJ benefits from a strong competitive position rooted in innovation and brand strength, the company faces significant risks related to regulation, litigation, intellectual property, economic conditions, and product development uncertainties. Management must navigate these challenges effectively to maintain long-term success.
