Amazon.com, Inc. operates as a diversified technology and retail company organized into three primary segments: North America, International, and Amazon Web Services (AWS). The company offers subscription services like Amazon Prime to drive engagement through fast shipping and digital media access. For sellers, developers, and enterprises, Amazon earns revenue through service fees, fixed fees, and a percentage of sales for marketplace participation, as well as through AWS, which provides on-demand cloud computing, storage, database, analytics, and artificial intelligence services. Additionally, the company generates income from advertising services and digital content subscriptions.

However, the 10-K does not explicitly define a traditional "moat" in the sense of insurmountable barriers to entry; rather, it acknowledges that the internet and artificial intelligence facilitate competitive entry and comparison shopping, allowing new or smaller businesses to compete effectively.

The company is currently investing heavily in technology and infrastructure, including artificial intelligence and machine learning initiatives, AWS expansion, and new ventures such as satellite broadband and autonomous vehicles, with the expectation that these investments will support long-term growth despite potential short-term impacts on free cash flow.
