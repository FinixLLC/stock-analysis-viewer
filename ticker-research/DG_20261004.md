## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (DG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Dollar General (DG) is the largest discount retailer in the United States by store count, operating 20,959 locations across 48 U.S. states and Mexico as of February 2026. The company’s business model centers on a "compelling value and convenience proposition," offering everyday low prices—typically $10 or less—on a focused assortment of consumables, seasonal items, home products, and basic apparel. Approximately 80% of stores are located in towns with populations of 20,000 or fewer, serving low- and fixed-income households who rely on DG for essential goods. The company maintains competitive pricing through a low-cost operating approach, a limited number of items per category to maximize purchasing power, and a mix of national brands and private-label products. While consumables drive the majority of sales (82% in 2025), non-consumables categories contribute higher gross margins.

DG’s competitive moat is derived from its extensive geographic density, with approximately 75% of the U.S. population living within five miles of a store, and its ability to serve underserved small markets where larger retailers have limited presence. The company differentiates itself through a time-saving, small-box shopping format that allows for quick in-and-out transactions, supported by a low-cost infrastructure that includes a private fleet and strategically located distribution centers. This density and low-cost structure allow DG to maintain strong cash flows and capital investment returns despite intense competition from Walmart, Family Dollar, and Dollar Tree.

Management’s stated long-term operating priorities are fourfold: driving profitable sales growth, capturing growth opportunities, enhancing the company’s position as a low-cost operator, and investing in the growth and development of its teams. To execute these priorities, DG is focusing on initiatives such as the "Project Elevate" and "Project Renovate" remodel programs to refresh store layouts, expanding digital tools like the DG app and delivery services to increase convenience, and optimizing inventory shrink and damage reduction. The company is also pursuing international expansion in Mexico and continuing to refine its store formats, with new stores averaging approximately 8,500 square feet to accommodate broader assortments and enhanced non-consumable offerings.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to the macroeconomic environment affecting its core customer base. DG notes that its customers are often among the first to be affected by negative economic conditions, with spending pressured by inflation, high costs for rent, healthcare, and energy, and uncertainty regarding government assistance programs. A significant risk highlighted is the impact of tariffs; while 2025 results were not materially impacted, the company warns that current and potential future tariffs could significantly affect business operations and customer budgets, particularly following the Supreme Court’s invalidation of IEEPA tariffs and the resulting uncertainty regarding refunds and replacement tariffs. Additionally, the company faces risks from rising labor costs, increased occupancy expenses, and the need to maintain store manager retention, which is critical for financial performance.
