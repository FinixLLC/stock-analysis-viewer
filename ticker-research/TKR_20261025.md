## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Timken is a global industrial manufacturer that designs, manufactures, and services engineered bearings and industrial motion products, operating through two primary segments: Engineered Bearings and Industrial Motion. Founded in 1899, the company leverages its historical expertise in tapered roller bearings to offer a broad portfolio of motion control solutions, including linear motion systems, industrial drives, automatic lubrication, belts, chains, couplings, and seals. Timken’s business model is built on a high-touch, customer-centric approach where sales engineers collaborate closely with original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to solve complex application challenges. This initial collaboration establishes the product in the customer’s equipment, creating a long-term revenue stream through the aftermarket, where Timken sells replacement parts and services via independent distributors and its own service centers. The company operates in 44 countries with approximately 19,000 employees, serving diverse end-markets such as renewable energy, automotive, agriculture, aerospace, and heavy industry.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its deep technical expertise in metallurgy, friction management, and industrial motion, which allows it to differentiate on application engineering, product performance, and quality rather than price alone. Timken maintains a position as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, a specialization that supports its broader portfolio. Additionally, the company benefits from significant end-market and geographic diversity, which mitigates sector-specific downturns, and a strong aftermarket mix that provides recurring revenue throughout the equipment lifecycle. While the industry is highly competitive with rivals such as SKF Group and Schaeffler Group, Timken competes on a total value proposition that includes global technical support and the ability to serve demanding, high-reliability applications.

Management’s stated strategic priorities focus on three core elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. To achieve profitable growth, Timken targets highly fragmented markets and applications with significant aftermarket demand, leveraging its knowledge of materials science and power transmission. Operational excellence is pursued through a continuous improvement culture aimed at increasing efficiency, lowering costs, and eliminating waste. Regarding capital deployment, the company prioritizes investing in the core business through R&D and capital expenditures, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. In 2025, the company highlighted its commitment to sustainability by targeting a 50% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 greenhouse gas emissions intensity by 2030 and continued its record of consecutive annual dividend increases.

The filing identifies several material risks that impacted 2025 results and are expected to influence 2026 performance. The most significant financial headwind is the incremental cost of tariffs, which reduced operating income in 2025 and is expected to continue offsetting favorable pricing and volume gains in 2026. Additionally, the company faces exposure to commodity price volatility for raw materials, specifically special bar quality (SBQ) steel, though it mitigates this through surcharge mechanisms in customer contracts. Other flagged risks include lower organic demand in key regions like the Americas and Europe, which drove a decline in net income in 2025, and the need to manage working capital increases to support higher demand in 2026. The company also noted risks related to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, which can impact both revenue and costs, and the inherent uncertainty in backlog data, as actual shipments depend on customers' ever-changing production schedules.
