## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically providing a self-service platform that empowers ad buyers—including advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers—to create, manage, and optimize digital advertising campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on a buy-side focus, positioning itself as an independent referee in the marketplace rather than a participant with conflicting interests on the sell side. TTD generates revenue primarily by charging clients a platform fee, which is generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform, along with fees for value-added services and data. Clients enter into ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic orders, a structure that supports a highly scalable model and has contributed to a customer retention rate exceeding 95% for over a decade. The platform leverages artificial intelligence capabilities, such as its "Koa" AI co-pilot, and a rich ecosystem of inventory, publisher, and data partner integrations to facilitate programmatic buying, including in connected television (CTV), display, audio, and native formats.

TTD’s competitive moat is derived from its independence, data-driven approach, and open platform architecture. By focusing exclusively on the buy side, TTD avoids the conflicts of interest associated with competitors who also sell owned advertising inventory, fostering long-term trust with clients who are willing to share proprietary first-party data. This data advantage is reinforced by strict protocols governing data ingestion and a data marketplace that provides holistic audience views for precise targeting and measurement. The company’s technology stack features a bid-factor-based architecture that allows for high "expressiveness" in campaign targeting, alongside real-time optimization engines and predictive models that improve with increased usage. Furthermore, TTD maintains an open platform strategy via enterprise APIs, allowing clients to customize functionality and integrate third-party tools, which deepens client reliance on the infrastructure. The company also cites its access to premium omnichannel inventory and its ability to consolidate fragmented media buying options as key differentiators in a market where advertisers are increasingly selective about their technology partners.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients’ digital advertising spend, growing the client base—particularly in the mid-market—and continuing to innovate in technology, data, and measurement. The company is actively investing in the development and incorporation of AI, including features like "Audience Unlimited" for simplified data buying, and is expanding its omnichannel reach, with a specific emphasis on CTV and other video channels. International expansion is a key strategic focus, with management identifying opportunities in Europe and Asia, including markets such as the U.K., Germany, France, China, Japan, India, and Australia. To support these goals, TTD anticipates increasing operating expenses for platform operations, technology development, and sales and marketing, acknowledging that these investments may impact near-term profitability but are intended to drive long-term growth and operating leverage.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to macroeconomic uncertainty and the competitive landscape. TTD notes that changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments could slow economic activity and reduce demand for advertising, while rising costs for hosting infrastructure components pose a challenge to margins. The company highlights the risk that its future revenue growth depends heavily on retaining existing clients and gaining a larger portion of their spend in a highly competitive market, where competitors may offer artificially low prices enabled by conflicts of interest. Additionally, TTD identifies the need to continuously innovate and manage the increasing costs associated with developing and hosting its AI-rich platform as critical to maintaining profitability. The filing also points to the dependence on the overall growth of the programmatic advertising market and the successful adoption of its platform in international markets, where success depends on continued client adoption and new client acquisition.
