## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (AMD_10-K_20260906.txt)

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) operates as a high-performance computing and artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor company, delivering full-stack solutions that span cloud infrastructure, embedded systems, AI PCs, and gaming. The company’s business model is built on a broad portfolio of AI-optimized CPUs, GPUs, networking hardware, and software, which it sells through three primary reportable segments: Data Center, Client and Gaming, and Embedded. AMD generates revenue by selling these hardware products to original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), original design manufacturers (ODMs), distributors, and direct customers, while also licensing portions of its intellectual property. In 2025, the company reported net revenue of $34.6 billion, with the Data Center segment contributing $16.6 billion and the Client and Gaming segment contributing $14.6 billion. AMD’s competitive position is anchored in its ability to provide differentiated, end-to-end AI solutions, including semi-custom System-on-Chips (SoCs), adaptive SoCs, and accelerated processing units (APUs). The company claims a differentiated technology footprint in AI PCs by being the first to integrate a dedicated neural processing unit (NPU) on the same SoC as an x86 CPU, supporting a growing ecosystem of AI-enabled applications. Furthermore, AMD leverages its broad IP portfolio, leadership in design and integration, and advanced packaging technologies, such as its custom-ready chiplet platform and Infinity Fabric switch, to maintain its position as a preferred provider for custom-design silicon. The company also emphasizes its comprehensive and open AI software stack, including the AMD ROCm platform, to support diverse AI ecosystems and improve developer experience.

Management has explicitly stated that its key priority for 2025 was accelerating growth in the Data Center segment, driven by strong demand for AI accelerator products like the AMD Instinct MI350 Series GPUs and 5th Gen AMD EPYC server processors. To execute this strategy, AMD is pursuing an annual cadence of leadership for its Instinct GPU solutions and expanding its data center portfolio with new networking solutions, such as the AMD Pensando Pollara 400 AI NICs. The company is also investing heavily in full-stack innovation, combining hardware with system integration expertise through initiatives like the Helios AI rack-scale platform. Strategic acquisitions have been a central part of this approach, including the acquisition of ZT Systems to retain design operations for end-to-end AI solutions, and other acquisitions aimed at enhancing software capabilities, co-packaged optics, and high-speed inference expertise. Additionally, AMD entered into a significant multiyear strategic partnership with OpenAI to deploy 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs, underscoring management’s focus on scaling AI infrastructure and reinforcing its enterprise AI software stack.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on the company’s reliance on a small number of customers who account for a substantial part of its revenue and receivables. AMD also faces significant regulatory and geopolitical risks, evidenced by approximately $440 million in net inventory and related charges associated with U.S. government export controls on its AMD Instinct MI308 Data Center GPU products for China. Although the company received licenses to ship some products to China, the filing notes that U.S. government officials have expressed an expectation of receiving 15% of the revenue from these licensed sales, a requirement that has not yet been formalized in regulation. Other flagged risks include the potential impact of future accounting rules, the ability to obtain sufficient external financing on favorable terms, and the ongoing costs associated with enhancing information security controls. The company also notes risks related to the development of the legal and regulatory environment surrounding emerging technologies such as AI, as well as the uncertainty inherent in forward-looking statements regarding demand, strategy benefits, and the competitive landscape.
