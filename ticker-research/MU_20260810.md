## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/MU_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2025-08-28 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text.

What the company does: Micron is "an industry leader in innovative memory and storage solutions," delivering DRAM, NAND, and NOR memory/storage products under the Micron® and Crucial® brands, sold as components, modules, SSDs, managed NAND, multi-chip packages and wafers. The company manufactures at wholly-owned facilities (with some subcontracted processes), organized around global "centers of excellence" that combine fabrication and back-end manufacturing (notably in Singapore and Taiwan). Key product technologies include DRAM, High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM — a 3D-stacked architecture central to the AI/data-center growth story), DDR5, LPDDR (mobile/low-power), and GDDR (graphics/gaming).

Segment/product mix: Post-reorganization (Q4 FY2025), the four reportable segments are CMBU (hyperscale cloud + HBM), CDBU (mid-tier cloud/enterprise/OEM data center + storage), MCBU (mobile/client), and AEBU (automotive/industrial/consumer). Management explicitly describes shifting DRAM supply mix toward "data center and hyperscale cloud markets... with emphasis on HBM products" during FY2025 — a deliberate pivot toward the highest-growth, AI-linked segment of the business.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: Continuous, capital-intensive investment in proprietary product/process technology (in 2025, the company began shipping its first 1γ/1-gamma DRAM node using EUV lithography) that increases bit density and lowers per-bit manufacturing cost each generation. The company explicitly states it "faces intense competition in the semiconductor memory and storage markets" and that success depends on R&D returns, manufacturing utilization efficiency, and capital-spending discipline — a moat built on technology-node leadership and manufacturing scale rather than any single durable structural advantage (memory is a commodity-adjacent, cyclical business by nature).

Management's stated priorities (from MD&A "Industry Conditions"): capturing AI-driven demand that is "outpacing industry supply" (their words), continuing the DRAM mix-shift toward HBM/data-center, and prudently managing NAND supply growth against demand projections rather than over-expanding into another downturn.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A Risk Factor Summary, company's own ordering):
1. Average selling price volatility — the single largest disclosed risk category, reflecting the fundamentally cyclical nature of memory pricing (illustrated concretely by the FY2023→FY2025 swing from a $(5.8)B net loss to an $8.5B net profit).
2. Gross margin variability from a range of factors.
3. International operations / geopolitical risk, explicitly including the ongoing China CAC restriction described above.
4. Intense competition in memory/storage technology.
5. Capacity-expansion execution risk — realizing expected returns from new fab capacity.
6. Government-incentive compliance — tied to subsidies/incentives received for capacity investments (e.g., CHIPS-Act-style programs).
7. Customer/end-market concentration, including international customers.
8. AI-specific uncertainty — the 10-K explicitly lists "uncertainties and outcomes associated with the use and evolution of AI" as a distinct risk category, an acknowledgment that the current AI-driven demand surge is itself a source of forecasting risk, not just upside.
