## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MRVL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Marvell Technology is a fabless supplier of high-performance semiconductor solutions focused on data infrastructure, spanning from the data center core to the network edge. The company’s business model relies on developing complex System-on-a-Chip (SoC) architectures that integrate analog, mixed-signal, and digital signal processing functionality. Marvell serves two primary end markets: data center and communications and other. In fiscal 2026, the data center segment accounted for 74% of total net revenue, driven significantly by AI-related demand for custom products and electro-optics, while the communications and other segment represented the remaining 26%. The company offers both standard product solutions and optimized, customized solutions tailored to specific customer requirements, with a growing emphasis on the latter as customers seek greater differentiation. Key product lines include custom Application Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs), high-speed interconnects (such as PAM and coherent DSPs, silicon photonics, and co-packaged optics), Ethernet switches, and storage controllers. Recent strategic moves include the divestiture of its automotive ethernet business to Infineon Technologies for $2.5 billion in cash and the subsequent acquisitions of Celestial AI and XConn Technologies to expand its portfolio in photonic fabric and PCIe/CXL switching, respectively.

Marvell’s competitive position is built on its core strengths in developing and scaling complex SoC architectures and its deep system-level expertise. The company leverages a broad suite of differentiated intellectual property, including ultra-high-speed SerDes, ARM compute, security, storage, silicon photonics, and advanced packaging technologies such as die-to-die interconnects, chiplets, and co-packaged optics. Marvell has successfully executed multiple 5-nanometer designs and is currently progressing through 3-nanometer designs while developing its 2-nanometer generation platform. This combination of leading IP, innovative security firmware, and the ability to integrate hardware, firmware, and software into highly integrated solutions allows Marvell to provide system-level differentiation for next-generation artificial intelligence, data center, and networking applications.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating the company’s connectivity strategy for next-generation AI and cloud data centers, particularly through the integration of newly acquired technologies like Celestial’s Photonic Fabric and XConn’s switching silicon. The company is committed to delivering stockholder value through its capital return program, which includes significant stock repurchases and dividends; in fiscal 2026, Marvell returned $2.2 billion to stockholders via repurchases and dividends. Additionally, management is focused on securing long-term manufacturing capacity through reservation arrangements with foundries and partners to meet demand, while also monitoring the impact of tariffs and export restrictions on supply and demand. The company continues to benefit from government incentives in certain jurisdictions, which reduce income tax rates, though it acknowledges the potential impact of international minimum tax legislation (Pillar Two) on these benefits.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of U.S. government export restrictions on Chinese customers, which may reduce demand, cause customers to amass large inventories, or lead to the replacement of Marvell products with indigenous or competitor solutions. The company also highlights risks associated with its global operations, as approximately 77% of its net revenue in fiscal 2026 was shipped to customers with operations in Asia, exposing it to geopolitical and supply chain vulnerabilities. Other significant risks include the long product development cycle, which can delay revenue recognition relative to expense incurrence, and the inherent volatility of customer orders, which may lead to excess or obsolete inventory if demand predictions are inaccurate. Furthermore, the company notes that changes in international taxation laws could significantly reduce the income tax benefits associated with its government incentive programs, and there is a risk of clawback if minimum investment levels are not met.
