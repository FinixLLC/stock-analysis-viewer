## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ANET_10-K_20260906.txt)

Arista Networks operates as a provider of data-driven, client-to-cloud networking-as-a-service, positioning itself as an industry leader in high-speed Ethernet switching. The company’s business model is anchored by its "Centers of Data" strategy, which unifies four primary domains—AI Centers, Data Centers, Campus Centers, and WAN Centers—through a single platform. This platform is built on the state-oriented Extensible Operating System (EOS) and the Network Data Lake (NetDL), designed to replace legacy siloed networking with a consolidated, software-driven approach. Arista sells its switching and routing hardware, along with support services, to a global customer base segmented into Cloud and AI Titans, AI and Specialty Providers, and Enterprise customers. In fiscal year 2025, the company generated $9.0 billion in revenue, with approximately 48% derived from Cloud and AI Titans, 32% from Enterprise, and 20% from AI and Specialty Providers.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from a broad, differentiated portfolio that utilizes best-of-breed merchant silicon to deliver high-performance, purpose-built platforms. Arista differentiates itself through a rich, software-driven architecture that relies on a single operating system, data lake, and management solution (CloudVision), which allows for feature consistency across the entire product line and rapid evolution of offerings. This modular approach avoids vendor lock-in by adhering to open and standards-based technologies, while providing uncompromising reliability through robust quality assurance and automated diagnostics. Additionally, Arista cites its award-winning support system and industry-leading Net Promoter Score as key strengths, reflecting deep collaboration and trust with customers who value operational simplicity and predictable performance.

Management’s stated priorities focus on sustaining success in a market characterized by rapid technological evolution and the expansion of generative and agentic AI. To adapt, the company aims to increase sales in cloud, AI, and enterprise data center Ethernet switching/routing markets, as well as campus workspace markets, by leveraging its ability to rapidly develop new features and software applications. The growth strategy relies on maintaining agility and increasing investment in research and development to enhance the functionality of the existing cloud networking platform and expand product offerings. Furthermore, management prioritizes expanding the global sales force and deepening channel partnerships to reach new customers more effectively and increase sales to existing accounts.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on customer concentration and demand volatility. Two customers accounted for 26% and 16% of total revenue in 2025, and the company notes that unpredictability in the timing of orders from these high-volume customers—driven by capital expenditure fluctuations, internal cost-reduction initiatives, and long evaluation periods—creates significant revenue volatility. Additionally, the company faces risks related to the long-term trajectory of AI infrastructure demand, which is currently difficult to forecast, leading to increased volatility in deferred revenue balances and potential inventory write-downs if customer trials are not satisfied. Macroeconomic uncertainties, including inflation, supply chain disruptions, and tariff risks, also pose threats to supply chain continuity and product costs. While gross margin remained stable at 64.1% in 2025, management warns that supply chain inflation, material scarcity (such as in the memory market), and pricing pressure from large customers could further impact margins if costs cannot be passed on.
