## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-19), read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text. A locally cached file existed at `company_profile/financial_reports/HUM_10-K_20260708.txt`, but on inspection it turned out to be a short (8-line) raw XBRL data stub rather than the readable 10-K prose — so the live filing was fetched directly instead.

What the company does: Headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, Humana operates through Humana insurance services and CenterWell health care services, serving approximately 15 million medical benefit plan members plus ~4.7 million specialty product members (as of 2025-12-31). 83% of total premiums and services revenue in 2025 came from contracts with the federal government, including 14% from individual Medicare Advantage contracts in Florida alone (~1.0 million members) — a significant single-state/single-program concentration.

Segments:
- Insurance segment ($123.8B in premiums/services revenue, 96.3% of consolidated total): dominated by Medicare — Individual Medicare Advantage $90.4B (70.3% of segment revenue), Group Medicare Advantage $9.0B (7.0%), Medicare stand-alone PDP $6.8B (5.3%) — Total Medicare is 82.6% of Insurance segment revenue. Also includes state-based (Medicaid) contracts ($14.5B, 11.2%), Medicare Supplement, specialty benefits, and the company's Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) business.
- CenterWell segment: health and wellness services for Humana's own health plan members and third parties — pharmacy solutions, primary care, home solutions, and population health capabilities, positioned as central to Humana's "integrated care delivery model" (simplifying consumer interaction, engaging members in clinical programs, supporting providers' transition from fee-for-service to value-based care).

Moat, as described/implied by the company: A >30-year national Medicare presence with plans offered in all 50 states, providing a geographically diverse membership base the company says supports network expansion (PPO/HMO providers). The integrated Insurance + CenterWell model is positioned as a differentiator — vertically combining the health plan with the care-delivery/pharmacy/home-solutions assets that plan members actually use, aimed at both improving outcomes and capturing more of the healthcare dollar rather than just underwriting risk.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, top-listed risk):
1. Benefit-expense estimation and pricing adequacy — the top-listed risk factor, explicitly and directly relevant to the Q2 2026 guidance cut discussed above. Premiums in the health care business are generally fixed for one-year periods, so costs incurred in excess of benefit-cost projections are generally *not* recoverable within the contract year through higher premiums. The company names a long list of factors that can and often do cause actual health care costs to exceed estimates: increased utilization/cost of medical facilities and prescription drugs (including specialty drugs), new/costly treatments or technologies, membership mix shifts, changes in utilization management, manufacturer rebate changes, catastrophes/pandemics, medical cost inflation, and government-mandated benefit or eligibility changes.
2. Heavy federal-government revenue concentration (83% of premiums/services revenue in 2025) — implies material exposure to CMS rate-setting, Medicare Advantage policy changes, and government reimbursement dynamics, though this specific framing is drawn from the Business section rather than quoted verbatim from Item 1A.
