## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

Snowflake Inc. operates as a cloud-native data platform provider, delivering an "AI Data Cloud" that allows organizations to consolidate structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data into a single source of truth. The company’s business model is consumption-based, generating the substantial majority of its revenue from fees charged for compute, storage, and data transfer resources consumed on its platform. Customers typically enter into capacity arrangements with terms of one to four years or use on-demand monthly billing, with consumption often accelerating over contract terms. Snowflake’s architecture consists of three independently scalable layers—storage, compute, and cloud services—deployed across three major public clouds in 53 regional deployments globally. This multi-cloud, multi-region strategy enables seamless data sharing and collaboration without moving underlying data, supporting use cases in analytics, data engineering, AI, and applications.

The company’s competitive moat is driven by powerful network effects inherent in the AI Data Cloud. As more customers, partners, and data providers join the ecosystem, the value of the platform increases for all users due to enhanced data availability and exchange capabilities. This is supported by a high net revenue retention rate of 125% as of January 31, 2026, indicating that existing customers significantly expand their usage over time. Additionally, Snowflake benefits from a strong enterprise foothold, with 790 Forbes Global 2000 companies as customers, contributing approximately 43% of fiscal 2026 revenue. The platform’s ability to integrate diverse data types, offer optimized price-performance, and provide secure, governed data sharing creates high switching costs and deepens customer reliance.

Management’s stated priorities focus on advancing the AI Data Cloud through continuous innovation, particularly in AI and machine learning capabilities such as Snowflake Intelligence and Snowpark. The company aims to drive growth by acquiring new customers, especially large enterprises and organizations in regulated markets like financial services and the public sector, while simultaneously driving increased usage within its existing customer base. Snowflake is also prioritizing the expansion of its global footprint in EMEA, APJ, and Latin America, and is investing in its partner network and the Snowflake Marketplace to expand data content and collaboration. Recent strategic acquisitions, including Crunchy Data and Observe, reflect a focus on integrating PostgreSQL technology and AI-powered observability into the platform.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the company’s dependence on market adoption of its platform and AI technologies, which may be hindered by customers' existing investments in legacy systems or big data offerings. Macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, high interest rates, and geopolitical conflicts, pose risks as customers rationalize budgets, shorten contract durations, and optimize consumption to manage cash flow. There is also a risk that technological improvements, such as better storage compression or open data formats, could lead to lower resource consumption without a corresponding increase in new workloads, thereby reducing revenue. Additionally, the company faces risks related to its ability to attract and retain top technical and sales talent, maintain its reputation for security and trustworthiness, and effectively compete in dynamic markets where pricing and competitive dynamics may shift.
