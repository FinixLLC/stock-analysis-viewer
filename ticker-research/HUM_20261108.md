## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (HUM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Humana Inc. operates as a diversified health care company with two primary reportable segments: Insurance and CenterWell. The Insurance segment, which generated 96.3% of total premiums and services revenue in 2025, focuses on providing medical benefit plans primarily to Medicare beneficiaries, including individual and group Medicare Advantage, stand-alone prescription drug plans, and Medicare Supplement products, as well as state-based contracts for Medicaid and long-term support services. The CenterWell segment provides adjacent health care services, including pharmacy solutions, primary care, and home solutions, designed to enhance the member experience and potentially lower utilization and drug costs. The company’s business model relies heavily on federal government contracts, with 83% of total premiums and services revenue derived from such contracts in 2025. Humana employs an integrated care delivery model that combines quality care, high member engagement, and data analytics to manage costs and improve outcomes, while also offering providers opportunities to transition from fee-for-service to value-based arrangements. In 2025, the company finalized its exit from the Employer Group Commercial Medical Products business, citing that this segment was no longer positioned to sustainably meet commercial member needs or support long-term strategic plans.

Humana’s competitive moat is rooted in its scale, national presence, and integrated care delivery model. The company has participated in the Medicare program for over 30 years and offers plans in all 50 states, providing a geographically diverse membership base that facilitates the expansion of provider networks. With approximately 15 million members in medical benefit plans and 4.7 million in specialty products as of December 31, 2025, Humana leverages its size to negotiate favorable contract terms with providers. The integration of the CenterWell segment allows for a continuum of care, including primary physician-directed care and pharmacy solutions, which the company believes improves health outcomes and affordability. Additionally, Humana’s reliance on sophisticated data analytics and risk-adjustment methodologies to manage Medicare Advantage payments provides a structural advantage in navigating the complex regulatory environment of the Medicare program.

Management’s stated priorities center on driving value creation through cost-saving and productivity initiatives while funding growth in core businesses. The company has initiated a substantial multi-year transformation program to re-align its cost structure, operating model, and technology footprint with evolving market conditions, resulting in significant charges for severance, asset impairments, and external consulting in 2023, 2024, and 2025. Management emphasizes the importance of the integrated care delivery model to improve member engagement and provider relationships, noting that 68% of individual Medicare Advantage members were in value-based relationships at the end of 2025. Furthermore, management prioritizes strategic focus by exiting non-core businesses, such as the Employer Group Commercial Medical Products, to concentrate resources on Medicare and state-based contracts where the company holds a stronger competitive position.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to regulatory and legislative changes. Humana is subject to significant regulatory oversight, including laws such as the Inflation Reduction Act, which has altered Medicare Part D benefit designs and eliminated the coverage gap, impacting seasonal cost patterns and benefit ratios. The company notes that changes in Medicare Advantage premium rates, risk-adjustment methodologies, and provider network restrictions could materially affect its results of operations. Additionally, Humana faces risks associated with its heavy reliance on federal government contracts, particularly in Florida, where 14% of consolidated premiums and services revenue is derived from individual Medicare Advantage contracts. The filing also highlights risks related to the company’s ability to manage medical costs, as evidenced by the increase in the benefit ratio to 90.2% in 2025, and the ongoing challenges of integrating acquired businesses and managing workforce optimization costs.
