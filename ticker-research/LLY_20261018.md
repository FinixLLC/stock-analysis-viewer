## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LLY_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eli Lilly and Company operates as a single-segment global pharmaceutical business dedicated to the discovery, development, manufacturing, and marketing of human medicines, with a strategic focus on therapeutic areas including cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The company’s business model relies on a continuous cycle of innovation to replace aging products, supported by a diversified global manufacturing footprint in the U.S., Europe, and Asia, and distribution across approximately 90 countries. In the United States, Lilly utilizes a hybrid distribution model that includes traditional wholesalers (McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health) and its own direct-to-patient digital platform, LillyDirect, which has become a growing portion of the business. Internationally, the company often leverages third-party commercial partners and collaborations, such as its long-standing partnership with Boehringer Ingelheim for insulin and SGLT2 inhibitors, to drive market access and sales.

Lilly’s competitive moat is anchored in its deep pipeline of next-generation incretin therapies, particularly the tirzepatide franchise (Mounjaro and Zepbound), which accounted for 56% of total revenues in 2025. The company maintains a significant first-mover advantage in obesity and type 2 diabetes treatment, reinforced by a robust pipeline of proprietary assets including orforglipron, retatrutide, and insulin efsitora alfa. This competitive position is further protected by extensive intellectual property, rapid regulatory approvals (including FDA Commissioner’s National Priority Vouchers), and the high barriers to entry associated with complex biologic manufacturing. However, the filing explicitly notes that this moat is under pressure from intensifying global competition, particularly from China, where competitors are accelerating R&D and licensing capabilities, as well as from the threat of generic and biosimilar erosion once exclusivity periods expire.

Management’s stated priorities center on sustaining growth in the cardiometabolic health sector while navigating significant regulatory and pricing headwinds. A key strategic initiative involves expanding manufacturing capacity to meet anticipated demand for incretin medicines, with new facilities expected to become operational over the next several years. Management also prioritizes navigating the complex U.S. regulatory landscape, specifically through preliminary voluntary agreements with the U.S. government to lower Medicaid prices and align international pricing, aiming to secure broader patient access for obesity medicines by July 2026. Additionally, the company is focused on advancing its clinical pipeline, with numerous Phase 3 trials ongoing for new indications in liver disease, cardiovascular outcomes, and other therapeutic areas, while simultaneously managing the financial impact of increased marketing, selling, and R&D expenses.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the substantial uncertainty surrounding the final terms of the U.S. government pricing agreements, which may result in revenue shortfalls if expanded access does not offset pricing concessions. The company faces significant regulatory risk from the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which mandates government-set prices for certain drugs, with Jardiance, Trulicity, and Verzenio already selected for future price reductions. Other critical risks include the threat of counterfeit and mass-compounded incretins, which undermine brand integrity and patient safety, as well as the high failure rate inherent in pharmaceutical R&D. Additionally, the filing highlights exposure to global trade tensions, including potential tariffs that could disrupt supply chains or increase costs, and ongoing litigation and investigations related to the 340B program, insulin access, and product safety.
