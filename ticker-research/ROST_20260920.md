## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ROST_10-K_20260906.txt)

Ross Stores, Inc. operates as a value-oriented off-price retailer through two distinct banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model is built on purchasing broad-based assortments of branded merchandise at compelling values, leveraging a "packaway" inventory strategy where goods are stored in warehouses for less than six months before being released to stores based on seasonality and product mix. This approach allows Ross to maximize bargain opportunities while managing inventory levels, with packaway representing 37% of total inventory at the end of fiscal 2025. The company generates significant cash flow from operations, which it uses to fund capital expenditures, repay debt, pay dividends, and aggressively repurchase its own stock. In fiscal 2025, the company reported sales of $22.75 billion, with comparable store sales increasing by 5% driven by growth in both customer traffic and basket size.

Ross’s competitive moat appears to be derived from its ability to deliver value and convenience, supported by a rapidly expanding footprint and efficient supply chain. The company ended fiscal 2025 with 2,267 stores, having added 90 new locations that year, including entries into new geographic markets such as Puerto Rico and the New York Metro area. Management cites the ability to leverage overhead expenses and the focus on customer value as key drivers for continued expansion, with plans to open approximately 110 new stores in fiscal 2026. Additionally, the company maintains a strong balance sheet, evidenced by its ability to repay $700 million in senior notes in April 2025 and maintain a $1.3 billion revolving credit facility with no outstanding borrowings as of January 31, 2026.

Management’s stated priorities for the current period focus on three key initiatives: merchandising, marketing, and store experience. Specifically, the company aims to deliver broad-based assortments timely with more brands at compelling values, advance marketing efforts to strengthen customer awareness and engagement, and make meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. These initiatives are central to the company’s strategy for driving sustainable, profitable growth, even as management monitors broader macroeconomic factors such as tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to legal proceedings and macroeconomic headwinds. Ross is currently named in class and representative action lawsuits, primarily in California, alleging violations of wage and hour laws, as well as other proceedings involving commercial, product safety, and labor claims. While the company believes these will not have a material adverse effect on its financial condition, the litigation remains pending. Additionally, the company highlights the impact of tariff-related costs, which decreased merchandise margin by 20 basis points in fiscal 2025 and resulted in an estimated unfavorable impact of $0.16 per share. The filing also notes uncertainties related to the broader economic environment, including inflation and geopolitical conditions, which could affect the company’s operations and growth trajectory.
