## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (DG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Dollar General is the largest discount retailer in the United States by store count, operating 20,959 locations across 48 U.S. states and Mexico as of February 2026. The company’s business model centers on providing everyday necessities and general merchandise at low prices, typically $10 or less, through a network of small-box stores averaging approximately 7,500 square feet. Approximately 80% of these stores are located in towns with populations of 20,000 or fewer, targeting low- and fixed-income households that are often underserved by other retailers. The merchandise mix is heavily weighted toward consumables, which accounted for 82.0% of net sales in fiscal year 2025, supplemented by seasonal items, home products, and apparel. The company sources merchandise from national brands and private labels, leveraging a limited assortment per category to maintain strong purchasing power and low average costs.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its combination of value and convenience, specifically its dense geographic footprint which places approximately 75% of the U.S. population within five miles of a store. This proximity, combined with a "no frills" low-cost operating approach and a focused merchandise selection, allows Dollar General to offer competitive pricing while maintaining strong cash flows and capital investment returns. Management asserts that this model differentiates the company from larger discount, grocery, and online retailers by providing a time-saving, easy-to-navigate shopping experience that meets basic needs in a single trip.

Management’s stated long-term operating priorities are fourfold: driving profitable sales growth, capturing growth opportunities, enhancing the company’s position as a low-cost operator, and investing in the growth and development of its teams. To execute these priorities, the company is focusing on initiatives such as the rollout of "Project Elevate" for incremental store remodels, the expansion of digital tools including home delivery and the DG Media Network, and the optimization of sales mix to improve margins. The company also continues to pursue store growth, with plans to open approximately 450 new stores in the U.S. and 10 in Mexico in 2026, while simultaneously managing its existing base through relocations and closures.

The filing identifies several material risks, with significant emphasis on the macroeconomic environment affecting its core customer base. Management notes that customers are value-conscious and often among the first to be affected by negative economic conditions, such as inflation, unemployment, and changes in government assistance programs like SNAP. A particularly highlighted risk is the uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policy and tariffs; while current tariff rates did not materially impact 2025 results, management warns that future tariff changes or expansions could significantly impact business operations and customer budgets. Additionally, the company faces intense competition from retailers with greater financial resources, such as Walmart and Dollar Tree, as well as rising operating costs driven by labor availability, minimum wage increases, and inflationary pressures on occupancy and new store opening costs.
