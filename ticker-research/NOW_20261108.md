## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

ServiceNow operates as a cloud-based enterprise software provider that delivers the "ServiceNow AI Platform," a unified infrastructure designed to govern, secure, and manage artificial intelligence while streamlining digital workflows. The company’s business model is predominantly subscription-based, with revenues recognized ratably over contract terms, and its product portfolio is organized into four areas: Technology, CRM and Industry, Core Business, and Creator and Other. Rather than replacing existing infrastructure, ServiceNow positions its platform as the connective tissue that bridges AI’s analytical capabilities with the execution layer of business processes, enabling organizations to orchestrate complex, cross-functional workflows and integrate disparate systems without disrupting established operations.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from two decades of institutional knowledge regarding how work flows across enterprise silos, which allows it to build cross-functional workflows that reflect practical operational realities rather than idealized models. Management argues that while competitors may offer point solutions for data analysis, ServiceNow possesses a distinct advantage in delivering comprehensive integration, workflow orchestration, and enterprise-grade reliability required to operationalize AI insights. This is supported by a "one platform" architecture that maintains data integrity and regulatory compliance, as well as strong customer stickiness evidenced by a 98% renewal rate and a growing base of large enterprise clients, including 603 customers with annual contract values exceeding $5 million as of year-end 2025.

Management’s stated priorities center on helping customers navigate the transition to AI by providing the infrastructure necessary to convert AI-generated insights into controlled, governed business outcomes. The strategy emphasizes maintaining the platform’s role as the underlying infrastructure that directs and manages actions after information is generated, focusing on security, governance, and operational continuity. Additionally, the company is actively integrating advanced capabilities, such as the Moveworks acquisition for enterprise search and virtual agents, to enhance workflow automation and ensure that AI tools operate with human oversight and predefined guardrails.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the dynamic and rapidly evolving technology landscape, where competitive dynamics may shift as new approaches emerge and customer preferences evolve. Management acknowledges that while the AI era intensifies the need for unified platforms, it also presents uncertainties reminiscent of prior technological inflection points. Furthermore, the company highlights exposure to macroeconomic events, including global conflicts, interest rates, inflation, and tariffs, noting that while it does not currently believe these will have a material impact, persistent or worsening global economic disruptions could materially impact its business and results of operations.
