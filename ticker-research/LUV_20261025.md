## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines operates as a major passenger carrier providing scheduled air transportation across the United States and near-international markets, utilizing a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft to serve 117 destinations. The company’s business model is built on a low-cost strategy that leverages a point-to-point route structure, high-frequency short-haul service, and a single-aircraft-type fleet to maximize asset utilization and minimize ground time. While historically distinct for its open seating and no-change-fee policies, Southwest is undergoing a significant transformation in 2025 and 2026, introducing assigned and extra-legroom seating, a redesigned boarding process, and new fare bundles (Basic, Choice, Choice Preferred, and Choice Extra) that include bag fees for most products. The company is also expanding its distribution channels to third-party platforms like Expedia and Google Flights, launching its first international airline partnerships, and introducing 24-hour operations with overnight "redeye" flights to increase capacity and network connectivity.

Southwest’s competitive moat is derived from its unique route network, which allows for direct nonstop routing and high-frequency service between major markets without the congestion associated with traditional hub-and-spoke systems. The use of a single aircraft type, the Boeing 737, simplifies maintenance, training, and scheduling, contributing to operational efficiency. Additionally, the company’s focus on secondary or downtown airports, such as Dallas Love Field and Chicago Midway, reduces congestion and enables higher employee productivity. The filing notes that Southwest’s unique network structure allows for flexible capacity adjustments and redeployment of underperforming flights without structurally disrupting core markets, a capability that supports its ability to maintain competitive fares and high asset utilization.

Management’s stated priorities center on elevating the customer experience, improving financial performance, and driving shareholder value through transformational initiatives. Key strategic actions include modernizing the fleet by retiring older Boeing 737-700s and adding newer 737-8s to improve fuel efficiency, optimizing the route network to shift capacity from short-haul to more profitable longer-haul markets, and enhancing operational reliability through digital processes and reduced aircraft turn times. The company is also focused on cost discipline, including a 15% reduction in corporate positions in early 2025 to create a leaner organization, and is pursuing supply chain efficiencies and automation. Furthermore, management is expanding the network into new markets, such as Alaska and St. Thomas, and is actively managing its fleet size through sales and accelerated retirements to support its capital allocation strategy.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include extreme volatility in fuel prices, which remained the second-largest operating cost category in 2025 despite lower average costs; the company terminated its fuel hedging program in 2025 due to high premiums. Labor costs constitute the largest operating expense, accounting for approximately 46.9% of operating expenses in 2025, with risks stemming from ratified contracts and rate inflation. The company also faces risks related to aircraft delivery delays from Boeing, which have necessitated amendments to its order book and reliance on supplemental agreements for credits and concessions. Additionally, the filing highlights the uncertainty surrounding the FAA certification of the Boeing 737-7, which could impact fleet modernization timelines, and notes that the company is exposed to broader labor market challenges and the need to continuously optimize its network in response to changing travel patterns and demand.
