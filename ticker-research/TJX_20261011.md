## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX is the leading global off-price apparel and home fashions retailer, operating over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx, Marshalls, and Sierra), HomeGoods (HomeGoods and Homesense), TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is built on "opportunistic buying," a strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise closer to the time of sale rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to offer a rapidly changing assortment of quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers, creating a "treasure hunt" shopping experience that encourages frequent customer visits. The model relies on a flexible supply chain, including distribution centers in six countries and a global buying organization of over 1,400 employees sourcing from more than 100 countries and approximately 21,000 vendors. By maintaining lean inventory levels and avoiding promotional pricing, TJX achieves rapid inventory turnover and a low cost structure, with advertising focused on brand promotion rather than specific products.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale, which provides substantial buying power and diversified access to merchandise, as well as its ability to purchase less-than-full assortments and quantities ranging from small to very large without demanding typical retail concessions such as markdown allowances or return privileges. This flexibility, combined with a strong credit rating and prompt payment practices, makes TJX an attractive channel for vendors. Additionally, the company leverages synergies across its retail chains to share best practices and talent, while its specialized inventory management systems enable it to tailor merchandise to local preferences and maintain disciplined markdowns.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day and expanding the business through new store growth, which remains a key part of the global growth strategy. The company aims to maintain its pricing gap relative to traditional retailers through various economic cycles by reacting to wholesale price fluctuations. Operational priorities include investing in the supply chain to operate with low inventory levels, shipping efficiently, and precisely allocating merchandise to stores. Furthermore, management emphasizes fostering a supportive and inclusive culture for its approximately 377,000 associates, whom it considers key to executing the flexible business model.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the volatility in the global economy and uncertainty regarding tariffs. While TJX states it has been successful in mitigating tariff pressures, the filing notes that tariffs on imports from China and other countries have led to significant economic volatility. The impact of recent U.S. Supreme Court decisions invalidating certain tariffs and subsequent executive orders imposing new global tariffs remains uncertain, affecting direct imports, vendor pricing, and consumer demand. Other flagged risks include the impact of foreign currency exchange rates on operating results, particularly for international segments, and the competitive nature of the retail apparel and home fashion industry, where TJX competes on brand, fashion, price, quality, and shopping experience against a wide range of traditional and non-traditional retailers.
