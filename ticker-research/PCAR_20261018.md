## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PCAR_10-K_20260827.txt)

PACCAR operates as a global technology company with a diversified business model spanning three primary segments: Truck, Parts, and Financial Services. The Truck segment involves the design and manufacture of light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial vehicles, marketed under the Kenworth and Peterbilt nameplates in North America, DAF in Europe, and Kenworth and DAF in Mexico, Australia, and South America. The Parts segment distributes aftermarket components for these vehicles, while the Financial Services segment generates revenue by financing or leasing PACCAR products across North America, Europe, Australia, and South America. This integrated approach allows the company to capture value across the vehicle lifecycle, supported by a global financial services network with operations in 26 countries and a loan and lease portfolio of $22.80 billion.

The company’s competitive position is anchored by its strong brand recognition in premium trucking segments, evidenced by a 29.9% heavy-duty market share in the U.S. and Canada and a 13.5% share in Europe’s over 16-tonne market in 2025. PACCAR maintains a significant operational advantage through local manufacturing capabilities that minimize tariff exposure; for instance, its North American factories are optimally located to operate under new Section 232 truck tariffs, and it produces trucks locally for the U.S., Canada, and Mexico. Additionally, the company leverages its financial services arm to support market share growth, with PACCAR Financial Services capturing a 27.0% finance market share of new PACCAR truck sales in 2025. The company also cites its "consistent focus on quality" and strong balance sheet as enablers of long-term profitability, having earned net income for 87 consecutive years.

Management’s stated priorities center on investing in next-generation clean diesel and alternative powertrains, integrated connected vehicle services, and autonomous and advanced driver assistance systems. The company is actively embedding artificial intelligence across its business to drive innovation and performance, while continuing to invest in its U.S.-based battery joint venture, Amplify Cell Technologies. Operational priorities include enhancing manufacturing capabilities, such as the construction of a robotic chassis paint facility in Ohio and a new engine remanufacturing facility, as well as expanding parts distribution infrastructure, exemplified by a new Parts Distribution Center in Calgary. These investments are supported by planned capital expenditures of $725 to $775 million and R&D spending of $450 to $500 million in 2026.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily driven by macroeconomic and regulatory factors. The most significant recent impact has been from import tariffs imposed by the U.S. government and other countries, which have negatively affected truck order intake and profit margins since March 2025, despite mitigating actions. The company notes that its results could be further impacted by changes in tariff policy, including the pending U.S. Supreme Court ruling on International Emergency Economic Power Acts (IEEPA) tariffs. Other key risks include shifts in emissions regulations, such as the EPA’s reaffirmed NOx limits, and broader economic conditions affecting freight fundamentals. The Financial Services segment faces specific credit risks, where a decline in freight transportation conditions could lead to increased past due accounts, repossessions, and credit losses. Additionally, the company recorded a $264.5 million after-tax charge in 2025 related to civil litigation in Europe, highlighting legal risks as a material factor affecting earnings.
