## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ALL_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Allstate Corporation operates as a diversified insurance and financial services provider, with its core business model centered on the Allstate Protection segment, which sells private passenger auto, homeowners, and other personal lines insurance to consumers through exclusive agents, independent agents, and direct channels. The company also maintains a Protection Services segment that generates revenue from extended warranties (Protection Plans), roadside assistance, dealer services, and identity protection, as well as a Run-off Property-Liability segment that manages legacy asbestos, environmental, and other long-tail claims. Allstate’s strategy emphasizes offering affordable, simple, and connected protection products that allow customers to interact with the company when, where, and how they want, while simultaneously pursuing a multi-year "Transformative Growth" initiative. In 2025, the company executed significant portfolio simplification by divesting its employer voluntary benefits and group health businesses, resulting in substantial after-tax gains and a strategic refocus on its core property and casualty operations.

The company’s competitive moat appears to be derived from its scale, brand recognition, and diversified distribution channels, which allow it to maintain a large base of policies in force—totaling approximately 38.3 million in the Allstate Protection segment alone. This scale supports efficient risk pooling and the ability to implement rate changes to keep pace with increasing costs in acceptable markets. Additionally, the company leverages significant reinsurance programs and indemnification mechanisms, such as the Michigan Catastrophic Claims Association (MCCA), to manage aggregate countrywide exposure and catastrophe risk, thereby protecting its capital base and financial strength ratings. The integration of technology-enabled services, such as the Arity platform for lead generation and data services, further supports its operational efficiency and customer acquisition capabilities.

Management’s stated priorities focus on delivering affordable, simple, and connected protection products while managing underwriting profitability through strategic rate implementation and expense control. The company prioritizes maintaining acceptable returns in specific states by implementing rates that keep pace with rising claim costs, particularly in auto and homeowners lines. Furthermore, management is focused on capital management, as evidenced by the divestiture of non-core health businesses to boost shareholder equity and return on equity, which reached 42.3% in 2025. The company also emphasizes robust risk management, including the annual review of run-off reserves and the active management of catastrophe reinsurance programs to mitigate exposure to severe weather events and long-tail liabilities.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the inherent uncertainty in estimating claims reserves, particularly for long-tail liabilities such as asbestos and environmental claims, where adverse development can significantly impact financial results. The company also highlights the impact of macroeconomic factors, including inflation, supply chain disruptions, and new U.S. tariffs, which may increase the cost of vehicle parts, building materials, and labor, thereby driving up claims costs and potentially reducing demand for new insurance policies. Catastrophe losses remain a significant volatility factor, with 2025 catastrophe losses totaling nearly $5 billion. Additionally, the company faces credit risk from reinsurers, noting that adverse industry developments have led to a decline in the financial strength of some reinsurance carriers, increasing the risk of uncollectible recoverables. Finally, the company acknowledges the risk of widespread disruptive or destabilizing events and the evolving global trade environment, which could adversely affect its business operations and investment valuations.
