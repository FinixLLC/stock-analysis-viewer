## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through a four-party model connecting consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. The company does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk; instead, it earns revenue by providing transaction processing services—primarily authorization, clearing, and settlement—via its proprietary VisaNet network, which spans more than 200 countries. Visa’s business model is expanding beyond traditional card-based payments to include digital banks, fintechs, and governments, leveraging a "Visa as a Service" stack that offers modular capabilities such as authentication, risk management, and fraud detection through APIs and other integration methods. In fiscal 2025, the company processed 258 billion transactions with a total payments and cash volume of $17 trillion, serving nearly 14,500 financial institution clients and over 5 billion payment credentials.

Visa’s competitive moat is anchored in its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by its presence at more than 175 million merchant locations and its role as a preferred network for cross-border travelers due to its risk management and settlement capabilities. The company differentiates itself through proprietary technology investments, including its Visa Token Service (VTS) for security, Tap to Everything capabilities for contactless payments, and early adoption of artificial intelligence, such as the acquisition of Featurespace for real-time fraud protection. Additionally, Visa maintains a "network of networks" strategy that allows it to act as a single connection point for money movement across various endpoints, unifying a complex payments ecosystem and enabling diverse payment types from person-to-person to government-to-consumer.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, Visa aims to strengthen its impact in card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved consumer spend. For CMS, the company is driving penetration in commercial payments and money movement. In VAS, Visa seeks to deepen client partnerships by delivering innovative services. The company is also prioritizing the integration of next-generation technologies, including generative AI, stablecoins, and agentic commerce, while continuing to invest in its core infrastructure to support these growth areas.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant legal liabilities, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL), for which Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion in fiscal 2025 and made further deposits into a litigation escrow account. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 2025. This litigation is managed through a U.S. retrospective responsibility plan that adjusts the conversion rates of Class B common stock to Class A, directly impacting equity structure and potential share repurchases. Additionally, the filing notes that GAAP operating expenses increased significantly, driven by higher litigation provisions and personnel expenses, although non-GAAP measures exclude these items to reflect ongoing operational performance.
