## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/WELL_10-K_20260708.txt`), Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors, read and synthesized directly.

What the company does: Welltower is a healthcare-focused REIT "positioned at the center of the silver economy" — it owns 2,500+ seniors housing and wellness housing communities across the U.S., U.K., and Canada, structured as an UPREIT (Welltower Inc. owns ~98.4% of the operating partnership, Welltower OP LLC). Three reportable segments: Seniors Housing Operating (SHO) — 78% of FY2025 revenue and growing (up from 72% in FY2023) — where Welltower earns operating income directly via RIDEA joint-venture structures with 62 operating partners (largest: Care UK 14%, Cogir Management 12%, Sunrise Senior Living 10% of SHO segment revenue); Triple-net (11% of revenue) — long-term (10-20yr) leases where tenants pay all operating costs; and Outpatient Medical (7% of revenue, actively being partially divested).

Moat, as described in the company's own words: Welltower explicitly frames its edge as a capital-allocation and data-science advantage, not a traditional physical-asset moat: "Through our disciplined approach to capital allocation powered by our Data Science platform and superior operating results driven by the Welltower Business System... we aspire to deliver long-term compounding of per share growth." The company seeks to "partner with aligned, high quality operators who demonstrate staying power," invest "at a compelling basis that provides a meaningful margin of safety," and target "affluent micro-markets benefitting from secular demand" — i.e., a disciplined, analytics-driven acquisition/operator-selection process is the stated differentiator, layered on top of the structural secular tailwind of an aging population.

Segment mix trend: The clear structural story is the multi-year shift toward SHO (RIDEA operating model, direct exposure to occupancy/pricing upside) and away from Triple-net and Outpatient Medical (the latter actively being sold down via the ~$7.2B OM divestiture) — Welltower is deliberately concentrating into the highest-growth, most operationally-levered segment of its portfolio.

Management's stated priorities (from Item 1 and Q2 earnings release): Continued SHO occupancy/RevPOR growth, disciplined capital deployment (the $15.5B YTD 2026 investment pace is aggressive), and portfolio optimization via the Outpatient Medical divestiture. Management explicitly frames the current environment as one of "durability of outsized levels of cash flow growth" underpinning the 15% dividend increase.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A Risk Factor Summary):
1. Operator/tenant payment risk — the ability of operators and tenants to make payments, and the risk of tenant/operator/manager insolvency or bankruptcy, given the RIDEA/lease-dependent revenue model.
2. Acquisition/investment risk — unknown liability exposure on acquired properties, competition for acquisitions driving up prices, and joint-venture partner risk (62 SHO operating partners represent meaningful counterparty diversification but also complexity).
3. Government reimbursement exposure — changes to Medicare/Medicaid or government funding programs affecting operators who serve government-reimbursed patients (most acute in Long-Term/Post-Acute Care, a smaller legacy exposure within Triple-net).
4. Public health / demographic-shock risk — severe cold/flu seasons or broader public health crises affecting senior-housing occupancy, an explicit and sector-specific risk given the post-COVID sensitivity of this asset class.
5. REIT-status/legislative risk and ground-lease/international ownership risk (U.K./Canada exposure) are also flagged.
