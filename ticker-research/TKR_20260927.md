## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company is a global designer and manufacturer of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, supported by related services. Founded in 1899, the company operates through two reportable segments: Engineered Bearings and Industrial Motion. Its business model relies on a customer-centric, collaborative technical selling approach where sales engineers work closely with customers to develop solutions for demanding applications. Timken serves a diverse customer base across sectors such as renewable energy, automotive, agriculture, aerospace, and heavy industries, with no single customer accounting for more than 5% of net sales. The company generates revenue through the sale of high-performance components—such as tapered roller bearings, linear motion systems, gear drives, and automatic lubrication systems—as well as aftermarket services like bearing repair and power systems maintenance. This mix allows Timken to capture value throughout the equipment lifecycle, leveraging its global footprint of 116 manufacturing facilities and 74 distribution centers across 44 countries.

Timken’s competitive moat is built on its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, a position it has leveraged to develop a broad portfolio of industry-leading products. The company differentiates itself through sophisticated application engineering, proprietary internal geometries, high-precision tolerances, and specialized metallurgy and friction management expertise. This technical depth enables Timken to offer high load-carrying capacity and long service lives in challenging environments, competing primarily on total value proposition—including product design, performance, quality, and global technical support—rather than price alone. The company also benefits from a diversified brand portfolio, including Timken, GGB, Philadelphia Gear, and Rollon, which provides access to various market niches and end-users.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three primary strategic elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. To achieve profitable growth, Timken aims to expand into new and existing markets by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and power transmission, with a specific emphasis on highly fragmented sectors that value reliability and offer significant aftermarket demand. Operational excellence involves a continuous improvement culture aimed at increasing efficiency, lowering costs, eliminating waste, and enhancing cash flow. Regarding capital deployment, the company prioritizes investing in the core business through capital expenditures and R&D, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. In 2025, the company highlighted its progress toward reducing greenhouse gas emissions intensity by 50% by 2030 and its commitment to consistent dividend growth.

The filing identifies several material risks and operational challenges. A significant headwind in 2025 was the impact of incremental tariff costs, which reduced operating income and increased cost of products sold. The company also faces exposure to raw material price volatility, particularly for special bar quality steel, though it mitigates this through surcharge mechanisms in customer contracts. Additionally, Timken is subject to demand fluctuations across its diverse end-markets, with 2025 results showing lower organic sales due to reduced demand in the Americas and Europe. The company also carries risks related to its pension obligations, evidenced by net actuarial losses in 2025, and faces competition from major global players such as SKF Group and Schaeffler Group. Finally, while the company maintains a substantial backlog, it notes that actual shipments depend on customers' ever-changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales.
