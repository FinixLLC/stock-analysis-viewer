## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LNT_10-K_20260906.txt)

Alliant Energy operates as a regulated investor-owned public utility holding company headquartered in Madison, Wisconsin, with a primary business model centered on providing electric and natural gas services to approximately 1.4 million customers in the Midwest. Through its two wholly-owned utility subsidiaries, Iowa Power & Light (IPL) and Wisconsin Power & Light (WPL), the company generates, distributes, and transports energy, while also maintaining a non-utility portfolio under Alliant Energy Financial (AEF) that includes interests in transmission assets, venture capital, wind energy, and supply chain solutions. The company’s competitive moat is derived from its status as a regulated monopoly within its service territories, supported by non-exclusive franchises in Iowa and permits of indefinite duration in Wisconsin, which secure its right to operate in public right-of-ways and municipalities. This regulatory framework, combined with the capital-intensive nature of utility infrastructure and the high barriers to entry for competing in regulated retail markets, provides a stable, predictable revenue base.

Management’s stated priorities focus on delivering affordable, reliable, and resilient energy while pursuing attractive investor returns through a strategy of "purpose-driven" growth. Key strategic elements include expanding generation capacity to meet evolving demand, particularly from data centers and industrial growth, with plans to add approximately 1,600 MW of new natural gas resources, 1,000 MW of energy storage, and 1,300 MW of renewable generation over the next five years. The company emphasizes modernizing its distribution system for resiliency, leveraging tax benefits from the Inflation Reduction Act to lower customer bills, and making customer-focused investments in technology and safety. Additionally, management prioritizes workforce development and safety culture, viewing an engaged and skilled workforce as critical to executing its strategic objectives.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily stemming from the company’s heavy reliance on regulatory approvals and the capital-intensive nature of its growth strategy. A significant risk is the uncertainty surrounding the timing and magnitude of load growth from new data center customers, as actual demand increases are subject to interconnection delays and other factors, meaning executed agreements do not guarantee immediate revenue. The company also faces regulatory risks, including the potential for rate adjustments if its return on common equity falls outside the authorized range set by the Iowa Utilities Commission (IUC), and the impact of a retail electric base rate moratorium on IPL through September 2029. Furthermore, the company is exposed to financial risks associated with its large capital expenditure plans, including the need for substantial financing, such as the conditional loan guarantees from the U.S. Department of Energy, and the operational risks inherent in constructing and operating new generation and storage facilities.
