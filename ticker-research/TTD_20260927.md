## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically functioning as a demand-side platform (DSP) that empowers ad buyers to create, manage, and optimize digital advertising campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on a self-service platform augmented by enterprise APIs, allowing clients to customize functionality. TTD generates revenue primarily by charging a platform fee, which is generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform, alongside fees for value-added services and data. Clients, which include advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers, enter into ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic insertion orders, a structure that supports a highly scalable model and a customer retention rate exceeding 95% for over a decade.

TTD’s competitive moat is rooted in its independence and objectivity, as the company focuses exclusively on the buy side of the advertising ecosystem, avoiding the conflicts of interest associated with competitors who also sell owned inventory. This alignment with buyers fosters trust, enabling clients to ingest proprietary first-party data into the platform under strict protocols. The company leverages a data-driven approach, having built a data-management platform before its ad-buying technology, which provides access to rich third-party datasets and facilitates closed-loop measurement. Additionally, TTD differentiates itself through an open platform architecture that supports a rich ecosystem of inventory, publisher, and data partner integrations, along with advanced AI capabilities such as "Koa," an AI co-pilot that enhances decisioning, optimization, and automation.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients’ digital advertising spend, particularly as budgets shift toward programmatic channels, and expanding the client base, including a focus on the mid-market. The company is actively pursuing international growth, with specific investments in markets such as the U.K., Germany, France, China, Japan, India, and Australia. Technologically, TTD prioritizes continuous innovation in AI, data usage, and measurement capabilities, including the development of new value-added services and the expansion of omnichannel inventory access, particularly in connected television (CTV), audio, and other video formats. Management also emphasizes the importance of maintaining transparency and channel agnosticism to support long-term client relationships.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily the dependence on the overall growth and adoption of the programmatic advertising market, as TTD’s revenue is directly tied to client spend on its platform. There is a risk that existing clients may reduce their spend or that the company may fail to differentiate its value against competitors who offer artificially low prices enabled by conflicts of interest. Macroeconomic uncertainty, including changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments, poses a risk to global economic activity and advertising demand. Additionally, the company faces rising costs for hosting infrastructure and computing power required to support its AI-rich platform, which could impact profitability. Finally, the success of international expansion initiatives depends on continued adoption in newer markets, and the company notes that seasonal fluctuations, particularly lower activity in the first quarter, affect revenue consistency.
