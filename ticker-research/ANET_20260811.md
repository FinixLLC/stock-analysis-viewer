## Business Profile

Source: live SEC 10-K fetch (FY2025, filed 2026-02-17, self-extracted and synthesized directly from Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors).

What the company does: Arista designs and sells "Centers of Data" networking-as-a-service infrastructure — high-performance Ethernet switches, routers, and the EOS operating system plus CloudVision management software — spanning four domains: AI Centers, Data Centers, Campus Centers, and WAN Centers. Core AI-era product line is the Etherlink portfolio (800G+ switches, moving toward 1.6 Tbps platforms) addressing AI cluster "Scale Up / Scale Out / Scale Across" networking needs as hyperscalers move from proprietary interconnects (InfiniBand, NVLink) toward open Ethernet standards.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: (1) A single unified operating system (EOS) and single data lake (NetDL) across the entire product line, versus competitors' fragmented domain-specific stacks; (2) "avoids expensive vendor lock-in" via open, standards-based technology — a direct positioning against Cisco's more proprietary approach; (3) an award-winning support model (TAC) driving high Net Promoter Scores; (4) deep silicon-vendor relationships (see Industry section) that let Arista focus R&D on software/systems rather than chip design.

Segment/customer mix: Product revenue is dominated by Core (AI/Cloud/Data Center) at 65% of product-category revenue; two customers alone represented 42% of FY2025 total revenue combined — a structural concentration risk balanced against Arista's position as the preferred Ethernet-networking supplier to multiple hyperscalers simultaneously (Google Cloud, AWS, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud all cited as customers per the EVCDM data below).

Management's stated priorities (from Q2 2026 earnings commentary): CEO Jayshree Ullal frames the "Arista 2.0" platform strategy — extending from data center into campus and WAN — as the current growth engine. R&D is focused on adapting EOS to new AI-workload silicon architectures, expanding CloudVision automation, and building security/NDR adjacencies.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, in the company's own risk-factor-summary order):
1. Sole/limited-source component supply — most acutely, Arista is "primarily reliant upon our predominant merchant silicon vendor, Broadcom, for our switching chips," with no exclusive-rights or guaranteed-supply agreement.
2. Customer concentration — "large purchases by a limited number of customers represent a substantial portion of our revenue," with unpredictable order timing tied to hyperscaler capex cycles.
3. Tariff/trade risk — escalating U.S. tariffs and retaliatory measures explicitly cited as a live risk to the business.
4. Third-party manufacturing dependence — products are built by contract manufacturers (Jabil, Sanmina, Foxconn Hon Hai) in Malaysia, Vietnam, Mexico, and elsewhere.
5. Rapidly evolving networking market / industry cyclicality and intense competition (Cisco, Dell/EMC, Extreme Networks, HPE, Huawei, Juniper, Nvidia, and "white box" open-source competitors).
