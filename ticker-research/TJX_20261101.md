## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

The TJX Companies operates as the leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer in the United States and worldwide, managing over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four primary segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is built on "opportunistic buying," a strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise close to the time it is needed rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to purchase closeouts, special productions, and overruns from a diverse universe of approximately 21,000 vendors across more than 100 countries, enabling it to offer quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers. By maintaining lean inventory levels and utilizing flexible store layouts without permanent department walls, TJX creates a "treasure hunt" shopping experience that encourages frequent customer visits and rapid inventory turnover.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from this flexible business model, which includes specialized inventory management systems, a low-cost operational structure, and substantial global buying power. TJX leverages its scale to negotiate favorable terms with vendors, often purchasing less-than-full assortments and avoiding typical retail concessions such as markdown allowances or performance-based return privileges. This flexibility allows the company to react quickly to changing market trends and consumer preferences, maintaining a pricing gap relative to traditional retailers even through economic cycles. Additionally, the company benefits from synergies across its retail chains, sharing best practices and talent, while its distribution network, comprising approximately 31 million square feet of space across six countries, is designed to support efficient, differentiated delivery of merchandise to stores.

Management’s stated priorities focus on delivering great value to customers every day through a value proposition of brand, fashion, price, and quality. The company emphasizes the importance of its "Associates," noting that its large, global workforce is key to executing the flexible business model, including the timing of store deliveries and the management of rapidly changing merchandise mixes. Strategic priorities also include store growth, with long-term potential for expansion in current geographies and new markets such as Spain, as well as the continuous renovation and upgrading of stores to enhance the customer experience. Furthermore, management highlights the importance of maintaining a prudent focus on expenses, including low advertising costs and efficient distribution operations, to support the company’s low-cost structure.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the volatility in the global economy driven by international trade relations and tariffs. The company notes that tariffs on imports from China and other countries have led to significant uncertainty, with the impact depending on trade negotiations, judicial review, and the ability of the buying organization to offset tariff effects through its sourcing model. Recent legal developments, including a U.S. Supreme Court decision invalidating certain tariffs and the imposition of new global tariffs, have introduced further uncertainty regarding potential refunds and ongoing costs. Additionally, the company faces risks related to foreign currency exchange rates, which affect the translation of foreign segment results and the mark-to-market value of inventory derivatives. Other flagged risks include the highly competitive nature of the retail apparel and home fashion industry, where TJX competes on brand, price, quality, and shopping experience against a wide range of traditional and online retailers.
