## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ROST_10-K_20260906.txt)

Ross Stores, Inc. operates as a value-oriented off-price retailer through two distinct banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model relies on purchasing broad-based assortments of apparel, shoes, accessories, and home goods from third-party sources, often acquiring "packaway" inventory that is stored in warehouses for less than six months before being released to stores. This approach allows Ross to offer a wide variety of brands at compelling values, with sales mix heavily weighted toward Home Accents and Bed and Bath (26%), Ladies (22%), and Men’s (15%). The company maintains a significant physical footprint, ending fiscal 2025 with 2,267 stores and 45.1 million square feet of selling space, and continues to expand through new store openings, including entry into new geographic markets like Puerto Rico and the New York Metro area.

The filing does not explicitly define a "competitive moat" in traditional strategic terms, but it highlights operational efficiencies and scale as key drivers of performance. Ross leverages its size to manage distribution costs, having opened its eighth distribution center in Buckeye, Arizona, and planning further supply chain investments. The company’s ability to maintain high comparable store sales growth (5% in fiscal 2025) driven by both traffic and basket size, while managing cost of goods sold and SG&A, suggests a strong execution capability in sourcing and retail operations. Additionally, the company’s financial strength is evidenced by its ability to generate substantial operating cash flow ($3.0 billion in fiscal 2025), which supports aggressive capital return policies, including a new $2.55 billion stock repurchase program and consistent dividend increases.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three key initiatives: merchandising, marketing, and store experience. Specifically, the company aims to deliver broad-based assortments timely, offer more brands at compelling values, advance marketing to strengthen customer awareness and engagement, and make meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. Management emphasizes that providing merchandise that resonates with customers is central to driving sustainable, profitable growth, while also monitoring macroeconomic factors such as tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include legal and regulatory proceedings, particularly class/representative action lawsuits in California alleging violations of wage and hour laws, which remain pending. Additionally, the company faces uncertainties related to the broader macroeconomic environment, specifically citing tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions as factors that could impact results. The filing also notes that fiscal 2025 earnings included an estimated unfavorable tariff-related impact of approximately $0.16 per share, highlighting the direct financial risk posed by trade policies. While management believes current legal proceedings will not have a material adverse effect on financial condition, the ongoing nature of these lawsuits and the volatility of the macro environment represent significant operational and financial risks.
