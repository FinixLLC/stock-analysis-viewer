## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (AMG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Affiliated Managers Group (AMG) operates as a strategic partner to independent, partner-owned investment firms, referred to as Affiliates, rather than acting as a traditional asset manager itself. The company’s business model involves acquiring meaningful equity interests in these high-quality independent firms while allowing their management teams to retain significant equity ownership and day-to-day autonomy. AMG generates value by leveraging its strategic capabilities—specifically access to growth capital, product strategy, distribution through its vertically integrated U.S. wealth platform, succession planning, and strategic advisory—to help Affiliates expand their reach and diversify their businesses. As of December 31, 2025, AMG’s Affiliates managed approximately $813 billion in assets under management (AUM), with the portfolio heavily diversified across private markets ($146 billion), liquid alternatives ($227 billion), equities ($312 billion), and multi-asset/fixed income strategies ($128 billion). This diversification is central to AMG’s model, as it aims to enhance earnings stability across market cycles by combining complementary return streams from alternative and differentiated long-only strategies.

AMG’s competitive moat is derived from its unique partnership structure that preserves the entrepreneurial culture and client alignment of independent firms while providing them with institutional-scale resources. The filing highlights that AMG’s model offers many benefits of consolidation, such as access to capital and distribution, but critically allows firms to retain their independence, which is a key differentiator from other strategic paths. The company cites its global reputation, established relationships, and more than three decades of experience in structuring these bespoke partnerships as key advantages. Furthermore, AMG’s vertically integrated U.S. wealth platform provides a distinct distribution advantage, enabling Affiliates to access the U.S. wealth marketplace at a scale that is difficult for independent managers to achieve alone. The filing notes that AMG has successfully launched innovative products, including one of the first evergreen funds in private equity, leveraging this platform to meet evolving client needs.

Management’s stated priorities focus on generating long-term value by allocating resources to areas of highest growth and return, with a deliberate strategic shift toward alternative strategies. The company aims to improve long-term organic growth and earnings stability by investing in private markets and liquid alternatives, which are aligned with secular growth trends and long-term client demand. Specific priorities include partnering with new high-quality Affiliates in areas of durable demand, investing in existing Affiliates to seed new products, and enhancing AMG’s own strategic value-add capabilities. In 2025, this strategy was executed through four new partnerships in alternative strategies, a strategic partnership with Brown Brothers Harriman, and the expansion of the U.S. wealth platform. Management also emphasizes a disciplined capital allocation framework, deploying proceeds from strategic transactions (such as the sales of interests in Peppertree, Comvest, and Montrusco Bolton) toward growth investments and the return of capital to shareholders.

The filing identifies several material risks inherent to this business model. A primary risk is the dependence on the performance and retention of Affiliates; while AMG supports independence, evolving conditions may lead Affiliates to consider other strategic options or exit the partnership, which could impact AMG’s revenue and AUM. The filing notes that AMG retains certain rights to protect shareholder interests in such cases, but the success of the model is inherently tied to the continued alignment and success of the independent firms. Additionally, the business is exposed to market volatility and client cash flows, as evidenced by net outflows in equity strategies in 2025, which were offset by inflows in alternatives. The filing also highlights the complexity of the partnership structures, where AMG’s economic participation varies by Affiliate (sharing in revenue with or without expenses), creating varying degrees of exposure to operating costs and revenue fluctuations. Finally, the reliance on a diverse portfolio of strategies means that performance is subject to the specific risks of each asset class, including the long-dated capital commitments and realization risks associated with private markets.
