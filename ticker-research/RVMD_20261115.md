## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (RVMD_10-K_20260827.txt)

Revolution Medicines (RVMD) is a clinical-stage precision oncology company focused on developing novel targeted therapies for RAS-addicted cancers. Its business model relies on a proprietary structure-based drug discovery platform that utilizes deep chemical biology and cancer pharmacology to create small molecules tailored to unconventional binding sites. Specifically, the company develops "RAS(ON) Inhibitors," which are oral, tri-complex inhibitors designed to directly bind and suppress active RAS variants, distinguishing them from first-generation KRAS(OFF) inhibitors. The pipeline includes multi-selective and mutant-selective agents such as daraxonrasib, zoldonrasib, elironrasib, and RMC-5127, intended for use as monotherapies or in combination with other agents to address genetic drivers and adaptive resistance mechanisms in cancers like pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and colorectal cancer.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its innovative, proprietary technologies that enable the creation of small molecules targeting unconventional binding sites, allowing for the development of inhibitors with high potency and selectivity for specific RAS mutations (e.g., G12D, G12C, G12V, Q61H). This approach is designed to overcome RAS-driven drug resistance and extend clinical benefit. Additionally, the company has secured significant regulatory milestones, including Breakthrough Therapy Designations for daraxonrasib, zoldonrasib, and elironrasib, as well as Orphan Drug Designation and a Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher for daraxonrasib in PDAC. The company also leverages strategic collaborations with major pharmaceutical partners, such as Bristol Myers Squibb, Amgen, Summit Therapeutics, and Tango Therapeutics, to evaluate combination therapies and enhance lead discovery through AI-driven models.

Management’s stated priorities center on advancing the deep pipeline of RAS(ON) Inhibitors through pivotal clinical trials, with a particular focus on registrational studies in PDAC and NSCLC. Key initiatives include completing enrollment in Phase 3 trials such as RASolve 301 and RASolute 302, and initiating new Phase 3 trials like RASolute 309 and RASolve 308. The company is also prioritizing the development of a new class of RAS(ON) Inhibitors to overcome drug resistance, with a first-in-human trial expected to begin in the fourth quarter of 2026. Furthermore, management is focused on expanding combination strategies in colorectal cancer and leveraging collaborations to identify biomarkers and optimize lead discovery processes.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily the inherent uncertainty and length of the clinical development and regulatory approval processes. The company notes that it has not yet obtained regulatory approval for any product candidate, and failure to secure approval could substantially harm the business. Significant risks include the potential for clinical trial delays or failures due to patient enrollment difficulties, adverse events, or regulatory holds. There is also a risk that interim or topline data may differ materially from final data, potentially impacting the value of the programs. Competition is cited as a major threat, with the possibility that competitors may develop safer, more effective, or cheaper products, or obtain marketing approval more rapidly. Additionally, the company faces risks related to its reliance on third-party contractors for manufacturing and clinical trials, as well as the potential for changes in regulatory policies, such as the EU Clinical Trials Regulation, which could impact development plans.
