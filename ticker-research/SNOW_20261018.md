## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

Snowflake operates as a cloud-native data platform provider, delivering an "AI Data Cloud" that allows organizations to consolidate, share, and monetize data without moving the underlying assets. The company’s business model is consumption-based, generating the substantial majority of its revenue from fees for compute, storage, and data transfer resources used by customers. This model is supported by a cloud-native architecture featuring independently scalable storage, compute, and cloud services layers, deployed across three major public clouds in 53 global regions. Snowflake’s competitive moat is derived from powerful network effects within its ecosystem; as more customers, partners, and data providers join the platform, the value of data exchange and collaboration increases for all users. The company also cites its multi-cloud strategy, which prevents customer reliance on a single cloud provider, and its secure, governed data sharing capabilities as key differentiators. Management’s stated priorities focus on advancing the platform through continuous innovation in AI and data engineering, acquiring new large enterprise customers, driving increased consumption among existing users, expanding the global footprint, and growing the partner network. The filing identifies several material risks, including the impact of adverse macroeconomic conditions—such as inflation and high interest rates—which may cause customers to rationalize budgets and optimize consumption. Additionally, the company flags risks related to the difficulty of predicting customer adoption rates for new AI technologies, the potential for lower revenue due to improved platform efficiency and open data formats that reduce resource consumption, and the challenges of retaining and integrating sales talent to drive growth in a competitive market.
