## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

ServiceNow operates as a cloud-based enterprise software provider that delivers a unified AI platform designed to govern, secure, and manage artificial intelligence while streamlining complex business workflows. The company’s business model is predominantly subscription-based, generating revenue from multi-year contracts that provide customers with access to its platform, support, and updates. This platform integrates three core capabilities—AI, data, and workflows—to connect disparate systems and automate cross-functional processes across four product areas: Technology, CRM and Industry, Core Business, and Creator and Other. By positioning itself as the infrastructure layer that translates AI-generated insights into executed business outcomes, ServiceNow allows organizations to modernize operations without replacing existing technology stacks, a strategy supported by a 98% renewal rate and a growing base of high-value customers, including 603 entities with annual contract values exceeding $5 million as of late 2025.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from two decades of institutional knowledge regarding how work flows across enterprise silos, which enables the creation of practical, cross-functional workflows that reflect operational realities rather than idealized models. ServiceNow argues that while competitors may offer point solutions for data analysis, they lack the comprehensive integration, workflow orchestration, and enterprise-grade reliability required to operationalize AI at scale. This advantage is reinforced by the platform’s architecture, which allows for the deployment of AI-enhanced workflows without disrupting established processes, and by the company’s operational expertise in maintaining the security and performance standards necessary for mission-critical business functions.

Management’s stated priorities focus on helping customers navigate the transition to AI by providing the infrastructure needed to direct, control, and manage actions after information is generated. The company emphasizes the importance of bridging the gap between AI’s analytical capabilities and the execution layer where business processes operate. Strategic initiatives include continuing to understand evolving customer requirements through ongoing dialogue, applying the platform’s ability to deliver integrated solutions that convert AI insights into governed business outcomes, and leveraging acquisitions, such as Moveworks, to strengthen enterprise workflow automation and front-end virtual agent technology.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the dynamic and rapidly evolving technology landscape. Management acknowledges that while the shift to AI presents opportunities, it also introduces uncertainty, noting that competitive dynamics may shift as new approaches emerge and customer preferences evolve. Additionally, the company highlights exposure to macroeconomic factors, including global conflicts, interest rates, inflation, and tariffs, which could materially impact business operations if they lead to greater global economic disruptions. Although the company states it has not experienced material impact from these events to date, it warns that persistence or worsening of these conditions could affect its results.
