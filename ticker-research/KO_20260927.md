## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KO_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Coca-Cola Company operates as a global total beverage company, selling products bearing its trademarks in over 200 countries and territories. Its business model is bifurcated into two primary lines: concentrate operations, which account for 59% of net operating revenues and 85% of unit case volume, and finished product operations, which account for the remaining 41% of revenues and 15% of volume. In the concentrate model, the company sells beverage bases, syrups, and certain finished beverages to independent bottling partners, who then mix, package, and distribute the final products to retailers. The finished product segment consists primarily of consolidated bottling and distribution operations, as well as non-bottling sales to distributors and direct-to-consumer retail stores operated by Costa. While the concentrate business generates higher gross profit margins, the finished product business generates higher net operating revenues. The company competes in a highly fragmented market against numerous global, regional, and local beverage companies, with its success dependent on maintaining brand relevance, managing supply chain costs, and adapting to shifting consumer preferences for health, sustainability, and digital engagement.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its extensive portfolio of world-leading nonalcoholic sparkling soft drink brands, including Coca-Cola, Sprite, Fanta, and Diet Coke, which collectively represent a significant portion of the estimated 65 billion daily beverage servings consumed worldwide. This brand equity is supported by a vast, established distribution network that leverages both independent bottling partners and consolidated operations to reach consumers globally. Management emphasizes that its ability to connect with consumers through a wide variety of beverage options and its scale in the "Coca-Cola ecosystem" provide a structural advantage. Furthermore, the company’s long history, dating back to 1886, and its investment in marketing and innovation allow it to maintain market share despite intense competition and evolving digital shopping habits.

Management’s stated priorities focus on five specific growth pillars: shaping a portfolio of loved brands, transforming the marketing and innovation agenda, optimizing the Coca-Cola ecosystem, building talent and capabilities, and enhancing the company’s "license to win." To support these goals, the company is investing in its brand portfolio, strategic capabilities, and people. Specific strategic responses to industry challenges include expanding the portfolio of low- and no-calorie options to address health concerns, investing in sustainable packaging, and increasing e-commerce capabilities to compete in the digital marketplace. Additionally, management highlights the critical importance of talent acquisition and retention, aiming to be a global employer of choice to drive execution and innovation.

The filing identifies several material risks, with regulatory and legal compliance being a primary concern. The company faces significant exposure to evolving laws regarding carbon emissions, plastic waste, water usage, and wastewater discharge, which could increase compliance costs, capital expenditures, and production costs. There is also substantial risk related to data privacy and cybersecurity, as the company relies heavily on information systems for operations, marketing, and financial reporting; the filing notes that cyberattacks, data breaches, or third-party system failures could disrupt operations, damage reputation, and result in significant financial losses. Financial risks include exposure to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, particularly given that $28.8 billion of 2025 revenues were derived from outside the United States, and the impact of interest rate changes on its debt financing. Additionally, the company flags the risk of impairment charges on noncurrent assets, including trademarks and goodwill, and the potential for counterparty default by financial institutions involved in its hedging activities. Finally, the filing highlights the strategic risk of failing to achieve long-term growth objectives due to shifting consumer preferences, health-related concerns regarding obesity, and the rapidly evolving competitive landscape driven by digital commerce and artificial intelligence.
