## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (AMD_10-K_20260906.txt)

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) operates as a high-performance and AI computing company, delivering full-stack solutions that span cloud infrastructure, embedded systems, AI PCs, and gaming. Its business model is built on a broad portfolio of AI-optimized CPUs, GPUs, networking hardware, and software, which it sells through three primary reportable segments: Data Center, Client and Gaming, and Embedded. In 2025, AMD generated $34.6 billion in net revenue, a 34% increase year-over-year, driven largely by strong demand for its 5th generation EPYC server processors and Instinct MI350 Series GPUs in the Data Center segment, as well as robust performance in the Client and Gaming segment. The company’s competitive advantage lies in its ability to offer differentiated, end-to-end AI solutions, including the first integration of a dedicated neural processing unit (NPU) on an x86 CPU for AI PCs, a comprehensive open AI software stack (ROCm), and leadership in advanced packaging and chiplet technology. This full-stack approach, combining industry-leading compute engines with networking and system integration expertise, positions AMD to compete effectively in the emerging era of intelligent computing.

Management’s stated priorities for 2025 focused on accelerating growth in the Data Center segment by establishing an annual cadence of leadership for its AI GPU roadmap and expanding its server CPU portfolio. A key strategic initiative was the execution of a comprehensive AI strategy, which included significant investments in software capabilities to support high-performance AI training and inference. To achieve this, AMD made several strategic acquisitions, including ZT Systems for end-to-end AI infrastructure design and other entities to bolster its software, photonics, and high-speed inference expertise. A major milestone in this strategy was the October 2025 product purchase agreement with OpenAI to deploy 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs, underscoring the company’s commitment to delivering large-scale AI infrastructure. Additionally, management prioritized returning capital to shareholders, having repurchased $1.3 billion in stock during the year, while maintaining a strong balance sheet with $10.6 billion in cash and short-term investments.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the impact of U.S. government export controls on its business. In 2025, AMD recorded approximately $800 million in inventory and related charges due to new restrictions on shipping certain semiconductor products to China, specifically the AMD Instinct MI308 Data Center GPU. Although the company later reversed a portion of these charges after obtaining licenses, the filing notes that U.S. officials have expressed an expectation that the government may receive 15% of revenue from licensed sales to China, a requirement that has not yet been formalized in regulation. Other significant risks flagged in the filing include the company’s reliance on a small number of customers for a substantial portion of its revenue, the ongoing and increasing costs associated with enhancing information security controls, and the uncertainties surrounding the development of the legal and regulatory environment for emerging technologies such as AI. Furthermore, the company notes that its forward-looking statements are subject to risks related to demand for its products, the competitive landscape, and its ability to obtain sufficient external financing on favorable terms.
