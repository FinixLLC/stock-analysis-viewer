## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ALL_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Allstate Corporation operates as a diversified insurance and financial services provider, with its core business model centered on the Allstate Protection segment, which offers private passenger auto, homeowners, and other personal lines insurance to consumers through exclusive agents, independent agents, and direct channels. The company also maintains a Protection Services segment that generates revenue through protection plans, roadside assistance, dealer services, identity protection, and lead generation via its Arity platform, while managing a Run-off Property-Liability segment for legacy asbestos, environmental, and other mass tort claims. In 2025, the company executed a strategic pivot by divesting its employer voluntary benefits and group health businesses, recording significant after-tax gains of $641 million and $499 million, respectively, to refocus on its core property and casualty operations.

Allstate’s competitive moat is derived from its scale and diversified distribution network, which allows it to offer "affordable, simple and connected protection products" across multiple channels. The company leverages significant reinsurance programs to manage aggregate countrywide exposure, catastrophe risk, and capital requirements, supporting the financial strength ratings of its subsidiaries. Additionally, the company benefits from substantial indemnification recoverables, particularly from the Michigan Catastrophic Claims Association (MCCA), which mitigates specific state-based liabilities. The integration of technology through Arity provides a recurring revenue stream from lead generation, further diversifying income sources beyond traditional premium-based underwriting.

Management’s stated priorities focus on a multi-year "Transformative Growth" initiative, with an emphasis on implementing rate increases to keep pace with rising costs in states where acceptable returns are achievable. The company prioritizes international growth within its Protection Plans business, which drove a 7.5% increase in premiums written in 2025. Strategic capital allocation is evident in the recent dispositions of non-core health businesses to improve return on equity, which reached 42.3% in 2025. Management also emphasizes rigorous reserve management, utilizing actuarial best practices to establish reserves for both active and run-off liabilities, while actively managing catastrophe exposure through the placement of excess catastrophe reinsurance programs.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the inherent uncertainty in reserve estimates, particularly for long-tail liabilities such as asbestos and environmental claims, where adverse development can significantly impact results. The company highlights the material impact of catastrophe losses, which affected the combined ratio by 8.6 points in 2025, and the volatility of prior year reserve reestimates, which provided a $1.8 billion favorable release in 2025 but have historically resulted in adverse developments. Additionally, the filing identifies macroeconomic risks, including the potential adverse effects of U.S. tariffs on vehicle pricing, replacement parts availability, and building material costs, which could increase claims costs and reduce demand. Credit risk associated with reinsurance recoverables is also noted, as the financial strength of some reinsurers has declined, increasing the risk of uncollectible amounts.
