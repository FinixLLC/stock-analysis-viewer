What the company does: NetApp describes itself as a global leader in "Intelligent Data Infrastructure" — having transformed since its 1992 founding from a storage-hardware pioneer into a software-driven, cloud-centric data infrastructure company. Its flagship ONTAP data management software, combined with all-flash, hybrid-flash, and cloud-native solutions, underpins enterprise data management, protection, and (increasingly) AI-readiness across on-premises, hybrid, and multi-cloud environments.

Moat / competitive differentiation (company's own framing):
- Deep, native cloud integration — NetApp positions itself as "the only enterprise data infrastructure provider natively integrated with all major public cloud providers" (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud) — offered as Azure NetApp Files, Amazon FSx for NetApp ONTAP, and Google Cloud NetApp Volumes.
- Decades of data-management leadership via ONTAP, described as recognized for reliability, scalability, and advanced data services.
- Security/cyber-resilience built in — autonomous, AI-powered ransomware protection operating natively at the storage layer.
- Ecosystem partnerships with NVIDIA, Cisco, Microsoft, and a broad channel network.

Segments and product mix: Hybrid Cloud (unified storage portfolio — AFF all-flash arrays, ASA block-optimized arrays, AFX disaggregated AI-focused storage, FAS hybrid-flash, E/EF-series high-performance SAN, StorageGRID object storage) and Public Cloud (as-a-service cloud storage/data/operational services). The company frames AI infrastructure (via its "NetApp AI Data Engine" and validated NVIDIA reference architectures) as a key current growth vector, positioning its storage layer as the foundation that makes enterprise data "AI-ready."

Recent inorganic moves (see News below): two bolt-on acquisitions in 2026 (DataPelago, JetStream Software) both explicitly targeted at strengthening the AI-data-infrastructure and cyber-resilience story.

Distribution model: primarily indirect — the majority of FY2026 net revenues moved through channel partners, with Arrow Electronics and TD Synnex as the two largest named distributors (21% and 24% of FY2025 net revenues, respectively — see concentration note above).
