## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (META_10-K_20260906.txt)

Meta Platforms operates as a technology company with a mission to build the future of human connection, generating substantially all of its revenue through advertising placements on its "Family of Apps" (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, and Threads). The company’s business model relies on a self-service ad platform and a global sales force to help marketers reach consumers, leveraging data signals from user activity to deliver targeted ads. While the core business is digital advertising, Meta is simultaneously investing heavily in Reality Labs, a segment focused on virtual and augmented reality hardware (such as Meta Quest and AI glasses) and software, which currently operates at a loss and is viewed as a long-term bet on the next computing platform.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its massive user base, which reached 3.58 billion daily active people in 2025, and its ability to integrate AI across its ecosystem to enhance content discovery, ad targeting, and user engagement. Meta differentiates itself through continuous innovation in "social by design" products and a strategy of open-sourcing certain AI models, such as Llama, to foster broader innovation. However, the filing notes that the company faces significant competition in every aspect of its business, including from other social platforms, AI developers, and hardware manufacturers, with particular pressure on engagement from younger demographics.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 focus on several key investment areas: artificial intelligence (including generative AI and superintelligence), Reels and the discovery engine, wearables, product monetization, youth engagement, platform integrity, and infrastructure capacity. The company emphasizes operating efficiently while making significant investments in these areas, with 82% of total costs and expenses allocated to the Family of Apps segment in 2025. Meta explicitly states that its ability to support the long-term, loss-making Reality Labs segment is dependent on generating sufficient profits from its advertising business.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include regulatory and legal challenges that restrict data usage for ad targeting, particularly in Europe (GDPR, Digital Markets Act) and the U.S., as well as changes by third-party platforms like Apple and Google that limit cross-app tracking. These factors are expected to continue adversely impacting advertising revenue and user engagement. Additionally, the company faces risks related to macroeconomic volatility, geopolitical conditions affecting user growth in specific regions, and the inherent uncertainty of its long-term Reality Labs investments, which may take a decade to fully realize. The filing also highlights the risk that Reels, while growing in usage, monetizes at a lower rate than other products, and that increased enforcement of advertising policies to protect platform integrity may further reduce revenue.
