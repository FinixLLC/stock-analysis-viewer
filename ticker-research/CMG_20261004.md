## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CMG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Chipotle Mexican Grill operates as a single-segment, company-owned fast-casual restaurant chain, generating revenue primarily from food and beverage sales at 3,938 U.S. locations and 104 international company-owned restaurants, supplemented by a smaller network of 14 partner-operated sites. The company’s business model is built on a "Food with Integrity" proposition, serving fresh, responsibly sourced ingredients without artificial additives, while leveraging a digital-first strategy where digital channels accounted for 36.7% of food and beverage revenue in 2025. This model is supported by a proprietary supply chain that utilizes regional distribution centers and a limited list of approved suppliers to ensure quality and mitigate pricing volatility, alongside a labor-intensive operational structure that employs over 130,000 people worldwide, with a heavy reliance on internal promotions for leadership roles.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its brand positioning as a provider of high-quality, wholesome food at accessible price points, distinct from competitors who may prioritize speed or lower cost over ingredient integrity. Chipotle differentiates itself through its "Responsibly Raised" meat standards and strict food safety protocols, including a dedicated Food Safety Advisory Council and advanced pathogen reduction technologies. Additionally, the company has developed a significant digital ecosystem, including its mobile app, Chipotlane drive-through format, and Rewards program, which drives customer engagement and convenience. The brand’s purpose-driven identity, focused on sustainability and animal welfare, further distinguishes it in a highly competitive market that includes national chains, local restaurants, and third-party delivery services.

Management’s stated priorities, outlined in their "Recipe for Growth" strategy, focus on five key areas: protecting the core business through operational and culinary excellence; evolving brand messaging and menu innovation to drive demand; modernizing the business model with technology, including AI and a relaunched Rewards program; expanding global reach through both company-owned and partner-operated markets; and cultivating top talent with a focus on speed and agility. The company emphasizes investing in its people, evidenced by initiatives such as the Debt-Free Degree program, mental health resources, and a strong internal promotion pipeline, while also prioritizing capital allocation toward new restaurant construction, with plans to open 350 to 370 new locations in 2026, approximately 80% of which will include a Chipotlane.

The filing flags several material risks, including exposure to ingredient price volatility and supply chain disruptions, noting that certain key ingredients are purchased from a small number of suppliers. The company explicitly cites the impact of 2025 tariffs, estimating a 15 basis point ongoing impact on food, beverage, and packaging costs, as well as inflationary pressures on beef and chicken. Labor costs remain a significant risk, having increased as a percentage of revenue due to wage inflation and lower sales volumes, which also impacted occupancy and other operating costs. Additionally, the company faces competitive pressures from food delivery services and other restaurant formats, as well as regulatory and environmental compliance costs, although management states these have not had a material effect on competitive position. Seasonality is also noted as a factor, with lower sales typically occurring in the first and fourth quarters due to weather and holiday patterns.
