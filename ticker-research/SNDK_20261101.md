## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNDK_10-K_20260906.txt)

Sandisk (SNDK) is a vertically integrated global semiconductor memory company that designs, manufactures, and sells NAND flash-based storage solutions, including solid-state drives (SSDs), embedded products, removable cards, and wafers. The company operates across three primary end markets: Datacenter (serving cloud and enterprise customers), Edge (serving OEMs in PC, mobile, gaming, and automotive sectors), and Consumer (retail and end-user products). Sandisk’s business model relies on its ownership of chip-level design and IP, front- and back-end manufacturing capabilities, and systems engineering. A critical component of its supply chain is its joint ventures with Kioxia, through which Sandisk obtains leading-edge flash memory wafers while co-developing flash technologies. The company recently separated from Western Digital (WDC) in February 2025 to become a standalone publicly traded entity.

Sandisk’s competitive moat is built on several pillars, including a broad and differentiated product portfolio, a premium consumer brand, and a "differentiated innovation engine" that drives advancements in storage technology. The company holds a significant intellectual property position with approximately 8,000 granted patents and 3,000 pending applications worldwide. Additionally, Sandisk cites its leadership in cost efficiency, extensive customer and channel relationships, and large-scale, efficient manufacturing supply chains as key differentiators. While the industry is highly competitive, with rivals including Kioxia, Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix, and Yangtze Memory Technologies, Sandisk positions itself as a key strategic supply partner by offering the broadest range of storage technologies and maintaining deep relationships across the data ecosystem.

Management’s stated priorities focus on leveraging innovation, technology, and execution capabilities to support the infrastructure enabling data proliferation. Key strategic elements include technology innovation and manufacturing leadership to drive cost-efficient memory solutions, maintaining a broad product portfolio to address emerging use cases, and achieving operational excellence through best-in-class capital efficiency and supply chain management. A major strategic shift highlighted in the filing is the transformation of the financial model through "New Business Models" (NBMs). These are long-term agreements with Datacenter and Edge customers that commit both parties to stated volumes over multi-year periods, supported by financial guarantees. Management believes NBMs will provide greater revenue predictability, improve production planning, and reduce exposure to industry cyclicality. Furthermore, management is actively investing in high-value opportunities driven by the rapid growth of AI infrastructure, which they expect to persist through calendar year 2027 and beyond.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policies and tariffs. Sandisk notes that while many of its products are currently exempt, pending investigations under Section 232 and Section 301 could lead to increased tariff rates, higher costs of goods sold, and potential negative impacts on margins and demand. The company also highlights the inherent cyclicality of the semiconductor memory industry, although it asserts that its new business model agreements are designed to mitigate this. Other risks include the reliance on joint ventures with Kioxia for flash wafers, the competitive intensity of the market, and the challenges associated with the recent separation from Western Digital, which resulted in a $1.8 billion goodwill impairment charge in fiscal year 2025. Additionally, the company faces risks related to the protection of its intellectual property and proprietary information, particularly in foreign jurisdictions where legal protections may be weaker.
