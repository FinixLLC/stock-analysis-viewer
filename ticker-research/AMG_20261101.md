## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (AMG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Affiliated Managers Group (AMG) operates as a strategic partner to independent investment firms, generating long-term value by acquiring meaningful equity interests in high-quality, partner-owned businesses known as Affiliates. The company’s business model is built on a distinctive partnership approach that preserves the autonomy and entrepreneurial culture of these independent firms while leveraging AMG’s strategic capabilities to enhance their growth. Specifically, AMG provides Affiliates with access to growth capital, product strategy and development, distribution through its vertically integrated U.S. wealth platform, succession planning, and strategic advisory services. In return, AMG holds structured partnership interests that align its economic interests with those of Affiliate management, who typically retain a significant majority of equity ownership and direct day-to-day operations. As of December 31, 2025, AMG’s Affiliates managed approximately $813 billion in assets under management (AUM), diversified across private markets ($146 billion), liquid alternatives ($227 billion), equities ($312 billion), and multi-asset/fixed income strategies ($128 billion). The company generates significant cash flow from asset-based and performance-based fees, which it deploys toward new partnerships, investments in existing Affiliates, and the return of capital to shareholders.

AMG’s competitive moat is derived from its ability to combine the inherent advantages of independent firms—such as deep alignment with clients through direct equity ownership by principals, nimble decision-making, and differentiated investment expertise—with the scale and resources of a larger strategic partner. The filing highlights that AMG’s model offers many benefits of consolidation, such as access to institutional and wealth client distribution channels and capital formation capabilities, without forcing the loss of independence that often accompanies traditional roll-ups. This unique value proposition is supported by a global reputation built over more than three decades of successful partnerships, a broad industry network for identifying high-quality prospects, and specialized expertise in structuring transactions. Furthermore, the diversification of AMG’s portfolio across uncorrelated return streams (alternatives and long-only strategies) enhances earnings stability across market cycles, distinguishing it from peers focused on single asset classes.

Management’s stated priorities center on allocating capital to areas of durable client demand and secular growth, with a deliberate shift toward alternative strategies to improve long-term organic growth and cash flow stability. In 2025, this strategy was executed through four new partnerships in alternative strategies (NorthBridge, Verition, Montefiore, and Qualitas Energy) and a strategic partnership with Brown Brothers Harriman, alongside the expansion of the U.S. wealth platform. Management also prioritizes disciplined capital allocation, evidenced by the sale of minority interests in Peppertree, Comvest’s private credit business, and Montrusco Bolton, which generated significant gains and provided flexibility to reinvest in higher-growth opportunities. The company emphasizes investing in its own strategic value-add capabilities, such as product development and distribution, to further scale and diversify its Affiliates’ businesses.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the dependence on the performance and retention of independent Affiliates. Because AMG’s model relies on preserving Affiliate independence, there is a risk that evolving conditions may lead Affiliates to consider strategic alternatives or exit the partnership, which could impact AMG’s revenue and AUM. Additionally, the business is exposed to market fluctuations that affect asset values and fee income, as seen in the net outflows from equity strategies in 2025, which were offset by inflows in alternatives. The filing also notes that AMG’s financial results are subject to the timing of recognition for performance-based fees, which are less frequent and more variable than asset-based fees. Finally, the company faces risks associated with the integration of new partnerships and the execution of its growth strategy, including the need to continuously identify and cultivate relationships with high-quality prospective Affiliates in a competitive landscape.
