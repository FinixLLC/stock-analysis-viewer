## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LLY_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eli Lilly and Company operates as a single-segment global human pharmaceutical company, discovering, developing, manufacturing, and marketing innovative medicines across four primary therapeutic areas: cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The company’s business model relies on a continuous cycle of discovering or acquiring new molecules, developing them through clinical trials, and commercializing them in approximately 90 countries. Revenue generation is driven by branded products, with a significant portion of 2025 revenue derived from its incretin-based therapies, specifically Mounjaro and Zepbound, which accounted for 56% of total revenues. Distribution occurs through a mix of traditional channels, including wholesalers and sales representatives, and increasingly through LillyDirect, a direct-to-patient digital platform that facilitates access and adherence. The company also engages in strategic collaborations with partners such as Boehringer Ingelheim, Incyte, and Almirall to co-develop and market specific products.

Lilly’s competitive moat is built on its extensive pipeline of new molecular entities and line extensions, particularly in the rapidly evolving cardiometabolic space where it holds a leading position with tirzepatide. The company emphasizes that its long-term success depends on its ability to innovate at the pace of global scientific advancement and to efficiently bring differentiated products to market. Key competitive factors include early market entry, rapid patient access, and the ability to navigate complex reimbursement landscapes. Lilly leverages its scale to undertake significant manufacturing expansion initiatives to support anticipated demand, while also utilizing regulatory expedited pathways, such as the FDA’s Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher for its oral obesity candidate orforglipron. However, the company acknowledges that its moat is under pressure from intensifying global competition, including from Chinese manufacturers, and the threat of generic and biosimilar entry, which can rapidly erode revenue for non-biologic products and impact pricing for biologics.

Management’s stated priorities center on sustaining growth in the cardiometabolic health sector while managing the transition to new revenue streams. A key strategic focus is navigating the complex regulatory and pricing environment in the United States, including the implementation of preliminary voluntary agreements with the U.S. government to lower Medicaid prices and expand access to obesity medicines. The company is prioritizing the commercialization of next-generation incretins, such as orforglipron and retatrutide, to maintain its competitive position as the therapeutic landscape evolves. Additionally, management is focused on operational productivity, manufacturing capacity expansion to meet demand, and mitigating risks associated with global trade policies, tariffs, and the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which mandates government-set prices for certain drugs. The company also emphasizes the importance of addressing patient safety concerns related to counterfeit and mass-compounded incretins through legal and regulatory channels.

The filing identifies several material risks that could adversely impact the business. The most significant is the dependence on the success of its incretin portfolio, as volume fluctuations in this segment can disproportionately affect results. Regulatory and pricing pressures are a major concern, including the IRA’s drug pricing provisions, which have already selected Jardiance, Trulicity, and Verzenio for government-set prices, and the uncertainty surrounding the definitive terms of the U.S. government pricing agreements. The company faces substantial competition from generic and biosimilar products, which can lead to rapid revenue loss upon expiration of exclusivity. Operational risks include potential supply chain disruptions, manufacturing quality issues, and the impact of tariffs and trade restrictions. Additionally, the company highlights risks related to data privacy, IT security, and litigation concerning the 340B program, insulin access, and product safety. The high cost and uncertainty of pharmaceutical research and development, with a high rate of failure inherent in drug discovery, also pose a risk to the company’s ability to replace revenues from aging products.
