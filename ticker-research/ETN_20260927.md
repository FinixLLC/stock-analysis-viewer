## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ETN_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eaton Corporation plc operates as an intelligent power management company with $27.4 billion in 2025 revenue, serving customers in 180 countries across data center, utility, industrial, commercial, machine building, residential, aerospace, and mobility markets. The company’s business model involves providing products and solutions that manage power reliably, efficiently, and sustainably, leveraging megatrends such as electrification, digitalization, and reindustrialization. Eaton actively manages its portfolio through strategic acquisitions, including Fibrebond, Resilient Power Systems, Ultra PCS, and Boyd Thermal, to expand its capabilities in data center cooling, solid-state transformers, and aerospace solutions. Conversely, the company is divesting its Mobility business (comprising Vehicle and eMobility segments) via a planned spin-off into an independent public company.

Eaton’s competitive moat is derived from its position as a market leader in many of its product lines, particularly in the Electrical and Aerospace segments. The company competes on product performance, technology, customer service, and price, with a strong emphasis on total cost of ownership and design engineering capabilities in aerospace. Eaton holds significant intellectual property, including patents and trade secrets, which management considers valuable to the business, although the loss of any single right is not expected to have a material adverse effect. Additionally, the company benefits from strong customer relationships, with a high degree of sales concentration among large original equipment manufacturers and data center customers, indicating deep integration into critical infrastructure supply chains.

Management’s stated priorities focus on capitalizing on growth in data center and utility end markets, strengthening participation across the entire electrical power value chain, and benefiting from the commercial aerospace and defense growth cycle. The company is also prioritizing operational sustainability, ethical standards, and supply chain resiliency. Strategic actions include expanding into next-generation technologies like liquid cooling and solid-state transformers, while simultaneously streamlining the portfolio by exiting the Mobility sector. Eaton also emphasizes human capital management, aiming to be a model of inclusion and diversity, maintaining high employee engagement (86% favorable in 2025), and achieving world-leading safety ratings.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to customer concentration, where a small number of large customers account for significant portions of sales in the Electrical (22% to six customers), Aerospace (20% to three customers), Vehicle (37% to four customers), and eMobility (18% to one customer) segments. The company also notes exposure to raw material costs, including iron, steel, copper, and electronic components, though it states it generally has no difficulty obtaining these materials. Environmental compliance is identified as a potential driver for increased capital expenditures in the future, although current compliance costs are not expected to have a material adverse effect. Finally, the company acknowledges that its products rely on a portfolio of intellectual property that may expire, and it faces competition based on performance, technology, and price in all its segments.
