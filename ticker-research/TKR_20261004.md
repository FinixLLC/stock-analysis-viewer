## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company is a global industrial manufacturer that designs, manufactures, and services engineered bearings and industrial motion products, operating through two primary segments: Engineered Bearings and Industrial Motion. Founded in 1899, the company leverages its historical expertise in tapered roller bearings to offer a broad portfolio of products—including spherical and cylindrical roller bearings, ball bearings, linear motion solutions, industrial drives, automatic lubrication systems, belts, chains, couplings, and seals—designed to reduce friction and manage motion in demanding environments. Timken’s business model relies on a customer-centric, technical selling approach where sales engineers collaborate closely with original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and end-users to solve specific application challenges. The company generates revenue through both new product sales and a significant aftermarket mix, capturing replacement cycles via independent distribution channels and its own service networks, which include bearing repair and power systems services. With approximately 19,000 employees operating in 44 countries, Timken serves a diverse customer base across sectors such as renewable energy, agriculture, construction, automotive, aerospace, and metals and mining, with no single customer accounting for more than 5% of total net sales.

Timken’s competitive moat is built on its position as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, which it uses to apply specialized engineering know-how, metallurgy, and friction management across its entire product portfolio. The company differentiates itself through high-precision tolerances, proprietary internal geometries, and quality materials that provide high load-carrying capacity and long service lives. This technical depth is supported by a global footprint of 116 manufacturing facilities, 29 technology and engineering centers, and 74 distribution centers, allowing Timken to compete on total value proposition—including application engineering, product performance, quality, and global technical support—rather than price alone. The company also benefits from end-market diversity and a strong aftermarket presence, which provides recurring revenue streams throughout the equipment lifecycle. While the industry is highly competitive, with rivals including SKF Group and Schaeffler Group, Timken maintains its position by targeting fragmented markets that demand high service levels and reliability.

Management’s stated priorities are organized around three strategic pillars: Profitable Growth, Operational Excellence, and Capital Deployment to Drive Shareholder Value. For profitable growth, Timken aims to expand into new and existing markets by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and power transmission, with a specific focus on highly fragmented sectors that value reliability and offer significant aftermarket demand. Operational excellence involves a continuous improvement culture focused on increasing efficiency, lowering costs, eliminating waste, and driving organizational agility. Regarding capital deployment, the company prioritizes investing in the core business through capital expenditures and R&D, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio (such as the 2024 acquisition of CGI, Inc.), returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. In 2025, the company highlighted its commitment to sustainability, noting a 42% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions intensity since 2018, and continued its record of consecutive annual dividend increases.

The filing identifies several material risks that impacted 2025 performance and may affect future results. The most significant financial headwind was incremental tariff costs, which added $65 million to the cost of products sold in 2025, significantly eroding operating margins. Additionally, the company faced lower demand across most market sectors, particularly in the Americas and Europe, which drove a decline in organic sales and contributed to a 15.5% decrease in net income. Other flagged risks include higher impairment and restructuring charges, driven by severance expenses related to a CEO transition and the closure of a manufacturing facility in Germany, as well as net actuarial losses on pension obligations. While the company manages raw material price variability through surcharge mechanisms, the availability and price of special bar quality steel remain subject to commodity market fluctuations. Finally, the company notes that while backlog provides some visibility, actual shipments depend on customers' changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales.
