## Business Profile

Danaher operates as a diversified, multinational life sciences and diagnostics company with global operations, deriving approximately 59% of its sales from customers outside the United States. The company’s business model is built on geographic and industry diversity, which management states helps mitigate the impact of any single industry or national economy. Its portfolio is organized into three primary segments: Biotechnology, which provides equipment, consumables, software, and services for the research, development, and manufacture of biological medicines such as monoclonal antibodies and vaccines; Life Sciences, which offers instruments and consumables for studying the basic building blocks of life and for filtering contaminants; and Diagnostics, which serves the clinical testing market. In 2025, the company generated total sales of $24.57 billion, with the Diagnostics segment being the largest contributor at $9.94 billion, followed by Life Sciences at $7.33 billion and Biotechnology at $7.29 billion.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its diversified global supply chain and broad product range, which allows it to navigate economic and political volatility more effectively than single-market competitors. Management emphasizes that long-term growth depends on the ability to expand in high-growth geographies and market segments, identify and integrate acquisitions, and develop innovative, differentiated products with higher gross profit margins. The company actively monitors key competitors and customers to gauge relative performance and leverages its scale to invest in rapid technological developments, including computing, automation, AI, and digitization, to maintain responsiveness to customer needs and improve operational efficiency.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 and beyond focus on sustaining growth in high-growth markets and higher-growth segments, particularly in Biotechnology where they anticipate continued growth in consumables driven by monoclonal antibody demand. They aim to improve operating efficiency and quality through cost reductions and the ongoing application of their business system (DBS), while also expanding the effectiveness of their sales force. Strategic priorities include navigating a rapidly evolving trade environment and addressing increasing global regulation. In the Diagnostics segment, management expects higher sales growth in 2026 as the company moves past the peak of headwinds from policy changes in China, while in Life Sciences, they anticipate modest end-market improvements but expect sales growth rates to remain below historical levels due to the macro environment.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the rapidly evolving trade environment and geopolitical factors. The implementation of significant new U.S. tariffs and retaliatory measures by other countries, including China, has resulted in incremental tariff costs of less than $300 million for 2025, which the company largely offset through supply chain adjustments and cost savings. However, management warns that if they cannot continue to offset these costs, or if additional tariffs are implemented, revenue and profitability could be adversely impacted. Other material risks include the uncertainty surrounding the U.S. Supreme Court’s ruling on the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) and the administration’s subsequent plans to impose tariffs under alternative statutory authority. Additionally, the company faces risks related to the elimination or downsizing of certain U.S. government agencies and programs, which may affect government contracts and research grants, as well as the general challenges of operating in a highly competitive environment with rapid technological change and increasing regulation.
