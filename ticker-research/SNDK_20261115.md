## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNDK_10-K_20260906.txt)

Sandisk (SNDK) is a vertically integrated global semiconductor memory company that designs, manufactures, and sells NAND flash-based storage solutions, having separated from Western Digital in February 2025 to become a standalone public entity. The company’s business model relies on owning chip-level design and IP, front- and back-end manufacturing, and systems engineering to serve three primary end markets: Datacenter, Edge, and Consumer. Sandisk sources its flash memory wafers through joint ventures with Kioxia, while designing controllers in-house or via third-party foundries, and utilizes in-house and contract assembly and test facilities to deliver products ranging from enterprise SSDs and embedded storage to retail cards and USB drives.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its vertically integrated structure, which allows for cost leadership and supply chain flexibility, combined with a broad product portfolio that spans high-performance datacenter solutions to consumer brands. Sandisk holds a significant intellectual property position with approximately 8,000 granted patents and 3,000 pending applications worldwide, alongside deep relationships with industry leaders and OEMs. Management emphasizes that its "differentiated innovation engine" and ability to deliver cost-efficient, high-reliability solutions across diverse applications distinguish it from competitors such as Kioxia, Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix.

Management’s stated strategic priorities focus on leveraging technology innovation and manufacturing leadership to drive cost efficiency and performance, while expanding its broad product portfolio to capture emerging AI-driven demand. A key strategic shift highlighted in the filing is the transition to "New Business Models" (NBMs), which are long-term agreements with Datacenter and Edge customers that commit to multi-year volume purchases with fixed and variable pricing components. Management believes these NBMs will reduce exposure to industry cyclicality, improve revenue predictability, and support production planning. Additionally, the company is prioritizing investment in high-value opportunities driven by the rapid growth of AI infrastructure, which management expects to persist through 2027 and beyond.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on trade policy and operational execution. Sandisk notes that pending investigations under Section 232 and Section 301 of U.S. trade laws may impact tariff rates; while most U.S. products are currently exempt, any loss of exemptions or tariff increases would raise costs and potentially reduce demand. The company also highlights the inherent cyclicality of the semiconductor memory industry, although it asserts that the new NBMs mitigate some of this risk. Other flagged risks include the reliance on joint ventures with Kioxia for wafer supply, the complexity of manufacturing processes requiring high yields and quality control, and the potential for competitors to obtain proprietary information despite safeguards. Furthermore, the company recorded a $1.8 billion goodwill impairment charge in fiscal 2025 following its separation, indicating that the estimated carrying value of its reporting unit exceeded its fair value at that time.
