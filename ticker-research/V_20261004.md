## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through a proprietary transaction processing network known as VisaNet. The company’s business model is based on the "four-party model," where it provides authorization, clearing, and settlement services among consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. Visa explicitly states it is not a financial institution; it does not issue cards, extend credit, set rates for account holders, or bear credit risk. Instead, it earns revenue through service fees, data processing fees, international transaction fees, and other value-added services. In fiscal 2025, Visa processed 258 billion transactions with a total payments and cash volume of $17 trillion, connecting nearly 14,500 financial institutions to over 175 million merchant locations across more than 200 countries. The company is expanding its ecosystem to include digital banks, fintechs, and governments, and is migrating toward a "Visa as a Service" stack that offers modular capabilities such as authentication, risk management, and fraud detection via APIs and its Model Context Protocol server.

Visa’s competitive moat is derived from its massive scale and network effects, evidenced by its position as a leader in digital payments with nearly 5 billion payment credentials and a global footprint that includes high-volume touchpoints like the Visa/PLUS Global ATM network. The company leverages its proprietary infrastructure to offer a "single connection point" for money movement, differentiating itself through advanced security technologies such as Visa Token Service (VTS), which has provisioned over 16 billion tokens, and its "Tap to Everything" capabilities, which accounted for 79% of face-to-face transactions globally in fiscal 2025. Additionally, Visa maintains a strong position in cross-border payments, citing its risk management, fraud prevention, and foreign exchange capabilities as key differentiators for travelers and businesses. The company also invests heavily in brand visibility through major sponsorships, including the International Olympic Committee, the FIFA World Cup 2026, and Formula One teams, to drive consumer preference and acceptance.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, the strategy involves strengthening card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved consumer spend. In CMS, the goal is to drive penetration in commercial payments and money movement. In VAS, the focus is on delivering innovative services to deepen client partnerships. Technologically, management emphasizes investing in next-generation technologies, including generative AI, stablecoins, and agentic commerce, while continuing to enhance core enablers like tokenization and cross-border capabilities. The company also prioritizes organizational optimization, as evidenced by recent severance costs recorded to realign the structure toward higher long-term growth areas.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are primarily legal and regulatory in nature, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a U.S. litigation escrow account to address claims associated with this litigation. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025. This litigation is significant enough that it impacts the company’s capital structure, as the U.S. retrospective responsibility plan allows Visa to recover monetary liabilities through downward adjustments to the conversion rates of Class B-1 and Class B-2 common stock into Class A common stock. The filing notes that the resolution of this litigation will be considered by the board regarding successive exchange offers for Class B stock. While the excerpts do not provide a comprehensive list of all potential risks (such as cybersecurity or macroeconomic factors), the interchange MDL is highlighted as a major financial and operational factor affecting earnings and capital structure.
