## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/META_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2025-12-31 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text.

What the company does: Meta's stated mission is "to build the future of human connection and the technology that makes it possible." It reports two segments: Family of Apps (FoA) — Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Meta AI, Threads, WhatsApp — which generates substantially all revenue from advertising; and Reality Labs (RL) — consumer VR/AR hardware (Meta Quest, AI glasses) and related software/content, which operates at a loss and is explicitly framed as a long-term, multi-year bet on "the next computing platform."

Product mix / where the money comes from: Virtually all revenue is advertising on Facebook and Instagram (impression- and action-based ad pricing). Non-advertising revenue (RL hardware sales, WhatsApp paid messaging, Meta Verified subscriptions, developer payment fees) is a small fraction of the total. RL's current lineup includes Meta Quest VR headsets, Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta AI glasses, the Orion AR prototype, and the newly introduced Meta Ray-Ban Display (with a wrist-worn Neural Band controller) — RL is guided to spend ~70% of its 2026 opex on wearables and ~30% on VR/Horizon.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: Scale of the user base/advertiser network across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Threads, and WhatsApp; deep AI investment underpinning content ranking, discovery, and ad targeting/measurement; company explicitly frames its 2026 priorities as AI, Reels/discovery engine, wearables, monetization, youth safety, platform integrity, and infrastructure capacity.

Management's stated priorities (2026): continued heavy AI investment (the driver of the capex guidance raises), Reels/discovery engine improvements, wearables expansion, monetization of existing products, youth-safety features, platform integrity, and infrastructure capacity to support AI compute needs.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Summary Risk Factors, Item 1A, company's own grouping):
1. Product/engagement risk — ability to retain users and engagement; loss of or reduced spend by marketers; reduced availability of ad-targeting data signals (a multi-year, ongoing headwind since the 2021 iOS privacy changes); new products/features failing to gain traction.
2. Business operations/financial risk — competitive intensity; fluctuating financial results; brand/reputation risk from unfavorable media coverage; ability to scale technical infrastructure to meet AI-driven compute demand; multi-country operating complexity; litigation exposure; M&A integration risk.
3. Government regulation/enforcement — country-level restrictions on ad delivery (company explicitly cites meaningful revenue from China-based ad resellers as exposed to trade/political risk, and notes Turkey-specific compliance risk); an extensive list of evolving privacy/content/competition regimes (GDPR, EU DMA, EU DSA, UK Online Safety Act, EU AI Act); an active FTC consent order on privacy compliance.
4. Data, security, platform integrity, IP — cybersecurity/data-breach risk, platform misuse, and IP protection/enforcement.

This aligns with the live news flow: the capex/AI-spending trajectory that management itself is guiding to (now $130-145B for 2026, nearly double 2025's $72.2B) is precisely the kind of "increasing investment to remain competitive" risk flagged in the filing, and it's the dominant factor behind the recent stock reaction.
