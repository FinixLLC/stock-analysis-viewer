## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ORCL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Oracle operates as a provider of enterprise information technology frameworks, delivering products and services that build, run, and support corporate IT environments. The company’s business model is structured around three distinct segments: cloud and software, hardware, and services. The cloud and software segment, which accounted for 87% of total revenues in fiscal 2026, is the primary growth driver, shifting the company’s revenue mix significantly toward subscription-based cloud models. Oracle offers a comprehensive suite of applications, including Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP, HCM, and CX, as well as NetSuite for small and medium-sized businesses, alongside infrastructure technologies such as Oracle AI Database, Java, and middleware. A core element of Oracle’s strategy is providing customers with flexible deployment options, including cloud-based, on-premise, and hybrid models like Oracle Exadata Cloud@Customer, allowing clients to choose how and when they deploy technologies. The company monetizes this through cloud subscriptions, perpetual software licenses, and recurring support contracts, with cloud revenues rising to 51% of total revenues in fiscal 2026 from 37% in fiscal 2024.

Oracle’s competitive moat is rooted in its deep integration of applications and infrastructure, where Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) powers Oracle Cloud Applications (OCA), creating a cohesive ecosystem that competitors may find difficult to replicate. Management asserts that the comprehensiveness and breadth of its cloud applications differentiate it from competitors offering more limited or specialized solutions. Additionally, Oracle leverages its massive installed base of on-premise software licenses, using migration programs to pivot existing customers to the cloud, thereby converting legacy license revenue into recurring cloud subscription revenue. The company also cites its flexible, interoperable deployment models and embedded AI capabilities as key differentiators that address customer needs for choice and flexibility better than competitors.

Management’s stated priorities center on the continued migration of the existing customer base from on-premise to cloud-based solutions, which they describe as being in the "early stages" of a material shift. The company emphasizes investing heavily in research and development, spending $10.3 billion in fiscal 2026, to enhance its portfolio and incorporate emerging technologies like AI, machine learning, and generative AI into its offerings. Strategic focus also includes expanding the geographic footprint of its cloud operations, increasing capacity to meet growing demand, and maintaining a selective acquisition program to integrate complementary technologies. Oracle prioritizes improving the performance, security, and cost-effectiveness of its offerings while maintaining a strong direct sales force to drive growth in both cloud and software segments.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to macroeconomic and geopolitical factors. Oracle notes that its revenue growth is affected by general economic conditions, inflation, tariffs, trade policy, and geopolitical tensions, which can impact customer demand and supply chain costs. The company specifically highlights the risk that it may not be able to pass on increased supply chain, energy, and tariff-related costs to customers, potentially adversely impacting margins. Additionally, Oracle faces risks associated with governmental budgetary constraints, foreign currency fluctuations, and the competitive position of its offerings relative to other cloud infrastructure providers. The filing also notes that quarterly hardware revenues are difficult to predict due to the reliance on a few large transactions and the finite life of hardware products, while the services business carries lower margins and is sensitive to customer IT department personnel reductions and discretionary spending controls.
