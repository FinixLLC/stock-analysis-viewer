## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

Snowflake operates as a cloud-native data platform provider, delivering its "AI Data Cloud" through a consumption-based business model where customers pay for compute, storage, and data transfer resources used. The company’s architecture consists of three independently scalable layers—storage, compute, and cloud services—deployed across three major public clouds in 53 global regions. This infrastructure allows organizations to consolidate siloed data into a single source of truth, enabling analytics, data engineering, and AI applications without the need to manage underlying infrastructure. Snowflake’s competitive moat is driven by powerful network effects; as more customers, partners, and data providers join the ecosystem, the value of the platform increases through seamless, governed data sharing and collaboration, which is difficult for competitors to replicate. Additionally, the company benefits from high switching costs and deep integration into enterprise workflows, evidenced by a net revenue retention rate of 125% and a customer base that includes 790 of the Forbes Global 2000 companies.

Management’s stated priorities focus on expanding the AI Data Cloud by driving increased usage among existing customers, particularly large enterprises, and acquiring new customers in regulated markets such as financial services and the public sector. The company is heavily investing in research and development to advance its AI capabilities, including the launch of Snowflake Intelligence and Snowpark Container Services, and is expanding its global footprint in EMEA, APJ, and Latin America. Furthermore, Snowflake is actively growing its partner network and native application ecosystem to foster broader data collaboration and monetization opportunities.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the company’s continued net losses, which reached $1.3 billion in fiscal year 2026 despite 29% revenue growth to $4.7 billion. A significant risk is the dependence on customer consumption patterns; because revenue is recognized based on usage rather than ratably over contract terms, fluctuations in customer spending, budget rationalization due to macroeconomic uncertainty, or efficiency improvements that reduce resource consumption can lead to revenue volatility. The company also highlights risks related to the difficulty of predicting adoption rates for its AI technologies, which depend on customers meeting evolving regulatory standards and completing internal compliance reviews. Additionally, Snowflake faces competitive pressure from legacy database products and big data offerings, as well as risks associated with cybersecurity threats and the need to maintain its reputation as a trustworthy vendor in highly regulated industries.
