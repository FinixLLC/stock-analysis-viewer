## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (BA_10-K_20260906.txt)

Boeing operates as a global aerospace leader organized into three reportable segments: Commercial Airplanes (BCA), Defense, Space & Security (BDS), and Global Services (BGS). The company’s business model relies on the design, development, manufacture, sale, and support of commercial jetliners, military aircraft, satellites, missile defense systems, and human space flight capabilities. As one of the only two major manufacturers of 100+ seat commercial airplanes, Boeing competes in a duopoly for the worldwide airline industry, while also serving as one of the largest U.S. defense contractors. Its Global Services segment provides a full spectrum of aftermarket support, including maintenance, upgrades, spare parts, training, and digital solutions, creating a recurring revenue stream that sustains aerospace platforms for both commercial and defense customers.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its position as a primary provider of essential national security and commercial aviation infrastructure, supported by a large portfolio of intellectual property, including patents, trade secrets, and unpatented engineering skills. Boeing’s scale allows it to leverage core businesses to capture next-generation defense programs and expand into adjacent international markets. Furthermore, the company benefits from a long-term demand outlook driven by fundamental economic growth, globalization, and the increasing propensity for travel, with Boeing projecting demand for approximately 43,600 new airplanes over the next 20 years. The integration of its defense, intelligence, communications, and space resources allows it to deliver capability-driven solutions that are difficult for competitors to replicate quickly.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on successful execution in these healthy core businesses, with a specific emphasis on improving quality, safety, and production stability. Following the January 2024 737-9 door plug accident, management prioritized slowing production rates to reduce traveled work, investing in workforce training, simplifying processes, and enhancing safety culture. Strategic moves to support this execution include the December 2025 acquisition of Spirit AeroSystems to vertically integrate fuselage production for key programs like the 737, 767, 777, and 787, and the October 2025 divestiture of the Digital Aviation Solutions business to focus resources. In the defense sector, the priority is to stabilize the production system and supply chain while managing the technical and schedule challenges associated with fixed-price development programs.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably labor instability and supply chain constraints. Boeing experienced significant work stoppages in 2024 and 2025, including a 53-day strike by IAM District 751 and a 101-day strike by IAM District 837, which halted production of major commercial and defense programs and materially impacted financial results. The company warns that additional work stoppages could occur, particularly as contracts with the Society of Professional Engineering Employees in Aerospace expire in October 2026. Other material risks include persistent inflationary pressures, supply chain disruptions, and the financial impact of legal and regulatory actions, such as the $445 million earnings charge in 2025 related to agreements with the U.S. Department of Justice. Additionally, the company faces risks from exogenous factors such as fuel price spikes, geopolitical conflicts, and the financial viability of airline customers, which directly influence demand for new aircraft.
