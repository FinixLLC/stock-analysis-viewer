## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (JNJ_10-K_20260906.txt)

Johnson & Johnson operates as a global healthcare company with approximately 138,200 employees, organized into two primary business segments: Innovative Medicine and MedTech. The Innovative Medicine segment focuses on therapeutic areas including Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, and Cardiovascular and Metabolic, distributing prescription products directly to retailers, wholesalers, hospitals, and healthcare professionals. The MedTech segment provides products for Surgery, Orthopaedic, Cardiovascular, and Vision fields, distributed to wholesalers, hospitals, and retailers for use by medical professionals. The company’s business model relies on internal research and development, collaborations, acquisitions, and licensing to develop new products, with new products introduced within the past five years accounting for approximately 25% of 2025 sales. In October 2025, the company announced its intention to separate its Orthopaedics business, targeting completion within 18 to 24 months.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its extensive intellectual property portfolio, including patents and proprietary rights essential to its products and manufacturing processes, as well as its established brand equity and consumer trust in its healthcare products. The filing notes that the company owns or licenses a significant number of patents, which are central to its business, although it faces increasing challenges from competitors seeking to manufacture generic and biosimilar versions of its key pharmaceutical products.

Management’s stated priorities center on innovation and the development of new, differentiated products and services to address evolving healthcare needs, which is necessary to offset revenue losses from competition and the loss of patent exclusivity. The company also prioritizes the integration of data science, machine learning, and artificial intelligence across its business and operations, while striving to maintain profitability in high-inflation economies through cost reduction, productivity improvements, and periodic price increases. Additionally, management focuses on mitigating supply chain constraints and ensuring compliance with rapidly changing government laws and regulations.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant legal proceedings, particularly numerous lawsuits arising from the use of body powders containing talc, which could result in settlements or judgments exceeding current accruals and potentially having a material adverse effect on results of operations. The company does not purchase third-party product liability insurance, relying instead on a wholly owned captive insurance company subject to certain limits. Other material risks include regulatory scrutiny and compliance costs associated with FDA approvals, Good Manufacturing Practices, and healthcare industry business practices, which could lead to fines, penalties, or debarment from government healthcare programs. The company also faces risks related to intellectual property challenges, including patent litigation and the potential loss of revenue due to generic or biosimilar competition. Additionally, the filing highlights risks from foreign currency exchange fluctuations, with approximately 43% of 2025 sales occurring outside the U.S., and the impact of high inflation and currency devaluation in countries such as Argentina, Turkey, Venezuela, and Egypt.
