## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically providing a self-service demand-side platform (DSP) that empowers ad buyers—including advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers—to create, manage, and optimize digital advertising campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on independence and objectivity, focusing exclusively on the buy side of the digital advertising ecosystem to avoid the conflicts of interest associated with competitors who also sell inventory. TTD generates revenue primarily by charging clients a platform fee, generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform, along with fees for value-added services and data. This model is supported by ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic insertion orders, which the company cites as a driver for its high customer retention rate, exceeding 95% for over a decade.

TTD’s competitive moat is derived from its position as an independent, buy-side focused platform that offers transparency and channel agnosticism. The company argues that its objectivity allows it to build long-term, trusting relationships with clients who are willing to ingest their proprietary first-party data into the platform, a level of trust TTD claims is difficult for conflicted competitors to achieve. Additionally, the company leverages a "data flywheel" where its platform’s depth, AI capabilities, and rich ecosystem of inventory and data partner integrations enable superior reach and decisioning. TTD differentiates itself through its "expressiveness," a bid-factor-based architecture that allows for highly precise targeting, and its open platform approach, which utilizes enterprise APIs to allow clients to customize and expand functionality. The company also highlights its ability to provide access to premium omnichannel inventory, including connected television (CTV), audio, and display, on a global scale.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients’ digital advertising spend, particularly as budgets shift toward programmatic channels, and growing the client base by expanding into the mid-market and international markets, specifically in Europe and Asia. The company emphasizes continued innovation in technology, data, and measurement, with a specific focus on integrating AI capabilities such as "Koa," its AI co-pilot, to automate campaign optimization and simplify data buying. TTD also prioritizes the expansion of its omnichannel reach, particularly in CTV and other video formats, and the adoption of third-party and first-party data to enhance campaign efficacy. The company notes that it anticipates increasing operating expenses to support these investments in platform operations, AI development, and sales and marketing efforts to drive long-term growth.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on macroeconomic uncertainty, including changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments that could decrease demand for advertising services and disrupt supply chains. TTD also identifies the risk that its future growth depends heavily on the overall adoption of programmatic advertising by both advertisers and inventory owners; any slowing of this market growth could negatively impact performance. Additionally, the company highlights the competitive threat from platforms that may offer artificially low prices enabled by inherent conflicts of interest, noting that TTD must continuously differentiate its value to retain clients. Other risks include the need to manage increasing costs associated with hosting and developing an AI-rich platform, the seasonal nature of the advertising industry (with Q4 typically being the highest revenue period), and the challenges of expanding in international markets where adoption may vary.
