## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MDB_10-K_20260906.txt)

MongoDB operates as a developer data platform company, with its core business model combining the adoption benefits of open source with the economic structure of a proprietary software subscription business. The company generates 97% of its revenue from subscriptions, primarily through two offerings: Atlas, a managed multi-cloud database-as-a-service (DBaaS) that accounted for 73% of total revenue in fiscal year 2026, and MongoDB Enterprise Advanced, a self-managed commercial database for enterprise customers. The platform is built on a unique document-based architecture designed to handle unstructured data while maintaining the reliability of relational databases, and it is integrated with complementary capabilities such as search, vector search, time series, and stream processing. Management’s stated priorities focus on extending product leadership to maintain developer mindshare, growing the customer base and global reach, and retaining and expanding revenue from existing customers through a "land-and-expand" strategy. To support these goals, the company emphasizes investing in engineering, sales, and marketing, and recently acquired Voyage AI to enhance its generative AI capabilities.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its position as the leading modern general-purpose database, which it claims combines the best features of relational and non-relational systems. Key differentiators include its document-based architecture, which allows for greater flexibility and developer productivity compared to legacy relational databases, and its ability to run in any environment, including fully managed, self-managed, cloud, on-premises, or hybrid settings. MongoDB cites its strong developer brand, evidenced by consistent high rankings in developer surveys, and its integrated platform that reduces the need for disparate single-purpose technologies. Additionally, the company leverages its multi-cloud availability across AWS, GCP, and Azure to provide customers with infrastructure optionality and avoid vendor lock-in.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the adverse macroeconomic environment and geopolitical landscape, which management expects will create headwinds for the growth rate of existing Atlas applications in the short term. The company notes that its operational and financial performance is subject to these external factors, including slower economic growth and higher inflation. Furthermore, the filing highlights the inherent risks associated with rapid growth and substantial investments in research and development, sales, and marketing, noting that results of operations may fluctuate as the company makes these investments to drive adoption. The company also acknowledges that its ability to increase sales to existing customers depends on customer satisfaction, competition, pricing, and overall changes in customer spending levels.
