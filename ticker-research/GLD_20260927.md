## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLD_10-K_20260906.txt)

SPDR Gold Trust (GLD) is a passive investment trust formed in 2004 under New York law, designed to provide investors with a cost-efficient and accessible means of gaining exposure to the price of gold bullion. The Trust’s business model is straightforward: it holds allocated gold bullion and issues shares in blocks called "Baskets" in exchange for deposits of gold, while distributing gold upon the redemption of Baskets. The Trust does not employ derivatives, hold cash for investment purposes, or engage in active management; its sole objective is for the shares to reflect the performance of the price of gold less the Trust’s expenses. The Trust’s assets consist exclusively of allocated gold bars held by custodians HSBC and JPMorgan in London, New York, or Zurich, with any cash held solely to pay expenses and expected to be zero at period end.

The Trust’s competitive moat is derived from its status as a highly liquid, exchange-traded vehicle that overcomes the logistical barriers of buying, storing, and insuring physical gold. By trading on the NYSE Arca and other global exchanges, the shares offer investors the convenience of a traditional brokerage account, margin eligibility, and the ability to buy, sell, or short the asset without the transaction costs associated with physical bullion. The Trust maintains a large scale, holding over 32 million ounces of gold as of September 30, 2025, and utilizes a network of Authorized Participants to facilitate the creation and redemption of shares, ensuring that the secondary market price closely tracks the Net Asset Value (NAV).

Management’s stated priorities, as reflected in the filing, focus on maintaining the Trust’s role as a simple, cost-efficient, and easily accessible investment vehicle. The Sponsor emphasizes the importance of the creation and redemption mechanism to ensure liquidity and price discovery, as well as the rigorous valuation process using the LBMA Gold Price PM to determine NAV. The Trustee and Sponsor prioritize the accurate valuation of gold and the timely sale of gold only as necessary to cover expenses, aiming to minimize the Trust’s holdings of non-gold assets. Additionally, the Trust maintains strict custodial controls, including annual full counts and random sample counts of the gold bars by Bureau Veritas, to ensure the integrity of the underlying assets.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the direct exposure to gold price volatility, which is the primary driver of the Trust’s performance. The filing explicitly states that investing in the shares does not insulate investors from price volatility and that past movements in the gold price are not indicators of future movements. Additionally, the Trust faces risks related to the valuation of its assets, as the use of the LBMA Gold Price for NAV determination may differ from other available indicators, potentially leading to different fair value pricing. The Trust also notes that the sale of gold to pay expenses is a taxable event for shareholders, and the amount of gold sold varies based on the level of expenses and the market price of gold. Finally, the Trust is subject to the risks associated with its custodians and the OTC gold market, including liquidity fluctuations and credit risks between parties involved in transactions.
