## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLW_10-K_20260906.txt)

Corning Incorporated is a diversified materials science company with a 175-year history, applying expertise in glass, ceramic, and optical physics to develop products for optical communications, displays, mobile consumer electronics, automotive, life sciences, semiconductors, and solar industries. The company operates through five reportable segments—Optical Communications, Display, Specialty Materials, Automotive, and Life Sciences—along with Hemlock and Emerging Growth Businesses, manufacturing in 14 countries. Its business model relies on proprietary manufacturing processes, such as the fusion process for display glass and the invention of low-loss optical fiber, to provide category-defining solutions that enable customers to advance in dynamic markets.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its extensive patent portfolios, proprietary trade secrets, and brand recognition across its segments. In Optical Communications, Corning leverages its 1970 invention of low-loss fiber and a broad product lineup, including specific fibers for submarine, long-haul, and data center applications, to maintain a unique position in network design. The Display segment’s leadership is derived from its proprietary fusion manufacturing process, which is described as the most cost-effective for large-size substrates, alongside a history of introducing first-to-market glass sizes. Specialty Materials benefits from strong brand loyalty for products like Gorilla Glass and Ceramic Shield, while the Automotive segment relies on its status as the developer of the standard cellular ceramic substrate for catalytic converters and over 100 years of OEM market access.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on the "Springboard" plan, introduced in late 2023, which aims to grow sales and enhance profitability. The company has upgraded its target to add $4 billion in incremental annualized core sales by the end of 2026 and achieve a 20% core operating margin, milestones it reports having achieved a full year ahead of schedule. Management emphasizes continued investment in research, development, and engineering to solve customer challenges, while also focusing on increasing capacity and technology capabilities to meet demand, particularly in areas driven by artificial intelligence and data center growth.

The filing flags several material risks, including exposure to foreign currency fluctuations, which the company manages through hedging contracts for currencies such as the Japanese yen, South Korean won, and Chinese yuan. There is also regulatory and tax risk, specifically regarding ongoing IRS examinations of U.S. federal income tax returns for 2015–2020, where an unsuccessful defense could have a material impact on financial statements. Additionally, the company notes dependence on patent protection and licenses from third parties, as well as market-specific risks such as softness in the heavy-duty diesel market affecting the Automotive segment and the impact of resetting core exchange rates on the Display segment's reported sales.
