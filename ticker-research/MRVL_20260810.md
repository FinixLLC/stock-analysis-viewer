## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2026 10-K (fiscal year ended 2026-01-31, filed 2026-03-11), Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) read and synthesized directly from source text; local cached 10-K text dump not present for MRVL in this repo.

What the company does: Marvell describes itself as "a leading supplier of data infrastructure semiconductor solutions, spanning the data center core to network edge," operating as a fabless supplier of high-performance semiconductors with core strengths in complex System-on-a-Chip (SoC) architectures integrating analog, mixed-signal and digital-signal-processing functionality. The company explicitly frames its mission as "empowering the data economy" via the data center and communications end markets.

Recent portfolio strategy (from Item 1 "Overview"): FY2026 was a year of active portfolio reshaping — Marvell divested its automotive ethernet business to Infineon for $2.5B (completed August 2025, generating a $1.8B pre-tax gain), while simultaneously acquiring Celestial AI (Photonic Fabric optical interconnect technology, closed February 2026) and XConn Technologies (closed February 2026) to strengthen its AI/cloud data-center connectivity roadmap. This is a clear strategic signal: Marvell is exiting a lower-growth, more cyclical automotive silicon business to concentrate capital and management attention on AI-datacenter infrastructure, where growth has been fastest (data-center revenue +46% YoY in FY2026).

Moat, as described in the company's own words (and corroborated by EVCDM edges below): Marvell's moat is built on deep, sole-source-style custom-silicon relationships with major hyperscalers — designing and supplying custom AI inference/networking ASICs directly for specific customer platforms (e.g., AWS Trainium/Inferentia, per the EVCDM graph) rather than selling merchant silicon broadly. This "designed-in" custom ASIC model creates high switching costs once a hyperscaler has committed a generation of infrastructure to Marvell silicon, though it also concentrates revenue risk among a small number of very large customers.

Segment/product mix: Data Center is now the dominant and fastest-growing end market (+46% YoY), followed by Communications and other (+31% YoY); automotive ethernet has been fully divested. The August 2026 FMS 2026 (Flash Memory Summit) showcase and subsequent AI Memory Infrastructure Portfolio launch (Bravera SC6 PCIe 6.0 SSD controllers, Structera X CXL-based memory expansion, Photonic Fabric optical shared-memory solutions) signal a further product push into memory/storage infrastructure specifically for "agentic AI inference" workloads — an emerging, still-nascent product category as of this report.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A "Summary of Factors That May Affect Our Future Results," company's own ordering):
1. Product/technology execution risk — ability to design, develop and introduce new/enhanced products in a timely, effective manner, particularly in Data Center and Communications, and to anticipate/adapt to technology change.
2. Customer concentration — dependence on a few customers for a significant portion of revenue, "particularly as our major customers comprise an increasing percentage of our revenue," plus concentration specifically in the data-center end market. This directly corroborates the EVCDM-graph evidence (below) of Marvell's revenue running through a small number of named hyperscaler relationships (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Meta).
