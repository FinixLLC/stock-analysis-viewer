## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (WELL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Welltower Inc. is a real estate investment trust (REIT) and S&P 500 company that positions itself at the center of the "silver economy" by owning and managing rental housing for aging seniors across the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada. The company operates through an umbrella partnership REIT (UPREIT) structure, with Welltower Inc. holding approximately 98.378% of Welltower OP LLC. Its business model is diversified across three reportable segments: Seniors Housing Operating, which accounted for 78% of total revenues in 2025 and involves active management of communities providing independent living, assisted living, and dementia care; Triple-net, which generated 11% of revenues and involves leasing properties to operators under long-term master leases where the tenant bears operating costs; and Outpatient Medical, which contributed 7% of revenues and consists primarily of triple-net leased medical buildings affiliated with health systems. Welltower deploys capital through acquisitions, developments, and joint ventures, aiming to generate long-term compounding of per-share earnings growth by investing in affluent micro-markets with secular demand.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale, with a portfolio of over 2,500 seniors and wellness housing communities, and its strategic positioning at the intersection of housing and hospitality. Welltower emphasizes a disciplined approach to capital allocation powered by a proprietary Data Science platform and superior operating results driven by the "Welltower Business System," an end-to-end operational platform. In its Triple-net segment, the company utilizes integrated master leases that bundle multiple properties under a single tenant, a structure that mitigates risk by preventing tenants from cherry-picking high-performing assets and providing a similar advantage in bankruptcy proceedings. Additionally, the company maintains a significant loan portfolio, with outstanding loans of approximately $2.08 billion yielding around 8.9% per annum, and holds investments in unconsolidated entities valued at approximately $1.81 billion, further diversifying its revenue streams beyond direct property ownership.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term compounding of per-share growth for existing investors. This objective is pursued through a singular focus on generating durable cash flow growth, partnering with aligned, high-quality operators who demonstrate staying power across economic cycles, and investing at a compelling basis that provides a meaningful margin of safety. The company explicitly seeks to structure investments to protect downside risk and avoid permanent capital loss while allowing for sustained long-term growth. Capital allocation is directed toward real estate in affluent micro-markets that benefit from secular demographic trends, with a specific emphasis on the needs of an aging global population.

The filing flags several material risks and accounting complexities. A significant portion of the company’s performance is tied to its relationships with third-party operators; for instance, Care UK, Cogir Management Company, and Sunrise Senior Living collectively accounted for 36% of Seniors Housing Operating Segment revenues in 2025, creating reliance on these partners for effective property management. The company also faces credit risk associated with its loan portfolio, requiring quarterly evaluations of collectability based on borrower financial strength and collateral value, with provisions for credit losses recognized when collectability is deemed probable to fail. Additionally, the Triple-net segment carries risks related to tenant creditworthiness and the potential exercise of purchase options at less than full market value. The filing also highlights the complexity of valuing executive compensation, specifically noting that $1.41 billion of the $1.56 billion in stock-based compensation expense in 2025 was related to Executive Key Employee LTIP Unit Awards, which require complex Monte Carlo valuation models due to market and service conditions.
