## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PFE_10-K_20260827.txt)

Pfizer Inc. operates as a research-based, global biopharmaceutical company that generates the majority of its revenue through the discovery, development, manufacture, marketing, and sale of biopharmaceutical products and vaccines. The company’s business model relies on a combination of internal research and development (R&D) to replenish its pipeline, strategic business development activities such as acquisitions (e.g., Seagen and Metsera), and collaborations to address critical unmet patient needs. Pfizer manages its commercial operations through a global structure, which as of 2026 includes a new Global Hospital and Biosimilars organization to handle off-patent branded and generic sterile injectables and biosimilars. The company emphasizes a science-driven approach, leveraging platform technologies and an aging global population as long-term growth drivers, while simultaneously pursuing cost realignment and manufacturing optimization programs to improve operational efficiency.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its intellectual property portfolio, specifically the patent rights and regulatory exclusivities that protect its marketed brands. Pfizer actively defends these rights against infringement and generic competition, noting that the loss or expiration of these rights would materially impact revenues. Additionally, the company cites its strong pipeline and R&D capabilities as central to its ability to deliver "breakthroughs that change patients' lives," positioning itself to capitalize on trends in biological science and platform technologies. However, the filing explicitly acknowledges that this moat is finite, with a significant reduction in revenue expected from patent-based or regulatory exclusivity expiries between 2026 and 2030, including a projected $1.5 billion impact in 2026 alone.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 focus on maximizing the value of key transactions, delivering on critical R&D milestones, investing to maximize post-2028 growth, and scaling artificial intelligence (AI) across the business. To support these goals, Pfizer is pursuing digital enablement, including automation and data-driven decision-making, to enhance productivity and accelerate innovation. The company also highlights its commitment to responsible capital deployment and optimizing operating cash flows to support ongoing operations and capital allocation objectives.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and pricing pressures. Pfizer faces significant headwinds from the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which has already negatively impacted 2025 revenues by approximately $1 billion through Medicare Part D redesign changes and will impose government-set "Maximum Fair Prices" on products like Eliquis, Ibrance, Xtandi, and Xeljanz in coming years. Additionally, the company recently entered an agreement with the U.S. government to lower drug prices for patients and avoid Section 232 tariffs in exchange for increased U.S. manufacturing investment. Other material risks include the high degree of uncertainty in R&D, where clinical trial failures or regulatory rejections could derail growth, and the ongoing threat of generic and biosimilar competition as patent exclusivities expire. The company also notes exposure to legal liabilities, including product liability claims related to discontinued or divested products, and the inherent volatility of pension and postretirement plan valuations.
