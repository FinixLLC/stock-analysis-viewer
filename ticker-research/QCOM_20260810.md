## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-09-28), Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) read and synthesized directly from source text; local cached 10-K text dump not present for QCOM in this repo.

What the company does: Qualcomm is a global technology company built around two core reportable segments: QCT (Qualcomm CDMA Technologies), a fabless semiconductor business that designs and supplies integrated circuit platforms (the Snapdragon® and Qualcomm Dragonwing™ families) for handsets, automotive (Snapdragon Digital Chassis — connectivity, digital cockpit, ADAS/AD), and IoT (consumer, edge networking, industrial); and QTL (Qualcomm Technology Licensing), which licenses the company's foundational cellular IP (4G/5G) and other patents. A third reportable segment, QSI (Qualcomm Strategic Initiatives), makes strategic investments; nonreportable segments include QGOV (government tech) and a nascent Data Center business.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: Qualcomm's moat rests on two pillars — (1) a deep patent portfolio covering 4G/5G cellular technology, monetized through QTL licensing regardless of who manufactures the underlying chip, and (2) 40 years of system-level engineering leadership that keeps QCT's integrated circuit platforms at the leading edge of on-device AI, high-performance/low-power computing, and wireless connectivity. Management frames the current moment as an inflection point where AI is "becoming ubiquitous" beyond smartphones and PCs into automotive, XR and industrial IoT — a deliberate diversification thesis away from pure handset dependence.

Segment/product mix: QCT (equipment/services) dominates revenue at $37.869B (85.5% of FY2025 total), with QTL licensing at $6.415B (14.5%). The company's own risk-factor language flags that revenue remains concentrated among a small number of large customers and licensees, particularly around premium-tier handsets — the diversification into automotive and IoT is real but not yet large enough to offset that concentration.

Management's stated priorities (from Item 1 "Industry Trends"): capturing the AI compute shift as it moves from cloud-only to edge/on-device processing (smartphones and PCs as "pervasive AI platforms"); extending Snapdragon Digital Chassis further into automotive; and growing IoT/industrial applications. The company is explicitly positioning itself as a beneficiary of distributed AI workloads (edge + cloud coordination) rather than purely a cloud-datacenter AI play.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A "Risk Factors Summary," in the company's own ordering):
1. Customer/licensee concentration — "a significant portion of our revenues" comes from a small number of customers and licensees, particularly tied to premium-tier handset sales; fluctuations in their business directly hit Qualcomm's results.
2. Customer vertical integration — explicit acknowledgment that the semiconductor business "may suffer as a result of our customers vertically integrating," i.e., building their own chips in-house (directly corroborated by the Apple-modem substitution risk found in the EVCDM industry graph, below).
3. China concentration — "a significant portion of our business is concentrated in China," with risk "exacerbated by U.S./China trade and national security tensions."
4. Fabless supply-chain dependency — reliance on a limited number of third-party foundries/suppliers for manufacture, assembly and testing; failure to secure supply assurance could harm results.
5. New-initiative execution risk — R&D and investment in new/expanded product areas (auto, IoT, data center) may not generate returns that meet expectations.

This directly aligns with the Q3 FY2026 guidance commentary: the same "supply crunch, especially memory" language management used on the July 2026 earnings call maps onto the fabless-supply-chain risk factor the company itself has flagged for years.
