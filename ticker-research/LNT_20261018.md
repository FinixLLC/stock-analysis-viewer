## Business Profile

Alliant Energy operates as a regulated investor-owned public utility holding company headquartered in Madison, Wisconsin, with a primary business model centered on providing electric and natural gas services to approximately 1.4 million customers in the Midwest. The company generates revenue through its two wholly-owned utility subsidiaries, Iowa Power & Light (IPL) and Wisconsin Power & Light (WPL), which serve retail customers in Iowa and Wisconsin, respectively, while also engaging in wholesale electricity sales. Beyond its core utility operations, Alliant Energy maintains a non-utility portfolio under Alliant Energy Financial (AEF) that includes minority interests in transmission companies (ATC), venture capital funds, a wind farm, a leased natural gas facility, and supply chain solutions. The company’s financial performance is driven by regulated rate bases, with recent results showing increased net income for its utility segment in 2025 due to higher revenue requirements from capital investments and temperature impacts, partially offset by increased operating and financing expenses.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its status as a regulated monopoly within its specific service territories, protected by state and federal regulatory frameworks. In Iowa, IPL operates under non-exclusive franchises for public right-of-way use and benefits from state laws prohibiting local regulation of natural gas sales, while in Wisconsin, WPL operates under permits of indefinite duration. This regulatory structure, overseen by the Iowa Utilities Commission (IUC), the Wisconsin Public Service Commission (PSCW), and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), provides a predictable, cost-of-service recovery model for capital investments. Additionally, the company leverages its existing infrastructure to capture new growth opportunities, such as data center development, by utilizing development-ready sites with transmission capabilities and entering into long-term electric service agreements that aggregate to approximately 3 gigawatts of peak demand.

Management’s stated priorities focus on delivering affordable, reliable, and resilient energy while advancing emerging technologies. Key strategic elements include expanding generation capacity through a balanced mix of natural gas, energy storage, and renewable resources, with specific plans to add approximately 1,600 MW of new natural gas resources, 1,000 MW of energy storage, and 1,300 MW of new renewable generation over the next five years. The company emphasizes customer value through technology investments that improve operational efficiency and service effectiveness, as well as growing customer demand by promoting electrification initiatives and supporting economic development, particularly in the data center sector. Alliant Energy also prioritizes safety and employee engagement, integrating safety metrics into executive compensation and maintaining comprehensive behavioral safety programs.

The filing flags several material risks, including regulatory uncertainty and the impact of rate-making proceedings. Specifically, IPL is subject to a retail electric base rate moratorium from October 2025 through September 2029, which limits its ability to recover costs during that period, although it has received approval for individual customer rates for data centers. The company faces risks related to the execution of its large-scale capital investment plans, including the need for financing, with conditional commitments for loan guarantees from the U.S. Department of Energy totaling approximately $3 billion. Additionally, the company notes risks associated with the timing and amount of load increases from new customers, such as data centers, which are subject to interconnection and actual demand factors. Other risks include the potential for asset valuation charges, as seen with IPL’s Lansing Generating Station, and the impact of changes in fuel costs and transmission service expenses on operating income.
