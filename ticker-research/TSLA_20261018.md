## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TSLA_10-K_20260906.txt)

TSLA operates as a vertically integrated technology and manufacturing company with two primary reportable segments: automotive and energy generation and storage. The company designs, develops, manufactures, sells, and leases high-performance fully electric vehicles (including the Model 3, Y, S, X, Cybertruck, and Semi) and energy storage systems (Powerwall and Megapack). Its business model is increasingly shifting toward a service-driven approach based on artificial intelligence, software, and fleet-based profits, leveraging its vehicle fleet to collect real-world data for training autonomous driving systems like Full Self-Driving (FSD) (Supervised) and its newly launched Robotaxi service. TSLA sells directly to customers through its own retail stores, website, and galleries, bypassing traditional dealer networks to control costs, pricing, and brand experience. The company also generates revenue from regulatory credits, used vehicle sales, insurance, and energy generation incentives.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its vertically integrated business model, which allows for significant cost control and rapid iteration. Key differentiators include its proprietary AI and vision-based autonomous driving technology, which is accelerated by custom-designed in-house inference chips and trained on massive amounts of real-world data collected from its vehicle fleet. TSLA maintains extensive in-house capabilities in software development, battery cell chemistry, powertrain engineering, and manufacturing processes, such as integrating batteries directly into vehicle body structures to optimize cost and weight. Additionally, the company benefits from a growing global Supercharger network, which has become an industry standard (NACS) adopted by other automakers, creating a sticky infrastructure advantage. The ability to update vehicles and energy products over-the-air allows for continuous improvement of safety, performance, and functionality, further differentiating TSLA from competitors who rely on static hardware specifications.

Management’s stated priorities center on bringing artificial intelligence into the real world through FSD, Robotaxi, and the development of Optimus, a general-purpose autonomous humanoid robot. The company is focused on achieving profitable growth by reducing manufacturing costs, increasing production capacity, and improving operational efficiency. Specific strategic goals include vertically integrating and localizing the supply chain to mitigate trade and tariff risks, expanding global infrastructure for service and charging, and ramping up energy storage production to meet demand driven by AI infrastructure load growth. TSLA is also investing heavily in AI compute infrastructure, including expanding its "Cortex" training clusters, and aims to launch new products on its next-generation vehicle platform while maintaining a strong balance sheet to fund these high-value investments.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to macroeconomic and regulatory uncertainty. Rapidly evolving trade and fiscal policies, including tariffs and the removal of electric vehicle tax credits under the OBBBA, pose significant risks to the company’s global supply chain, cost structure, and consumer demand, particularly for its energy generation and storage business. The automotive industry’s cyclical nature, combined with inflationary pressures, rising interest rates, and shifting consumer trends, creates volatility in demand and pricing. TSLA also faces execution risks associated with ramping new manufacturing operations, concurrent international projects, and the introduction of new technologies such as autonomous driving and robotics. Furthermore, the company notes that supplier liquidity issues and component constraints could impact production, while the need to expand delivery, servicing, and charging infrastructure to match production growth presents ongoing operational challenges.
