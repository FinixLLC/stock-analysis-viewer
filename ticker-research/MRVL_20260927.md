## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MRVL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Marvell Technology operates as a fabless supplier of high-performance semiconductor solutions focused on data infrastructure, spanning from the data center core to the network edge. The company’s business model relies on developing and scaling complex System-on-a-Chip (SoC) architectures that integrate analog, mixed-signal, and digital signal processing functionality. Marvell serves two primary end markets: data center and communications and other. In fiscal 2026, the data center segment accounted for 74% of total net revenue, driven by AI-related demand for custom products and electro-optics, while the communications and other segment represented the remaining 26%. The company generates revenue through both standard product sales and optimized solutions customized to specific customer requirements, with a particular emphasis on custom Application Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) for next-generation AI and data center applications. Recent strategic moves include the divestiture of its automotive ethernet business to Infineon Technologies for $2.5 billion in cash and the subsequent acquisitions of Celestial AI and XConn Technologies to expand its interconnect and switching portfolios for scale-up AI networking.

Marvell’s competitive moat is rooted in its core strengths in developing complex SoC architectures and its deep system-level expertise, which allows it to integrate hardware, firmware, and software into highly differentiated solutions. The company leverages a broad suite of proprietary intellectual property, including ultra-high-speed SerDes, ARM compute, security, storage, and silicon photonics. Its ability to execute advanced manufacturing processes, having successfully completed multiple 5nm designs and progressing through 3nm and 2nm generations, further differentiates its offerings. Additionally, Marvell provides a comprehensive portfolio of interconnect solutions, including PAM and coherent DSPs, silicon photonics, and co-packaged optics (CPO), which are essential for high-bandwidth, low-latency connectivity in AI data centers. The recent acquisition of Celestial AI adds Photonic Fabric technology, while XConn contributes advanced PCIe and CXL switching silicon, reinforcing Marvell’s position in the emerging scale-up and scale-out AI interconnect market.

Management’s stated priorities center on accelerating the company’s connectivity strategy for next-generation AI and cloud data centers. This is evidenced by the strategic acquisitions of Celestial AI and XConn, which are intended to expand Marvell’s interconnect and switching capabilities for AI scale-up networking. The company is also focused on delivering stockholder value through its capital return program, which includes significant share repurchases and dividends. In fiscal 2026, Marvell returned $2.2 billion to stockholders through repurchases and dividends, with $5.5 billion remaining available for future repurchases as of January 31, 2026. Furthermore, management is committed to maintaining long-term supply capacity through reservation arrangements with foundries and partners, while also monitoring the impact of tariffs and export restrictions on its supply chain and demand.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to geopolitical and regulatory factors. Marvell notes that U.S. government export restrictions on certain Chinese customers continue to impact its revenue, potentially causing customers to amass large inventories or replace Marvell products with those from other suppliers or indigenous solutions. The company also faces risks associated with its global operations, as a majority of its products are manufactured and sold outside the United States, with sales to customers in Asia representing approximately 77% of net revenue in fiscal 2026. Additionally, Marvell is subject to order and shipment uncertainties, where inaccurate demand predictions can lead to excess or obsolete inventory, reducing gross margin, or insufficient inventory, resulting in lost revenue opportunities. The company also highlights the risk of changes in international taxation, particularly the enactment of minimum tax legislation modeled after the OECD’s Pillar Two framework, which could significantly reduce the income tax benefits associated with government incentives it relies on.
