## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX Companies operates as the leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer in the United States and worldwide, managing over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four primary segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is built on "opportunistic buying," a strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise closer to the time of need rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to offer a rapidly changing assortment of quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers, creating a "treasure hunt" shopping experience that encourages frequent customer visits. By maintaining lean inventory levels and utilizing a flexible store layout with minimal permanent fixtures, TJX can quickly adjust assortments to local preferences and market trends, supported by a global buying organization that sources from over 21,000 vendors across more than 100 countries.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale, buying power, and operational flexibility. TJX leverages its substantial global buying power to secure merchandise at significant discounts from closeouts, special productions, and order cancellations, while its willingness to purchase less-than-full assortments and its prompt payment practices make it an attractive channel for vendors. This access to a diverse and changing universe of vendors, combined with a low-cost operating structure—characterized by low advertising spend relative to sales and efficient distribution networks—allows TJX to maintain its pricing gap and merchandise margins through various economic cycles. The company’s ability to disperse merchandise across a geographically diverse store network and its specialized inventory management systems further reinforce its ability to deliver fresh selections efficiently.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day through a value proposition of brand, fashion, price, and quality. Key strategic initiatives include the continued expansion of the store base, with long-term store growth potential estimated at approximately 7,000 stores globally, and the enhancement of the customer shopping experience through strategic store renovations and upgrades. The company also emphasizes the importance of its workforce, viewing its approximately 377,000 Associates as key to executing its flexible business model, while continuing to invest in its supply chain to maintain low inventory levels and efficient logistics. Additionally, management focuses on maintaining a prudent expense structure and leveraging synergies across its retail chains to share best practices and develop talent.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant uncertainty surrounding international trade relations and tariffs, particularly those imposed on imports from China and other countries. While TJX states it has been successful in mitigating tariff pressures, the filing notes that tariffs have led to significant global economic volatility and that the ultimate impact on the company depends on trade negotiations, judicial reviews, and the cost of alternative sourcing. The filing also highlights risks related to foreign currency exchange rates, which can affect operating results through translation, mark-to-market adjustments on inventory derivatives, and transactional gains or losses. Furthermore, the company faces risks associated with its reliance on a rapidly changing merchandise mix and the competitive nature of the retail apparel and home fashion industry, where it competes on brand, price, quality, and shopping experience against a wide range of full-price and discount retailers.
