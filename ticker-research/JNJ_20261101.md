## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (JNJ_10-K_20260906.txt)

Johnson & Johnson operates as a diversified healthcare company with approximately 138,200 employees, organized into two primary business segments: Innovative Medicine and MedTech. The Innovative Medicine segment focuses on prescription products in therapeutic areas including Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, and Cardiovascular and Metabolic, distributing directly to retailers, wholesalers, hospitals, and healthcare professionals. The MedTech segment provides products for Surgery, Orthopaedic, Cardiovascular, and Vision fields, distributed to wholesalers, hospitals, and retailers for use by medical professionals. The company’s business model relies on internal research and development, collaborations, acquisitions, and licensing to develop new products, with new products introduced within the past five years accounting for approximately 25% of 2025 sales. In October 2025, the company announced its intention to separate its Orthopaedics business, targeting completion within 18 to 24 months.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its extensive intellectual property portfolio, which it describes as essential to its business, and its ability to innovate and develop differentiated products to address evolving healthcare needs. It leverages data science, machine learning, and AI across its operations to drive efficiency and product development. However, the filing notes that public policy has become increasingly unfavorable toward intellectual property rights, and competitors routinely challenge the validity of J&J’s patents, particularly regarding generic and biosimilar versions of key pharmaceutical products.

Management’s stated priorities center on the continued growth and success of the company through innovation, specifically the development of new and differentiated products and services to offset revenue losses from competition and patent expirations. The company emphasizes the importance of securing regulatory approvals on a timely basis and successfully differentiating its products in the marketplace. Additionally, management is focused on navigating significant regulatory scrutiny and compliance costs, as well as managing the financial and operational risks associated with its global operations, including currency fluctuations and inflation in high-inflation economies like Argentina, Turkey, Venezuela, and Egypt.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the significant legal proceedings and litigation, particularly regarding body powders containing talc, which could result in substantial expenses, fines, and reputational damage. The company does not purchase third-party product liability insurance, relying instead on a wholly owned captive insurance company, which exposes it to potentially significant settlement amounts that may exceed accruals. Other material risks include the challenges of product development and regulatory approval, where delays or failures can adversely impact long-term success, and the increasing challenges to intellectual property rights. The company also faces risks related to product safety and effectiveness, which can lead to recalls, government investigations, and declining sales. Furthermore, the company is subject to significant regulatory scrutiny regarding compliance with manufacturing practices, marketing, pricing, and anti-corruption laws, which can result in penalties, debarment from government healthcare programs, or criminal prosecution. Finally, the company faces financial risks from foreign currency exchange rates, inflation, and illegal importation of pharmaceutical products, as well as risks related to changes in tax laws and global minimum taxes.
