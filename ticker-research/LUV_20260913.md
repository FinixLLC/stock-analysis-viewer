## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines Co., operating as Southwest Airlines, is a major U.S. passenger airline offering scheduled air transportation within the United States and near-international markets. The company has a unique point-to-point route network that emphasizes direct flights between cities, avoiding traditional hub-and-spoke operations. This approach allows for more nonstop routes, frequent service, and competitive fares. Southwest serves 117 destinations across 42 states, Puerto Rico, and ten near-international countries, using a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft.

The company's business model is centered on cost discipline, operational efficiency, and customer experience. Key initiatives include assigned seating with extra legroom options, a redesigned boarding process, global airline partnerships to expand connectivity, and the introduction of overnight redeye flights to maximize aircraft utilization. Southwest also launched Getaways by Southwest, offering customizable vacation packages with flexible policies.

Southwest's competitive moat stems from its point-to-point network, which provides greater flexibility and efficiency compared to hub-and-spoke systems. The company's use of a single aircraft type, the Boeing 737, simplifies operations and maintenance, reducing costs and improving asset utilization. Additionally, Southwest's focus on customer experience through initiatives like assigned seating and loyalty programs enhances its appeal.

Management priorities include optimizing the route network for profitability, enhancing operational efficiency, and driving shareholder value. The company is expanding its redeye flights and partnerships with international carriers to increase connectivity. It also aims to reduce costs through workforce optimization and fleet modernization, replacing older aircraft with more fuel-efficient models.

The 10-K filing highlights several risks: high and volatile fuel prices significantly impact profitability, as fuel remains a major operating expense. Labor costs are another concern, constituting nearly half of operating expenses, with challenges in managing workforce size and productivity. Additionally, the company faces risks from economic downturns affecting travel demand, competitive pressures, and potential disruptions from geopolitical events or pandemics.

In summary, Southwest Airlines operates a low-cost, high-frequency point-to-point network with a focus on customer experience and operational efficiency. Its competitive advantages include its route structure and single-aircraft fleet, but it faces material risks related to fuel prices, labor costs, and economic conditions.
