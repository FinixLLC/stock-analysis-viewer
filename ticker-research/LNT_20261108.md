## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LNT_10-K_20260906.txt)

Alliant Energy operates as a regulated investor-owned public utility holding company headquartered in Madison, Wisconsin, with a primary business model centered on providing electric and natural gas services to approximately 1.4 million customers in Iowa and Wisconsin through its two wholly-owned subsidiaries, IPL and WPL. The company’s strategy is purpose-driven, focusing on serving customers and building stronger communities while delivering affordable, safe, and reliable energy. Alliant Energy’s revenue model relies on regulated retail rates approved by state commissions (IUC for Iowa and PSCW for Wisconsin) and FERC-regulated wholesale and transmission rates, supplemented by non-utility holdings such as a minority interest in ATC (a transmission-only company), venture investments, a wind farm, and logistics assets. The company is currently executing a significant capital expansion plan to meet growing demand, particularly from data centers, which includes adding approximately 1,600 MW of new natural gas resources, 1,000 MW of energy storage, and 1,300 MW of new renewable generation over the next five years.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its status as a regulated monopoly within its specific service territories in Iowa and Wisconsin, protected by non-exclusive franchises in Iowa and permits of indefinite duration in Wisconsin. This regulatory framework allows for the recovery of capital investments through rate cases, providing a stable, predictable revenue stream. Additionally, Alliant Energy benefits from economies of scale in its generation mix, which includes zero-fuel cost renewable resources (wind and solar) that generate tax credits passed to customers, and a diversified generation portfolio that includes natural gas, hydro, and emerging long-duration storage technologies. The company also leverages its existing infrastructure, such as underground conduit and development-ready sites, to attract large industrial loads like data centers, creating a virtuous cycle of demand growth and infrastructure utilization.

Management’s stated priorities are threefold: providing affordable energy solutions, making customer-focused investments, and growing customer demand. To achieve affordability, the company is utilizing tax benefits from the Inflation Reduction Act and Iowa tax reform to lower customer bills, while maintaining flat base rates for IPL retail electric and gas customers through September 2029 due to a regulatory moratorium. Customer-focused investments prioritize reliability and resiliency, including upgrades to existing natural gas facilities, refurbishments of wind farms, and the construction of new energy storage projects. Finally, management is actively pursuing load growth by partnering with commercial and industrial customers, particularly data center developers, and leveraging state incentives to attract economic development to its service territories.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to regulatory and operational dependencies. The company is subject to strict regulation by FERC, the IUC, and the PSCW, which controls retail rates, service standards, and asset acquisitions. A key risk is the retail electric base rate moratorium for IPL from October 2025 through September 2029, which limits its ability to recover costs and invest in new infrastructure during that period. Additionally, the company faces execution risks associated with its large-scale capital expansion, including the timely construction of new generation and storage facilities to meet MISO resource adequacy requirements and data center load growth. Other risks include reliance on third-party transmission systems (ITC and ATC), potential changes in federal tax laws affecting renewable energy credits, and the operational complexities of integrating new technologies like compressed CO2-based energy storage. The company also notes that actual load growth from data centers is subject to interconnection timelines and customer demand, which may not align with its investment plans.
