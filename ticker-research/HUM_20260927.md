## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (HUM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Humana Inc. operates as a diversified health care company with two primary reportable segments: Insurance and CenterWell. The Insurance segment generates the vast majority of revenue, with 83% of total premiums and services revenue derived from federal government contracts, primarily through Medicare Advantage (MA) and stand-alone prescription drug plans (PDPs). As of December 31, 2025, the company served approximately 15 million members in medical benefit plans and 4.7 million in specialty products. The CenterWell segment provides adjacent health care services, including pharmacy solutions, primary care, and home solutions, which are designed to enhance member health and reduce costs for the insurance segment. The company’s business model relies on an integrated care delivery model that combines quality care, high member engagement, and data analytics to transition providers from fee-for-service to value-based arrangements. In 2025, Humana finalized its exit from the Employer Group Commercial Medical Products business to focus on its core Medicare and state-based contract businesses.

The company’s competitive position is anchored by its national presence in all 50 states and its long-standing participation in the Medicare program, which provides a geographically diverse membership base that facilitates provider network expansion. A key differentiator is its integrated care delivery model, under which 68% of individual Medicare Advantage members were in value-based relationships as of late 2025. This model aims to improve health outcomes and affordability through physician-directed care and sophisticated data analytics. Additionally, Humana benefits from scale in its provider networks, allowing it to secure more favorable contract terms, and it holds specific administrative contracts, such as the LI-NET prescription drug plan program, which it has administered since 2010.

Management’s stated priorities center on creating capacity to fund growth through cost-saving and productivity initiatives, while simultaneously improving member and patient outcomes. The company has initiated a multi-year transformation program to re-align its cost structure, operating model, and technology footprint with evolving market conditions. This strategy includes driving value through workforce optimization and asset impairments, as evidenced by significant charges recorded in 2023, 2024, and 2025. Management emphasizes offering affordable health care combined with a positive consumer experience in growing markets, specifically targeting Medicare and Medicaid participants, while assisting providers in transitioning to value-based care arrangements.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily stemming from regulatory and legislative changes. Humana is heavily exposed to federal regulations, including the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which eliminated the Medicare Part D coverage gap and altered benefit designs, impacting cost-sharing and seasonal earnings patterns. The company faces risks related to CMS risk-adjustment models, where payments are based on diagnosis codes submitted by providers, creating potential compliance and payment accuracy challenges. Additionally, the filing highlights risks associated with the sustainability of the Employer Group Commercial business, which led to its exit, and ongoing pressures from macroeconomic, regulatory, and competitive factors that have necessitated substantial impairment charges and severance costs. There is also noted risk regarding the potential for regulatory restrictions on profitability, provider network management, and reimbursement rates, which could materially affect results of operations and financial position.
