## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/TKR_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2025-12-31 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text.

What the company does: Timken designs, manufactures and services a portfolio of engineered bearings and industrial motion products. Founded in 1899 by Henry Timken (inventor of the tapered roller bearing), the company remains "the world's leading authority on tapered roller bearings" and has extended that engineering base into a much broader portfolio via acquisitions — brands include GGB, Philadelphia Gear, Cone Drive, Rollon, Nadella, Groeneveld, Lovejoy, PT Tech, Lagersmit and others. Global footprint: 116 manufacturing/service facilities, 29 technology/engineering centers, 74 distribution centers, ~19,000 employees across 44 countries.

Segments (FY2025):
- Engineered Bearings ($3,018.1M, ~66% of sales): tapered, spherical, cylindrical, ball, housed and plain bearings serving industrial distribution, renewable energy, automotive OE, agriculture/turf, rail, aerospace, auto/truck aftermarket, construction, metals/mining, heavy truck OE and marine.
- Industrial Motion (~$1,563.7M, ~34% of sales): linear motion, industrial gear drives, automatic lubrication systems, belts, chain, couplings, clutches/brakes, seals and drivetrain repair services — a more diversified, faster-growing segment bolstered by the CGI acquisition (Q3 2024).

Customer base: highly diversified — "no single customer accounts for more than 5% of total net sales," a structural de-risking feature.

Moat, as described in the company's own words:
- Century-plus of metallurgy, friction-management and tribology expertise, leveraged across an expanding product portfolio via bolt-on acquisitions (a repeatable playbook — CGI in 2024, prior deals building the Industrial Motion segment).
- Deep application engineering and customer-collocated sales force ("sales engineers primarily working in close proximity to customers rather than at production sites").
- Backlog discipline: $2,213.7M total backlog at year-end 2025 (+9.5% YoY), ~92% scheduled for delivery within 12 months — though management explicitly cautions this is not a reliable predictor of future shipments given customers' changing production schedules.
- Not dependent on any single raw-material supplier for its primary input (SBQ steel) — "the Company is not dependent on any single source of supply."

Management's stated priorities: the "Elevate to Outperform" plan, newly formalized with dedicated Chief Commercial Officer and Chief Operating Officer roles (announced with Q2 2026 earnings) — an explicit pivot toward commercial execution and operational efficiency, following a FY2025 in which margins compressed on flat sales.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, in the company's own ordering):
1. Pricing pressure from a highly competitive, consolidating industry — primary named competitors SKF Group and Schaeffler Group; company explicitly notes it "may not be able to continue to increase prices... to achieve desired profitability."
2. Capital intensity / cyclicality — ~$137M in impairment/restructuring charges taken over the last five years; downturns in served industries could force curtailment of operations.
3. Raw material price/availability risk — steel (SBQ) price volatility, partially offset by surcharge mechanisms in customer contracts, but with potential lag or inability to fully pass through costs.
4. Acquisition integration risk — company explicitly grows "in part through strategic acquisitions" and flags the risk of failing to capture revenue/cost synergies or uncovering undisclosed liabilities (environmental, trade, tax, compliance) post-close.
5. Tariff/trade policy exposure — "The U.S. government has imposed tariffs on certain foreign goods, including steel and other raw materials... These tariffs have adversely affected our results of operations and profit margins and could continue to do so," alongside risk of retaliatory tariffs from trading partners.
6. AI/data-privacy/cybersecurity — newer risk-factor category acknowledging the company has "begun to incorporate AI technologies into certain aspects of our operations," with attendant risks around errors, workforce disruption and evolving AI regulation.
7. Pension/defined-benefit plan risk — funding obligations tied to plan-asset performance, discount rates and actuarial experience, which could require material future cash contributions.

The balance sheet remains solid (net debt/capital moderate, FCF consistently positive and growing) and the company is diversifying away from its historically dominant Engineered Bearings segment toward the faster-growing, acquisition-fed Industrial Motion segment, backed by a newly formalized "Elevate to Outperform" commercial/operational plan. Company-flagged risks — competitive pricing pressure from SKF/Schaeffler, steel-input cost/tariff exposure, cyclicality, and acquisition-integration risk — are the standard risks for a diversified industrial manufacturer rather than anything idiosyncratic. Note a data-quality wrinkle worth being aware of: PaoBull's cached "FCF" figure ($554.3M) is actually the company's operating cash flow, not free cash flow after capex (true FY2025 FCF was $406.1M) — a modest but real definitional gap between the cached synthesis and the underlying 10-K. Net: an inflecting industrial business trading at a price the market has, for now, treated skeptically relative to its own recently-improved guidance.
