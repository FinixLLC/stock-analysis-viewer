## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to bridge the historical separation between prestige, mass, and salon product channels. The company’s business model centers on a "beauty enthusiast" target consumer, offering a differentiated assortment of approximately 30,000 products from around 600 brands across all price points, complemented by in-store salon services and an omnichannel digital experience. Ulta’s competitive moat is derived from several key differentiators: a best-in-class loyalty program with over 46 million members that drives approximately 95% of total sales and provides deep consumer data for personalization; a convenient store footprint of over 1,500 U.S. locations, predominantly in high-traffic power strip centers; and exclusive or early-access brand partnerships, including private-label products that contribute to margin and differentiation. Internationally, the company expands through its Space NK subsidiary in the U.K. and Ireland, a joint venture in Mexico, and a franchise in the Middle East.

Management’s stated priorities, outlined in the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan unveiled in 2025, focus on three strategic pillars: driving core business growth through operational excellence and an elevated go-to-market approach; scaling new, accretive businesses such as wellness, the UB Marketplace, and international expansion; and aligning the company’s foundation for future success by optimizing ways of working, streamlining costs, and cultivating an associate-centered culture. Specifically, the company aims to grow its U.S. store footprint to more than 1,800 locations, accelerate digital capabilities to capture high-value omnichannel guests, and leverage its position as a retail partner for brand launches and global scaling.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the company’s ability to maintain profitability amidst macroeconomic headwinds. Management notes that persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures could impact consumer spending habits, the company’s ability to grow sales, and its historical profitability levels. Additionally, these pressures may keep interest rates elevated, increasing the cost of debt, as evidenced by the shift from net interest income in fiscal 2024 to net interest expense in fiscal 2025 due to increased borrowings. The company also highlights the volatility of comparable sales, which are sensitive to general economic conditions, changes in merchandise strategy, and the effectiveness of marketing activities. Furthermore, the filing notes that the company’s financial performance is dependent on its ability to execute its strategic priorities, with SG&A expenses deleveraging in fiscal 2025 due to higher incentive compensation, store payroll, and corporate overhead related to strategic investments.
