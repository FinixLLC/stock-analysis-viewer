## Business Profile

Source: live SEC 10-K fetch (FY2025, filed 2025-12-18, fiscal year ended 2025-11-02, self-extracted and synthesized directly from Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors).

What the company does: Broadcom is a global technology leader (60+ year lineage tracing to AT&T/Bell Labs, Lucent, HP, built up through major acquisitions including LSI, Broadcom Corp., Brocade, CA, Symantec Enterprise Security, and VMware) spanning two broad businesses: Semiconductor Solutions (custom AI accelerators/XPUs, Ethernet switching/routing silicon and NICs, optical components, wireless RF/connectivity chips, server/storage connectivity chips, broadband/set-top-box SoCs, industrial semiconductors) and Infrastructure Software (VMware Cloud Foundation private-cloud platform, mainframe software, Symantec/Carbon Black cybersecurity, enterprise software, FC SAN management).

Moat, as described in the company's own words: On the semiconductor side, Broadcom's differentiator is custom-silicon design partnership depth — it provides "advanced technology and IP platforms for customers to design and develop ASICs for AI and high-performance computing," working directly with hyperscalers/AI-frontier-model companies to build their own custom accelerators (XPUs) rather than selling off-the-shelf GPUs — a fundamentally different competitive position from NVIDIA's merchant-silicon model. On the software side, VMware Cloud Foundation is positioned to deliver "public cloud scale and agility with private cloud security... at lower total cost of ownership as compared to native public cloud" — an on-premises/hybrid-cloud alternative to the public hyperscalers, which is a somewhat unusual position given Broadcom is simultaneously a critical AI-networking supplier *to* those same hyperscalers (see Arista/AWS/Google Cloud edges in the ANET and AMZN reports in this batch).

Segment mix: Semiconductor Solutions has become the dominant growth engine (68% of Q2 FY26 revenue, +79% YoY) and is now overwhelmingly AI-driven ($10.8B of the segment's ~$15B was AI-related in Q2). Infrastructure Software (32% of revenue, +9% YoY) is the far more mature, cash-generative-but-slow-growing VMware-centric business.

Management's stated priorities (from Item 1 and Q2 FY2026 earnings commentary): CEO Hock Tan's central message is unambiguous acceleration in AI semiconductor demand — guiding Q3 AI-semiconductor revenue growth of "over 200 percent year-over-year," driven by "increasing demand for custom AI accelerators and AI networking." CFO Kirsten Spears reinforced "strong operating leverage" with non-GAAP operating margin guided to remain stable at 67% despite the 84% revenue growth guidance — i.e., management is explicitly signaling that this growth is scaling profitably, not diluting margins.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A Risk Factors Summary, business-risk-first ordering):
1. Semiconductor industry cyclicality "undergoing profound change due to AI" — explicitly acknowledges the current AI-driven upcycle could reverse as sharply as it accelerated.
2. Customer concentration — "a significant reduction in demand or loss of one or more of our significant customers may adversely affect us"; aggregate top-5-customer sales were ~40% of net revenue in both FY2025 and FY2024, with distributors alone accounting for 48% of revenue.
3. Contract-manufacturer/supply-chain dependency — Broadcom outsources the majority of its front-end wafer manufacturing to external foundries, explicitly naming TSMC, plus assembly/test partners (ASE, Foxconn, Amkor, Siliconware) — "dependence on a limited number of contract manufacturers and suppliers of critical materials... may adversely affect our ability to bring products to market."
4. Gross margin maintainability — explicitly flagged as a distinct risk factor given the rapid mix-shift toward AI semiconductors.
5. Cybersecurity and open-source software risk in the software portfolio (VMware/Symantec), standard for an enterprise software vendor at this scale.
