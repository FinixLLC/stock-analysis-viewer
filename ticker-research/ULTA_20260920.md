## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to bridge the historical separation between prestige, mass, and salon product channels. The company’s business model centers on a "specialty retail concept" that offers a differentiated assortment of approximately 30,000 products from roughly 600 brands across all price points, combined with in-store salon services and an omnichannel digital presence. Ulta targets "beauty enthusiasts," a demographic it estimates at 140 million in the U.S., leveraging a network of over 1,500 U.S. stores—typically around 10,000 square feet with dedicated salon space—alongside international operations through its Space NK subsidiary in the U.K. and Ireland, a joint venture in Mexico, and a franchise in the Middle East. The company’s competitive moat is derived from several strategic differentiators: a broad, exclusive, and private-label product assortment (including the Ulta Beauty Collection); a best-in-class loyalty program with over 46 million members that drives approximately 95% of total sales and provides deep consumer data for personalization; and a convenient omnichannel footprint that integrates physical store discovery with digital capabilities such as same-day delivery and buy-online-pickup-in-store.

Management’s stated priorities are outlined in the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan, which focuses on three areas: driving core business growth through operational excellence, digital acceleration, and merchandising innovation; scaling new, accretive businesses by expanding into wellness, launching the UB Marketplace online platform, and growing its retail media network (UB Media); and aligning the company’s foundation for future success by optimizing workflows, streamlining costs, and fostering an associate-centered culture. The filing flags several material risks, including the impact of persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures on consumer spending and profitability, the potential for elevated interest rates to increase debt costs, and the inherent volatility of comparable sales driven by general economic conditions, competition, and the effectiveness of marketing activities. Additionally, the company notes that its growth strategy depends on its ability to execute these strategic priorities while managing the deleverage of fixed costs associated with store expansion and strategic investments.
