## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates through a dual-engine business model comprising the Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL) segments, supplemented by a smaller strategic investment arm (QSI). QCT generates the majority of revenue by selling integrated circuit products, primarily Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, modems, and radio frequency components for handsets, automotive digital cockpits, and IoT devices. QTL generates high-margin revenue by licensing its intellectual property portfolio, including patents essential to wireless standards, to device manufacturers. In fiscal 2025, total revenues reached $44.3 billion, driven by a 16% increase in QCT revenues from higher handset, IoT, and automotive sales, while QTL revenues remained flat. The company’s competitive position relies on its leadership in on-device AI, high-performance low-power computing, and cellular technology standardization, allowing it to embed its technology into premium-tier Android devices and expanding into non-mobile industries. Management explicitly prioritizes maintaining leadership in technology development, standardization, and IP creation to sustain long-term growth, while also investing in key diversification initiatives such as automotive and IoT.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant tax-related impacts and global trade uncertainties. In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Qualcomm recorded a $5.7 billion charge to income tax expense to establish a valuation allowance against federal deferred tax assets. This was triggered by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB), which subjects the company to the corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) starting in fiscal 2026, leading management to conclude that substantially all existing federal deferred tax assets will not be realized. Additionally, the company faces inherent demand seasonality and volatility tied to consumer device launch cycles and innovation transitions. Management also highlights the uncertainty surrounding global trade policies, including tariffs and related actions by the U.S., China, and other countries, which could negatively impact demand, pricing, costs, and inventory levels. Finally, the filing notes ongoing legal and regulatory proceedings with inherently uncertain outcomes, as well as the risk that changes in future taxable income or tax laws could further alter the determination regarding the realizability of deferred tax assets.
