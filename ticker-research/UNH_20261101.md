## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (UNH_10-K_20260906.txt)

UnitedHealth Group operates as a vertically integrated health care and well-being company with two complementary businesses: Optum, an information and technology-enabled health services provider, and UnitedHealthcare, a health benefits provider. The company’s business model relies on leveraging data analytics, clinical expertise, and technology to serve patients, providers, payers, and governments. Optum serves the broad health care marketplace through three segments: Optum Health, which delivers patient-centered care and value-based arrangements; Optum Insight, which provides data, analytics, and technology solutions to health systems, plans, and governments; and Optum Rx, which manages pharmacy care services and prescription drug spend. UnitedHealthcare offers health benefits to consumers and employers across three distinct markets: Employer Individual, Medicare Retirement, and Community State. The company generates revenue through premiums for risk-based health plans, fees for administrative and management services, and sales of products and services, while utilizing its internal capabilities to coordinate care, manage costs, and improve outcomes.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its scale, data capabilities, and integrated ecosystem. UnitedHealthcare cites its strong local-market relationships, breadth of product offerings, and competitive medical and operating cost positions as key differentiators. Optum’s advantage lies in its ability to combine clinical expertise with technology and data to offer end-to-end solutions that improve health system performance, reduce costs, and enhance patient experience. This integration allows the company to serve multiple stakeholders in the health care system simultaneously, creating a seamless consumer experience and supporting clinicians with personalized, evidence-based care. The company also benefits from significant scale in pharmacy management, having managed $188 billion in pharmaceutical spending in 2025, and a large consumer base, with Optum Health serving 95 million consumers and UnitedHealthcare Employer Individual providing access to medical services for 29.7 million people.

Management’s stated priorities focus on building a modern, high-performing health system through improved access, affordability, outcomes, and experiences. A key strategic priority is accelerating the transition from fee-for-service care delivery and payment models to fully accountable value-based care, which requires investments in system enhancements, integrated care coordination technology, and clinical engagement. The company also emphasizes the use of data, analytics, and AI to provide clinicians with information for cost-efficient care. In 2025, management took actions to refine its portfolio, including divestitures and restructuring, to operationally advance and scale core businesses, particularly the value-based care business at Optum Health. Additionally, the company is focused on managing medical cost trends, which have been elevated above expectations, and adjusting pricing and benefit designs to maintain profitability while serving its members.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to regulatory pressures and medical cost trends. Medicare Advantage funding is under sustained pressure due to rate notices that have historically been below industry forward medical cost trends, with the 2027 Advanced Notice significantly below expected trends. This has led to expected membership contraction in 2026. The company also faces risks from the Inflation Reduction Act, which has shifted more risk to plans in Medicare Part D, increasing both premiums and medical costs. Medicaid funding and payment rates are described as insufficient to meet patient needs, creating downward pressure on margins, and the company expects membership losses in 2026 due to reduced eligibility and exits from certain states. Additionally, the company experienced elevated medical cost trends in 2025, driven by increased care patterns, unit costs, and service intensity, which significantly impacted earnings. The filing also notes risks related to the Change Healthcare cyberattack, which required the company to provide interest-free loans to care providers and increase reserves for net collection expectations.
