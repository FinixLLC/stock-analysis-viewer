## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (UNH_10-K_20260906.txt)

UnitedHealth Group operates as a vertically integrated health care and well-being company through two complementary businesses: Optum, an information and technology-enabled health services provider, and UnitedHealthcare, a health benefits provider. The company’s business model relies on leveraging deep health care expertise and complex data analytics to serve patients, providers, payers, and governments with end-to-end offerings. Optum serves the broad health care marketplace through three segments: Optum Health, which delivers patient-centered care and value-based arrangements; Optum Insight, which provides data, analytics, and technology solutions to health systems and plans; and Optum Rx, which manages pharmacy care services and prescription drug spend. UnitedHealthcare offers health benefits to consumers and employers across three distinct markets: Employer Individual, Medicare Retirement, and Community State. The company generates revenue through premium income for risk-based products, fees for administrative and management services, and sales of products and services, while utilizing its extensive provider networks and Optum’s capabilities to coordinate care and manage costs.

The company’s competitive moat is built on strong local-market relationships, the breadth of its product offerings, and its ability to integrate clinical expertise with technology and data. UnitedHealthcare cites its competitive medical and operating cost positions, effective clinical engagement, and advanced digital consumer engagement as key differentiators. Optum’s advantage lies in its distinctive capabilities in data and analytics, population health, and health financial services, which allow it to offer seamless consumer experiences and support clinicians with personalized, evidence-based care. Additionally, Optum Insight maintains a significant order backlog, approximately $31.1 billion as of December 31, 2025, derived from long-term contracts with health systems, plans, and government entities, indicating sticky, recurring revenue streams.

Management’s stated priorities focus on building a modern, high-performing health system by improving access, affordability, outcomes, and experiences. A key strategic focus is accelerating the transition from traditional fee-for-service models to fully accountable value-based care, which requires investments in system enhancements, integrated care coordination technology, and clinical engagement. The company also prioritizes the use of data, analytics, and AI to provide clinicians with information for cost-efficient care delivery. In 2025, management executed a strategic review of assets to scale core businesses, resulting in portfolio divestitures and restructuring actions, including workforce reductions and real estate rationalization, to improve operational efficiency. Furthermore, the company is focused on mitigating medical cost increases through enhanced audit, clinical policy, and payment integrity tools.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory pressures and medical cost trends. Management notes that Medicare Advantage funding has been pressured by base rates that have historically fallen below industry forward medical cost trends, with the 2027 Advanced Notice significantly below expected trends, leading to anticipated membership contraction in 2026. The Inflation Reduction Act has altered the Medicare Part D model, shifting more risk to plans and increasing both premiums and medical costs. Additionally, the company faces elevated medical cost trends driven by increased care activity, higher unit costs, and provider pricing increases, which have significantly impacted earnings. In the Medicaid market, management identifies a timing mismatch between the health status of people served and state rate updates, creating downward pressure on margins. Other flagged risks include the competitive nature of the commercial risk market, potential membership losses due to regulatory impacts and benefit design changes, and the financial impact of the Change Healthcare cyberattack, which required the company to provide interest-free loans to care providers and increase reserves for net collection expectations.
