The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its long-term strategic partnership with OpenAI, established in 2019 and extended in 2025 and 2026, which grants Microsoft rights to OpenAI’s intellectual property, including models and infrastructure, for integration into its products, as well as revenue-sharing payments. Management’s stated priorities center on making digital technology and artificial intelligence available broadly and responsibly, with a heavy emphasis on investing in cloud and AI infrastructure, research and development compute capacity, and AI talent to support product development across the portfolio. The company is actively expanding datacenter locations and server capacity to meet growing demand for AI services, while also investing in commercial sales and advertising for products like Microsoft 365 Copilot. These strategic investments are intended to drive growth in cloud services and maintain leadership in a dynamic and highly competitive industry.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the high costs associated with these strategic investments. The company also highlights supply chain vulnerabilities, noting that while it can often switch manufacturers for devices, some products rely on components with very few qualified suppliers, and datacenters depend on the availability of land, energy, and specific hardware like graphics processing units. Furthermore, the filing identifies exposure to global macroeconomic and geopolitical factors, including foreign exchange rate fluctuations and changes in global trade policies such as tariffs, which could impact supply chains, cost volatility, and consumer demand. Finally, the company emphasizes that its success is highly dependent on its ability to attract and retain qualified employees in a competitive global labor market.
