## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (WELL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Welltower Inc. is a real estate investment trust (REIT) and S&P 500 company that positions itself at the center of the "silver economy" by owning and managing rental housing for aging seniors in the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada. The company operates through an umbrella partnership REIT (UPREIT) structure, with Welltower Inc. holding approximately 98.4% of Welltower OP LLC, which conducts the day-to-day business. Welltower’s portfolio consists of over 2,500 seniors and wellness housing communities, which it categorizes into three reportable segments: Seniors Housing Operating, Triple-net, and Outpatient Medical. The Seniors Housing Operating segment, which generated 78% of total revenues in 2025, involves active management of properties ranging from independent living to assisted living and Alzheimer’s/dementia care, often through incentive-based contracts with external partners such as Care UK, Cogir Management Company, and Sunrise Senior Living. The Triple-net segment, accounting for 11% of 2025 revenues, involves leasing properties to operators under long-term master leases (typically 10 to 20 years) that require the tenant to pay all operating costs, utilities, and taxes, with Welltower not involved in property management. The Outpatient Medical segment, representing 7% of 2025 revenues, consists primarily of triple-net leased buildings affiliated with health systems. Additionally, Welltower engages in development, construction, and real estate lending, holding $2.08 billion in loans with an approximate 8.9% yield and $1.81 billion in investments in unconsolidated entities as of year-end 2025.

The company’s competitive moat is described as being driven by a "disciplined approach to capital allocation" powered by a proprietary Data Science platform and "superior operating results" driven by the Welltower Business System, an end-to-end operational platform. Management emphasizes that this combination allows for the long-term compounding of per-share growth. Furthermore, the Triple-net segment’s use of integrated master leases creates a structural advantage by bundling properties, which prevents tenants from cherry-picking high-performing assets during renewals or bankruptcies, thereby spreading risk across the entire leased group.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term compounding of per-share earnings growth for existing shareholders. To achieve this, the company focuses on deploying capital into real estate in affluent micro-markets that benefit from secular demand, seeking to generate durable cash flow growth. The investment strategy involves partnering with aligned, high-quality operators who demonstrate staying power across economic cycles, investing at a compelling basis with a meaningful margin of safety, and structuring investments to protect downside risk and avoid permanent capital loss.

The filing flags several material risks and accounting complexities. In the Seniors Housing Operating segment, the company relies heavily on external partners for effective and efficient property management, with the top three partners accounting for 36% of segment revenues in 2025, creating concentration risk. In the Triple-net segment, the company notes that certain leases contain purchase options that could result in property dispositions for less than full market value if exercised. Additionally, the filing highlights significant credit risk associated with its loan portfolio, requiring quarterly evaluations of collectability based on borrower financial strength and collateral value, with the potential for loans to be placed on non-accrual status if credit quality deteriorates. The company also faces operational risks related to its construction investments, where it must monitor cost overruns and completion guarantees, and it carries substantial obligations, including $738.9 million in outstanding construction investments and $493 million in committed additional funds. Finally, the company acknowledges the complexity of valuing stock-based compensation, particularly for Executive Key Employee LTIP Unit Awards, which requires the use of Monte Carlo valuation models and significant management judgment regarding fair value and expense recognition.
