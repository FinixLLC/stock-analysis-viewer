## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX Companies operates as the leading global off-price retailer of apparel and home fashions, managing over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is defined by "opportunistic buying," where it acquires merchandise from more than 100 countries through closeouts, special productions, and order cancellations rather than ordering far in advance like traditional retailers. This approach allows TJX to offer a rapidly changing assortment of brand-name and designer goods at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers, creating a "treasure hunt" shopping experience that drives frequent customer visits. The model relies on a flexible supply chain, lean inventory levels, and low-cost operations, including minimal advertising spend and flexible store layouts that allow for quick adjustments to merchandise mix.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its scale and buying power, which enables it to source from a diverse universe of approximately 21,000 vendors while maintaining favorable terms, such as prompt payment and no demands for retail concessions. TJX differentiates itself through its ability to react quickly to market trends and price fluctuations, maintaining its pricing gap and margins through various economic cycles. Additionally, the company leverages synergies across its global retail chains to share best practices and develop talent, while its specialized inventory management systems allow for rapid turnover and precise allocation of merchandise to local store demographics.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day and continuing global growth through store expansion, with an estimated long-term store potential of 7,000 locations. The company emphasizes maintaining a low cost structure, investing in supply chain efficiency to support low inventory levels, and enhancing the customer shopping experience through store renovations and service. Recent financial highlights indicate a focus on returning capital to shareholders, with $4.3 billion returned via repurchases and dividends in fiscal 2026, alongside efforts to mitigate the impact of global economic volatility and tariffs on merchandise sourcing.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to global economic conditions and international trade relations. Specifically, the company flags significant uncertainty regarding tariffs on imports from China and other countries, noting that while it has been successful in mitigating some pressures, the overall impact on direct imports, vendor pricing, and consumer demand remains uncertain. The filing also highlights risks associated with foreign currency exchange rates, which affect both translation of foreign operations and transactional costs, as well as the potential for legal and regulatory developments, such as the recent U.S. Supreme Court decision invalidating certain tariffs and the subsequent imposition of new global tariffs. Additionally, the company notes the inherent risks of operating in a highly competitive retail environment and the dependence on its buying organization’s ability to execute its sourcing model effectively amidst these external pressures.
