## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NWPX_10-K_20260906.txt)

NWPX Infrastructure, Inc. operates as the largest manufacturer of engineered water transmission systems in North America, generating approximately 67% of its revenue from this segment and the remainder from precast infrastructure and engineered systems. The company’s business model involves selling steel casing pipe, bar-wrapped concrete cylinder pipe, and precast concrete products to installation contractors, who then bid on projects for federal, state, and municipal agencies, private water companies, and developers. Demand is driven by urban growth, new infrastructure construction, and a growing trend toward replacement and repair of existing systems. With 13 manufacturing facilities across North America, NWPX leverages a long-term project horizon, with water transmission projects often part of 50-year build-out plans, while precast demand is more sensitive to housing starts, population growth, and interest rates.

The company’s competitive position is anchored by its status as the market leader in engineered water transmission systems and its strategic geographic expansion, including the recent acquisition of Boughton’s Precast in Colorado to enhance regional availability for stormwater and sanitary sewer products. NWPX differentiates itself through a diversified product portfolio that addresses both high-pressure, large-diameter pipeline applications (steel) and municipal stormwater/sewer needs (precast), allowing it to capture demand across different infrastructure cycles.

Management’s stated priorities focus on meeting growing water and wastewater infrastructure needs through quality, innovation, and core values of accountability, commitment, and teamwork. Strategically, the company is broadening its manufacturing footprint to increase capacity and improve regional availability, as evidenced by investments in new reinforced concrete pipe mills and catch basin facilities in Utah. Capital allocation priorities include organic growth initiatives, acquisitions, share repurchases, and maintaining liquidity through operating cash flows and credit facilities, with a specific emphasis on funding working capital requirements that arise from the timing mismatch between material payments and customer collections.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on economic and regulatory uncertainty. Key risks include the potential for delayed federal funding from the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) and the Inflation Reduction Act due to executive orders, staffing cuts, and funding disputes, which could impact future project bids. Additionally, the company faces exposure to volatile steel markets, as purchased steel represents approximately 29% of water transmission system costs and contracts are often fixed-price, creating margin risk if steel prices fluctuate. Other flagged risks include the impact of tariffs and global economic policy on construction activity and costs, as well as the general economic headwinds of inflation, potential recession, and elevated interest rates that may temper demand for precast products.
