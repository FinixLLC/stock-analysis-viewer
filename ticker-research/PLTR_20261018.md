## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that builds platforms to help organizations integrate data, decisions, and operations at scale. The company’s business model centers on four principal software platforms: Gotham, which serves defense and intelligence missions; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform for commercial and government enterprises; Apollo, a continuous delivery platform that manages infrastructure and software updates across various environments; and AIP, a generative AI platform launched in 2023 that connects large language models to enterprise data and operations. Palantir generates revenue primarily through subscription contracts, typically ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the contract term. In 2025, the company generated $4.5 billion in revenue, with 54% derived from government customers and 46% from commercial customers, while 74% of total revenue came from the United States.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its vertically integrated platforms and the "Palantir Ontology," a core architecture that maps data to meaningful context and integrates data, logic, and actions into a decision-centric system. Management argues that this architecture allows users of varying technical skills to collaborate effectively and scale operations more efficiently than traditional software development, which often suffers from long timelines and high failure rates. Palantir targets large-scale, complex, and high-risk opportunities that smaller competitors may avoid due to installation costs and execution risks. Additionally, the company leverages its ability to deploy solutions rapidly—often in days via AIP bootcamps—and maintains deep, long-term partnerships with customers, evidenced by a dollar-weighted average contract duration of four years and a total remaining deal value of $11.2 billion as of the end of 2025.

Management’s stated priorities include expanding access to its platforms across a broader swath of the market in both commercial and government sectors, developing industry-specific operating systems for sectors such as healthcare, automotive, and defense, and capturing a greater share of U.S. federal government software spending. The company also emphasizes building strategic relationships through cloud partnerships, joint ventures, and channel sales to extend its distribution capabilities. Furthermore, management is focused on driving account growth by scaling contracts horizontally to include additional divisions and vertically to include more users, while continuing to invest in an account-based sales force to identify new opportunities.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the company’s heavy reliance on government customers, who are subject to budget uncertainties, changes in spending priorities, and regulatory shifts. Many contracts contain termination for convenience provisions, and U.S. federal government contracts are subject to specific rules regarding the exercise of options, creating uncertainty in revenue realization. Additionally, the company faces risks from macroeconomic trends, including geopolitical tensions, fluctuating interest rates, and foreign currency exchange rates, which can disrupt customer operations and impact financial results. While management states that current geopolitical conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East are not currently material to its financial position, it acknowledges that worsening conditions could negatively impact its business.
