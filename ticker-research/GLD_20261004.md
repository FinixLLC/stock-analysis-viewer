## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLD_10-K_20260906.txt)

SPDR Gold Trust (GLD) is a passive investment trust formed in 2004 under New York law, designed to provide investors with a cost-efficient and accessible means of gaining exposure to gold bullion prices without the logistical burdens of physical storage and insurance. The Trust’s business model is straightforward: it holds allocated gold bullion in vaults in London, New York, or Zurich, and issues shares in blocks of 100,000 (Baskets) in exchange for gold deposits from Authorized Participants, while distributing gold upon redemption. The Trust does not engage in active management, hold derivative securities, or generate income from cash; its sole asset is gold, and its only expenses are paid by selling small amounts of that gold. Consequently, the Trust maintains a zero cash balance, and the value of each share reflects the price of gold less cumulative expenses.

The Trust’s competitive position relies on its status as a highly liquid, exchange-traded vehicle that overcomes the barriers to entry associated with physical gold ownership. By trading on NYSE Arca and several international exchanges, GLD offers investors the ability to buy, sell, or short gold through traditional brokerage accounts with lower transaction costs than purchasing allocated bullion. The Trust’s integrity is underpinned by its structure: it holds 100% allocated London Good Delivery gold bars, verified by independent counts by Bureau Veritas, and is valued daily using the LBMA Gold Price PM, a widely recognized benchmark administered by ICE Benchmark Administration. This combination of physical backing, transparent valuation, and high liquidity distinguishes it from unallocated gold accounts or derivative-based products.

Management’s stated priorities, as reflected in the filing, center on maintaining the Trust’s role as a simple, cost-efficient proxy for gold prices. The Sponsor emphasizes the importance of minimizing expenses to preserve the correlation between share price and gold price, noting that the divergence between the share price and gold price over time is solely due to the cumulative effect of Trust expenses. The filing highlights the Trust’s commitment to transparency, with daily NAV reporting and regular independent audits of gold holdings. There is no indication of strategic initiatives to expand into other assets or active trading; the focus remains strictly on passive gold exposure.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are directly tied to the nature of the asset and the Trust’s structure. Primary among these is price volatility, as the value of the shares is entirely dependent on the market price of gold, which can fluctuate significantly. The filing explicitly states that investing in the shares does not insulate investors from this risk. Additionally, the Trust faces operational risks related to the custodians (HSBC and JPMorgan), including the potential for loss or depreciation of gold during storage or sale, although the Trustee and Sponsor disclaim liability for such losses. The filing also notes that the sale of gold to pay expenses is a taxable event for shareholders, which is a unique tax consideration compared to other investment vehicles. Finally, the reliance on the LBMA Gold Price for NAV determination introduces a risk that if this benchmark is deemed inappropriate, an alternative valuation method could result in materially different fair value pricing, affecting redemption values and market adjustments.
