## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KO_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Coca-Cola Company operates as a global "total beverage company," selling products in over 200 countries and territories. Its business model is bifurcated into concentrate operations and finished product operations. The concentrate segment, which accounts for 59% of net operating revenues and 85% of unit case volume, involves selling beverage bases, syrups, and certain finished beverages to independent bottling partners who handle manufacturing, packaging, and distribution. The finished product segment, representing the remaining 41% of revenue, consists primarily of consolidated bottling and distribution operations where the company directly sells finished beverages to retailers, distributors, and wholesalers, as well as direct-to-consumer sales through Costa retail stores. While the finished product segment generates higher revenue, the concentrate operations typically yield higher gross profit margins. The company’s portfolio includes major brands such as Coca-Cola, Sprite, Fanta, and Diet Coke, spanning categories from sparkling flavors to water, sports, coffee, tea, and plant-based beverages.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its extensive global distribution network and brand equity, which allows it to reach consumers through a vast ecosystem of independent bottling partners, distributors, and retailers. Management notes that the company is well-positioned to create value despite a highly competitive landscape, leveraging its ability to connect with consumers through a wide variety of beverage options. The scale of its operations is significant, with beverages bearing its trademarks accounting for 2.2 billion of the estimated 65 billion servings of all beverages consumed worldwide daily. This global footprint, combined with the asset-light nature of its primary concentrate business, provides a structural advantage in reaching diverse markets.

Management’s stated priorities focus on five growth pillars: shaping a portfolio of loved brands, transforming the marketing and innovation agenda, optimizing the Coca-Cola ecosystem, building talent and capabilities, and enhancing the company’s "license to win." The company emphasizes the importance of adapting to evolving consumer preferences, including the demand for low- and no-calorie options, sustainability, and digital engagement. It is actively investing in e-commerce, data analytics, and artificial intelligence to maintain brand relevance in the digital marketplace. Additionally, management highlights the critical importance of talent acquisition and retention, aiming to be a global employer of choice, and is committed to addressing health-related concerns by offering transparent nutrition information and responsible marketing practices.

The filing flags several material risks, with regulatory and legal compliance being a primary concern. The company faces significant exposure to evolving laws regarding climate change, plastic waste, water scarcity, and wastewater discharge, which could increase compliance costs, capital expenditures, and production costs. There is also substantial risk related to cybersecurity and data privacy, as the company relies heavily on information systems for operations and is subject to increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks and complex, cross-border data protection regulations. Financial risks include exposure to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, given that $28.8 billion of 2025 net operating revenues were derived from outside the United States, and interest rate volatility affecting its debt financing. Furthermore, the company identifies risks related to the potential impairment of noncurrent assets, including trademarks and goodwill, and the possibility of significant losses from counterparty defaults in its hedging activities and cash holdings. Finally, the company acknowledges the ongoing challenge of shifting consumer preferences toward health and sustainability, which requires continuous innovation and portfolio adaptation to maintain market share.
