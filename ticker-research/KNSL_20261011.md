## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KNSL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Kinsale Capital Group is a U.S.-based property and casualty insurer that operates exclusively within the excess and surplus lines (E&S) market, specializing in hard-to-place risks for small- to medium-sized commercial and personal accounts. The company’s business model relies on selling insurance products across all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands primarily through a network of independent brokers, while retaining full control over underwriting and claims management. Kinsale differentiates itself by leveraging a proprietary technology platform to drive operational efficiency, resulting in a significantly lower expense ratio than competitors, and by maintaining a "contrarian" risk appetite that allows it to underwrite risks that standard carriers may decline, thereby capturing higher margins in less competitive niches. In 2025, the company generated $2.0 billion in gross written premiums, with 97% derived from commercial lines and 70.7% from casualty coverages, achieving a combined ratio of 75.9% and a return on equity of 29.3%.

The company’s competitive moat is built on three pillars: exclusive focus on the E&S market, which historically offers higher margins than the standard admitted market; a proprietary digital infrastructure that enables rapid quoting, data analysis, and low-cost operations; and a highly experienced management team with an average of over 30 years of relevant industry experience. Kinsale emphasizes that it does not delegate underwriting authority to brokers, ensuring consistent pricing and coverage quality, and it manages all claims in-house to maintain reserve accuracy and control outcomes. This combination of technological efficiency, disciplined expense management, and specialized underwriting expertise allows Kinsale to maintain a low expense ratio (20.8% in 2025) while serving a broad spectrum of hard-to-place risks.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term stockholder value through consistent underwriting profits and prudent capital management. The strategic goals include expanding the company’s presence in the E&S market, which currently represents only about 1.5% of the total $129.8 billion market, by leveraging its service levels and speed to win business from independent brokers. Kinsale aims to maintain a strong, conservative balance sheet to support its growth and protect policyholder confidence, while continuing to invest in technology to drive efficiencies and scalability. The company seeks to balance growth with profitability, focusing on attractive returns rather than just volume, and maintaining a diversified book of business to mitigate risk.

The filing flags several material risks, including the inherent volatility of the insurance industry, such as fluctuations in loss ratios due to catastrophe losses, social inflation, and changes in legal environments. Kinsale notes that its Commercial Property division faced rate declines and increased competition from standard carriers, leading to a 17.9% decrease in gross written premiums in that specific line. Additionally, the company highlights risks related to the accuracy of loss reserves, noting that 91.2% of total net loss reserves are for claims incurred but not reported, which requires ongoing actuarial estimation and review. Other risks include the potential for administrative errors in policy forms, the impact of inflation on material, medical, and wage costs, and the need to continuously update its technology platform to maintain its competitive advantage.
