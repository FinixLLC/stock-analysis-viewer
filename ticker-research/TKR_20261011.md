## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Timken is a global industrial manufacturer that designs and manages a portfolio of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, supported by related services. Founded in 1899, the company operates through two reportable segments: Engineered Bearings and Industrial Motion. Its business model relies on leveraging deep expertise in metallurgy, friction management, and industrial motion to provide high-reliability components for demanding environments across diverse sectors such as automotive, aerospace, agriculture, mining, and renewable energy. Timken sells primarily through internal sales organizations and authorized distributors, emphasizing a collaborative, technical selling approach where sales engineers work closely with customers during product development and implementation. The company generates revenue from both original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and the aftermarket, capturing value throughout the equipment lifecycle through replacement cycles and services such as bearing repair and drivetrain maintenance.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, a position it has leveraged to develop a broad, differentiated portfolio of industry-leading products. Timken differentiates itself through sophisticated application engineering, proprietary internal geometries, high-precision tolerances, and quality materials that ensure high load-carrying capacity and long service lives. This technical depth is supported by a global footprint of 116 manufacturing facilities, 29 technology and engineering centers, and 74 distribution centers across 44 countries. The company competes based on a total value proposition that includes product performance, quality, technical support, and global service capabilities, distinguishing itself from competitors like SKF Group and Schaeffler Group by offering highly customized solutions for challenging applications and maintaining a strong aftermarket presence.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three primary strategic elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. For profitable growth, Timken aims to expand into new and existing markets by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and power transmission, with a specific emphasis on highly fragmented sectors that value reliability and offer significant aftermarket demand. Operational excellence involves a continuous improvement culture aimed at increasing efficiency, lowering costs, eliminating waste, and building brand equity. Regarding capital deployment, the company prioritizes investing in the core business through capital expenditures and R&D, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. Timken also highlights its commitment to sustainability, targeting a 50% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions intensity by 2030.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of incremental tariff costs, which significantly increased cost of products sold in 2025. The company also notes exposure to commodity price volatility for raw materials, specifically special bar quality (SBQ) steel, which is subject to changes in supply, demand, and energy costs. While Timken uses surcharge mechanisms to partially recover these costs, it remains exposed to market fluctuations. Additionally, the company faces risks related to lower demand in key regions such as the Americas and Europe, as well as operational risks associated with restructuring, including severance expenses and facility closures, such as the announced closure of its bearing manufacturing facility in Heilbronn, Germany. The filing also highlights the impact of foreign currency exchange rate changes on both revenue and costs, as well as pension remeasurement charges that affected net income.
