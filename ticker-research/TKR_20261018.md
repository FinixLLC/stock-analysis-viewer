## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company (TKR) operates as a global designer and manufacturer of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, supported by related services. Founded in 1899, the company leverages its historical expertise in tapered roller bearings to offer a broad portfolio of motion control solutions, including linear motion systems, industrial drives, automatic lubrication, belts, chains, couplings, and seals. Timken’s business model is built on a customer-centric, collaborative technical selling approach where sales engineers work closely with customers to develop unique solutions for demanding applications. The company serves a diverse customer base across sectors such as renewable energy, automation, automotive, agriculture, rail, aerospace, and heavy industries, with no single customer accounting for more than 5% of total net sales. Distribution is managed primarily through internal sales organizations and authorized distributors, supported by a global footprint of 116 manufacturing facilities, 29 technology centers, and 74 distribution centers across 44 countries.

Timken’s competitive moat is derived from its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, which allows it to apply deep metallurgical and friction management expertise across its entire product line. The company differentiates itself through sophisticated application engineering, high-precision tolerances, proprietary internal geometries, and quality materials that ensure high load-carrying capacity and long service lives. This technical depth is reinforced by a strong brand portfolio, including Timken, GGB, Philadelphia Gear, and Rollon, and a significant aftermarket presence that captures revenue throughout the equipment lifecycle. Timken competes primarily on total value proposition—design, performance, engineering support, and global service—rather than price alone, against major competitors such as SKF Group and Schaeffler Group.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three strategic elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. The company aims to expand into attractive, fragmented market sectors that value reliability and offer significant aftermarket demand, while maintaining a relentless drive for efficiency, cost reduction, and waste elimination. Capital allocation is directed toward core business investments, strategic acquisitions to broaden the portfolio, and returning capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases. In 2025, Timken highlighted its commitment to sustainability, noting a 42% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions intensity since 2018, and maintained its financial discipline by reducing total debt by $141 million and paying its 414th consecutive quarterly dividend.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of incremental tariff costs, which added $65 million to cost of products sold in 2025 and are expected to continue affecting 2026 results. The company also faces exposure to raw material price volatility, particularly for special bar quality (SBQ) steel, though it mitigates this through surcharge mechanisms in customer contracts. Operational risks include lower demand in key regions such as the Americas and Europe, which contributed to a decline in organic sales in the Engineered Bearings segment. Additionally, the company recognized higher impairment and restructuring charges in 2025, driven by severance related to a CEO transition and the closure of a manufacturing facility in Germany. While Timken expects revenue growth in 2026 driven by higher demand and pricing, it acknowledges that actual shipments depend on customers' ever-changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales.
