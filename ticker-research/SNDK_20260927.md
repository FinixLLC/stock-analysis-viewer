## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNDK_10-K_20260906.txt)

Sandisk (SNDK) is a vertically integrated global semiconductor memory company that specializes in NAND flash technology, having separated from Western Digital (WDC) and begun trading on the Nasdaq in February 2025. The company’s business model relies on owning chip-level design and IP, front- and back-end manufacturing, and systems engineering to deliver a broad portfolio of solid-state drives (SSDs), embedded products, removable cards, and wafers. Sandisk serves three primary end markets: Datacenter (cloud and enterprise), Edge (OEMs for PCs, mobile, automotive, and industrial), and Consumer (retail brands). A critical component of its operational structure is the "Flash Ventures" joint venture with Kioxia, through which Sandisk holds a 49.9% ownership stake and co-develops and manufactures flash memory wafers in Japan, while controllers are designed in-house or sourced from third parties.

The company’s competitive moat is built on several pillars: a vertically integrated supply chain that allows for cost leadership and manufacturing flexibility; a substantial intellectual property portfolio comprising approximately 8,000 granted patents and 3,000 pending applications; and deep, long-standing relationships with industry leaders across data ecosystems. Sandisk also leverages its strong consumer brand recognition and global retail distribution presence. Furthermore, the company is transitioning to a "New Business Model" (NBM) involving long-term agreements with Datacenter and Edge customers that include fixed and variable pricing mechanisms and financial guarantees, which management states reduces exposure to industry cyclicality and improves revenue predictability.

Management’s stated priorities focus on leveraging its innovation engine to drive technology leadership and cost efficiency, particularly in high-performance storage solutions for artificial intelligence (AI) workloads. The company aims to maintain operational excellence through best-in-class capital efficiency, quality, and supply chain management. A key strategic shift is the expansion of the NBM framework to create a more durable and predictable business model. Additionally, management emphasizes investing in high-value opportunities driven by AI infrastructure growth, which they expect to persist through calendar year 2027 and beyond.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policies, specifically pending investigations under Section 232 and Section 301 that could impact tariff rates. While most U.S. products are currently exempt, the loss of these exemptions or additional tariff increases would raise costs and potentially reduce demand. The company also notes the inherent risks of the semiconductor industry, including intense competition from vertically integrated suppliers like Kioxia, Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix. Furthermore, Sandisk faces execution risks related to its transition to the NBM, noting that while these agreements reduce cyclicality, they do not eliminate risks associated with customer demand, market conditions, or operational execution. Finally, the company recorded a $1.8 billion goodwill impairment charge in fiscal 2025 following the separation, highlighting the valuation challenges associated with its standalone status.
