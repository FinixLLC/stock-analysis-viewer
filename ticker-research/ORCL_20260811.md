## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2026 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/ORCL_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2026-05-31 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text.

What the company does: Oracle provides enterprise IT products and services — applications and infrastructure, increasingly AI-enhanced — delivered via cloud, on-premise, and hybrid deployment models. The company organizes itself into three reportable segments/businesses: Cloud and Software (87% of FY2026 revenue), Hardware (5%), and Services (8%). Cloud offerings split into Oracle Cloud Applications (OCA — ERP, HCM, CX, NetSuite, Oracle Health) and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI — compute, storage, networking, AI infrastructure, and the Oracle Autonomous AI Database).

Segment/product mix and trend: The defining trend in this 10-K is the cloud-infrastructure inflection: OCI (cloud infrastructure) revenue grew 77% YoY to $18.1B, now the largest single driver of cloud revenue mix growth (cloud infrastructure rose from 35% to 53% of total cloud revenue over three fiscal years, while cloud applications fell from 65% to 47% of cloud revenue over the same span — not because applications shrank, but because infrastructure grew so much faster). Legacy on-premise software license revenue continues to decline (−9% YoY), the expected mirror image of the cloud migration the company has been guiding toward for years.

Moat, as described in the company's own words:
- Full-stack integration across database (Oracle AI Database), middleware, applications and infrastructure, engineered to work together (e.g., Oracle Exadata Database Machine) — the company's stated differentiation versus point-solution competitors.
- Deep, multi-decade installed base of on-premise Oracle Database/applications customers with high switching costs, who the company is actively migrating to OCI/OCA — management states $5.4B of annualized cloud revenue growth over the past three years came specifically from customers migrating off legacy software-support contracts.
- Multicloud interoperability strategy (Oracle databases now available inside AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure environments) — a "coopetition" positioning rather than a walled garden, which the EVCDM data below corroborates.
- Scale of R&D investment: $10.3B in FY2026 (up from $9.9B and $8.9B in the prior two years).

Management's stated priorities (from Item 1): continued OCI capacity expansion (new data centers, new geographies) to meet what the company describes as accelerating demand; deepening AI capabilities embedded across both OCA and OCI (Autonomous AI Database, AI Vector Search, Select AI, Private AI Agent Factory); and continued migration of the legacy on-premise installed base to the cloud.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A Risk Factor Summary, company's own categorization):
1. Business/operational: may be unsuccessful developing/selling new products, including AI products that "may not operate as anticipated"; may be unable to secure data-center capacity at affordable rates or accurately plan infrastructure capacity requirements (a direct, self-disclosed risk given the scale of the current buildout); complexity of sourcing technologies/components for cloud and hardware offerings; intense competition (named directly: Adobe, Alphabet, Amazon, Cisco, Intel, IBM, Microsoft, Salesforce, SAP, plus HPE and Workday; and post-Cerner-acquisition, healthcare IT competitors Allscripts, Arcadia Solutions, athenahealth, Epic Systems, InterSystems).
2. Data privacy, cybersecurity and IP: cybersecurity incidents/data breaches; evolving global privacy regulation; third-party IP infringement claims (both defending against and asserting).
3. Legal/regulatory: litigation risk; international tax law changes; trade restrictions/export controls/sanctions affecting global operations and customer base; heavy healthcare-industry-specific regulation post-Cerner; government-customer-specific risks (budget cycles, shutdowns, procurement rules).
4. Financial risks — explicitly flagged, and now clearly materializing in the numbers above: quarterly result volatility; currency exposure; "risks associated with our outstanding and future indebtedness" (directly relevant given the $43B of new FY2026 senior notes); accounting-estimate risk.
5. Stock-specific risks: volatility; dilution risk from the 6.50% Series D Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock and the At-the-Market (ATM) common stock issuance program — i.e., Oracle has multiple disclosed levers (debt, preferred, ATM equity) it may pull to fund the AI buildout, each with its own dilution/leverage tradeoff.

## Bottom Line

Oracle's FY2026 (ended 2026-05-31) was a genuine inflection year on the top line — total revenue +17% to $67.4B, cloud revenue crossing 51% of the total for the first time, and OCI (cloud infrastructure) growing 77% — with a record $638B Remaining Performance Obligations backlog (up 363% YoY) that the market has largely adopted as the bull-case anchor for the stock. But that growth has come at a steep and clearly disclosed cost: capex more than doubled to $55.7B, driving free cash flow from a modest −$394M in FY2025 to a deeply negative −$23.7B in FY2026, funded substantially by $43B of new senior notes issued during the year, which pushed leverage (D/E) to roughly 3.9x on a live-market basis — a figure PaoBull's June cache (generated before this 10-K was filed) does not reflect at all (it shows D/E as 0.0, which should be disregarded, and an FCF figure that is now two full fiscal years stale in direction and roughly 60x smaller in magnitude than the current reality). The stock's price action mirrors this tension: down ~28% year-to-date, including its worst week since the 2001 dot-com bust in late June on AI-financing-related concerns, before rallying sharply (+9% in a single session) in early August on partnership news (Google Cloud/Gemini) and contract wins, and now trading (~$149-151) inside the lower half of PaoBull's own modeled entry-to-target range (entry ~$140.5, target $345.72, i.e., the 52-week high). PaoBull's own cached investment thesis explicitly flagged "FCF concerns weigh on valuation" as the core bear case as of late June — that concern has since been validated and intensified by the actual FY2026 numbers, not resolved by them. The EVCDM industry graph confirms Oracle occupies an unusually "coopetitive" position: it is named as a direct substitute/competitor by essentially every major enterprise software and hyperscale cloud vendor (AWS, Microsoft, Google, SAP, Salesforce, IBM, and others), while simultaneously running multicloud cooperation agreements with those same hyperscalers and depending on NVIDIA for the AI compute silicon underpinning its own infrastructure buildout. Net: a company executing a real, revenue-validated cloud/AI transition, financed by an aggressive and rapidly escalating debt-funded capex program whose success depends on converting a very large backlog into cash-generative revenue before the leverage becomes a binding constraint — the September 8, 2026 fiscal Q1 2027 earnings report will be an important next data point on that conversion.
