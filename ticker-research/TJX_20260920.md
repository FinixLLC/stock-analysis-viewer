## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX Companies is the leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer in the United States and worldwide, operating over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is built on "opportunistic buying," a strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise closer to the time of sale rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to offer a rapidly changing assortment of quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers, creating a "treasure hunt" shopping experience that encourages frequent customer visits. The model relies on a flexible supply chain, lean inventory levels, and a global buying organization that sources from over 100 countries and approximately 21,000 vendors, often purchasing closeouts, overruns, and special production runs.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale, buying power, and operational flexibility. With a global footprint and substantial buying power, TJX can leverage synergies across its retail chains to share best practices and talent while maintaining a low cost structure. The company notes that it does not typically ask for retail concessions such as advertising allowances or performance-based return privileges, and it pays promptly, making it an attractive channel for vendors. Additionally, its distribution network, comprising approximately 31 million square feet of distribution centers, is designed to support quick, efficient delivery and rapid in-store inventory turnover, allowing the company to adjust merchandise assortments more frequently than traditional retailers.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day through a value proposition of brand, fashion, price, and quality. The company emphasizes maintaining a low cost structure, investing in its supply chain to operate with low inventory levels, and strategically renovating stores to enhance the shopping experience. Growth remains a key focus, with the company citing store expansion as an important part of its global strategy, noting estimated long-term store growth potential of approximately 7,000 stores globally. In fiscal 2026, management highlighted the importance of mitigating tariff pressures and maintaining pricing gaps relative to traditional retailers despite economic volatility.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the impact of international trade relations and tariffs. Management states that tariffs have led to significant volatility in the global economy and that uncertainty remains regarding their impact on direct imports, vendor pricing, and consumer demand, despite efforts to mitigate these pressures through sourcing strategies. Other flagged risks include the impact of foreign currency exchange rates on operating results, particularly for the Canadian and International segments, and the competitive nature of the retail apparel and home fashion industry, where TJX competes with department, specialty, discount, and online retailers on factors such as price, selection, and shopping experience. The company also notes that its results are subject to risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially from forward-looking statements.
