## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CVS_10-K_20260906.txt)

CVS Health operates as an integrated health solutions company that connects care across four distinct but synergistic segments: Health Care Benefits, Health Services, Pharmacy Consumer Wellness, and Corporate/Other. The company’s business model relies on a vertically integrated ecosystem that combines insurance, pharmacy benefit management (PBM), and direct care delivery. As of December 31, 2025, the company serves approximately 37 million people through its Aetna insurance operations, manages benefits for roughly 87 million plan members through its CVS Caremark PBM, and operates approximately 9,000 retail pharmacy locations alongside more than 1,000 walk-in and primary care medical clinics. This integration allows CVS to leverage data and scale to offer personalized, technology-driven care, aiming to improve health outcomes while lowering overall costs for consumers and employers.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from this unique combination of assets, which creates a "world of health" around each consumer. By owning the payer (Aetna), the PBM (CVS Caremark), and the provider network (including retail pharmacies, Oak Street Health, and Signify Health), CVS controls a significant portion of the healthcare value chain. This structure enables the company to negotiate with a nationwide provider network of approximately 2.0 million participating providers and pharmaceutical manufacturers, while simultaneously delivering care directly to patients. The filing highlights that this integrated model allows for the creation of new sources of value through personalized care delivery and the ability to guide members through their healthcare journey using enterprise technology and data analytics, including investments in artificial intelligence.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on simplifying healthcare experiences, improving engagement, lowering costs, and delivering better health outcomes. The company’s ambition is to be America’s most trusted healthcare company, with a purpose to simplify care for individuals, families, and communities. Strategically, management focuses on best-in-class execution, transforming consumer experiences, and harnessing enterprise capabilities enabled by innovation and capital stewardship. Specific operational priorities include expanding Medicare Advantage offerings, leveraging technology to automate and augment operational capabilities, and maintaining a focus on safety and quality.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant regulatory and legislative pressures, particularly regarding PBM activities and government funding for Medicare and Medicaid programs. The company notes that proposed or enacted regulations in a majority of states and at the federal level could adversely affect its ability to conduct business on commercially reasonable terms and standardize services across state lines. Additionally, the filing highlights persistent elevated healthcare utilization levels, which may pressure the Health Care Benefits and Health Services segments. Financial risks are also prominent, evidenced by a $5.7 billion goodwill impairment charge related to the Health Care Delivery reporting unit and over $1.5 billion in litigation charges in 2025 related to past business practices and opioid litigation. Furthermore, the company faces margin pressures from pharmaceutical manufacturers regarding rebate calculations and collection, as well as consumer spending shifts toward value retailers that could impact front-store sales in the Pharmacy Consumer Wellness segment.
