## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MP_10-K_20260827.txt)

MP Materials Corp. is the largest producer of rare earth materials in the Western Hemisphere, operating a vertically integrated business model that spans the entire supply chain from mining to magnet manufacturing. The company’s operations are divided into two segments: Materials, which encompasses the upstream mining and midstream refining of rare earth oxides at the Mountain Pass facility in California, and Magnetics, which involves the downstream production of neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) permanent magnets and precursor products at the Independence Facility in Texas. Historically, MP Materials generated the majority of its revenue by selling rare earth concentrate to a single distributor for resale to Chinese refiners; however, in July 2025, the company ceased all sales to China to align with a new strategic focus on domestic supply chain security. This shift has redirected the business model toward selling separated rare earth products, such as neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) oxide, to customers in Japan, South Korea, and broader Asia, as well as producing magnets for U.S. automotive and defense clients.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its status as the only scaled, vertically integrated rare earth mining and processing site in North America, combined with a "world-class" low-cost production structure at Mountain Pass. This geographic and operational uniqueness is reinforced by a "transformational" public-private partnership with the U.S. Department of War (DoW), formerly the Department of Defense. This partnership provides a significant structural advantage through a ten-year price floor protection agreement for NdPr products, a guaranteed minimum EBITDA of $140 million annually for the future 10X magnet facility, and substantial financial support, including a $400 million investment in preferred stock, a $150 million loan, and access to committed bank financing. Additionally, MP Materials has secured long-term demand visibility through definitive supply agreements with major corporate customers, including General Motors and Apple, the latter of which includes $200 million in prepayments for magnets and the development of recycling capabilities.

Management’s stated priorities center on restoring the full rare earth magnetics supply chain to the United States to maximize long-term stockholder returns while supporting national security. Key strategic initiatives include the "Upstream 60K" strategy to increase annual rare earth oxide production to approximately 60,000 metric tons, the expansion of the Independence Facility to 3,000 metric tons of magnets annually, and the construction of a second, larger magnet facility (the 10X Facility) expected to produce 7,000 metric tons annually by 2028. The company is also prioritizing the extension of heavy rare earth element (HREE) refining capabilities at Mountain Pass, specifically for samarium oxide, and the implementation of commercial-scale magnet recycling lines to utilize post-industrial and end-of-life feedstock.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the immediate negative impact on revenue and liquidity resulting from the cessation of sales to China, which caused a 21% year-over-year revenue decline in the Materials segment. The company also highlights the execution risks associated with its ambitious expansion plans, including the construction of the 10X Facility and the HREE Facility, which require significant capital expenditure and operational scaling. Furthermore, MP Materials faces market risks related to the volatility and disruption in the global rare earth market caused by Chinese export controls and trade tensions, as well as the inherent risks of relying on a limited number of large customers for its downstream magnet sales.
