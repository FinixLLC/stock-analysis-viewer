## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QLYS_10-K_20260827.txt)

Qualys operates as a leading provider of a cloud-based platform that delivers integrated information technology (IT), security, and compliance solutions, primarily through a software-as-a-service (SaaS) model with renewable annual subscriptions. The company’s Enterprise TruRisk Platform enables customers to identify and manage IT and operational technology assets across on-premises, endpoint, cloud, container, and mobile environments, while simultaneously collecting security data, prioritizing vulnerabilities, quantifying cyber risk, and verifying remediation actions. By consolidating these functions into a single, multi-tenant cloud infrastructure, Qualys aims to replace fragmented point solutions with a unified view of an organization's security posture. The company generates revenue by invoicing customers for the full subscription amount upfront, which is then recognized ratably over the subscription term, creating a deferred revenue model that supports predictable cash flows. In 2025, the company reported revenues of $669.1 million, a 10% increase from the prior year, driven by both new customer acquisition and expansion within its existing base of over 10,000 customers, which includes a majority of the Forbes Global 100.

Qualys’s competitive moat is rooted in its integrated, single-platform architecture that eliminates the complexity and cost associated with managing disparate security tools. The company highlights several key advantages: the elimination of hardware management for customers, real-time visibility via a web interface without plugins or VPNs, and seamless scalability that allows customers to add coverage and users without significant deployment friction. A critical differentiator is the company’s extensive knowledge base of vulnerability signatures, which is updated in real-time, and its TruRisk scoring capabilities that quantify cyber risk in financial terms by combining severity, exploitability, asset criticality, and business context. Furthermore, the platform’s ability to ingest and correlate high volumes of sensor data across millions of devices using Elasticsearch clusters provides a depth of analytics and asset inventory management (via the GAV framework) that is difficult for point-solution competitors to replicate. The company also leverages a global distribution network of 15 shared cloud platforms and a private cloud offering (PCP) to serve diverse customer needs, reinforcing its position as a comprehensive, end-to-end solution provider.

Management’s stated priorities focus on continuous investment in research and development to improve existing solutions and develop new capabilities, as well as expanding sales and marketing efforts to support new solutions and drive growth. The company explicitly states its intention to continue devoting resources to R&D to enhance its platform and to invest in sales and marketing teams to support new solutions, acknowledging that these investments will increase absolute operating expenses. Additionally, management emphasizes the importance of maintaining a strong market position to drive demand from both new and existing customers, noting that 76% of the revenue increase in 2025 came from existing customers, indicating a strategic focus on customer retention and expansion (upselling) alongside new business development. The company also prioritizes operational efficiency by leveraging its cloud infrastructure to reduce total cost of ownership for customers while maintaining high levels of security and compliance support.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the macroeconomic environment and its impact on customer spending. Management warns that inflationary pressure, high interest rates, market volatility, and geopolitical conflicts could lead to reduced IT security spending by customers, potentially adversely affecting long-term business performance. The company also notes that extended sales cycles and reduced spending could expose it to greater risk, particularly as it continues to invest in headcount and marketing. While the excerpts do not provide a comprehensive list of all risk factors, they highlight the vulnerability of the business to broader economic disruptions that may cause customers to delay or eliminate security investments. Additionally, the company acknowledges the risks associated with hiring new sales personnel, noting that new hires may not be immediately productive, which could result in increased expenses without corresponding revenue growth. The filing also references the impact of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) on tax provisions, indicating that changes in tax law could affect financial results, although the company has already recognized applicable effects for 2025.
