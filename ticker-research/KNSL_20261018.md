## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KNSL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Kinsale Capital Group is a U.S.-domiciled property and casualty insurance company that operates exclusively within the excess and surplus lines (E&S) market, focusing on hard-to-place risks for small- to medium-sized commercial accounts and a smaller segment of personal lines. The company’s business model relies on selling policies through a network of independent brokers and a wholly-owned broker subsidiary, Aspera, while retaining full control over underwriting and claims management. Kinsale differentiates itself by leveraging a proprietary technology platform to drive operational efficiency, resulting in a significantly lower expense ratio than competitors, and by maintaining a "contrarian" risk appetite that targets risks with less competition and better pricing. In 2025, the company generated $2.0 billion in gross written premiums, with 97% derived from commercial lines and 70.7% from casualty coverages, achieving a combined ratio of 75.9% and a return on equity of 29.3%.

The company’s competitive moat is built on three primary pillars: exclusive focus on the E&S market, which historically offers higher margins than the standard admitted market; a proprietary digital platform that enables rapid response times (often within 24 hours) and low administrative costs; and a management team with an average of over 30 years of relevant experience, led by CEO Michael Kehoe, who previously founded James River Insurance. Kinsale emphasizes that it does not delegate underwriting authority to brokers, ensuring consistent pricing and risk selection, and it manages all claims in-house to maintain reserve accuracy and contest meritless claims.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term stockholder value through consistent underwriting profits and prudent capital management. The strategic goals include expanding market share in the E&S sector (currently approximately 1.5% of the $129.8 billion market), maintaining a conservative balance sheet with robust loss reserves, and continuing to leverage technology to scale the business while keeping expense ratios low. The company aims to grow by targeting hard-to-place risks where it has specific underwriting expertise, rather than competing on price in highly competitive standard markets.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to the volatility of the insurance cycle and specific market conditions. A key risk highlighted is the increasing competition from standard carriers in certain lines, such as Commercial Property, which saw a 17.9% decline in gross written premiums in 2025 due to rate declines and a more competitive environment. Additionally, the company faces risks associated with catastrophe losses, which impacted the 2025 loss ratio, as well as broader economic factors such as social inflation, wage inflation, and medical cost inflation that drive up loss and loss adjustment expenses. The reliance on a small number of large brokers or specific lines of business, though diversified, remains a structural consideration, and the company notes that its performance is sensitive to changes in the legal and regulatory environment affecting its target markets.
