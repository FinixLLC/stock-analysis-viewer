## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically providing a self-service platform that empowers ad buyers—including advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers—to create, manage, and optimize digital advertising campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on a "buy-side" focus, positioning itself as an objective referee rather than a participant in the supply side of the market. TTD generates revenue primarily by charging clients a platform fee, which is generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform for advertising inventory, value-added services, and data. Additionally, the company offers enterprise APIs that allow clients to customize and expand platform functionality, fostering an open ecosystem where third-party partners can integrate their technology. This model relies on ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic insertion orders, contributing to a highly scalable business model and a customer retention rate that has exceeded 95% for over a decade.

TTD’s competitive moat is derived from its independence, data-driven approach, and technological depth. By focusing exclusively on the buy side, TTD avoids the conflicts of interest inherent in competitors who serve both buyers and sellers, thereby building long-term trust with clients who are willing to share proprietary first-party data. The company emphasizes its "expressiveness," a bid-factor-based architecture that allows for highly precise targeting with quadrillions of permutations, and its AI capabilities, including the "Koa" AI co-pilot and "Audience Unlimited" features, which automate and optimize campaign decisioning. Furthermore, TTD maintains a rich ecosystem of integrations with premium omnichannel inventory, including connected television (CTV), audio, and mobile, as well as a broad network of data partners. This combination of objectivity, advanced AI-driven optimization, and access to diverse inventory sources creates a barrier to entry for competitors who may lack the same level of client trust or technological sophistication.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients’ digital advertising spend, expanding the client base (particularly in the mid-market), and continuing to innovate in technology, data, and measurement. The company is actively investing in the development and incorporation of AI into its platform, such as the recent "Kokai" upgrade, to enhance automation and predictive capabilities. Additionally, TTD is prioritizing international expansion, with specific focus on markets in Europe and Asia, including the U.K., Germany, France, China, Japan, India, and Australia. Management also highlights the importance of expanding access to omnichannel inventory, particularly in CTV and other video formats, and driving the adoption of third-party and first-party data to create a "natural flywheel" in the business. The company anticipates that operating expenses will continue to increase as it invests in platform operations, technology development, and sales and marketing efforts to support these growth initiatives.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to macroeconomic uncertainty, which could decrease demand for advertising and disrupt supply chains. TTD notes that changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments may impact its revenue and results of operations, although the extent of this impact is unpredictable. The company also highlights the risk of client spend retention, noting that future revenue growth depends on retaining existing clients and gaining a larger portion of their spend in a highly competitive market. Additionally, TTD faces risks associated with the growth of the programmatic advertising market, as any slowing in adoption could affect its performance. The company also identifies the need to manage increased costs associated with developing and hosting its AI-rich platform, as well as the challenges of expanding into international markets where adoption may vary. Seasonality is another noted risk, with the fourth quarter typically reflecting the highest level of advertising activity and the first quarter the lowest.
