## Business Profile

Source: FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2026-01-31, filed 2026-03-31), fetched live from SEC EDGAR and read directly (Item 1 Business and Item 7 MD&A).

What the company does: Ross Stores operates two off-price retail apparel and home fashion chains — Ross Dress for Less (1,904 stores in 44 states, DC, Guam, and Puerto Rico as of 2026-01-31; the largest off-price apparel/home fashion chain in the U.S.) and dd's DISCOUNTS (363 stores in 22 states, targeting a more moderate-income customer base). Both brands sell first-quality, in-season, brand-name and designer merchandise at 20–70% discounts to department/specialty store prices.

Business model / moat: An off-price "opportunistic buying" model — purchasing advance-of-season, in-season, and past-season merchandise (including manufacturer/brand overruns and canceled orders), often later in the buying cycle than traditional retailers, which lets Ross exploit imbalances between manufacturer supply and retailer demand. "Packaway" merchandise (purchased and stored until a later, often more favorable, selling window — typically under six months) is a distinctive inventory-management lever. Buyers average 7+ years of experience; the company had over 800 merchants across both brands at FY2025 year-end. Distribution runs through large, automated distribution centers plus regional cross-dock facilities, with three to six deliveries per week to stores.

Management/leadership: James G. Conroy became CEO in 2025 (previously President/CEO of Boot Barn Holdings, 2012–2024); William W. Sheehan II became CFO in October 2025. A largely internally promoted senior team otherwise (Group President/COO Michael Hartshorn has been with the company in various finance roles since 2000).

Workforce: ~111,000 total associates as of 2026-01-31 (over 85% in retail stores), no collective bargaining coverage.

Risks the company itself flags as most material:
1. Macroeconomic / tariff exposure — more than half of the goods Ross sells originate from China; the company explicitly flags elevated and changing U.S. tariffs on Chinese-made goods as a direct cost and margin risk, alongside broader inflation/consumer-spending sensitivity.
2. Competitive intensity — a highly fragmented, low-barrier-to-entry off-price/discount retail sector; e-commerce growth cited as an ongoing structural pressure even though Ross itself is exclusively brick-and-mortar.
3. Packaway/inventory execution risk — mistimed packaway releases relative to shifting consumer preferences could force markdowns.
4. Cybersecurity — payment-card and customer data security flagged as a growing threat given reliance on third-party payment infrastructure.
5. Real estate / new-market expansion risk — new stores may underperform or take longer to reach target profitability, particularly in newer markets like the FY2025 Puerto Rico and NY Metro entries.
