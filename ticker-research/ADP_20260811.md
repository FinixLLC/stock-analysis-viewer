## Business Profile

Source: live SEC 10-K fetch (FY2026, filed 2026-08-05, self-extracted and synthesized directly from Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors).

What the company does: ADP is a 77-year-old global leader in Human Capital Management (HCM) — payroll, HR, benefits administration, talent management, workforce management, compliance, retirement services, and insurance services — organized into two reportable segments: Employer Services (payroll/HCM technology plus HR outsourcing for businesses of all sizes, from RUN Powered by ADP for small business to ADP Lyric HCM for large global enterprises) and Professional Employer Organization (PEO) (ADP TotalSource, a co-employment full-service HR outsourcing model). ADP pays over 42 million workers across 140+ countries and moved $3.5 trillion in client funds in fiscal 2026.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: (1) Scale of proprietary workforce data — "the industry's largest workforce dataset" across 1.1M+ clients and 42M+ workers — increasingly leveraged for AI-driven "ADP Assist" agents grounded in ADP's own institutional data; (2) 77 years of regulatory/compliance domain expertise across HR, tax, and employment law in 140+ countries — a genuine barrier to entry given regulatory fragmentation; (3) the ADP Trust Bank (OCC-chartered national trust bank, sole trustee of ADP Client Trust) and Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs) for cross-border EU data transfer, both cited as protections "most competitors cannot offer/provide"; (4) long client retention (~13 years average in Employer Services, ~6 years in PEO) reflecting high switching costs.

Segment mix: Employer Services is the larger, higher-margin core; PEO (TotalSource) is smaller with thinner and slightly declining margins in FY2026 (segment margin -110bps YoY) but growing worksite-employee volume (+2% YoY).

Management's stated priorities (from Item 1 Business Strategy and Q4 earnings commentary): Three explicit strategic priorities — (1) "Lead with Best-in-Class HCM Technology" (AI-native platforms, ADP Assist persona-based agents); (2) "Provide Unmatched Expertise and Outsourcing Solutions" (human-judgment-paired-with-AI HRO); (3) "Benefit our Clients with Our Global Scale" (140+ country footprint, "final mile" tax-authority/banking integrations). R&D spend was $1.405B in FY2026 (+1.2% YoY), consistently ~$1.3-1.4B across the last three years.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, by category):
1. Legal/compliance risk — the single largest risk category in ADP's own filing, spanning wide-ranging U.S./foreign employment, tax, data-privacy (GDPR, CPRA), and AI-specific regulation (EU AI Act) that could require costly changes to how ADP operates or force modification of its client-funds investment strategy.
2. PEO co-employment liability — as a co-employer, ADP can be exposed to worksite-employee wage/tax/benefits obligations even though clients are contractually responsible; risk "magnified during significant financial disruptions" (e.g., a client-side bank failure).
3. Cybersecurity/data breach risk — ADP explicitly notes it collects "large amounts of personal and business information" plus client funds, making it an attractive target; cites past cybersecurity incidents (none material to date) but flags AI-enabled attacks as an increasing threat vector.
4. AI-specific risk — both the opportunity and the liability side: fragmented state/EU AI regulation could constrain ADP's own AI-driven product roadmap, and AI-generated outputs (bias, accuracy) could expose ADP to litigation/reputational risk.
