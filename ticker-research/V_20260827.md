## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa is a global leader in digital payments, facilitating secure, reliable, and efficient money movement across over 200 countries and territories. The company operates on a four-party model, connecting consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers through its advanced transaction processing network, VisaNet. This ecosystem has expanded to include digital banks, wallets, fintechs, governments, and NGOs. Visa's business model revolves around providing transaction processing services—authorization, clearing, and settlement—and offering value-added services such as risk management, fraud detection, and advisory solutions. The company generates revenue from service fees, data processing, international transactions, and client incentives.

Visa's competitive moat is built on its extensive global network, advanced technology like VisaNet, and a focus on innovation. The company leverages tokenization, AI, and other technologies to enhance security and user experience. Its "Tap to Everything" initiative has significantly expanded contactless payment adoption, with over 2.4 billion transactions processed in fiscal 2025. Additionally, Visa's investments in next-generation technologies, such as generative AI and stablecoins, position it for future growth.

Management emphasizes three key priorities: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). CP focuses on expanding card-based and non-card-based payments, CMS targets commercial and cross-border transactions, while VAS delivers innovative services to deepen client partnerships. Visa also prioritizes financial inclusion through prepaid solutions and partnerships with fintechs.

The company faces several risks, including legal liabilities, particularly from the interchange multidistrict litigation, which resulted in additional accruals of $2.2 billion in fiscal 2025. Regulatory changes could impact its operations, especially regarding interchange fees and data privacy. Additionally, Visa must navigate competitive pressures as digital payment alternatives emerge. Economic downturns or shifts in consumer behavior could also affect transaction volumes and revenue growth.

Visa's financial performance in fiscal 2025 showed net revenue of $40 billion, up 11% year-over-year, driven by increased processed transactions and cross-border volume. However, operating expenses rose 30%, largely due to higher litigation provisions and personnel costs. Non-GAAP metrics indicated a more stable growth trajectory, with non-GAAP net income increasing 11%. Despite these challenges, Visa remains committed to innovation and expanding its payment ecosystem to maintain its leadership in the global payments industry.
