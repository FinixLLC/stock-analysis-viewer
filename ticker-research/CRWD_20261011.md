## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CRWD_10-K_20260826.txt)

CrowdStrike, founded in 2011, operates as a cloud-native cybersecurity provider that delivers its Falcon platform via a SaaS subscription model. The company’s core business model relies on a single, lightweight sensor installed on endpoints and cloud workloads to collect data, which is then ingested once and reused across 33 different cloud modules spanning endpoint security, identity protection, SIEM, and AI detection. Revenue is generated primarily through per-endpoint, per-module subscription fees recognized ratably over terms generally lasting one to three years, supplemented by professional services such as incident response, which the company views as a lead generator for new subscriptions. The go-to-market strategy utilizes a direct sales team segmented by endpoint volume, employing a "land-and-expand" approach where customers start with specific modules and add more over time.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its "AI Security Cloud" architecture, which creates a network effect by leveraging crowdsourced data from its global customer base. By processing trillions of cybersecurity events weekly, CrowdStrike continuously trains its AI models to detect threats in real-time, arguing that this cloud-scale data advantage and the integration of human expertise into its "Enterprise Graph" (comprising Threat, Intel, and Asset Graphs) provide superior efficacy and lower false positives compared to legacy on-premise or signature-based competitors. Management asserts that this architecture allows for rapid extension into new security domains, such as generative AI protection, while reducing complexity and total cost of ownership for customers.

Management’s stated priorities focus on maintaining high customer retention and driving expansion through the addition of new modules and endpoints, while continuing to invest significantly in sales, marketing, and research and development to capture a large market opportunity. The company also emphasizes the importance of its open cloud architecture to rapidly deploy new capabilities and may pursue acquisitions to complement its platform. Additionally, a key priority in the current period is managing the operational and financial impacts of the July 19, 2024 incident, which involved a content configuration update that caused system crashes for certain Windows users.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the ongoing adverse effects of the July 19 Incident, which has resulted in lawsuits, increased legal and professional service costs, elongated sales cycles, and customer commitment packages that include discounting and flexible terms, thereby impacting net retention and upsell values. The company also highlights the risk that future success depends on the continued adoption of cloud-based security solutions over legacy on-premise products, as well as the challenge of acquiring new customers in a competitive market. Furthermore, the filing notes that customer churn and contraction may occur in future periods due to the long-term nature of subscription contracts and the potential for customers to renew with smaller contract values or shorter terms following the incident.
