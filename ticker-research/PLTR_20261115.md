## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that integrates data, decisions, and operations for government and commercial clients. Its business model centers on four principal platforms: Gotham, which supports defense and intelligence missions; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform serving as a central operating system for enterprises; Apollo, a continuous delivery platform that manages infrastructure and software updates across any environment; and AIP, a generative AI platform that connects large language models to organizational data and workflows. The company generates revenue primarily through long-term contracts, typically ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the contract term. As of 2025, the company had 954 customers, with 54% of revenue derived from government segments and 46% from commercial segments, while 74% of total revenue originated from the United States.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its vertically integrated platform architecture and the "Palantir Ontology," which systematically maps data to meaningful context, integrating data, logic, and actions into a decision-centric architecture. This approach allows users of varying technical skills to collaborate and build interconnected workflows, turning specialized expertise into shared infrastructure. Palantir targets large-scale, complex, and high-risk implementation opportunities that smaller competitors often avoid due to high installation costs and failure risks. The company leverages its ability to deploy solutions in days rather than months or years, a speed advantage that appeals to risk-averse institutions that have previously failed with internal software development efforts. Additionally, the company expands its reach through cloud partnerships, joint ventures, and industry-specific operating systems, while its Apollo platform ensures secure, continuous delivery in virtually any environment, including rugged or on-premises settings.

Management’s stated priorities include expanding access to its platforms across a broader swath of the market in both commercial and government sectors, with a specific focus on capturing a greater share of U.S. federal government software spending. The company is actively pursuing "industry operating systems" to manage operations across entire sectors, such as healthcare, automotive, and defense. In the short term, management prioritizes making its principal software platforms available to a wider range of potential customers, including through AIP bootcamps and developer tier offerings, to lower entry barriers. Long-term, the company aims to build strategic relationships that transform how clients use data, while also identifying component parts of its platforms that can be commercialized as standalone products. Management emphasizes a partnership approach, embedding directly with customers to tackle complex challenges and continuously enhance platform capabilities.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the public sector and macroeconomic conditions. A significant portion of revenue comes from government customers, which are subject to uncertainties regarding budgets, spending levels, timing, and regulatory or policy changes, making it difficult to predict contract awards. Many contracts, including those with the U.S. federal government, contain termination for convenience provisions, and the government is prohibited from exercising contract options more than one year in advance, creating revenue volatility. The company also faces risks from geopolitical tensions, such as the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, which could disrupt operations or the global economy, although management states current operations in these regions are not material to financial results. Additionally, the company is exposed to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, interest rate changes, and potential trade restrictions or tariffs, which could adversely affect its international business and results of operations.
