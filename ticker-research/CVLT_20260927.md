## Business Profile

Commvault Systems, Inc. operates as a provider of cyber resiliency solutions, delivering a cloud-native platform called Commvault Cloud that unifies data protection, cyber recovery, data security, and identity resilience across on-premise, hybrid, multi-cloud, and SaaS environments. The company’s business model is primarily subscription-based, generating revenue through term-based software licenses, SaaS offerings, customer support contracts, and professional services. While Commvault sells directly to end-users, approximately 90% of its revenue is derived from indirect distribution channels, including value-added resellers, systems integrators, OEMs, and cloud marketplaces. The company serves a broad global customer base, with nearly half of its revenue coming from outside the United States, and focuses on regulated and mission-critical industries such as banking, healthcare, government, and energy.

The company’s competitive positioning relies on a comprehensive platform approach that integrates operational, autonomous, and cyber recovery capabilities, including features like AI-assisted data discovery, immutability, air-gapped protection, and orchestrated recovery workflows. Commvault differentiates itself through its ability to support modern data architectures, including cloud-native workloads, containers, and AI-driven environments, while maintaining data sovereignty and regulatory compliance. However, the filing explicitly states that the market is highly competitive, fragmented, and rapidly evolving, with Commvault facing competition from vendors such as Rubrik, Cohesity, and Veeam, as well as hyperscalers and other providers who may possess greater financial resources, larger installed bases, and the ability to bundle solutions into broader infrastructure platforms.

Management’s stated priorities center on the growth of recurring revenue streams, specifically subscription arrangements, which are described as central to the long-term growth strategy. The company emphasizes the transition toward SaaS delivery, noting that SaaS ARR grew 42% in fiscal 2026, and highlights the importance of maintaining high net dollar retention rates (122% in fiscal 2026) to drive expansion within the existing customer base. Additionally, management prioritizes the expansion of strategic relationships with technology, channel, and service provider partners to enhance interoperability, extend market reach, and support customer adoption across diverse IT environments.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the heavy reliance on indirect distribution channels, particularly the concentration of revenue among specific partners, with one partner accounting for approximately 32% of total revenues in fiscal 2026. The filing warns that the failure of these channels to effectively sell products, or the termination of agreements with major partners, could have a material adverse effect on revenues. Furthermore, the company faces significant competitive pressure from better-resourced rivals and hyperscalers, as well as exposure to foreign currency fluctuations, which impacted fiscal 2026 results by reducing total revenues by $29.5 million when adjusted for exchange rate changes.
