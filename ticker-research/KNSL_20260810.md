What the company does: Kinsale is a property and casualty insurer that focuses exclusively on the U.S. excess and surplus (E&S) lines market — insuring "hard-to-place" risks (newly established companies/industries, high-risk operations, insureds in litigious venues, or companies with poor loss histories) that don't fit the standard/admitted insurance market. Sold in all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, primarily through independent insurance brokers. Core client focus is small-to-medium-sized accounts (average premium per policy ~$13,400 in 2025), which the company says face less competition and better pricing than large accounts.

Segment/product mix (2025 gross written premiums, $2.0B total, +5.7% YoY): 70.7% casualty / 29.3% property; 97.0% commercial lines / 3.0% personal lines. Largest individual divisions: Commercial Property (18.9% of GWP), Excess Casualty (14.0%), General Casualty (10.5%), Small Business Casualty (10.2%), Construction (7.5%). Highly diversified across ~10+ named divisions plus "all other commercial lines" (16.3%).

Moat, as described by the company:
- Exclusive E&S focus — the E&S/non-admitted market has structurally lower loss ratios and higher margins and has grown faster than the standard P&C market (2001–2024: E&S composite averaged 68.7% loss ratio and 10.0% annual premium growth, vs. 73.6%/4.7% for the broader P&C industry, per A.M. Best data cited in the 10-K).
- Underwriting expertise across hard-to-place risks — underwriters specialize by individual line, don't extend underwriting authority to brokers/agents (unlike many competitors), and individually price/structure each risk; 2025 loss and LAE ratio was 55.1%.
- Proprietary technology platform — company describes itself as being "at the digital forefront of the insurance industry," enabling faster data collection/analysis and quicker customer response times, which it says differentiates it from competitors on cost control and underwriting/claims discipline.
- Track record: 2016–2025 compounded annual stock gain of 40.8% vs. 14.9% for the S&P 500 (per the 10-K's own performance table) — though 2025 itself was a down year for KNSL stock (−15.8%) versus a positive S&P 500 (+17.9%), the first year in that decade-plus table where Kinsale underperformed the index.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A):
1. Loss reserve adequacy — the top-listed risk factor. Reserves are estimates, not exact calculations; ~84.2% of net casualty loss reserves are tied to "occurrence" policies where claims can arise years after a policy lapses, and new liability theories can be applied retroactively by courts. Explicitly cites claims inflation, litigation/jury-award trends (including third-party litigation funding), and social/economic pattern shifts as reserve-adequacy pressures.
2. Reinsurance counterparty and program risk — Kinsale cedes meaningful risk via quota-share and catastrophe reinsurance treaties (e.g., property catastrophe coverage up to $500M aggregate including reinstatement); reinsurance recoverables on unpaid losses were $381.4M at year-end 2025. Reinsurer insolvency/non-performance is a named risk, mitigated by using only A.M. Best "A-" or better rated reinsurers.
3. Standard property-catastrophe exposure (the Commercial Property division, the single largest by GWP, is inherently exposed to catastrophe-driven loss volatility — consistent with the Q2 2026 pullback in that division's premium volume noted above).
