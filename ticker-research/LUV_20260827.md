## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines Co., operating as Southwest Airlines, is a major U.S. passenger airline offering scheduled air transportation within the United States and near-international markets. The company's business model emphasizes a unique point-to-point route network, competitive fares, and customer-centric service known for its "Hospitality." As of December 31, 2025, Southwest operated a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft, serving 117 destinations across 42 states, Puerto Rico, and ten near-international countries. The company has been modernizing its operations to enhance customer experience, financial performance, and shareholder value through initiatives such as assigned seating, expanded global partnerships, and the introduction of overnight flights.

Southwest's competitive moat stems from its efficient point-to-point network, which allows for more direct nonstop routes compared to hub-and-spoke models. This structure enables frequent, conveniently timed flights with high asset utilization. The company's use of a single aircraft type, the Boeing 737, simplifies operations and reduces costs. Additionally, Southwest has focused on optimizing its route network by expanding longer-haul flights and introducing redeye service to maximize capacity.

Management priorities include enhancing customer experience through initiatives like assigned seating and Getaways by Southwest vacation packages, improving operational efficiency with digital processes, and reducing labor costs through workforce optimization. The company also aims to modernize its fleet by replacing older aircraft with more fuel-efficient models.

Key risks identified in the filing include volatile fuel prices, which significantly impact profitability, and rising labor costs due to inflation and contractual obligations. The company is also exposed to economic downturns affecting travel demand and faces competitive pressures from other airlines. Regulatory uncertainties, such as delays in aircraft certifications, could further affect operations.
