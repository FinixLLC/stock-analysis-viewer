## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNDK_10-K_20260906.txt)

Sandisk (SNDK) is a vertically integrated global semiconductor memory company that designs, manufactures, and sells NAND flash-based storage solutions, having separated from Western Digital (WDC) to become a standalone public entity in February 2025. The company’s business model relies on owning chip-level design and IP, front- and back-end manufacturing, and systems engineering to serve three primary end markets: Datacenter (enterprise SSDs for cloud and AI workloads), Edge (embedded storage and SSDs for PCs, mobile, automotive, and industrial devices), and Consumer (retail products like cards and USB drives). A critical component of this model is the company’s joint ventures with Kioxia, through which Sandisk sources its flash memory wafers, while controllers are designed in-house or sourced from third parties, and assembly and testing are handled through a mix of in-house facilities in Malaysia and contract manufacturers.

Sandisk’s competitive moat is built on its vertical integration, which allows for cost leadership and supply chain flexibility, as well as its extensive intellectual property portfolio comprising approximately 8,000 granted patents and 3,000 pending applications. The company cites its "differentiated innovation engine," deep relationships with industry leaders across data ecosystems, and strong consumer brand recognition as key differentiators. Furthermore, management highlights the transition to "New Business Models" (NBMs)—long-term agreements with Datacenter and Edge customers that include volume commitments and financial guarantees—as a strategic mechanism to reduce exposure to industry cyclicality and improve revenue predictability.

Management’s stated priorities focus on leveraging technology innovation and manufacturing leadership to drive cost-efficient memory solutions, particularly for AI-driven workloads. The company aims to maintain operational excellence through high manufacturing yields, capital efficiency, and supply chain management, while simultaneously transforming its financial model via NBMs to create a more durable and predictable business. Strategic resources are being allocated to high-value opportunities in AI infrastructure, with management expecting AI-driven demand to persist through 2027 and beyond.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the company’s heavy reliance on joint ventures with Kioxia for flash wafer supply and the inherent cyclicality of the semiconductor memory industry, which NBMs are intended to mitigate but not eliminate. Significant regulatory and trade risks are also highlighted, specifically regarding pending U.S. investigations under Section 232 and Section 301 that could lead to increased tariffs, higher costs of goods sold, and reduced demand for products sold in the U.S. Additionally, the company notes risks associated with protecting proprietary information and trade secrets in foreign jurisdictions, as well as the operational complexities of maintaining high-volume, high-yield manufacturing in clean-room environments.
