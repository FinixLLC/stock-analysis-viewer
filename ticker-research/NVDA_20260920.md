## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NVDA_10-K_20260906.txt)

NVIDIA has evolved from a graphics processor manufacturer into a data center-scale AI infrastructure company that reshapes industries through accelerated computing. Its business model is built on a platform strategy that integrates hardware, systems, software, algorithms, and services to address computationally intensive workloads such as AI training and inference, scientific computing, and 3D graphics. The company reports results in two segments: Compute Networking, which includes Data Center and Automotive solutions, and Graphics, covering gaming and professional visualization. NVIDIA’s core technology stack is anchored by the CUDA development platform and a broad ecosystem of software libraries and APIs that run on its GPUs, allowing the company to support multiple multi-billion-dollar end markets with a unified underlying programmable architecture. This approach leverages shared underlying technology to drive efficiency, with over half of its engineers focused on software development to maintain a competitive edge in full-stack innovation.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its deep software stack, which includes the CUDA platform and hundreds of domain-specific libraries, creating a large and growing developer ecosystem that increases the value of its platform for customers. NVIDIA emphasizes "extreme co-design," where chips, networking, systems, software, and algorithms are holistically architected to maximize performance and scale, enabling the interconnection of hundreds of thousands of GPUs to function as a single giant computer. This capability is critical for modern AI applications and is supported by a history of significant R&D investment, exceeding $76.7 billion since inception. The company’s ability to deliver order-of-magnitude performance advantages relative to legacy CPU-only approaches, combined with its control over the entire stack from silicon to software, creates significant barriers to entry. Additionally, NVIDIA’s presence in over 78% of the global TOP500 supercomputers underscores its dominance in high-performance computing.

Management’s stated priorities center on advancing the accelerated computing platform to solve complex problems with lower power consumption and faster processing times. A key strategic focus is maintaining a one-year product cadence for data center compute introductions, as evidenced by the transition from Hopper to Blackwell and the upcoming Rubin platform. NVIDIA is also prioritizing the expansion of its AI infrastructure buildout, which involves significant investments in private companies and infrastructure funds to support early-stage startups and AI model makers. The company is actively working to strengthen its supply chain resilience by expanding manufacturing capabilities into the U.S. and Latin America, while also managing the complexities of product transitions and supply-demand balance. Furthermore, management is focused on supporting the broader AI ecosystem through commercial arrangements, including guarantees for land, power, and shell for early-stage companies, to facilitate the deployment of complex data center infrastructures.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the availability of data centers, energy, and capital required to support the buildout of AI infrastructure by customers and partners. Shortages in these resources could impact future revenue and financial performance, as expanding energy capacity is a complex, multi-year process with significant regulatory and technical challenges. The company also highlights risks associated with product transitions, noting that the complexity of sophisticated system configurations has caused and may continue to cause delays in production, supply and demand management challenges, revenue volatility, and quality issues. Regulatory and geopolitical risks are significant, particularly regarding exports to China; the U.S. government’s requirement for licenses for H20 and H200 products led to a $4.5 billion charge for excess inventory and purchase obligations in fiscal year 2026. Additionally, the rise of high-quality open-source foundation models is flagged as a risk, as their deployment on competitors' platforms could reduce demand for NVIDIA’s products. Finally, the company notes that its supply chain is mainly concentrated in Asia, and while it is expanding into other regions, the ability to increase manufacturing capabilities depends on the local ecosystem's capacity to ramp production to required volumes on a timely basis.
