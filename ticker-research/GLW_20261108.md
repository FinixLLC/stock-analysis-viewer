## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLW_10-K_20260906.txt)

Corning Incorporated is a diversified materials science and manufacturing company that leverages expertise in glass, ceramic, and optical physics to serve industries including optical communications, display, mobile consumer electronics, automotive, life sciences, semiconductors, and solar. The company operates through five reportable segments—Optical Communications, Display, Specialty Materials, Automotive, and Life Sciences—and manufactures products in 14 countries. Its business model relies on developing category-defining products, such as low-loss optical fiber, precision display glass substrates, and durable cover glass for mobile devices, often utilizing proprietary manufacturing processes like its fusion process for display glass. Corning sells these products directly to end users, third-party integrators, and distributors, with international markets accounting for 57% of net sales in 2025.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its extensive patent portfolios, proprietary trade secrets, and brand recognition across its segments. In Optical Communications, Corning holds an extensive portfolio of patents related to fiber and cable technologies, which it also licenses to third parties. The Display segment relies on its proprietary fusion manufacturing process, which the company believes is the most cost-effective for producing large-size substrates, alongside patent protection. Specialty Materials and Automotive segments also cite extensive patent portfolios and, in the case of Specialty Materials, strong brand loyalty through trademarks like Gorilla Glass. Additionally, the company notes that patent protection is important to operations across all segments, with some segments licensing patents to generate revenue, although royalty income is not currently material to operating results.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on the "Springboard" plan, introduced in late 2023, which aims to grow sales and enhance profitability. Specifically, management targets adding $4 billion in incremental annualized core sales by the end of 2026 and achieving a 20% core operating margin by the same date. The company reports that it achieved both growth and profitability targets a full year ahead of plan as of the fourth quarter of 2025. To sustain this trajectory, management intends to increase capacity and technology capabilities as required, while sharing risk appropriately to achieve the returns underpinning the Springboard plan. The company also emphasizes sustained investment in research, development, and engineering to solve customer challenges and capture new opportunities in dynamic industries.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to market demand and regulatory environments. In the Automotive segment, management cites softness in the heavy-duty diesel market and unfavorable foreign exchange movements as drivers of decreased sales. The Display segment faced headwinds from a weaker Japanese yen environment, which required pricing actions to offset the impact of resetting the core rate. Additionally, the company notes that the IRS is conducting examinations of its U.S. federal income tax returns for 2015–2020, including the one-time transition tax under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act; while Corning believes it is more likely than not to sustain its position, it acknowledges that if unsuccessful, the impact could be material to its consolidated financial statements. The filing also references the adoption of the EU Pillar Two Framework and the U.S. One Big Beautiful Bill Act, though it states these did not have a material impact on results for the periods presented.
