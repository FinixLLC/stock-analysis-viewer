## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company operates as a global designer and manufacturer of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, supported by related services. Founded in 1899, the company leverages its historical expertise in tapered roller bearings to offer a broad portfolio that includes spherical and cylindrical roller bearings, ball bearings, linear motion solutions, industrial drives, automatic lubrication systems, belts, chains, couplings, and clutches. Timken’s business model is built on a customer-centric, collaborative technical selling approach where sales engineers work closely with customers during product development and implementation. The company serves a diverse customer base across sectors such as renewable energy, automation, automotive, agriculture, rail, aerospace, and heavy industries, with no single customer accounting for more than 5% of total net sales. Distribution is handled primarily through internal sales organizations and authorized distributors, supported by a global footprint of 116 manufacturing facilities, 29 technology centers, and 74 distribution centers across 44 countries.

Timken’s competitive moat is derived from its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, which allows it to apply deep metallurgical and friction management expertise across its entire product line. The company differentiates itself through application engineering, high-performance product quality, and the ability to provide technical support on a global basis. This is reinforced by a portfolio of strong, recognized brands (such as Timken, Philadelphia Gear, and Rollon) and a significant aftermarket presence, where the company captures replacement cycles through independent channels. Additionally, Timken benefits from end-market diversity and geographic reach, allowing it to serve attractive, often fragmented markets that value reliability and efficiency.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three primary strategic elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. To achieve profitable growth, Timken aims to expand into new and existing markets by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and power transmission, with an emphasis on highly fragmented sectors and applications with significant aftermarket demand. Operational excellence involves a continuous improvement culture aimed at increasing efficiency, lowering costs, eliminating waste, and building brand equity. Regarding capital deployment, the company prioritizes investing in the core business through capital expenditures and R&D, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. The company also notes it may restructure or divest underperforming assets as part of this framework.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of incremental tariff costs, which significantly increased cost of products sold in 2025. The company also highlights exposure to raw material price variability, specifically for special bar quality steel, which is subject to commodity price fluctuations and supply/demand changes. While Timken uses surcharge mechanisms to partially recover these costs, the filing notes that actual shipments depend on customers' ever-changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales. Additionally, the company faces competitive pressure from major domestic and foreign manufacturers, including SKF Group and Schaeffler Group, and must manage the risks associated with lower demand in key regions like the Americas and Europe, which offset gains in other areas.
