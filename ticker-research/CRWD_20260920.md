## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CRWD_10-K_20260826.txt)

CrowdStrike, founded in 2011, operates as a cybersecurity company that delivers the Falcon platform, an AI-native, cloud-native solution designed to function as the operating system for cybersecurity. The company’s business model is primarily subscription-based, selling access to 33 cloud modules via a SaaS model that spans endpoint security, cloud workloads, identity protection, and threat intelligence. Revenue is generated through per-endpoint and per-module subscriptions, which are generally priced for terms exceeding one year and recognized ratably, alongside professional services such as incident response that serve as a lead generator for new subscriptions. The company employs a "land-and-expand" go-to-market strategy, utilizing a direct sales team segmented by endpoint volume to acquire new customers and drive expansion by adding endpoints or modules to existing deployments.

The company asserts a competitive moat rooted in its cloud-native architecture and the resulting network effects of its "Security Cloud." By ingesting data once through a single lightweight sensor and reusing it across multiple security functions, CrowdStrike claims to overcome the architectural limitations of legacy on-premise or signature-based products. This approach allows the company to correlate trillions of cybersecurity events per week, using this high-fidelity, crowdsourced data to continuously train AI models. Management argues that this creates a powerful network effect where increased data volume improves detection efficacy and reduces false positives, a capability they state is unmatchable by competitors relying on on-premise data storage or fragmented, bolt-on cloud solutions.

Management’s stated priorities focus on consolidating fragmented legacy security stacks into a unified platform to reduce customer complexity and total cost of ownership. The company emphasizes the need to automate security operations to address the growing cyber skills gap and the increasing speed of adversaries, particularly those leveraging AI. Strategic priorities include expanding the platform’s capabilities through research and development, potentially pursuing acquisitions to complement the Falcon platform, and continuing to invest in sales and marketing to grow the customer base across all organization sizes. Additionally, management highlights the importance of maintaining high retention rates while navigating the operational and financial impacts of recent incidents.

The most material risks flagged in the filing center on the "July 19 Incident" of 2024, a content configuration update that caused system crashes for certain Windows users. This event has resulted in significant legal and professional services expenses, adverse effects on results of operations, and elongated sales cycles due to delays in customer purchasing decisions. The filing notes that customer commitment packages introduced to mitigate churn have included discounting and flexible terms, leading to increased contraction and decreased upsell values. Beyond this specific incident, the company identifies risks related to the difficulty of predicting customer adoption of cloud-based solutions over legacy on-premise products, the dependence on successful new customer acquisition, and the need to retain existing customers in a competitive market where legacy vendors may still hold significant market share.
