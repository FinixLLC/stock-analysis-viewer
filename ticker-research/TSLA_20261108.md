## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TSLA_10-K_20260906.txt)

Tesla positions itself as an artificial intelligence company that leverages its existing automotive and energy operations to bring AI into the real world. Its core business model involves the design, development, manufacture, sale, and lease of high-performance fully electric vehicles and energy generation and storage systems. The company operates through two reportable segments: automotive and energy generation and storage. In the automotive segment, Tesla sells vehicles directly to customers via its website and company-owned stores, bypassing traditional dealer networks to control costs, manage inventory, and gather rapid customer feedback. The product lineup includes the Model 3, Model Y, Model S, Model X, Cybertruck, and the Tesla Semi. Beyond hardware sales, Tesla is transitioning toward a service-driven business model based on AI, software, and fleet-based profits, exemplified by its Full Self-Driving (FSD) (Supervised) subscriptions and the Robotaxi service launched in June 2025. The energy segment focuses on Powerwall and Megapack battery storage systems and solar generation products, which are increasingly integrated with AI-driven software platforms for grid optimization.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its vertically integrated business model, engineering expertise, and advancements in real-world AI. Tesla differentiates itself through proprietary technology, including vision-based autonomous driving systems trained on massive amounts of field data from its vehicle fleet, custom-designed inference chips, and in-house development of almost all vehicle control and infotainment software. This vertical integration extends to battery cell chemistry, powertrain engineering, and manufacturing processes, allowing for continuous cost reduction and performance optimization. Additionally, Tesla maintains a global network of Superchargers, which has become an industry standard as other automakers adopt the North American Charging Standard (NACS), creating a significant infrastructure advantage. The company also emphasizes its ability to update vehicles remotely via over-the-air software, enhancing safety, performance, and user experience without physical service visits.

Management’s stated priorities center on profitable growth through a differentiated product portfolio and efficient management of existing factories. Key strategic goals include improving and deploying FSD capabilities, scaling the Robotaxi service, and developing AI robots such as Optimus. The company is focused on reducing manufacturing costs, increasing production capacity and utilization, and vertically integrating and localizing its supply chain to mitigate external risks. Significant capital is being allocated to AI initiatives, including the expansion of compute infrastructure like the Cortex training cluster and data centers, as well as the ramp-up of new production lines for vehicles, bots, and energy storage. Management also prioritizes expanding global infrastructure, including service and charging networks, to support the growing fleet and Robotaxi operations.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to rapidly evolving trade and fiscal policies, including tariffs and the removal of electric vehicle tax credits under the OBBBA, which could adversely impact demand, cost structure, and profitability. The company notes that the current tariff regime poses a relatively larger impact on its energy generation and storage business compared to automotive. Other significant risks include the cyclical nature of the automotive industry, sensitivity to shifting consumer trends, inflationary pressures, rising energy prices, and interest rate fluctuations, all of which affect vehicle affordability and financing. Tesla also highlights risks associated with its ambitious technological targets for battery cells and autonomous driving, as well as the uncertainties inherent in ramping new manufacturing operations and concurrent international projects. Supply chain constraints, labor shortages, and the liquidity of suppliers are identified as potential threats to component availability and cost. Finally, the company acknowledges the competitive landscape, noting that as additional competitors enter the marketplace, it must continue to execute well to maintain momentum.
