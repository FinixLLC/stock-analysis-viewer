## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MU_10-K_20260906.txt)

Micron Technology operates as a leading provider of memory and storage solutions, with a business model centered on the manufacturing and sale of DRAM and NAND products. The company’s recent strategic focus has been a significant pivot toward high-growth, high-margin segments, specifically shifting DRAM supply to data center and hyperscale cloud markets to meet accelerating AI-driven demand. This shift has resulted in a revenue mix increasingly weighted toward Cloud and Data Center business units (CMBU and CDBU), which together generated 55% of total revenue in fiscal 2025, up from 35% in fiscal 2024. Micron’s competitive moat is derived from its ability to execute on advanced product technologies, particularly High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), and its capacity to manage supply discipline in a market where AI demand is currently outpacing industry supply. The company leverages manufacturing cost reductions driven by process technology improvements and maintains a strong position in high-value server DRAM, allowing it to capture substantial pricing power and margin expansion, as evidenced by the improvement in consolidated gross margin from 22% in 2024 to 40% in 2025.

Management’s stated priorities are focused on aligning supply growth and technology node cadence with demand projections, particularly in the NAND business, while aggressively capitalizing on AI-driven opportunities in the DRAM portfolio. The company emphasizes prudent management of its NAND business to avoid overcapacity, while simultaneously increasing R&D investments to support new product development and process technology advancements. Additionally, management is focused on maintaining liquidity and funding capital expenditures, which are estimated at approximately $4.5 billion per quarter, through a combination of operating cash flow, capital markets financing, and government incentives.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the regulatory impact of China’s Cyberspace Administration (CAC) decision, which restricts critical information infrastructure operators in China from purchasing Micron products. This has negatively impacted the company’s domestic data center and networking markets in China, a risk the company is actively working to mitigate. Furthermore, the company highlights the inherent volatility of its cash flows, which are highly dependent on selling prices that can vary significantly from period to period. Other material risks include the uncertainty surrounding the impact of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) and the OECD’s Pillar Two tax reforms, which could materially affect the company’s effective tax rate and financial statements. The company also notes that its government incentives, which are crucial for funding significant capital projects, are conditional and subject to reduction, termination, or clawback if specific business operations and employment thresholds are not met.
