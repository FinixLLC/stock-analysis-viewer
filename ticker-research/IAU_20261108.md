## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (IAU_10-K_20260906.txt)

The iShares Gold Trust (IAU) is a passive, grantor trust formed under New York law that serves as a vehicle for investors to gain exposure to the price of gold without the logistical burdens of physical ownership. Its business model is strictly mechanical: the Trust issues shares in exchange for gold deposited with its custodian and redeems shares by delivering gold, with no active management, lending, or trading strategies employed to generate profit or mitigate losses. The Trust’s sole recurring expense is a sponsor fee of 0.25% of net asset value, which is paid by selling small amounts of gold; the sponsor, a subsidiary of BlackRock, assumes all other administrative, legal, and listing costs. This structure allows the Trust to track the London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) Gold Price, with its net asset value fluctuating directly with the price of gold and the number of outstanding shares.

The Trust’s competitive advantage lies in its scale and the efficiency of its passive structure, which disperses the high costs of physical gold storage, insurance, and transportation among a large base of investors. By listing on NYSE Arca and utilizing an arbitrage mechanism where authorized participants create and redeem baskets of shares in exchange for physical gold, the Trust maintains liquidity and keeps trading prices closely aligned with net asset value. This provides a cost-effective and accessible alternative to direct physical gold investment, particularly for retail investors who may find the expense and complexity of holding bullion prohibitive.

Management’s stated priorities, as reflected in the filing, are limited to maintaining the Trust’s objective of reflecting the performance of the price of gold before expenses. The Sponsor focuses on administrative efficiency, ensuring that the Trust’s only ordinary expense remains the sponsor fee, and maintaining the integrity of the custody and valuation processes. There is no indication of strategic initiatives to diversify assets, engage in active trading, or alter the fundamental passive nature of the vehicle.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the inherent volatility of gold prices, which directly drives the Trust’s net asset value and can result in significant gains or losses. Additionally, the filing notes that shares may trade at a premium or discount to net asset value, particularly during periods of reduced liquidity in global gold markets relative to NYSE Arca trading hours. The Trust also faces operational risks related to the custodian, including potential loss of gold due to negligence, fraud, or force majeure events, although the custodian is required to maintain insurance and undergo regular inspections. Finally, the Trust is not registered as an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940, meaning investors do not receive certain regulatory protections afforded to shareholders in registered funds, such as limits on affiliate transactions or prohibitions on suspending redemptions.
