## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (JNJ_10-K_20260906.txt)

Johnson & Johnson operates as a global healthcare company with approximately 138,200 employees, organized into two primary business segments: Innovative Medicine and MedTech. The Innovative Medicine segment focuses on therapeutic areas including Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, and Cardiovascular and Metabolic, distributing prescription products directly to retailers, wholesalers, hospitals, and healthcare professionals. The MedTech segment provides products for Surgery, Orthopaedic, Cardiovascular, and Vision fields, distributed to wholesalers, hospitals, and retailers for use by medical professionals. The company’s business model relies on internal research and development, collaborations, acquisitions, and licensing to develop new products, with new products introduced in the past five years accounting for approximately 25% of 2025 sales. In October 2025, the company announced its intention to separate its Orthopaedics business, targeting completion within 18 to 24 months.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its extensive intellectual property portfolio, including patents and proprietary rights essential to its products and manufacturing processes, as well as its established brand equity and consumer trust. However, the filing notes that public policy has become increasingly unfavorable toward intellectual property rights, and competitors routinely challenge the validity of JNJ’s patents, particularly regarding generic and biosimilar versions of key pharmaceutical products. The company also leverages data science, machine learning, and AI across its business, though it acknowledges these technologies are evolving and may not yield the anticipated competitive advantages.

Management’s stated priorities center on innovation and the development of new, differentiated products and services to address evolving healthcare needs and offset revenue losses from patent expirations or competition. The company emphasizes the importance of securing regulatory approvals, maintaining product safety and effectiveness, and managing compliance with extensive government laws and regulations. Additionally, management highlights the need to mitigate risks associated with supply chain constraints, manufacturing delays, and the integration of emerging technologies like AI.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the significant legal proceedings and litigation, particularly regarding body powders containing talc, which could result in substantial settlements or judgments exceeding current accruals. The company does not purchase third-party product liability insurance, relying instead on a wholly owned captive insurance company. Other material risks include regulatory scrutiny and compliance costs related to FDA approvals, Good Manufacturing Practices, and healthcare industry business practices, which could lead to fines, penalties, or debarment from government programs. The company also faces risks from changes in tax laws, intellectual property challenges, delays in product development and regulatory approval, and financial risks associated with international operations, including foreign currency exchange fluctuations, inflation, and illegal importation of pharmaceutical products.
