## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that integrates data, decisions, and operations for government and commercial clients. Its business model centers on four principal platforms: Gotham, which supports defense and intelligence missions; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform serving as a central operating system for enterprises; Apollo, a cloud-agnostic continuous delivery platform that manages infrastructure and software updates; and AIP, a generative AI platform launched in 2023 that connects large language models to organizational data and workflows. The company’s core differentiator is the Palantir Ontology, a systematic mapping of data to meaningful context that integrates data, logic, and actions into a decision-centric architecture. This allows users of varying technical skills to build interconnected workflows and optimize decision-making in real-time. Palantir generates revenue primarily through subscription contracts, typically ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the contract term. In 2025, the company generated $4.5 billion in revenue, with 54% derived from government customers and 46% from commercial customers, while 74% of revenue came from the United States.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its ability to target large-scale, complex, and high-risk implementation opportunities that smaller competitors often avoid due to high installation costs and failure risks. Palantir leverages its vertically integrated platforms and the Ontology to solve problems that traditional data pipelines fail to address, offering solutions that can be deployed in days rather than the months or years required for internal development. This speed, combined with fine-grained access controls and secure connectivity to third-party LLMs within legal and security constraints, creates a sticky product ecosystem. Furthermore, the company has established deep partnerships with major cloud providers and industry-specific alliances, such as with Fujitsu, to expand distribution and embed its software into broader industrial operating systems. The high barriers to entry for competitors are reinforced by the complexity of the data environments Palantir serves and the long sales cycles associated with these institutional clients.

Management’s stated priorities focus on expanding the reach of its platforms to a broader swath of the market while maintaining depth in mission-critical operations. Key strategic initiatives include accelerating customer acquisition through "AIP bootcamps" that deliver real workflows on actual customer data in days, and offering a "Developer Tier" to lower entry barriers for innovation. The company is also prioritizing the development of industry-specific operating systems for sectors such as healthcare, automotive, and government, aiming to become a central infrastructure layer for entire industries. Additionally, management is focused on growing its account base by scaling contracts horizontally across divisions and vertically across user groups, while continuing to invest in an account-based sales force to capture new opportunities in both the U.S. federal government and commercial sectors.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the public sector and macroeconomic conditions. A significant portion of revenue comes from government contracts, which are subject to budget uncertainties, changes in spending priorities, and regulatory shifts that can make sales timing and size unpredictable. Many contracts, including those with the U.S. federal government, contain termination for convenience provisions, and the government is prohibited from exercising contract options more than one year in advance, creating revenue volatility. The company also notes exposure to geopolitical tensions, such as conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, which could disrupt operations or the global economy, although it states current operations in these regions are not material to its financial position. Additionally, Palantir faces risks from foreign currency fluctuations, as its international revenue is exposed to exchange rate volatility, and it must navigate the complexities of integrating third-party AI models while maintaining strict security and audit controls.
