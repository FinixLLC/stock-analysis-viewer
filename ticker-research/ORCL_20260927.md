## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ORCL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Oracle operates as a global provider of enterprise information technology frameworks, delivering products and services that build, run, and support business operations through a hybrid model of cloud-based, on-premise, and hybrid deployments. The company’s business is structured into three segments: cloud and software, which accounted for 87% of total revenues in fiscal 2026; hardware, representing 5%; and services, representing 8%. The core of Oracle’s strategy is the transition of its extensive installed base of on-premise applications and infrastructure to Oracle Cloud, a shift that has driven cloud revenues to 51% of total revenues in fiscal 2026, up from 37% in fiscal 2024. This cloud offering comprises Oracle Cloud Applications (OCA), such as Fusion Cloud ERP, HCM, and CX, and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI), which powers these applications. Oracle differentiates itself by offering flexible, interoperable deployment models, including multicloud options and the Exadata Cloud@Customer hybrid model, allowing customers to choose how and when they deploy technologies. The company emphasizes embedded AI capabilities for automation and analytics, supported by significant research and development investments that reached $10.3 billion in fiscal 2026. Revenue is generated through subscription agreements for cloud services, perpetual software licenses, and recurring support contracts, with the latter being a critical component as substantially all customers opt to purchase support for their software and hardware.

Oracle’s competitive moat is rooted in its comprehensive, integrated portfolio of enterprise-grade applications and infrastructure technologies, which management argues differentiates the company from competitors offering more limited or specialized solutions. The company leverages a deep understanding of how applications and infrastructure interact, using OCI to power OCA and creating a common data model that spans core business functions. This integration, combined with the breadth of its industry-specific applications and the flexibility of its deployment models, creates high switching costs and stickiness, particularly as customers migrate workloads to the cloud. Additionally, Oracle’s extensive installed base of on-premise licenses provides a substantial pipeline for cloud migration, supported by programs that allow customers to pivot existing licenses and support contracts to cloud deployments. The company also maintains a strong position in infrastructure technologies, including the Oracle AI Database and Java, which are described as industry standards, further entrenching its presence in customer IT environments.

Management’s stated priorities center on expanding the business through the continued migration of customers to Oracle Cloud, with an expectation that this trend will accelerate. The company focuses on improving the performance, security, reliability, and cost-effectiveness of its offerings relative to competitors while incorporating emerging technologies like AI to enable leaner business processes and automation. Oracle prioritizes providing choice and flexibility to customers regarding deployment models, viewing this as a key element of its corporate strategy to address customer needs better than competitors. Additionally, management emphasizes selective acquisitions as a strategic tool to enhance the portfolio, evaluating potential deals based on earnings, operating margin, cash flow, and return on invested capital targets. The company also continues to invest heavily in research and development to enhance its existing portfolio and develop new technologies, while maintaining a strong direct sales force and partner network to drive global demand.

The filing flags several material risks that could impact Oracle’s financial condition and results of operations. These include the impact of general economic conditions, inflation, tariffs, trade policy, and geopolitical conditions on customer demand and costs. Specific risks cited include the company’s ability to pass on increased supply chain, energy, and tariff-related costs to customers, as well as the effects of foreign currency rate fluctuations. Governmental budgetary constraints are also noted as a factor affecting demand, particularly in the public sector. The company highlights the uncertainty in quarterly software license revenues due to the point-in-time nature of revenue recognition for large transactions, which can substantially affect quarterly results. Furthermore, Oracle faces risks related to its ability to manage Oracle Cloud capacity to meet demand, the renewal rates of its cloud and software support contracts, and the competitive position of its offerings relative to other cloud infrastructure providers. The filing also notes that the final outcome of income tax estimates, which involve significant judgment regarding global transactions and preferential tax treatments, could differ from reported amounts.
