## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-07-31, filed 2025-09-26), read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text. No locally cached 10-K text dump existed for CPRT in `company_profile/financial_reports/`.

What the company does: Copart is a leading global provider of online auctions and vehicle remarketing services, operating in the U.S., U.K., Germany, Brazil, Canada, U.A.E., Spain, Finland, Oman, the Republic of Ireland, and Bahrain. It provides vehicle sellers — primarily insurance companies (81% of vehicles processed in FY2025), but also dealers, individuals, charities, rental car companies, banks, finance companies, and fleet operators — with end-to-end services to process and sell vehicles, primarily online through its proprietary VB3 (Virtual Bidding Third Generation) internet auction platform. Buyers are principally licensed vehicle dismantlers, rebuilders, repair licensees, used vehicle dealers, exporters, and the general public. Most vehicles sold on behalf of insurers are total-loss, uneconomical-to-repair, or recovered-stolen vehicles.

Revenue model: In most countries (U.S., Canada, Brazil, Republic of Ireland, Finland, U.A.E., Oman, Bahrain) Copart operates primarily as an agent, earning auction and auction-related transaction fees plus post-auction service fees (delivery, storage) — an asset-light, high-margin model. In the U.K., Germany, and Spain, Copart operates both as agent and on a principal basis (purchasing salvage vehicles outright and reselling for its own account), which carries more inventory/pricing risk but also more revenue-per-transaction upside; the U.K. business also derives revenue from dismantled-parts sales via its Green Parts Specialist (GPS) subsidiary.

Auction technology (VB3): A proprietary two-step bidding process — an open preliminary bidding period (with the BID4U feature auto-bidding on a member's behalf up to their max bid, eBay-like in format) followed by a real-time internet-only virtual auction — designed to widen the buyer pool per sale and increase realized sale prices for sellers.

Moat, as described/implied by the company: Deep, multi-decade relationships with a limited number of major insurance-company sellers, who represent the substantial majority of vehicle supply; a proprietary global online auction technology platform (VB3) deployed across markets, which the company continues rolling out to newly acquired international operations; environmental/social positioning (facilitating vehicle re-use, parts recycling, and raw-material recovery) that supports insurer/regulatory goodwill; and a track record of successfully executing international expansion (Canada 2003; U.K. 2007-2008; U.A.E., Brazil, Germany, Spain 2013; Bahrain/Oman 2015; Republic of Ireland/India 2016; Finland 2018; U.K. parts recycler acquisition 2022) alongside real-world disaster-response scale (e.g., mobilizing to process tens of thousands of flood-damaged vehicles in South Florida after Hurricanes Helene and Milton in fall 2024) that smaller competitors likely cannot match.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, listed first under "Risks Related to Our Business and Industry"):
1. Concentration in a limited number of major vehicle sellers — the top-listed risk. Although no single customer exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in FY2023-2025, a limited number of insurance-company sellers have historically accounted for a substantial portion of revenue collectively; sellers have terminated agreements in particular markets before, with no assurance existing agreements won't be canceled or that vehicle supply can be maintained/grown.
2. International expansion execution risk — explicitly tied to the long history of acquisitions/expansions listed above; risk of failing to realize anticipated synergies, unanticipated integration costs, and uncertainty around whether deploying VB3 technology at acquired foreign operations will successfully lift revenue/operating efficiency to match historic performance.
