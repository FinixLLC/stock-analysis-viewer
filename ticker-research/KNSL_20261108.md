## Business Profile

Kinsale Capital Group is a U.S.-based property and casualty insurer that operates exclusively within the excess and surplus lines (E&S) market, specializing in hard-to-place risks for small- to medium-sized commercial and personal accounts. The company’s business model relies on selling insurance products in all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands primarily through a network of independent brokers, while retaining full control over underwriting and claims management. In 2025, the company generated $2.0 billion in gross written premiums, with a portfolio composition of 70.7% casualty and 29.3% property, and achieved a combined ratio of 75.9% and a return on equity of 29.3%.

Kinsale’s competitive moat is built on four distinct pillars: exclusive focus on the E&S market, which historically offers higher margins than the standard admitted market; a proprietary technology platform that enables rapid data analysis, 24-hour response times to brokers, and significant operational efficiency; a culture of rigorous expense control, resulting in a 20.8% expense ratio in 2025; and a "contrarian" risk appetite that allows the company to underwrite complex risks that competitors may decline, often securing better pricing due to reduced competition. Additionally, the company maintains a low average premium per policy ($13,400 in 2025), targeting segments with less competition, and manages claims in-house to ensure reserve accuracy and cost containment.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term stockholder value through consistent underwriting profits and prudent capital management. The strategic roadmap includes expanding the company’s presence in the E&S market, where it currently holds approximately 1.5% share, by leveraging its service levels and technology to capture more market share. Key operational goals include maintaining underwriting profitability regardless of market cycles, continuing to leverage technology to drive efficiencies and reduce human error, and sustaining a conservative balance sheet to maintain confidence among policyholders, reinsurers, and regulators. The management team, led by CEO Michael Kehoe, emphasizes its entrepreneurial nature and deep industry experience, with an average of over 30 years of relevant expertise.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the competitive pressure in the Commercial Property division, which saw a 17.9% decline in gross written premiums in 2025 due to rate declines and increased competition from standard carriers. The company also flags the volatility of its results, noting that net income is influenced by factors outside its direct control, such as changes in the legal or regulatory environment, social inflation, wage inflation, and trends in legal defense and medical costs. Additionally, the company acknowledges the inherent uncertainty in loss reserves, with 91.2% of total net loss reserves representing incurred but not reported claims, and notes that its performance is sensitive to the frequency and severity of claims, including catastrophe losses, which accounted for 1.9% of the loss ratio in 2025.
