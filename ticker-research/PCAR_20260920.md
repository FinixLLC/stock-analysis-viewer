## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PCAR_10-K_20260827.txt)

PACCAR operates as a global technology company with a diversified business model comprising three primary segments: Truck, Parts, and Financial Services. The Truck segment involves the design and manufacture of light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial trucks, marketed under the Kenworth and Peterbilt nameplates in North America, DAF in Europe, and Kenworth and DAF in Mexico, Australia, and South America. The Parts segment focuses on the distribution of aftermarket parts for trucks and related commercial vehicles, while the Financial Services segment generates earnings primarily by financing or leasing PACCAR products across North America, Europe, Australia, and South America. This integrated approach allows the company to capture value across the entire lifecycle of the vehicle, from initial sale through aftermarket support and financing.

The company’s competitive moat is evidenced by its consistent market leadership and operational scale. In 2025, PACCAR maintained a 29.9% heavy-duty truck retail market share in the U.S. and Canada and a 13.5% share in the European over-16-tonne market. The Financial Services arm supports this ecosystem with a global portfolio of loans and leases totaling $22.80 billion, leveraging a rigorous credit application process and operations spanning 26 countries. Furthermore, the company’s ability to maintain an 87th consecutive year of net income, despite cyclical downturns in truck demand, underscores the resilience of its diversified revenue streams and strong balance sheet, which has enabled $9.2 billion in capital investments over the past decade.

Management’s stated priorities center on maintaining a consistent focus on quality, investing in next-generation technologies, and optimizing manufacturing locations to mitigate trade risks. The company is actively embedding artificial intelligence across its business to drive innovation and performance, while investing in clean diesel, alternative powertrains, autonomous systems, and connected vehicle services. Additionally, PACCAR is enhancing its manufacturing footprint, including the construction of a robotic chassis paint facility in Ohio and a new engine remanufacturing facility, while strategically locating its North American factories in Ohio, Texas, and Washington to operate optimally under new Section 232 truck tariffs. The company also continues to invest in its U.S.-based battery joint venture, Amplify Cell Technologies.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily driven by macroeconomic and regulatory factors. The most significant immediate impact comes from import tariffs imposed by the U.S. government and other countries, which have negatively affected truck order intake and profit margins since March 2025, despite mitigating actions and local production strategies. The company also cites exposure to changes in tariff policy, including the pending U.S. Supreme Court ruling on International Emergency Economic Power Acts (IEEPA) tariffs. Other material risks include the impact of emissions regulations, such as the EPA’s reaffirmed NOx limits, and the sensitivity of the Financial Services segment to economic conditions; a weaker economy could lead to increased past due accounts, truck repossessions, and credit losses, while also reducing new business volume and average earning assets.
