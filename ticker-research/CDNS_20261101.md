## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CDNS_10-K_20260906.txt)

Cadence Design Systems operates as a global technology leader providing computational, AI-driven software, accelerated hardware, and silicon intellectual property (IP) to semiconductor and systems companies. The company’s business model is anchored in its Intelligent System Design (ISD) strategy, which organizes offerings into three integrated categories: Core EDA (70% of fiscal 2025 revenue), Semiconductor IP (14%), and System Design and Analysis (16%). Cadence generates the majority of its revenue—80% in fiscal 2025—from recurring sources, including software licensing, maintenance, and royalties, while the remaining 20% consists of up-front revenue from hardware sales and specific IP licenses. The company serves customers across diverse industries, including automotive, data centers, aerospace, and life sciences, by providing tools that manage the increasing complexity of electronic design, optimize performance, power, and area (PPA), and accelerate time-to-market.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its position as an industry standard in custom and analog IC design (via the Virtuoso platform) and digital IC design (via the Innovus platform), as well as its deep integration into the semiconductor ecosystem through foundry process design kits. Cadence differentiates itself through the integration of agentic and generative AI, machine learning, and digital twin algorithms into its core products, which it claims enables customers to overcome the limitations of traditional human-intensive optimization processes. Furthermore, the company maintains a strategic position by expanding its capabilities beyond traditional chip design to encompass full electromechanical systems, including multiphysics simulation for PCBs, advanced packaging, and 3D-ICs, thereby embedding itself in the broader product development lifecycle of its customers.

Management’s stated priorities focus on leveraging AI as a pivotal force across three key horizons: Infrastructure AI (high-performance computing and data centers), Physical AI (autonomous vehicles and robotics), and Life Sciences AI. The company emphasizes reducing customer time-to-market and advancing sustainability goals through energy-efficient design. To support these goals, Cadence prioritizes significant investment in Research and Development and strategic acquisitions to integrate cutting-edge technologies, such as the recent acquisitions of VLAB Works for automotive virtual prototyping, the Arm Artisan foundation IP business for standard cell libraries, and Secure-IC for embedded security. Additionally, management highlights the importance of cloud scalability through the Cadence OnCloud Platform to address growing computational needs.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to the macroeconomic and geopolitical environment. Significant concerns include expanded trade control laws and regulations, specifically regarding exports to China, where Cadence experienced reduced revenue due to license requirements that were later rescinded but remain subject to potential reimposition. The company also faces risks from new and higher U.S. tariffs and retaliatory measures from other countries, which could impact its hardware business and downstream effects. Furthermore, Cadence recently settled with the U.S. Department of Justice and the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) regarding export control violations involving sales to a Chinese customer, resulting in a loss and ongoing audit and compliance obligations. Other flagged risks include volatility in foreign currency exchange rates, inflation, changes in interest rates, and the impact of global geopolitical conflicts, although the company notes that these conflicts have not yet materially limited its ability to develop or support products.
