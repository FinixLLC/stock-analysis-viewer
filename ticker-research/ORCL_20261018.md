## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ORCL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Oracle operates as a provider of enterprise information technology frameworks, delivering products and services that build, run, and support IT infrastructure through a hybrid model of cloud-based, on-premise, and hybrid deployments. The company’s business model is structured around three distinct segments: cloud and software, hardware, and services, with the cloud and software segment representing the vast majority of revenue at 87% in fiscal 2026. Oracle monetizes its offerings through a mix of subscription-based cloud services (Oracle Cloud Applications and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure), perpetual software licenses, recurring software and hardware support contracts, and professional services. A central strategic pillar is the migration of its extensive installed base of on-premise customers to the Oracle Cloud, a transition that has driven cloud revenues to 51% of total revenues in fiscal 2026, up from 37% in fiscal 2024. The company emphasizes flexibility and interoperability, allowing customers to choose deployment models that suit their specific needs, including multicloud options and the Oracle Exadata Cloud@Customer hybrid model.

Oracle’s competitive moat is derived from its comprehensive, integrated portfolio of applications and infrastructure technologies, which management argues differentiates it from competitors offering more limited or specialized solutions. The company leverages a deep understanding of how applications and infrastructure interact, using its own Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) to power its Oracle Cloud Applications (OCA), creating a tightly integrated ecosystem. This integration is supported by industry-leading assets such as the Oracle AI Database and Java, which are described as the world’s most popular database management system and the most widely used language for cloud-native development, respectively. Furthermore, Oracle maintains a significant advantage through its ability to offer broad, flexible, and interoperable deployment models, which management states better addresses customer needs relative to competitors. The company also invests heavily in research and development, spending $10.3 billion in fiscal 2026, to embed artificial intelligence capabilities into its products, enhancing automation, analytics, and generative AI features to improve customer workflows.

Management’s stated priorities center on expanding the Oracle Cloud business by migrating existing on-premise customers to cloud-based services and capturing new demand from small and medium-sized businesses, developers, and partners. The company focuses on improving the performance, security, reliability, and cost-effectiveness of its offerings while incorporating emerging technologies like AI to enable leaner business processes. Oracle also prioritizes maintaining customer choice and flexibility in deployment models as a key growth strategy. Additionally, the company maintains a selective acquisition program to integrate complementary technologies and products, evaluating potential deals based on earnings, operating margin, cash flow, and return on invested capital targets. Management expects total cloud and software revenues to continue increasing on a constant currency basis, driven by cloud growth and continued demand for software offerings, which will fund further investments in R&D and cloud capacity expansion.

The filing flags several material risks that could impact financial results. These include the impact of general economic conditions, inflation, tariffs, trade policy, and geopolitical conditions on customer demand and costs. Oracle notes that its margins may be adversely affected by increases in supply chain and energy costs, the inability to pass tariff-related costs to customers, and foreign currency rate fluctuations. Governmental budgetary constraints are also cited as a factor affecting demand, particularly for government agencies. In the hardware segment, risks include the difficulty of predicting quarterly revenues due to the timing of large transactions, the finite life of hardware products, and competition from cloud infrastructure offerings. The services business faces risks related to personnel reductions in customer IT departments and tighter controls over discretionary spending. Finally, the company highlights uncertainty in income tax provisions due to complex global transactions, revenue sharing arrangements, and the identification of items qualifying for preferential tax treatment, noting that final tax outcomes could differ from estimates.
