## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that provides integrated data, decision-making, and operational infrastructure for government and commercial organizations. Its business model centers on four principal platforms: Gotham, which serves defense and intelligence missions; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform acting as a central operating system for enterprises; Apollo, a cloud-agnostic continuous delivery platform for infrastructure management; and AIP, a generative AI platform that connects large language models to organizational data and operations. The company generates revenue through software licenses and services, typically under contracts ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the contract term. In 2025, the company generated $4.5 billion in revenue, with 54% derived from government customers and 46% from commercial customers, while maintaining an 82% gross margin.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its vertically integrated platforms and the "Palantir Ontology," a systematic mapping of data to meaningful context that integrates data, logic, and actions into a decision-centric architecture. This architecture allows users of varying technical skills to collaborate and build interconnected workflows, creating high switching costs and deep operational integration. Palantir targets large-scale, complex, and high-risk implementation opportunities that smaller competitors often avoid due to high installation costs and failure risks. The company leverages its ability to deploy solutions rapidly—often in days via AIP bootcamps—compared to the months or years required for internal development, thereby capturing opportunities where traditional digital transformation projects have failed. Additionally, Palantir utilizes channel sales through cloud partnerships and industry-specific operating systems to expand its distribution reach.

Management’s stated priorities include expanding access to its platforms across a broader swath of the market in both commercial and government sectors. The company is focused on building long-term strategic relationships, evidenced by a total remaining deal value of $11.2 billion as of December 31, 2025, which represents a 105% increase year-over-year. Management emphasizes the growth of its U.S. customer base, which accounted for 74% of 2025 revenue, and aims to capture a greater share of U.S. federal government software spending. Furthermore, the company is prioritizing the development of industry-specific operating systems and partnerships, such as its strategic alliance with Fujitsu, to embed its software into broader data infrastructure solutions.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the public sector and macroeconomic conditions. A significant portion of revenue comes from government customers, which are subject to budget uncertainties, changes in spending priorities, and regulatory or policy shifts that can impact contract timing and size. Many contracts contain termination for convenience provisions, and U.S. federal contract options cannot be exercised more than one year in advance, creating revenue volatility. The company also notes exposure to geopolitical tensions, such as conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, which could disrupt operations or the global economy, although it states these currently do not have a material impact on its financial position. Additionally, the company faces risks from foreign currency fluctuations and macroeconomic factors like interest rates and trade relations, which may adversely affect its international business and customer operations.
