## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (HUM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Humana Inc. operates as a vertically integrated healthcare company headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, serving approximately 15 million members in medical benefit plans and 4.7 million in specialty products. The company’s business model is built on two reportable segments: Insurance and CenterWell. The Insurance segment generates the vast majority of revenue, with 83% of total premiums and services revenue derived from federal government contracts, primarily through Medicare Advantage (MA) and stand-alone prescription drug plans (PDP). Humana acts as a managed care organization, contracting with CMS to provide comprehensive health benefits to Medicare beneficiaries in exchange for fixed per-member-per-month payments, while also serving state-based Medicaid and long-term support services clients. The CenterWell segment provides adjacent care delivery services, including pharmacy solutions, primary care, and home solutions, which are designed to enhance member health and reduce costs. A core component of this model is an "integrated care delivery model" that unites quality care, member engagement, and data analytics, with a specific focus on transitioning providers from fee-for-service to value-based arrangements; as of late 2025, 68% of individual MA members were in such value-based relationships.

The company’s competitive moat appears to stem from its scale, national presence, and deep integration with the federal Medicare program. Humana has participated in the Medicare program for over 30 years and offers plans in all 50 states, which provides a geographically diverse membership base and greater ability to expand provider networks. The filing suggests that this scale allows for more favorable contract terms with providers due to shared networks across segments. Additionally, the company leverages sophisticated data analytics and risk-adjustment methodologies to manage costs and optimize payments from CMS, relying on accurate diagnosis coding and medical record reviews to maximize health status-adjusted premium payments.

Management’s stated priorities center on creating value through cost savings, productivity, and a multi-year transformation program to re-align the cost structure, operating model, and technology footprint with evolving market conditions. In 2025, Humana finalized its exit from the Employer Group Commercial Medical Products business, determining it was no longer positioned to sustainably meet commercial member needs or support long-term strategic plans. The company is focused on driving growth in its core Medicare and state-based contract businesses, improving member and patient outcomes, and supporting operational efficiency. Management emphasizes a strategy of offering affordable healthcare combined with a positive consumer experience, aiming to improve health outcomes and affordability for both individuals and the broader health system.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to regulatory and macroeconomic pressures. Humana is heavily dependent on federal government contracts, with 83% of revenue from such sources, exposing it to significant regulatory changes, including those under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) and potential restrictions on Medicare Advantage profitability. The company notes that regulatory bodies may compare MA profitability to non-MA businesses or require profitability ranges, which could restrict revenue and enrollment growth. Additionally, the filing highlights risks associated with the CMS risk-adjustment model, where reliance on provider coding accuracy and medical record reviews is critical for payment accuracy. Other material risks include the impact of sustained macroeconomic, regulatory, and competitive pressures, which have necessitated substantial value creation initiatives and impairment charges. The company also faces risks related to the elimination of the Medicare Part D coverage gap under the IRA, which has altered seasonal cost patterns and increased net prescription costs, as well as potential restrictions on provider network management and product sales.
