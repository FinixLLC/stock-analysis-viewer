## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CVS_10-K_20260906.txt)

CVS Health operates as an integrated health solutions company that connects care across four reportable segments: Health Care Benefits, Health Services, Pharmacy Consumer Wellness, and Corporate/Other. As of December 31, 2025, the company’s scale includes approximately 9,000 retail locations, over 1,000 walk-in and primary care clinics, and a pharmacy benefits manager (PBM) serving roughly 87 million plan members. Through its Aetna operations, the company serves an estimated 37 million people with traditional, voluntary, and consumer-directed health insurance products, including Medicare Advantage, Medicare Part D, and Medicaid managed care. The business model relies on an integrated approach where the company leverages its PBM, retail pharmacy, and insurance assets to deliver personalized, technology-driven care, aiming to improve health outcomes and lower overall costs. The Health Services segment specifically provides PBM solutions through CVS Caremark, while also operating value-based care entities like Signify Health and Oak Street Health.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from this vertical integration and scale, which allows it to create new sources of value by connecting care delivery with benefits administration and retail access. Management cites its "unique collection of businesses" and the ability to harness enterprise capabilities—supported by investments in artificial intelligence, cloud capabilities, and digital products—as key differentiators. This integrated model enables the company to offer seamless experiences, such as coordinating 100% of care for dually eligible Medicare and Medicaid members, and to utilize a nationwide provider network of approximately 2.0 million participating providers to manage utilization and quality.

Management’s stated priorities focus on simplifying health care experiences, improving engagement, lowering costs, and delivering better health outcomes. The company aims to be "America’s most trusted health care company" by transforming consumer experiences and being the partner of choice for employers and individuals. Strategic execution involves best-in-class operational performance, capital stewardship, and expanding into personalized, technology-driven care delivery. Additionally, the company is actively managing its portfolio, as evidenced by the exit from public health insurance exchanges effective January 2026 and the deconsolidation of its Omnicare subsidiary.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and economic pressures. The company faces significant exposure to changes in Medicare and Medicaid funding and regulation, including risk adjustment mechanisms and potential surcharges. Legislative and regulatory activity seeking to regulate PBM activities is a major concern, as it could adversely affect the company’s ability to conduct business on commercially reasonable terms and standardize products across state lines. Financially, the company is exposed to elevated medical utilization levels, which may pressure its Health Care Benefits and Health Services segments. Furthermore, the company faces increasing pressure from pharmaceutical manufacturers regarding rebate calculations and collection, as well as limited ability to offer retail network differential pricing due to marketplace dynamics. Other flagged risks include the impact of inflation, tariffs, and supply chain disruptions on costs and consumer demand, as well as specific litigation risks, including legacy litigation charges and ongoing opioid litigation matters.
