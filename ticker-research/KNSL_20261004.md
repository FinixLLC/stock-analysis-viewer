## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KNSL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Kinsale Capital Group is a U.S.-domiciled property and casualty insurance company that operates exclusively within the excess and surplus lines (E&S) market, specializing in hard-to-place risks for small- to medium-sized commercial accounts and high-value homeowners. The company’s business model relies on selling policies in all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands primarily through a network of independent brokers, while retaining full control over underwriting and claims management. In 2025, the company generated $2.0 billion in gross written premiums, with a portfolio composition of 70.7% casualty and 29.3% property, and achieved a combined ratio of 75.9% and a return on equity of 29.3%.

Kinsale’s competitive moat is built on four distinct pillars: exclusive focus on the E&S market, which historically offers higher margins and faster growth than the standard admitted market; a proprietary technology platform that drives efficiency, reduces administrative errors, and enables rapid response times to brokers; a significantly lower expense ratio (20.8% in 2025) resulting from this technological efficiency and a culture of expense control; and a "contrarian" risk appetite that allows the company to underwrite complex risks that competitors may decline, often resulting in better pricing due to reduced competition. Additionally, the company maintains a conservative balance sheet and manages all claims in-house, leveraging a management team with an average of over 30 years of relevant experience.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term stockholder value by growing the business while generating consistent underwriting profits. Key strategic initiatives include expanding the company’s presence in the E&S market, where Kinsale currently holds approximately 1.5% market share, by leveraging its service levels and speed to capture more business. The company aims to maintain underwriting profitability regardless of market cycles by focusing on hard-to-place risks and maintaining adequate rate levels. Furthermore, management prioritizes the continued investment in technology to drive efficiencies and scalability, while maintaining a strong, conservative balance sheet to preserve confidence among policyholders, reinsurers, and investors.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the competitive pressure in the Commercial Property division, where gross written premiums decreased by 17.9% in 2025 due to rate declines and an increasingly competitive environment from standard carriers. The company also highlights the inherent volatility of its results, noting that net income is influenced by factors outside its direct control, such as social inflation, wage inflation, medical cost inflation, and legal defense costs. Additionally, the filing notes that while the company benefits from favorable prior-year reserve development, it faces risks related to catastrophe losses, which increased in 2025, and the potential for adverse development in loss reserves, which constitute a significant portion of its liabilities.
