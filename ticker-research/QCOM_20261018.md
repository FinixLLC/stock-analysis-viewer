## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates through a dual-segment business model comprising the Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL) segments, supported by a smaller strategic investments segment (QSI). QCT generates the majority of revenue by selling equipment and services, specifically Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, processors, modems, and integrated chipsets for handsets, automotive, and IoT applications. QTL generates high-margin revenue by licensing its intellectual property portfolio, including patent rights essential to wireless products, to device manufacturers. The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its position as a leader in technology development, standardization, and intellectual property creation, particularly in on-device AI, high-performance low-power computing, and cellular technologies like 5G. Management explicitly states that sustaining this leadership in IP creation and integrated circuit supply is critical to long-term growth.

Management’s stated priorities include maintaining leadership in technology development and standardization, expanding into non-mobile industries such as automotive and IoT, and navigating the complexities of global trade policies. The company emphasizes the importance of remaining a leading developer of integrated circuit products to sustain its business. In fiscal 2025, the company reported revenues of $44.3 billion, up 14% year-over-year, driven primarily by a 16% increase in QCT revenues from higher handset, IoT, and automotive sales. However, net income decreased by 45% to $5.5 billion, largely due to a $5.7 billion charge to establish a valuation allowance on federal deferred tax assets resulting from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB).

The filing flags several material risks. First, the company faces significant exposure to global trade policy changes, including tariffs from the U.S., China, and other countries, which could negatively impact demand, pricing, and costs, potentially leading to increased inventory write-downs. Second, the company is subject to seasonality and fluctuations in consumer demand for wireless devices, which directly affects QCT revenues and QTL licensing royalties. Third, legal and regulatory proceedings remain a material risk, with the company noting that outcomes are inherently uncertain and potential losses could exceed recorded amounts. Fourth, the company faces tax-related risks, specifically the impact of the OBBB, which subjects it to the corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) beginning in fiscal 2026 and alters the treatment of foreign-derived intangible income, affecting its effective tax rate and deferred tax asset realization. Finally, the company acknowledges risks related to the impairment of non-marketable equity investments and the need for accurate estimates of future customer demand for inventory valuation.
