## Business Profile

Source: live SEC 10-K fetch (FY2025, filed 2026-02-12, self-extracted and synthesized directly from Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors).

What the company does: Eli Lilly (founded 1876, incorporated 1901) operates a single reportable business segment — human pharmaceutical products — across four core therapeutic areas: Cardiometabolic Health (Mounjaro, Zepbound, Trulicity, Jardiance, Basaglar, Humalog/Humulin insulins), Oncology (Verzenio, Jaypirca, Retevmo, Erbitux, Cyramza, Inluriyo), Immunology (Ebglyss, Omvoh, Taltz, Olumiant), and Neuroscience (Kisunla for Alzheimer's, Emgality for migraine). Products are sold in ~90 countries, manufactured in the U.S. (including Puerto Rico), Europe, and Asia.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: IP protection and market exclusivity ("Loss of effective patent protection...can result in the loss of effective market exclusivity...often leading to a severe and rapid decline in revenues") is the foundational, explicitly-stated moat mechanism for pharma economics generally. Beyond patents, Lilly cites its direct-to-patient LillyDirect platform (growing self-pay channel, particularly relevant for obesity medicines given inconsistent payer coverage) and its manufacturing scale investment (the $4.5B Indiana expansion) as increasingly important competitive assets given current GLP-1 demand that exceeds even Lilly's own production capacity in places.

Segment/product mix: GLP-1 franchise (Mounjaro + Zepbound + Trulicity) is now the dominant growth driver — Q2 2026 "Key Products" revenue (Ebglyss, Foundayo, Inluriyo, Jaypirca, Kisunla, Mounjaro, Omvoh, Zepbound) hit $15.7B of the $23.0B quarterly total, with Immunology/Oncology/Neuroscience Key Products revenue growing 121% YoY — indicating genuine diversification beyond the GLP-1 story is also accelerating, not just riding on Mounjaro/Zepbound alone.

Management's stated priorities (from Q2 2026 CEO commentary and Item 1): CEO David Ricks: "Lilly's momentum continues, as we delivered 48% revenue growth and raised our full-year guidance... With our next-generation weight-loss medicine retatrutide and its complete clinical data package in hand, new manufacturing capacity coming online, and exciting new assets entering our pipeline through business development, Lilly's future, after 150 years, has never been brighter." The explicit strategic priorities are: (1) scale GLP-1 manufacturing to meet demand, (2) advance retatrutide toward a 2027 FDA filing, and (3) aggressively pursue business-development/M&A to refill the pipeline (five-plus acquisitions in a single quarter).

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A):
1. Pharmaceutical R&D failure risk — "high rate of failure inherent in drug discovery and development," explicitly citing named late-stage candidates like orforglipron as subject to this uncertainty.
2. Intense competition, including explicitly "from China and other markets" with rapidly expanding R&D capabilities increasingly licensing/partnering directly with multinational pharma or competing head-on.
3. Government/payer pricing pressure — the most concretely material item in this filing: Lilly discloses that in November 2025 it entered preliminary voluntary agreements with the U.S. government to lower Medicaid and certain other U.S. drug prices and adopt "a more balanced pricing approach across developed nations" for new launches. Separately, HHS selected Jardiance for Medicare government-set pricing effective 2026, and in January 2026 selected Trulicity and Verzenio for government-set Medicare pricing effective 2028 — direct, dated, company-disclosed evidence of accelerating U.S. drug-pricing regulatory pressure hitting Lilly's own named products.
4. Counterfeit/mass-compounded GLP-1 risk — explicitly flagged given the scale of demand for Mounjaro/Zepbound outstripping supply in some markets, creating incentive for illegal compounding/counterfeiting that could both harm patients and erode brand trust.
