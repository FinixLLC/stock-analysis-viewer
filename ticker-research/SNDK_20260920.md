## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNDK_10-K_20260906.txt)

Sandisk Corporation (SNDK) is a vertically integrated global semiconductor memory company that designs, manufactures, and sells NAND flash-based storage solutions. Following its separation from Western Digital in February 2025, the company operates as a standalone entity with a business model centered on owning chip-level design and IP, front- and back-end manufacturing, and systems engineering. Its product portfolio spans solid-state drives (SSDs), embedded products, removable cards, and wafers, serving three primary end markets: Datacenter (cloud and enterprise), Edge (OEMs for PCs, mobile, automotive, and industrial), and Consumer (retail and channel). The company sources its flash memory wafers through joint ventures with Kioxia, while designing controllers in-house or via third-party foundries, and utilizes a mix of in-house and contract manufacturing for assembly and testing.

Sandisk’s competitive moat is derived from its vertical integration, which allows it to control costs and quality across the supply chain, and its extensive intellectual property portfolio, comprising approximately 8,000 granted patents and 3,000 pending applications. The company cites its "differentiated innovation engine," deep relationships with industry leaders, and a strong consumer brand as key differentiators. Additionally, management highlights its leadership in cost efficiency and its ability to deliver high-performance, cost-effective solutions for AI workloads as critical advantages in a highly competitive market against rivals such as Kioxia, Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix.

Management’s stated strategic priorities focus on technology innovation and manufacturing leadership to drive cost efficiency and performance. A significant strategic shift involves transforming the financial model through "New Business Models" (NBMs), which are long-term agreements with Datacenter and Edge customers that commit to stated volumes over multi-year periods. These agreements are designed to provide greater revenue visibility, improve production planning, and reduce exposure to industry cyclicality. Furthermore, management is prioritizing investment in high-value opportunities driven by AI infrastructure growth, which they expect to persist through 2027 and beyond, while maintaining operational excellence in supply chain management and capital efficiency.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policies, specifically pending investigations under Section 232 and Section 301 that could impact tariff rates. While most products are currently exempt, the loss of these exemptions or additional tariff increases would raise the cost of goods sold and potentially negatively impact margins and demand. The company also notes that while NBMs reduce cyclicality, they do not eliminate risks associated with customer demand, market conditions, or operational execution. Additionally, the filing highlights the inherent risks of relying on joint ventures with Kioxia for flash wafers and the competitive pressure from other vertically integrated suppliers.
