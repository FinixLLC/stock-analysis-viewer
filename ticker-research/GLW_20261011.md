## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLW_10-K_20260906.txt)

Corning Incorporated is a diversified materials science and technology company that leverages deep expertise in glass science, ceramic science, and optical physics to manufacture category-defining products for industries including optical communications, display, mobile consumer electronics, automotive, life sciences, semiconductors, and solar. The company’s business model relies on sustained investment in research, development, and engineering to solve complex customer challenges, operating through five reportable segments: Optical Communications, Display, Specialty Materials, Automotive, and Life Sciences. In 2025, Optical Communications was the largest segment, representing 38% of total segment net sales, driven by strong demand for fiber and connectivity solutions for carrier networks, enterprise data centers, and artificial intelligence infrastructure. The Display segment, accounting for 23% of sales, manufactures glass substrates for flat-panel displays using a proprietary fusion process, while the Specialty Materials segment (13% of sales) produces durable cover glass for mobile devices (Gorilla Glass) and precision optics for semiconductors. The Automotive segment (11% of sales) supplies ceramic substrates for emissions control and technical glass for vehicles, and the Life Sciences segment provides laboratory products for drug discovery and bioproduction.

Corning’s competitive moat is built on its proprietary manufacturing processes, extensive patent portfolios, and brand recognition. In the Display segment, the company cites its invented fusion manufacturing process as the cornerstone of its technology leadership, yielding glass with superior surface quality and thermal stability that is scalable and cost-effective for large substrates. In Optical Communications, the company leverages its history of inventing low-loss optical fiber and holds an extensive portfolio of patents relating to products, technologies, and manufacturing processes, some of which are licensed to third parties. The Specialty Materials segment relies on brand loyalty through trademarks like Gorilla Glass and a growing patent portfolio, while the Automotive segment benefits from over 100 years of market access with automotive OEMs and proprietary ceramic substrate technology that became the global standard for catalytic converters. Across all segments, the company emphasizes that patent protection and proprietary trade secrets are important to operations, creating barriers to entry through technical complexity and established customer relationships.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on the "Springboard" plan, introduced in late 2023, which aims to grow sales and enhance profitability. The company set a high-confidence target to add $4 billion in incremental annualized core sales by the end of 2026 and achieve a 20% core operating margin. Management reports that as of the fourth quarter of 2025, both the growth and profitability targets were achieved a full year ahead of plan. To sustain this trajectory, management prioritizes increasing capacity and technology capabilities to meet demand, particularly in areas like generative AI infrastructure and mobile consumer electronics, while sharing risk appropriately to maintain returns. The company also focuses on operational efficiency, evidenced by actions to raise prices, reduce costs, and increase productivity, which contributed to a 3 percentage point increase in gross margin in 2025.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to market demand volatility and regulatory environments. In the Automotive segment, management notes softness in the heavy-duty diesel market and unfavorable foreign exchange movements as drivers of decreased sales. The Display segment faced headwinds from a weaker Japanese yen environment, which required pricing actions to offset the impact of resetting the core exchange rate. Additionally, the company highlights tax-related risks, including ongoing IRS examinations of U.S. federal income tax returns for years 2015 through 2020, noting that if the company is unsuccessful in defending its positions, the impact could be material to its consolidated financial statements. The filing also references the implementation of the EU Pillar Two Framework and the U.S. One Big Beautiful Bill Act, although management determined these did not have a material impact on results for the reported periods.
