## Business Profile

Source: FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-25), fetched live from SEC EDGAR and read directly (Item 1 Business).

What the company does: Fortinet is a cybersecurity leader built around the convergence of networking and security, delivered through its "Fortinet Security Fabric" platform — an integrated set of 50+ products spanning secure networking, unified Secure Access Service Edge (SASE), and AI-driven security operations (SecOps). As of 2025-12-31, the company held 1,064 U.S. patents (1,405 global, including 321 AI-related) and served customers in over 100 countries.

Core differentiating technologies (company's own framing):
- FortiASIC — proprietary Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (security processing units) that the company says deliver superior performance-per-watt versus industry alternatives — a genuine hardware moat versus software-only competitors.
- FortiOS — the unified operating system underpinning the entire Security Fabric.
- FortiAI — a dual-layer "AI for Security" (FortiAI-Assist, for SOC/NOC teams) and "Security for AI" (FortiAI-Protect, FortiAI-SecureAI, protecting customers' own AI/LLM infrastructure) framework.
- FortiCloud / FortiGuard Labs — a private-cloud SaaS platform plus a large threat-intelligence research organization mining global sensor data for new threats.

Three solution pillars: Secure Networking (FortiGate firewalls, FortiSwitch, FortiAP — the historical core business), Unified SASE (single-vendor SASE combining firewall, SD-WAN, secure web gateway, ZTNA, DLP, etc.), and AI-Driven SecOps (FortiAnalyzer, FortiSIEM, FortiSOAR, FortiEndpoint).

Manufacturing and supply chain (material detail from the 10-K): Fortinet outsources hardware manufacturing to contract manufacturers including Accton, IBASE, Micro-Star, Senao, and Wistron — approximately 87% of its hardware is manufactured in Taiwan. Its proprietary ASICs are built by Toshiba America and Renesas, using foundries in Taiwan and Japan operated by TSMC or the contract manufacturer itself. Key components (CPUs from Intel/AMD, networking chips from Broadcom/Marvell/Qualcomm/Intel, memory from Micron/Samsung/Toshiba/Western Digital/ADATA) are sourced from limited or sole-source suppliers, and the company states it has no long-term contracts guaranteeing capacity or pricing for ASIC/component manufacturing.

Risks the company itself flags as most material: the heavy Taiwan manufacturing concentration (geopolitical/supply-disruption risk shared broadly across the tech/semiconductor sector), reliance on single/limited-source component suppliers, and no long-term manufacturing capacity guarantees.
