## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (RVMD_10-K_20260827.txt)

Revolution Medicines (RVMD) is a clinical-stage precision oncology company focused on developing novel targeted therapies for RAS-addicted cancers. Its business model relies on a proprietary structure-based drug discovery platform that creates small molecules designed to bind directly to active RAS variants (RAS(ON) Inhibitors), rather than the inactive forms targeted by first-generation KRAS inhibitors. The company’s pipeline includes multi-selective and mutant-selective inhibitors such as daraxonrasib, zoldonrasib, elironrasib, and RMC-5127, which are being evaluated as monotherapies or in combination with other agents. While RVMD has historically generated collaboration revenue (e.g., from a terminated Sanofi agreement), it currently operates as a development-stage entity that expensed all research and development costs in the periods incurred, relying on third-party contractors for preclinical studies, clinical trials, and manufacturing.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its deep chemical biology and cancer pharmacology know-how, which enables the creation of small molecules tailored to unconventional binding sites. RVMD differentiates itself by targeting the RAS(ON) state to overcome drug resistance and by leveraging a broad portfolio of inhibitors that cover major RAS mutation hotspots (G12, G13, and Q61). This approach allows for the development of both multi-selective inhibitors and highly specific mutant-selective agents, providing a breadth of therapeutic options that competitors may not possess. Additionally, the company has secured significant regulatory milestones, including Breakthrough Therapy Designations for daraxonrasib, zoldonrasib, and elironrasib, as well as Orphan Drug Designation and a Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher for daraxonrasib in pancreatic cancer.

Management’s stated priorities center on advancing its deep pipeline of RAS(ON) Inhibitors through pivotal Phase 3 registrational trials, particularly in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Key initiatives include completing enrollment in the RASolve 301 trial for NSCLC and generating readouts for the RASolute 302 trial in PDAC, both expected in 2026. The company is also prioritizing combination strategies, evidenced by recent collaborations with Bristol Myers Squibb, Amgen, Summit Therapeutics, and Tango Therapeutics to evaluate RAS(ON) inhibitors alongside PRMT5 inhibitors and bispecific antibodies. Furthermore, management is focused on expanding its discovery capabilities through an AI-driven collaboration with Iambic Therapeutics and preparing to initiate first-in-human trials for a new class of RAS(ON) inhibitors designed to overcome resistance, with a target initiation date of the fourth quarter of 2026.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily the inherent uncertainty and length of the regulatory approval process, noting that RVMD has not yet obtained approval for any product candidate. The company highlights the risk that clinical trials may fail to demonstrate safety or efficacy, or that interim data may differ materially from final results. Significant operational risks include delays in patient enrollment, particularly for trials requiring specific RAS mutations, and the potential for clinical holds or terminations by regulatory authorities. The filing also emphasizes the competitive threat from other biopharmaceutical companies that may develop safer, more effective, or cheaper products, as well as the risk that technological advances could render RVMD’s product candidates obsolete. Finally, the company notes its reliance on third-party contractors for manufacturing and clinical operations, which introduces risks related to supply chain quality, regulatory compliance, and the ability to meet development timelines.
