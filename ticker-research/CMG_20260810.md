## Business Profile

Source: live WebSearch of Chipotle's own investor communications and financial media coverage (no locally cached 10-K was available this session; not independently fetched from SEC given time constraints — flagging this as a gap).

What the company does: Chipotle operates and franchises (in select international markets) fast-casual Mexican-inspired restaurants built around a customizable assembly-line format — proteins, rice, beans, salsas, and toppings assembled to order. It is the clear scale leader in the U.S. fast-casual category ($11.9B 2025 revenue).

Strategic framework ("Recipe for Growth"), under CEO Scott Boatwright (named CEO in 2024, succeeding Brian Niccol who departed for Starbucks): a five-pillar strategy consisting of (1) operational/culinary excellence to protect the core business, (2) evolving brand messaging and accelerating menu innovation, (3) modernizing the business model through AI and technology, (4) expanding global reach via company-owned and partner-operated international markets, and (5) cultivating talent with an emphasis on speed and agility.

Menu innovation cadence: Chipotle is targeting four limited-time-offer (LTO) launches in 2026, starting with the return of Chicken al Pastor (described as the brand's most popular LTO ever). A high-protein menu introduced in December 2025 has been a notable success — protein add-ons now account for ~25% of all transactions. Second-half 2026 plans include additional side items, two more LTO proteins, and expanded beverage offerings (the fastest-growing category in the restaurant industry generally). CEO Boatwright has explicitly ruled out breakfast, fish tacos, and desserts, keeping the menu focused on core lunch/dinner occasions — a deliberate strategic constraint, not an oversight.

International expansion: a development agreement with Alsea to open Chipotle's first restaurants in Mexico, with the first location targeted for early 2026 — notable given the brand's Mexican-inspired positioning has, until now, not included an actual Mexico presence.

Moat (synthesis from PaoBull + market commentary): scale leadership in U.S. fast-casual, a debt-free balance sheet enabling continued unit growth/reinvestment without leverage risk, strong digital/loyalty infrastructure, and now-proven menu-innovation execution (high-protein LTO success). CMG's discount valuation relative to CAVA (29x vs. 118-172x forward P/E) reflects the market pricing CMG as the mature, high-quality compounder versus CAVA as the higher-growth, higher-multiple upstart — not a quality gap.

Risks — most material and current, based on recent events rather than a stated Item 1A list (no local 10-K pulled this session):
1. Food-safety/reputational risk is the dominant, currently-live risk. Chipotle has a well-documented history here — implicated in at least five separate foodborne-illness outbreaks between 2015 and 2018 — and the market's sharp (~10%) sell-off reaction to the August 4, 2026 salmonella news reflects investors pricing this as a recurring, structural vulnerability rather than a one-off event.
