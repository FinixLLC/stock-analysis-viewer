## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines operates as a major passenger carrier providing scheduled air transportation across the United States and near-international markets, utilizing a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft to serve 117 destinations. The company’s business model is built on a low-cost strategy that leverages a point-to-point route structure, high-frequency short-haul service, and service to secondary or downtown airports to minimize congestion and maximize asset utilization. This approach is supported by the use of a single aircraft type, which simplifies maintenance, training, and scheduling, while the company increasingly blends this with hub-and-spoke connectivity through core stations to improve reliability and capture longer-haul demand. In 2025, Southwest executed several transformational initiatives to modernize its customer experience and cost structure, including the introduction of assigned and extra legroom seating, a redesigned boarding model, and the launch of its first international airline partnerships. The company also expanded its distribution channels to include third-party online travel agencies and introduced 24-hour operations with redeye flights to increase aircraft utilization.

Southwest’s competitive moat is derived from its unique route network, which enables direct nonstop routing and frequent, conveniently timed flights that competitors using traditional hub-and-spoke models may not match. The company’s focus on cost discipline is evident in its use of less congested airports, which reduces ground time and allows for higher employee productivity and lower capital requirements for gates and aircraft. Additionally, the single-fleet strategy of Boeing 737s provides operational efficiencies in fuel consumption, maintenance, and training. The company’s ability to adjust its network quickly—such as redeploying capacity from underperforming markets like Atlanta and Fort Lauderdale to higher-demand areas like Nashville and San Diego—without structurally disrupting core markets further underscores its operational flexibility.

Management’s stated priorities center on elevating the customer experience, improving financial performance, and driving shareholder value through modernization efforts. Key strategic actions include optimizing the route network to match capacity with demand, shifting resources from short-haul to more profitable longer-haul flights, and enhancing operational reliability. The company is also focused on reducing unit costs through initiatives such as digital turnarounds, supply chain improvements, and corporate efficiency measures, including a 15% reduction in corporate positions in early 2025. Furthermore, management is prioritizing fleet modernization by retiring older aircraft and accelerating the delivery of new Boeing 737 MAX aircraft, while also expanding into new markets and international partnerships to generate additional demand.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the volatility and unpredictability of fuel prices, which remain the second largest operating cost category despite the termination of the fuel hedging program in 2025. Labor costs, which constituted approximately 46.9% of operating expenses in 2025, are also a significant risk, driven by ratified contracts and rate inflation, although the company has implemented workforce reductions to mitigate this. Additionally, the company faces risks related to aircraft delivery delays from Boeing, which have necessitated amendments to its order book and reliance on supplemental agreements for credits and concessions. The timing of FAA certifications for the Boeing 737-7 is also uncertain, posing a risk to fleet modernization timelines. Finally, the company notes that structural changes in the domestic travel market have reduced demand for short-haul travel, requiring continuous network optimization to maintain profitability.
