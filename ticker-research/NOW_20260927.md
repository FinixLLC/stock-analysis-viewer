## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

ServiceNow operates as a cloud-based software-as-a-service provider that delivers the "ServiceNow AI Platform," a unified infrastructure designed to help public and private organizations govern, secure, and manage artificial intelligence while streamlining enterprise workflows. The company’s business model is primarily subscription-based, with revenues derived from non-cancellable contracts that are recognized ratably over the contract term. The platform integrates three core capabilities—AI (via the Now Assist suite), data (through a single data fabric and RaptorDB), and workflow orchestration—to connect disparate systems and automate cross-functional processes. ServiceNow groups its product portfolio into four areas: Technology, CRM and Industry, Core Business, and Creator and Other, serving diverse industries with solutions for IT operations, risk management, human resources, and customer service.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its two-decade history of partnering with enterprise customers, which has provided deep institutional knowledge of how work flows across organizational silos and systems. Management argues that this expertise allows ServiceNow to build cross-functional workflows that reflect practical operational realities rather than idealized models, creating a "connective tissue" that bridges the gap between AI’s analytical capabilities and the execution layer where business processes operate. Additionally, the platform’s architecture allows organizations to deploy AI-enhanced workflows without replacing existing technology infrastructure, a capability that distinguishes ServiceNow from point-solution competitors who may lack comprehensive integration, governance frameworks, and enterprise-grade reliability. The company also cites its operational expertise in maintaining reliability, security, and performance at scale as a key advantage.

Management’s stated priorities focus on continuing to understand and meet evolving customer needs as they adopt AI technologies, specifically by delivering integrated solutions that convert AI-generated insights into controlled, governed business outcomes. The strategy emphasizes leveraging the platform’s ability to orchestrate action across systems, enforce governance policies, and manage complex workflows, positioning the company to capitalize on the shift toward AI-powered automation. Recent strategic moves include the acquisition of Moveworks, Inc. to strengthen enterprise workflow automation with advanced search and virtual agent technology, and a 5-for-1 stock split approved in December 2025.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the dynamic and rapidly evolving technology landscape. Management acknowledges that while the AI era presents opportunities, it also introduces uncertainties reminiscent of prior technological shifts, such as the move to cloud computing. There is a specific risk that competitive dynamics may shift as new approaches emerge and customer preferences evolve, potentially challenging ServiceNow’s established position. Additionally, the company notes exposure to macroeconomic events, including global conflicts, interest rates, inflation, and tariffs, which could materially impact business results if they lead to greater global economic disruptions. The filing also highlights risks associated with foreign currency exchange rates, as an increasing percentage of contracts are denominated in currencies such as the Euro and British Pound Sterling, causing variability in reported metrics like Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO).
