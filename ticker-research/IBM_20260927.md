## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (IBM_10-K_20260906.txt)

IBM is a globally integrated enterprise that creates value for clients by helping them leverage hybrid cloud and artificial intelligence to drive digital transformation, modernize applications, and increase operational efficiency. The company’s business model relies on a combination of software, consulting services, and deep incumbency in mission-critical infrastructure, all supported by one of the world’s leading research organizations. IBM operates through several distinct segments, including Software, Consulting, Infrastructure, and Financing, with the latter facilitating client acquisition of hardware, software, and services while managing credit and residual value risk. The company actively partners with a broad ecosystem of hyperscalers, service providers, and technology vendors—including AWS, Microsoft, Salesforce, and SAP—to deliver end-to-end solutions, although it notes that some partners are also competitors in other areas.

IBM’s competitive moat is built on its breadth and depth of expertise, which allows it to integrate different technologies to solve complex business issues. The company differentiates itself through its long-standing relationships and trust with enterprises, deep industry and technology expertise, and a broad go-to-market reach supported by its partner ecosystem. In the Infrastructure segment, IBM claims a competitive advantage through investments in higher-value capabilities such as security, scalability, and reliability specifically designed for mission-critical and AI workloads. In Financing, the ability to manage credit and residual value risk provides a distinct edge over non-captive financing entities. Management states that the company is the leader or among the leaders in each of its business segments and differentiates itself by combining incumbency with innovation, positioning itself for future opportunities such as quantum computing.

Management’s stated priorities center on executing a hybrid cloud and AI strategy to support clients’ digital transformations and help them reimagine critical workflows at scale. The company aims to optimize its portfolio through both organic and inorganic innovations and effective resource allocation to drive current performance and extend innovation leadership into the future. A key focus is delivering business outcomes and value to clients, leveraging IBM’s research capabilities and broad capabilities in software, consulting, and infrastructure to maintain leadership in a highly competitive environment.

The filing flags that IBM operates in a highly competitive environment with hundreds of competitors worldwide, ranging from large multinational enterprises to smaller, niche players. The company notes that as it executes its hybrid cloud and AI strategy, it is regularly exposed to new competitors. Specific competitive threats include major technology firms like Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Oracle, and SAP in software; global firms like Accenture and Capgemine in consulting; and hardware providers like Dell, HPE, and Intel in infrastructure. Additionally, the filing highlights that cloud service providers are leveraging innovation in technology and service delivery to compete with traditional providers and offer alternative routes to market for server and storage systems.
