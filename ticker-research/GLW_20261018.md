## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLW_10-K_20260906.txt)

Corning Incorporated is a diversified materials science and manufacturing company that leverages deep expertise in glass science, ceramic science, and optical physics to develop category-defining products for industries including optical communications, display, mobile consumer electronics, automotive, life sciences, semiconductors, and solar. The company operates through five reportable segments—Optical Communications, Display, Specialty Materials, Automotive, and Life Sciences—along with Hemlock and Emerging Growth Businesses, manufacturing in 14 countries. Its business model relies on sustained investment in research and development to solve complex customer challenges, with a focus on high-performance materials such as low-loss optical fiber, precision display glass substrates, chemically strengthened cover glass (Gorilla Glass), ceramic substrates for emissions control, and laboratory consumables. In 2025, Optical Communications was the largest segment, representing 38% of total segment net sales, followed by Display at 23%, Specialty Materials at 13%, Automotive at 11%, and Life Sciences at approximately 6%.

Corning’s competitive moat is anchored in its proprietary manufacturing processes and extensive intellectual property portfolio. In the Display segment, the company utilizes a proprietary fusion manufacturing process that it invented, which it believes is the most cost-effective method for producing large-size glass substrates, yielding products with superior surface quality and dimensional uniformity. In Optical Communications, Corning holds an extensive portfolio of patents related to fiber, cable, and connectivity technologies, including specific innovations for high-density data center environments driven by generative AI demand. The Specialty Materials segment relies on brand recognition and loyalty for its Gorilla Glass and other premium materials, supported by a growing patent portfolio. Across all segments, the company emphasizes that patent protection and proprietary trade secrets are critical to operations, creating barriers to entry through technical leadership in materials science and engineering capabilities that competitors find difficult to replicate.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on the "Springboard" plan, introduced in late 2023, which aims to grow sales and enhance profitability. The company has upgraded its target to add $4 billion in incremental annualized core sales by the end of 2026 and achieve a 20% core operating margin by the same date. Management reports that both the growth and profitability targets were achieved a full year ahead of plan as of the fourth quarter of 2025. Strategic priorities include leveraging demand for innovations in optical communications for artificial intelligence and data centers, expanding capacity and technology capabilities to meet future growth, and maintaining a firm financial foundation. The company also emphasizes sharing risk appropriately to achieve returns that underpin the Springboard plan, while continuing to invest in research, development, and engineering to remain ready to solve customer challenges.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to market demand volatility and operational dependencies. In the Automotive segment, management notes softness in the heavy-duty diesel market and unfavorable foreign exchange impacts as drivers of decreased sales. The Display segment faced headwinds from resetting the core exchange rate from 107 to 120 Japanese yen to USD, which required pricing actions to offset the weaker yen environment. Additionally, the company highlights significant tax-related risks, including ongoing IRS examinations of U.S. federal income tax returns for years 2015 through 2020; while Corning believes it is more likely than not to sustain its positions, it acknowledges that if unsuccessful, the impact could be material to its consolidated financial statements. The filing also notes that the company is licensed to use certain patents owned by others that are considered important to the operations of the Optical Communications and Automotive segments, indicating a dependency on external intellectual property.
