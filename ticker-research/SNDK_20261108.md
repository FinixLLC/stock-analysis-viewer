## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNDK_10-K_20260906.txt)

Sandisk (SNDK) is a vertically integrated global semiconductor memory company that designs, manufactures, and sells NAND flash-based storage solutions, including solid-state drives (SSDs), embedded products, removable cards, and wafers. Following its separation from Western Digital in February 2025, Sandisk operates as a standalone public entity with a business model centered on three end markets: Datacenter, Edge, and Consumer. The company’s competitive moat is derived from its vertical integration, which encompasses chip-level design, intellectual property, and front- and back-end manufacturing, allowing it to control costs and drive innovation. Sandisk holds a significant intellectual property portfolio of approximately 8,000 granted patents and 3,000 pending applications, and it maintains a strategic manufacturing partnership with Kioxia through joint ventures that provide access to leading-edge flash memory wafers. Additionally, the company leverages a strong consumer brand and deep relationships with original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and cloud service providers to secure broad routes to market.

Management’s stated priorities focus on leveraging technology innovation and manufacturing leadership to deliver cost-efficient, high-performance storage solutions, particularly for artificial intelligence (AI) workloads. A key strategic shift highlighted in the filing is the transition to "New Business Models" (NBMs), which are long-term agreements with Datacenter and Edge customers that commit both parties to stated volumes over multi-year periods. Management believes these agreements will reduce exposure to industry cyclicality, improve revenue predictability, and support production planning. The company also emphasizes operational excellence, including capital efficiency and supply chain management, while continuing to invest in next-generation flash technologies to meet growing data storage demands.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to the company’s recent financial restructuring and external trade environments. Sandisk recorded a $1.8 billion goodwill impairment charge in fiscal 2025 following its separation from Western Digital, indicating that the estimated carrying value of its reporting unit exceeded its fair value. Furthermore, the company faces significant uncertainty regarding U.S. trade policies, specifically pending investigations under Section 232 and Section 301 that could impact tariff rates. While most products sold in the U.S. are currently exempt from tariffs, the filing warns that additional tariff increases or the loss of exemptions would increase the cost of goods sold, potentially negatively impacting margins and financial performance. The company also notes that while NBMs provide stability, they do not eliminate risks associated with customer demand, market conditions, or operational execution.
