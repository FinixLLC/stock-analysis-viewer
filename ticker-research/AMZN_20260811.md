## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/AMZN_10-K_20260708.txt`), Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors, read and synthesized directly.

What the company does: Amazon operates three reportable segments — North America, International, and Amazon Web Services (AWS) — serving consumers, sellers, developers/enterprises, content creators, and advertisers. Core businesses: online/physical retail stores, third-party seller marketplace (fee-based, Amazon is not seller of record), AWS cloud infrastructure (compute, storage, database, analytics, AI/ML), advertising (sponsored ads, display, video), and content/devices (Kindle, Fire TV, Echo, Ring, Blink, eero, Prime Video).

Moat, as described in the company's own words: Amazon does not claim market dominance in every category but leans on scale advantages: massive fulfillment network, Prime subscription lock-in (fast/free shipping plus content bundling), and AWS's technology breadth (compute, storage, and now a fast-growing AI/chips stack — Trainium, Bedrock foundation-model marketplace, agentic AI tooling). The AWS flywheel — Graviton custom silicon (used by 98% of top 1,000 EC2 customers), Trainium AI chips, and Bedrock's growing foundation-model catalog — is increasingly the articulated moat, alongside the sheer difficulty of replicating AWS's global infrastructure footprint.

Segment mix: AWS is ~21% of total net sales but generated ~60% of total operating income in Q2 2026, underscoring that Amazon is increasingly an AWS-profit story wrapped around a lower-margin retail business. Advertising (+26% YoY per management commentary) is a fast-growing, high-margin adjacent business layered on top of retail traffic.

Management's stated priorities (from Q2 2026 earnings commentary and 10-K): Continued AWS AI infrastructure buildout (Trainium adoption, Bedrock model expansion, agentic AI tooling like AgentCore, Kiro, and Amazon Quick), faster retail delivery (Amazon Now 30-minute delivery, expanding to 250+ cities), and expanding advertising. Management explicitly frames the current capex surge as an AI infrastructure investment cycle.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A):
1. Intense competition across e-commerce, cloud/infrastructure computing, devices, advertising, grocery, healthcare, and logistics — many competitors have greater resources or brand recognition in specific niches.
2. Expansion risk — new products/services/technologies (including AI/ML investment) may not generate expected returns; failed bets could result in written-down investments.
3. International operations risk — political/economic conditions, regulation, tariffs, nationalization risk, IP enforcement variability across the ~140+ countries Amazon and AWS customers operate in.
4. Amazon explicitly cites its own reliance on continued AI/ML investment paying off — a two-sided risk given the current FCF pressure from AI capex.
