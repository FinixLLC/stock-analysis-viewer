## Business Profile

Ross Stores, Inc. (ROST) operates as a value-oriented off-price retailer through two distinct banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model relies on purchasing merchandise opportunistically, often in bulk, and holding it in "packaway" inventory—warehouses where goods are stored for less than six months—before releasing it to stores based on seasonality and assortment needs. This approach allows ROST to offer broad-based assortments of brand-name apparel, home accents, and accessories at compelling values. In fiscal 2025, the company generated $22.75 billion in sales, with a comparable store sales increase of 5%, driven by growth in both customer traffic and basket size. The company maintains a significant physical footprint, ending fiscal 2025 with 2,267 total stores, and plans to open approximately 110 new stores in fiscal 2026, including reaccelerated expansion for the dd’s DISCOUNTS banner.

The filing does not explicitly define a traditional "competitive moat" in strategic terms, but it highlights operational efficiencies and scale as key drivers of performance. ROST cites its ability to leverage overhead expenses, its extensive distribution network (including the recent opening of an eighth distribution center in Arizona), and its strong accounts payable leverage (91% of inventory) as structural advantages. The company also notes that its long-term strategy for store expansion is based on market penetration, local demographics, and the ability to leverage fixed costs, suggesting that scale and supply chain integration are central to its competitive positioning.

Management’s stated priorities for fiscal 2026 and beyond focus on three key initiatives: merchandising, marketing, and store experience. Specifically, the company aims to deliver broad-based assortments timely with more brands at compelling values, advance marketing efforts to strengthen customer awareness and engagement, and make meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. Additionally, management emphasizes a focus on sustainable, profitable growth while monitoring macroeconomic factors such as tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions. The company expects operating income as a percentage of sales to be slightly higher in fiscal 2026 than in fiscal 2025, driven by higher merchandise margins and lower distribution costs, partially offset by increased store-related costs associated with these initiatives.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include legal and regulatory proceedings, particularly class action lawsuits in California alleging violations of wage and hour laws, which remain pending. While management believes these proceedings will not have a material adverse effect on financial condition, they represent a significant legal uncertainty. Additionally, the company highlights the impact of tariff-related costs, which decreased merchandise margin by 20 basis points in fiscal 2025 and resulted in an estimated unfavorable impact of $0.16 per share. Other risks include the uncertainty of the broader macroeconomic environment, including inflation and geopolitical conditions, as well as the ongoing litigation with insurance companies regarding business interruption claims related to the COVID-19 pandemic. The company also notes that its stock performance has lagged behind the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Apparel Retailers Index over the past five years, indicating potential market perception risks relative to peers.
