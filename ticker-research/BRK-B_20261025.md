## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (BRK-B_10-K_20260906.txt)

Berkshire Hathaway operates as a diversified conglomerate whose business model is anchored by a massive insurance float that funds a broad portfolio of operating businesses and equity investments. The company generates earnings through four primary channels: insurance underwriting, insurance investment income, earnings from its operating subsidiaries (including BNSF Railway, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, and various manufacturing, service, and retailing entities), and investment gains or losses. The insurance segment, comprising GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group, and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group, serves as the core engine, writing primary and reinsurance policies for property, casualty, life, and health risks. This structure allows the company to leverage time-value-of-money concepts to earn underwriting profits while deploying the resulting capital into interest-bearing investments and equity securities.

The company’s competitive moat is evidenced by its scale and the exceptional underwriting results reported over the past three years, which management notes were superior to longer-term historical averages. GEICO maintains a low-cost structure through direct response methods, achieving expense ratios as low as 9.7% in 2024, while the broader insurance group generated pre-tax underwriting earnings of $9.5 billion in 2025. Furthermore, the company possesses significant pricing power and operational efficiency in its industrial segments; for instance, BNSF increased earnings in 2025 primarily through improved operating efficiencies and lower expenses, and the manufacturing and service segments have shown consistent earnings growth. The ability to maintain underwriting profitability across diverse lines, even while exiting unprofitable lines at subsidiaries like GUARD, underscores a disciplined approach to risk selection and cost control that competitors may struggle to replicate at this scale.

Management’s stated priorities, as inferred from the MD&A, focus on generating long-term pre-tax underwriting earnings in all business categories, with the explicit exception of retroactive reinsurance and periodic payment annuity businesses, which are in run-off. The company prioritizes operational efficiency, as seen in BNSF’s focus on lower operating expenses and GEICO’s management of advertising and policy acquisition costs. Additionally, management emphasizes the importance of time-value-of-money concepts in premium setting and maintains a stance that investment gains and losses are "generally meaningless" for evaluating periodic economic performance, suggesting a priority on underlying operational cash flows and underwriting discipline over market-driven valuation fluctuations.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the volatility in periodic earnings caused by significant property catastrophe loss events, such as the Southern California wildfires in 2025 and Hurricanes Helene and Milton in 2024. Management explicitly states that it is currently unable to reliably predict the ultimate impact of ongoing macroeconomic and geopolitical conflicts, including international trade policies and tariffs, on product availability, supply chain costs, and customer demand. Other significant risks include rising claim cost trends, unfavorable social inflation trends affecting liability coverages (such as jury awards and litigation costs), and the inherent volatility of investment gains and losses driven by market prices and foreign currency exchange rates. The company also notes that changes in estimates for unpaid losses and loss adjustment expenses can significantly affect periodic results, highlighting the uncertainty in long-tail liability reserves.
