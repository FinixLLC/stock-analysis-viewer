## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (DIS_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Walt Disney Company operates as a diversified worldwide entertainment enterprise structured around three primary segments: Entertainment, Sports, and Experiences. Its core business model relies on the production of original content—spanning film, episodic television, and live events—under prominent brands such as Disney, Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars, and National Geographic, which is then distributed across multiple channels to maximize revenue. The Entertainment segment, which generated $42.5 billion in fiscal 2025, monetizes this intellectual property through a multi-window strategy that includes linear cable networks (earning affiliate fees and advertising), direct-to-consumer streaming platforms like Disney+ and Hulu (earning subscription and advertising fees), and content sales/licensing (earning revenue from theatrical distribution, TV/VOD licensing, and home entertainment). The Sports segment generates revenue primarily through affiliate and subscription fees, advertising, and pay-per-view, while the Experiences segment derives income from theme park admissions, resort operations, cruise vacations, and consumer products licensing.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its extensive library of high-value intellectual property and its integrated distribution capabilities. The filing highlights a content library spanning approximately 100 years, including over 5,300 live-action and 460 animated film titles, which provides a durable asset base for both linear and streaming distribution. Furthermore, Disney maintains full production and distribution rights to most of its IP, allowing it to control the value chain from creation to consumer. This vertical integration is reinforced by its ownership of major domestic broadcast assets, including the ABC Network and eight owned television stations that reach nearly 100% of U.S. households, as well as a global network of approximately 180 international channels. The company also leverages strategic equity investments, such as its 50% stake in A+E Global Media and its 37% interest in the Star India joint venture, to extend its reach and content offerings.

Management’s stated priorities, as inferred from the financial results and operational descriptions, focus on growing the Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) business and optimizing the portfolio of linear assets. The filing notes that DTC operating income grew significantly, driven by higher subscription revenue, while the company continues to produce a significant number of episodic and film titles for initial distribution on its own platforms. Additionally, management is actively restructuring its linear and live TV offerings, evidenced by the combination of Hulu Live TV assets with FuboTV to form a new joint venture in which Disney holds a 70% interest. The company also emphasizes cost discipline, as seen in the reduction of restructuring and impairment charges in fiscal 2025 compared to the prior year, and continues to invest in marketing to drive consumer awareness for theatrical releases and streaming services.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the declining performance of the linear networks business, where affiliate fees and advertising revenues decreased due to fewer subscribers and lower viewership. The company also faces execution risks associated with its strategic shifts, such as the Star India transaction, which resulted in significant impairment charges and a loss in the current year, and the integration of the Hulu Live TV and FuboTV assets. Furthermore, the business is exposed to competitive pressures in the programming market, where the ability to charge for networks depends on the quality of programming and the competitive landscape. The filing also notes risks related to the high costs of content production and marketing, which can result in losses on theatrical distribution, as well as the impact of inflation on the Experiences segment. Finally, the company faces regulatory and tax-related uncertainties, as illustrated by the significant non-cash tax benefit resulting from a change in Hulu’s U.S. income tax classification, which heavily influenced the net income results for fiscal 2025.
