## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to consolidate prestige, mass, and salon products that were previously sold through distinct channels. The company’s business model centers on a "beauty enthusiast" consumer, offering a differentiated assortment of approximately 30,000 products from roughly 600 brands across all price points, alongside full-service salon capabilities in its stores. Ulta leverages an omnichannel strategy that integrates over 1,500 U.S. locations with digital platforms, utilizing diverse fulfillment options such as buy-online-pickup-in-store and same-day delivery. Internationally, the company expands through its subsidiary Space NK in the U.K. and Ireland, a joint venture in Mexico, and a franchise in the Middle East.

The company’s competitive moat is built on several strategic differentiators, primarily its best-in-class loyalty program, Ulta Beauty Rewards, which has over 46 million members and accounts for approximately 95% of total sales. This program provides deep consumer data that drives personalization and marketing efficiency. Additionally, Ulta maintains a strong position through its exclusive and private-label offerings, which represented about 11% of net sales in fiscal 2025, and its ability to offer a seamless, welcoming guest experience that fosters high engagement. The company also benefits from a high-performing real estate portfolio, with a long-term goal of expanding to more than 1,800 freestanding stores.

Management’s stated priorities are outlined in the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan, which focuses on three areas: driving core business growth through operational excellence and digital acceleration; scaling new, accretive businesses such as wellness products, the UB Marketplace online platform, and international expansion; and aligning the company’s foundation for future success by optimizing workflows, streamlining costs, and cultivating an associate-centered culture. The company emphasizes maintaining its leadership position through continued innovation, brand building, and leveraging its data to personalize guest experiences.

The filing flags several material risks, including the impact of persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures on consumer spending and profitability. There is also a noted risk regarding the company’s debt structure, as increased borrowings in fiscal 2025 led to a shift from net interest income to net interest expense, with variable interest rates posing a potential cost increase. Furthermore, the company acknowledges that comparable sales have fluctuated in the past and are expected to continue fluctuating due to factors such as general economic conditions, changes in merchandise strategy, and the effectiveness of marketing activities. The filing also notes that the company’s growth is dependent on its ability to execute its strategic priorities and respond timely to changes in consumer preferences and competition.
