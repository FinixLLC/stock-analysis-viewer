## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QLYS_10-K_20260827.txt)

Qualys operates as a leading provider of a cloud-based platform delivering integrated IT, security, and compliance solutions, primarily through its Enterprise TruRisk Platform. The company’s business model is centered on a software-as-a-service (SaaS) approach, where customers pay renewable annual subscription fees to access a suite of "Qualys Cloud Apps." These applications enable organizations to identify and manage IT and operational technology assets, collect and analyze security data, discover and prioritize vulnerabilities, quantify cyber risk, and verify remediation actions across on-premises, endpoint, cloud, container, and mobile environments. Qualys positions its platform as a solution to the fragmentation of disparate security tools, offering a unified view of security and compliance posture that reduces total cost of ownership by eliminating the need for customers to buy and manage hardware. Revenue is recognized ratably over the subscription term, with the majority of invoiced amounts initially recorded as deferred revenue. In 2025, the company generated $669.1 million in revenue, a 10% increase from 2024, driven by both new customer acquisition and expansion from existing customers, with 76% of the revenue increase attributable to existing customers.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its integrated, multi-tenant cloud infrastructure and the extensive scale of its data processing capabilities. Qualys claims to possess one of the largest knowledge bases of vulnerability signatures in the industry, allowing for real-time security updates and continuous visibility through physical and virtual sensors and cloud agents. The platform’s ability to ingest, process, and correlate high volumes of sensor data across millions of devices provides a level of real-time visibility and seamless scaling that is difficult for point-solution competitors to replicate. Furthermore, the company leverages its "TruRisk" scoring capabilities, which quantify cyber risk in financial terms by combining severity, exploitability, asset criticality, and business context, offering a vendor-agnostic approach to risk prioritization. The sticky nature of the subscription model, combined with the platform’s role as a central hub for asset inventory and compliance reporting, creates high switching costs for customers who have integrated Qualys into their core security and compliance workflows.

Management’s stated priorities, as reflected in the MD&A and business descriptions, focus on continuing to invest in research and development to improve existing solutions and develop new capabilities, as well as expanding sales and marketing efforts to support new solutions and drive growth. The company explicitly states its intention to continue devoting resources to R&D and investing in sales and marketing teams, which it expects will increase these expense categories in absolute dollars. Additionally, management emphasizes the importance of maintaining and expanding its shared cloud platform infrastructure to support customer growth. While the filing does not provide a specific list of strategic initiatives beyond these operational investments, the emphasis on "continuously improve our existing solutions as well as develop new solutions and capabilities" suggests a focus on product innovation and platform expansion.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the macroeconomic environment and competitive dynamics. Management notes that uncertainty surrounding inflationary pressure, high interest rates, global market volatility, and geopolitical conflicts could lead to reduced spending on IT security by customers, potentially having a material adverse effect on the business. The company also highlights the risk that new sales personnel may not become productive immediately, which could impact future operating results if hiring does not translate to incremental revenue. Furthermore, the filing notes that the company is subject to income taxes in various jurisdictions and that the release of valuation allowances on deferred tax assets is subject to achieving sustained profitability, indicating that tax efficiency remains a variable factor. While the provided excerpts do not include the full "Risk Factors" section, the MD&A explicitly warns that macroeconomic factors and the timing of sales force productivity are key uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ from expectations.
