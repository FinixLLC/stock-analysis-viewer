## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (META_10-K_20260906.txt)

Meta operates as a technology company with a dual-segment structure, comprising the Family of Apps (FoA) and Reality Labs (RL). Its core business model is an advertising-driven ecosystem where it generates substantially all of its revenue by selling ad placements to marketers across its social platforms, including Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, and Threads. This is supported by a self-service ad platform and a global sales force. The second segment, Reality Labs, generates revenue from the sale of consumer hardware, software, and content, specifically focusing on virtual reality (VR) devices like Meta Quest, augmented reality (AR) initiatives, and AI glasses such as Ray-Ban Meta. While FoA is highly profitable, RL operates at a significant loss, with management explicitly stating that RL’s ability to continue is dependent on the profits generated by the FoA segment.

The company’s competitive moat is built on a massive user base, evidenced by 3.58 billion daily active people (DAP) in 2025, and a proprietary technical infrastructure that includes self-designed data centers and key portions of its technical stack. Meta leverages this scale to power AI-driven discovery engines and ad targeting systems, which it claims improve engagement and monetization. Additionally, the company maintains a history of open-sourcing AI models, such as Llama, to foster ecosystem innovation, while simultaneously developing closed models and proprietary hardware interfaces, including neural band controls for AR glasses.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 focus on several key investment areas: artificial intelligence (including generative AI and superintelligence), Reels and the discovery engine, wearables, product monetization, youth engagement, platform integrity, and infrastructure capacity. The company emphasizes a long-term vision of building the "next computing platform" through immersive experiences, with approximately 70% of Reality Labs operating expenses directed toward wearables and 30% toward VR. Management also highlights a commitment to operating efficiently while making significant capital expenditures, which reached $72.22 billion in 2025, largely driven by AI infrastructure needs.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and competitive pressures that impact advertising revenue. Meta explicitly states that legislative and regulatory developments, such as the GDPR, ePrivacy Directive, and U.S. state privacy laws, have limited its ability to use data signals for ad targeting and measurement, a trend it expects to continue adversely impacting revenue. The company notes that changes by third-party platforms, such as Apple’s iOS updates, have reduced the effectiveness of ad targeting, forcing Meta to invest in privacy-enhancing technologies. Furthermore, the filing highlights intense competition for user engagement, particularly among younger demographics, and notes that while Reels is growing, it monetizes at a lower rate than Feed and Stories. Finally, Meta identifies macroeconomic factors, including inflation, high interest rates, and geopolitical uncertainty, as ongoing pressures on advertiser spending and user growth in certain regions.
