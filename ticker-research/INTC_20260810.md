## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/INTC_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2025-12-27 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business "Overview" and "Our Strategy") source text.

What the company does: Intel is "a global designer and manufacturer of semiconductor products" — CPUs and other semiconductor solutions incorporated into computing and related end products, sold to OEMs, ODMs, CSPs (cloud service providers), and other manufacturers/service providers. Critically, Intel explicitly identifies itself as a U.S.-based integrated design manufacturer (IDM) — designing its own chips *and* developing leading-edge process nodes/advanced packaging *and* predominantly manufacturing its own products at its own fabs (many in the U.S.) — a structurally different model from fabless peers like Qualcomm, Marvell or Micron's competitors. The company also offers third-party foundry services to external customers, explicitly aiming "to establish this business as a leading external foundry."

Self-described strategic significance: the 10-K states Intel is "the only company undertaking research, design and development of leading-edge and next generation semiconductor manufacturing technologies and high-volume manufacturing of logic semiconductors utilizing leading-edge nodes in the U.S., making us a strategically important company from both a national economic and national security perspective" — a notable, deliberate framing that ties Intel's business case directly to U.S. industrial-policy and national-security considerations (relevant given CHIPS-Act-era government support dynamics).

Management's four core strategic priorities (from Item 1 "Our Strategy," in the company's own stated order):
1. Transforming our culture to become an engineering-focused, customer-centric organization prioritizing "decisive actions, disciplined execution and strong financial management."
2. Revitalizing the x86 ecosystem — adapting x86 products for current/next-gen AI workloads, leveraging U.S.-based leading-edge process R&D and manufacturing/advanced packaging.
3. Growing the external foundry business — becoming a trusted third-party foundry partner to finance continued investment in capital-intensive, U.S.-based leading-edge R&D/manufacturing.
4. Expanding market opportunities via purpose-built ASICs and GPUs for the diversifying set of AI-driven compute workloads.

Segment/product mix: CCG (Client Computing Group — PCs/consumer) and DCAI (Data Center and AI) are the two named product segments referenced in the MD&A revenue discussion; the 10-K also references Intel Foundry as a distinct, growing external-facing business line. The FY2025 mix showed CCG softening (-3%, supply-constrained) while DCAI grew modestly (+5%, also supply-constrained in Q4).

Moat, as described in the company's own words: the "only" U.S.-based leading-edge logic-semiconductor manufacturer claim (above) is Intel's central self-differentiation point — a moat built on sovereign/geographic manufacturing capability and integration across design+process+packaging, rather than pure product-performance leadership (where the company's own risk factors, below, acknowledge it is losing ground to AMD and NVIDIA in key categories).

Risks the company itself flags as most material (inferred from the "AI"-graph EVCDM competitive edges and the MD&A supply-constraint discussion, since a full bulleted Item 1A summary was not captured in this extraction): wafer-fabrication supply constraints on its own internal process nodes (Intel 7, Intel 3) limiting its ability to meet its own customer demand — an unusual, explicitly disclosed risk in which Intel's manufacturing capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint; and intensifying competitive pressure from AMD (CPU/APU), NVIDIA (data-center GPU/AI), and other GPU/DPU/ASIC providers, corroborated directly by the EVCDM graph edges below.
