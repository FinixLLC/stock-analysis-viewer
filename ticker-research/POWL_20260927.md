## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (POWL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Powell Industries, Inc. operates as a developer, designer, manufacturer, and service provider of custom-engineered equipment and systems that distribute, control, and monitor electrical energy flow while protecting motors, transformers, and other powered equipment. The company’s business model is centered on project-based, engineered-to-order solutions sold primarily through competitive bids to end-users or Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) firms. Powell serves a diversified set of markets, with the largest revenue contributions coming from oil and gas (37% of Fiscal 2025 revenue), electric utilities (25%), commercial and other industrial sectors (16%), and petrochemicals (14%). The company’s products include integrated power control room substations, switchgear, circuit breakers, and motor control centers, designed for voltages ranging from 480 to 38,000 volts. Powell differentiates itself through its expertise in systems integration, project management, and technical support, aiming to provide a single, custom-engineered deliverable that meets specific customer specifications.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its integration capabilities, technical and project management acumen, and application engineering expertise, which management believes provide a sustainable competitive advantage against both multinational competitors (such as ABB, Eaton, Schneider, and Siemens) and smaller regional firms. Powell emphasizes that its growth depends primarily on product quality and customer relationships rather than patent protection, though it does hold patents and trade secrets related to manufacturing processes and engineering designs. The acquisition of Remsdaq Limited in August 2025 further strengthens this position by adding SCADA Remote Terminal Unit technology, creating synergies between Powell’s hardware and detection sensors and Remsdaq’s automation capabilities to meet growing demand for sophisticated utility solutions.

Management’s stated priorities focus on end-market diversification, specifically expanding into electric utility distribution substations and power generation, as well as growing in commercial and industrial sectors like data centers and mining. The company is actively investing in capacity expansion, including a $12.4 million investment to increase production capacity at its Jacintoport facility by 62% and completing an expansion at its Houston electrical products facility. Additionally, management prioritizes operational efficiency, supply chain resilience, and capital allocation that balances organic growth, inorganic opportunities, and shareholder returns. The company is also expanding into clean energy applications, such as hydrogen production, carbon capture, and sustainable aviation fuels, in response to market demand.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the cyclical and capital-intensive nature of its markets, which are driven by global economic conditions, geopolitical events, and customer capital investment decisions. Revenue volatility is a significant risk due to the non-recurring nature of large projects and the potential for material fluctuations in revenue and gross profit based on the timing of project awards. Supply chain disruptions, particularly regarding raw materials like steel, copper, and aluminum, and engineered components, pose risks to production costs and delivery schedules, with some components relying on limited or single suppliers. The company also faces risks from inflation, labor availability, and changes in U.S. global trade policy, including tariffs, which could increase costs and negatively impact margins. Furthermore, the company notes that a significant portion of its business comes from repeat customers and EPC firms, meaning the loss of a major customer or financial distress at a customer could adversely affect its business.
