## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates through a dual-engine business model comprising the Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL) segments, supplemented by a smaller Qualcomm Strategic Investments (QSI) segment. QCT generates the majority of revenue by selling integrated circuit products, including Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, modems, and radio frequency components, to customers in the handset, automotive, and IoT markets. QTL generates high-margin revenue by licensing its intellectual property portfolio, specifically patent rights essential to wireless technology, to device manufacturers. In fiscal 2025, total revenues increased 14% to $44.3 billion, driven primarily by a 16% increase in QCT revenues from higher handset, IoT, and automotive sales, while QTL revenues remained flat. The company’s competitive position relies on its leadership in technology development, standardization, and intellectual property creation, with management explicitly prioritizing the expansion of on-device AI and high-performance, low-power computing beyond mobile handsets into automotive and IoT applications.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the impact of global trade policies, including tariffs and related actions by the U.S., China, and other countries, which may negatively impact demand, pricing, and costs. Additionally, the company faces significant tax-related risks stemming from the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBB) enacted in July 2025. This legislation subjects Qualcomm to the corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) beginning in fiscal 2026, leading management to conclude that substantially all existing federal deferred tax assets will not be realized. Consequently, the company recorded a $5.7 billion charge to income tax expense to establish a valuation allowance, which contributed to a 45% decrease in net income to $5.5 billion despite revenue growth. Other flagged risks include seasonality in consumer wireless device demand, the inherent uncertainty of legal and regulatory proceedings, and the potential for inventory write-downs if customer demand estimates prove overly optimistic.
