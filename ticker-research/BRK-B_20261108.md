Berkshire Hathaway operates as a diversified conglomerate whose business model is anchored by a massive insurance float, which funds a broad portfolio of operating subsidiaries and equity investments. The company’s primary revenue drivers include insurance underwriting and investment income, supplemented by significant earnings from its rail (BNSF), energy (Berkshire Hathaway Energy), and manufacturing, service, and retailing segments. The insurance division, comprising GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group, and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group, writes property and casualty, life, and health policies, generating substantial cash flow that is then deployed into short-term investments like U.S. Treasury Bills and long-term equity positions. While management explicitly states that realized and unrealized investment gains and losses are "generally meaningless" for evaluating periodic economic performance due to their volatility, these items remain a dominant component of reported net earnings, as evidenced by the $30.7 billion in investment gains in 2025 versus operating earnings of approximately $28 billion across all other segments.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale in the insurance industry, particularly through GEICO’s direct-response distribution model which allows for lower underwriting expenses, and its ability to retain large amounts of capital for investment. GEICO’s low expense ratio, which was 12.4% in 2025, provides a structural cost advantage over competitors who rely on traditional agency networks. Furthermore, the sheer size of the insurance float allows Berkshire to invest in a diversified portfolio of operating businesses and equities, creating a self-reinforcing cycle where underwriting profits fund investments, and investment income supports the company’s overall financial strength. The filing notes that overall underwriting results over the past three years have been "exceptional compared to results over longer periods," suggesting a sustained ability to price risk competitively while maintaining profitability.

Management’s stated priorities, as inferred from the MD&A, focus on generating long-term pre-tax underwriting earnings in all business categories except for specific run-off lines like retroactive reinsurance and periodic payment annuities. The company emphasizes the importance of time-value-of-money concepts in premium setting and seeks to improve operating efficiencies, as seen in BNSF’s 2025 earnings increase driven by lower operating expenses and improved efficiencies. Additionally, management prioritizes capital allocation, evidenced by the large capital distributions to Berkshire at the end of 2024, which subsequently impacted 2025 insurance investment income. The filing also highlights a strategic focus on exiting unprofitable lines of business, such as GUARD’s reduction in volume across personal lines and workers compensation, to tighten underwriting standards and improve overall profitability.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the inherent volatility of insurance underwriting results due to the timing and magnitude of significant property catastrophe loss events, such as the Southern California wildfires in 2025 and Hurricanes Helene and Milton in 2024. The company also identifies rising claim cost trends, particularly in bodily injury coverages where severities increased by 12-14% in 2025, and unfavorable social inflation trends affecting liability coverages. Macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties, including international trade policies and tariffs, are cited as potential drivers of adverse consequences on operating businesses and equity investments, with the company noting it is currently unable to reliably predict the ultimate impact. Additionally, the filing highlights the risk of significant volatility in periodic earnings due to changes in market prices of equity securities and foreign currency exchange rates, as well as the ongoing impact of lower interest rates on insurance investment income.
