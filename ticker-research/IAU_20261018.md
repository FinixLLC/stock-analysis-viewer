## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (IAU_10-K_20260906.txt)

The iShares Gold Trust (IAU) is a passive, grantor trust formed under New York law that serves as a simple and cost-effective vehicle for investors to gain exposure to the price of gold without the logistical burdens of physical ownership. The Trust’s business model is strictly limited to three activities: issuing shares (in blocks called Baskets) in exchange for gold deposited by Authorized Participants, selling gold to cover the Sponsor’s fees and other liabilities, and redeeming Baskets by delivering gold back to the participants. It does not actively manage its assets, lend its gold, or engage in any trading strategies to profit from or hedge against gold price movements. The Trust’s assets consist primarily of physical gold bullion held by JPMorgan Chase Bank N.A. as custodian, with The Bank of New York Mellon serving as the trustee. The Sponsor, iShares Delaware Trust Sponsor LLC (a subsidiary of BlackRock), earns a fee of 0.25% of the Trust’s net asset value annually, which is its only ordinary recurring expense; in exchange, the Sponsor assumes most administrative costs, including trustee and custodian fees, listing fees, and audit costs.

The Trust’s competitive position relies on its status as a large, liquid, and accessible exchange-traded product listed on NYSE Arca. Its primary advantage is the removal of the high costs and complexities associated with physical gold investment—such as assay, transportation, warehousing, and insurance—by dispersing these expenses across a large base of shareholders. The Trust offers a transparent structure where the Net Asset Value (NAV) is directly tied to the LBMA Gold Price, providing investors with a straightforward alternative to physical bullion. While the filing does not explicitly define a traditional "moat," the scale of the Trust (with NAV increasing to over $68 billion in 2025) and the established arbitrage mechanism between the secondary market and the physical gold market help maintain liquidity and price efficiency.

Management’s stated priorities, as reflected in the Sponsor’s operations, focus on maintaining the Trust’s role as a passive vehicle that accurately reflects the performance of gold prices. The Sponsor’s primary operational duty is to ensure the Trust’s NAV is calculated correctly using the LBMA Gold Price and to manage the creation and redemption of Baskets to facilitate market liquidity. The filing notes that the Sponsor believes arbitrage opportunities provide a mechanism to mitigate premiums or discounts to NAV, particularly during periods when trading hours for gold markets and NYSE Arca do not align. There is no indication of strategic expansion or active management initiatives; the priority remains the faithful tracking of gold prices less expenses.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are inherent to the structure of a passive gold trust. First, the Trust is not registered as an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940, meaning shareholders do not have the regulatory protections afforded to investors in registered funds, such as limits on affiliate transactions or prohibitions on suspension of redemptions. Second, the Trust is not a commodity pool under the Commodity Exchange Act, so it is not subject to CFTC registration or the associated disclosure requirements. Third, the Trust’s value is entirely dependent on the price of gold, and while the Custodian is responsible for safekeeping, its liability is limited to the value of gold lost due to negligence, fraud, or willful default, with broad exclusions for acts of God, terrorism, or government actions. Additionally, the filing highlights that Shares may trade at a premium or discount to NAV due to market supply and demand, particularly during hours when major gold markets are closed but NYSE Arca is open. Finally, the Trust’s liquidity is derived solely from the sale of gold, and while the Sponsor assumes most expenses, any unassumed costs must be covered by selling gold, which can affect the Trust’s holdings.
