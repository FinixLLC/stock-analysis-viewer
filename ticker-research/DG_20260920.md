## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (DG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Dollar General is the largest discount retailer in the United States by store count, operating 20,959 locations across 48 U.S. states and Mexico as of February 2026. The company’s business model centers on providing value-conscious consumers, particularly those with low or fixed incomes, with everyday necessities and general merchandise at low prices, typically $10 or less, through a network of convenient, small-box stores. Approximately 80% of these stores are located in towns with populations of 20,000 or fewer, positioning the company to serve markets often underserved by larger retailers. The merchandise mix is heavily weighted toward consumables, which accounted for 82.0% of net sales in fiscal 2025, supplemented by seasonal items, home products, and apparel. The company sources both national brands and private-label goods, leveraging a limited assortment per category to maintain strong purchasing power and low average costs.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its extensive geographic footprint, with approximately 75% of the U.S. population living within five miles of a store, and its low-cost operating structure. This model relies on a "no frills" store format, limited inventory depth to drive volume, and a distribution network supported by a private fleet and third-party logistics. Management asserts that this combination of value and convenience differentiates Dollar General from mass merchants, grocery stores, and online retailers, allowing it to maintain positive same-store sales growth in most years since 1990. The company also cites its ability to serve rural and suburban communities where shopping alternatives are limited as a key differentiator.

Management’s stated long-term operating priorities are fourfold: driving profitable sales growth, capturing growth opportunities, enhancing the company’s position as a low-cost operator, and investing in team development. To achieve these goals, the company is executing several strategic initiatives, including the rollout of "Project Elevate" and "Project Renovate" to remodel and optimize existing stores, and the expansion of digital tools such as the Dollar General app and delivery services to increase customer traffic and average transaction amounts. The company also continues to expand its store base, planning for approximately 450 new U.S. stores and 10 in Mexico in 2026, while pausing the expansion of its pOpshelf concept to evaluate its strategy. Additionally, management focuses on margin enhancement through inventory shrink and damage reduction, pricing optimization, and global sourcing.

The filing identifies several material risks, with significant emphasis on the macroeconomic environment and trade policy. The company notes that its core customers are highly sensitive to inflation, unemployment, and changes in government assistance programs, which can constrain spending. A particularly highlighted risk is the uncertainty surrounding U.S. tariffs; while tariff rates did not materially impact financial results in 2025, management warns that current and future tariffs could significantly impact business operations and customer budgets, especially following the Supreme Court’s invalidation of tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. Other flagged risks include rising labor costs due to minimum wage increases and labor availability, increased occupancy and new store opening costs driven by inflation and interest rates, and intense competition from well-resourced rivals such as Walmart, Family Dollar, and Dollar Tree. The company also acknowledges risks related to inventory shrink and damages, although it reports significant improvement in shrink levels in 2025.
