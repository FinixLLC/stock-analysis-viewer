What the company does: Alphabet is a collection of businesses, the largest of which is Google. Google is reported in two segments — Google Services (Search, YouTube, and the broader consumer/advertising ecosystem) and Google Cloud — with all non-Google businesses reported collectively as Other Bets. Centralized AI research and frontier-model development is reported separately as "Alphabet-level activities." More than 70% of total revenue in 2025 came from online advertising, per the company's own 10-K disclosure — advertising remains the financial core of the business despite the AI/Cloud growth narrative dominating recent headlines.

AI strategy, in the company's own words: Alphabet describes AI as "a profound platform shift" and states its approach is a "full-stack" one spanning AI-optimized infrastructure, world-class research (frontier models), and products/platforms distributing AI to billions of users, developers, and enterprises. Key infrastructure differentiator: custom-built Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), with "Ironwood" cited as the seventh-generation TPU — a genuine hardware moat distinct from reliance on third-party GPU supply alone (though the company also uses GPUs). Gemini 3 is described as the company's most capable model yet, with enhanced reasoning and agentic/generative-coding capabilities. R&D investment context: more than $200B invested in R&D over the last five years.

Moat, as described/evidenced: (1) full-stack AI infrastructure including proprietary TPU silicon, reducing dependence on external GPU supply relative to some competitors; (2) massive existing distribution surface (Search, YouTube, Android/Chrome ecosystem) to deploy AI products at scale — Gemini App already at 950M MAU; (3) Google Cloud's 82% YoY growth (most recent quarter) suggests genuine, accelerating enterprise AI monetization, not just consumer-scale vanity metrics; (4) low leverage (D/E 0.12) and large absolute FCF generation give Alphabet unusual flexibility to fund the ~$200B 2026 capex plan largely from its own balance sheet.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, company's own framing):
1. Advertising revenue concentration — explicit disclosure that >70% of 2025 revenue came from online advertising, and that AI is "quickly reshaping the advertising industry," with no assurance Alphabet "will adapt effectively and competitively" to that shift. Partners/advertisers can terminate contracts at any time; ad-blocking and privacy-technology trends are cited as ongoing threats to the core ad business.
2. Macroeconomic sensitivity of ad spend — advertiser expenditure "tends to correlate with overall economic conditions," a direct, disclosed cyclicality risk to the dominant revenue line.
3. Investment risk from the scale of new business/AI spending — the company explicitly flags that its "increasing investment in new businesses, products, services, and technologies is inherently risky" and could "divert management attention and harm" financial results — directly relevant given the newly raised $195-205B 2026 capex commitment.
4. Regulatory/antitrust risk is a major, ongoing theme (see Current News below) though the specific Item 1A risk-factor language on this point was not fully extracted in this pass — flagging as a partial gap; the live-search findings below substantiate this as a live, material risk regardless.
