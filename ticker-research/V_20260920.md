## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through a proprietary transaction processing network known as VisaNet. Its business model is based on the "four-party structure," where Visa provides authorization, clearing, and settlement services among consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers, without acting as a financial institution itself. Visa does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk; instead, it earns revenue through service fees, data processing fees, international transaction fees, and other value-added services. The company has expanded its traditional card-based model to include digital banks, fintechs, and governments, leveraging a "Visa as a Service" stack that offers modular capabilities such as tokenization, risk management, and API access to enable various payment form factors across more than 200 countries.

Visa’s competitive moat is anchored in its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by processing 329 billion transactions in fiscal 2025 and maintaining nearly 5 billion payment credentials available at over 175 million merchant locations. The company differentiates itself through its proprietary infrastructure, including Visa Token Service (VTS) for security and fraud mitigation, and its "network of networks" strategy, which positions Visa as a single connection point for money movement regardless of the originating or receiving network. Additionally, Visa leverages its brand strength through high-profile sponsorships and its dominance in cross-border payments, where it offers differentiated risk management and currency settlement capabilities.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, the company aims to strengthen its impact in card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based areas such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion in underserved consumer spend. Key initiatives include expanding "Tap to Everything" use cases, enhancing token technology for agentic commerce, and improving cross-border performance. In CMS, Visa seeks to drive penetration in commercial payments and money movement for businesses and governments. Finally, in VAS, the company focuses on delivering innovative services, such as AI-driven fraud protection (exemplified by the acquisition of Featurespace), to deepen client partnerships.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are primarily legal and regulatory in nature, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a U.S. litigation escrow account during fiscal 2025 to address these claims. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 2025. This litigation is significant enough that it influences corporate governance, as the board considers its resolution when deciding on successive exchange offers for Class B common stock. Additionally, the filing notes that GAAP operating expenses increased significantly due to higher litigation provisions and personnel costs, highlighting the financial volatility associated with these legal matters.
