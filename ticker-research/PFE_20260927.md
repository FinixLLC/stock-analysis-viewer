## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PFE_10-K_20260827.txt)

Pfizer Inc. operates as a research-based, global biopharmaceutical company focused on the discovery, development, manufacture, marketing, sale, and distribution of biopharmaceutical products worldwide. The company’s business model relies heavily on the manufacture and sale of branded medicines and vaccines, with revenues driven by a portfolio that includes key products such as Eliquis, Vyndaqel, Padcev, Lorbrena, and Abrysvo, alongside legacy COVID-19 products like Comirnaty and Paxlovid. Pfizer manages its commercial operations through a global structure that, as of 2025, consisted of three operating segments—Biopharma, PC1, and Pfizer Ignite—with Biopharma being the only reportable segment. To support long-term growth, the company actively pursues business development initiatives, including the acquisitions of Seagen and Metsera, and seeks to expand its portfolio through internal R&D, collaborations, and licensing agreements.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its intellectual property rights, patent litigation defense capabilities, and its extensive research and development pipeline. Pfizer emphasizes that innovation, drug discovery, and development are critical to its success, leveraging advances in biological science and platform technologies to address unmet patient needs. The company views its R&D organization and commercial execution as central to its strategy, aiming to translate advanced science into impactful therapies while continuously evaluating how to collaborate with patients, physicians, and payors to support access to affordable healthcare.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 are to maximize the value of key transactions, deliver on critical R&D milestones, invest to maximize post-2028 growth, and scale AI across the business. To achieve these goals, Pfizer is implementing digital enablement strategies, including expanding automation, data-driven decision-making, and enterprise AI solutions to strengthen productivity and accelerate innovation. The company is also executing multi-year cost realignment and manufacturing optimization programs to reduce costs of goods sold and realign its cost base with longer-term revenue expectations, driven in part by enhanced digital enablement and process simplification.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the significant reduction of revenue expected from patent-based or regulatory exclusivity expiries between 2026 and 2030, with a specific impact of $1.5 billion anticipated in 2026. Pfizer faces increasing pricing and access pressures globally, including the implementation of the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) in the U.S., which has already negatively impacted 2025 revenues by approximately $1 billion due to Medicare Part D redesign and Maximum Fair Price selections for products like Eliquis, Ibrance, Xtandi, and Xeljanz. Additionally, the company highlights the inherent risks associated with R&D, including the uncertainty of clinical trial success, regulatory approval, and commercial viability, as well as the potential adverse effects of the loss, expiration, or invalidation of intellectual property rights.
