## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically serving as an independent, buy-side demand-side platform (DSP) that empowers advertising agencies and advertisers to create, manage, and optimize digital campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices. The company’s business model is built on a self-service platform supported by enterprise APIs, allowing clients to customize functionality and integrate proprietary data. TTD generates revenue primarily by charging a platform fee, which is generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform for advertising inventory, value-added services, and data. Unlike competitors who may serve both buyers and sellers, TTD focuses exclusively on the buy side, positioning itself as an objective referee in the marketplace to avoid conflicts of interest. This approach has fostered long-term relationships, evidenced by a customer retention rate exceeding 95% for over a decade, with revenue derived substantially from ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic orders.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its independence, data-driven architecture, and AI capabilities. By aligning solely with buyers, TTD avoids the inherent conflicts of interest associated with platforms that also sell inventory, which management argues allows for greater trust and the ingestion of sensitive first-party client data. The platform utilizes a bid-factor-based architecture that offers high "expressiveness," enabling precise targeting through complex permutations of parameters. Additionally, TTD leverages a rich ecosystem of third-party data partners and proprietary AI tools, such as "Koa," its AI co-pilot, to automate optimization, scoring, and decisioning. The company emphasizes that its open platform approach, which allows clients to build custom features via APIs, and its access to premium omnichannel inventory—including a growing focus on Connected TV (CTV)—create a scalable, sticky business model that competitors with conflicting incentives may struggle to replicate.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing share of wallet from existing clients, expanding the client base into the mid-market, and continuing innovation in technology, data, and measurement. Key strategic initiatives include the global expansion of the platform, particularly in Europe and Asia, and the deepening of omnichannel capabilities, with a specific emphasis on CTV and other video formats. The company is heavily investing in AI to drive automation and personalization, viewing these advancements as fundamental to the future of programmatic advertising. Furthermore, management prioritizes the integration of high-quality, privacy-compliant data and advanced measurement tools to help clients demonstrate value in a fragmented media landscape. The filing notes that while the company anticipates continued growth in operating expenses to support these investments in infrastructure, AI, and sales, it believes its operating leverage will allow it to support long-term growth profitably.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include dependence on the overall growth of the programmatic advertising market and the ability to retain and grow spend from existing clients in a highly competitive environment. TTD acknowledges that its revenue is tied to the total addressable market for digital advertising and that any slowing in the adoption of programmatic methods could negatively impact performance. Additionally, the company highlights macroeconomic uncertainty, including changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments, which could decrease demand for advertising services and disrupt supply chains. Specific operational risks include the rising costs of hosting infrastructure and the need to continuously innovate to maintain differentiation against competitors who may offer artificially low prices enabled by conflicts of interest. Finally, the filing notes that the company’s international expansion efforts, while seen as a significant opportunity, may negatively impact near-term profitability due to increased investments in sales, marketing, and product development in newer markets.
