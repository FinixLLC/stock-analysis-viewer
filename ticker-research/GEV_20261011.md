## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GEV_10-K_20260906.txt)

GE Vernova is a global leader in the electric power industry, spun off from General Electric Company in April 2024, that designs, manufactures, and services technologies to generate, transfer, orchestrate, convert, and store electricity. The company operates through three primary segments—Power, Wind, and Electrification—serving utilities, developers, governments, and industrial customers worldwide. Its business model relies on a diversified portfolio of equipment and services, including gas and steam turbines, nuclear and hydro solutions, onshore and offshore wind turbines, and grid infrastructure such as transformers and high-voltage direct current transmission systems. A significant portion of the company’s revenue and strategic focus is derived from servicing its existing installed base, which generates approximately 25% of the world’s electricity, alongside the sale of new technologies to meet growing demand for reliable, affordable, and sustainable power.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its unique scope and scale of integrated solutions, a strong history of innovation, and its extensive installed base. GE Vernova differentiates itself by offering a broad array of advanced technologies for an intelligent, sustainable power system, allowing it to serve customers across the entire energy value chain. The company competes based on product performance, quality, branding, service, and price against major global rivals such as Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Power, Vestas, and Hitachi Energy. Management notes that the ability to reduce cycle times, ensure available capacity, and maintain underwriting discipline are critical to remaining competitive in a market where demand is exceeding available capacity.

Management’s stated priorities focus on delivering global sustainability by enabling electrification and decarbonization, while simultaneously strengthening profitability and stockholder returns. Key strategic initiatives include improving margins and lowering risk through better underwriting, streamlining the product portfolio to focus on core "workhorse" products to improve cost and quality, and using lean methodologies to enhance cost structure and productivity. The company aims to allocate capital to generate cash flow for core business investments, targeted mergers and acquisitions, and returning at least one-third of cash generation to stockholders. Additionally, management prioritizes maintaining strong relationships with leading utilities and governments, investing in new technologies such as small modular reactors and direct air capture, and adapting to market conditions through pricing adjustments and supply chain collaboration.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the execution of its business strategy in a volatile regulatory and geopolitical environment. Significant risks include the impact of global tariffs, which cost the company approximately $250 million in 2025, and the potential for future costs to vary significantly based on scope and duration. In the Wind segment, the company faces pressure from project cost overruns and execution timelines, exacerbated by U.S. government actions such as the pause on large-scale offshore wind project leases, which directly impacted the Vineyard Wind project. The Onshore Wind business faces demand volatility due to changes in U.S. sector-specific tariffs and production tax credits. Furthermore, the company highlights risks associated with operating in emerging markets, including uncertainties in deal closures due to financing complexities, as well as broader risks from inflationary pressures, supply chain constraints, and the need to continuously invest in emerging technologies to remain competitive.
