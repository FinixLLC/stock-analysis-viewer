## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-01-29), read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business Overview/MD&A) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text. A locally cached file existed at `company_profile/financial_reports/GE_10-K_20260708.txt`, but on inspection it turned out to be a short (8-line) raw XBRL data stub rather than the readable 10-K prose — so the live filing was fetched directly instead.

What the company does: Following the 2024 separations of GE Vernova (energy) and GE HealthCare, "GE" now operates exclusively as GE Aerospace — a global aerospace leader with the industry's largest and growing commercial propulsion fleet. Installed base of ~50,000 commercial and ~30,000 military engines (including parked aircraft) supports an aftermarket services business that represents ~70% of revenue. Operates via FLIGHT DECK, its proprietary lean operating model, prioritizing safety, quality, delivery, and cost. Serves customers in ~120 countries; manufacturing/service operations at 70 U.S./Puerto Rico facilities and 62 facilities in 23 other countries.

Segments (FY2025):
- Commercial Engines & Services (CES), ~73% of total revenue (services = 75% of CES revenue): designs, develops, manufactures, and services jet engines for commercial airframes plus business aviation/aeroderivative applications. Customers primarily airframers and airlines including both Boeing and Airbus. Includes CFM International, a 50-50 non-consolidated JV with Safran (France) — the LEAP engine (in a major production ramp, expected to overtake the mature CFM56 as the industry's largest fleet) is a key growth driver alongside widebody platforms (CF6, GE90, GEnx, and the new GE9X for the Boeing 777X). Notably, GE states that "as in the case of some Boeing models, our engines may be the sole source engine for a particular aircraft" — a meaningful competitive-moat data point.
- Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT), ~23% of total revenue (services = 51% of DPT revenue): Defense & Systems (engines/avionics/power systems for military — F110, F404, F414 combat engines; T408, T700, T901 rotorcraft engines; LM2500 marine engines; achieved first flight of the T901 on a Black Hawk in 2025) and Propulsion & Additive Technologies (Avio Aero, Unison, Dowty Propellers, Colibrium Additive brands — small turboprop engines, additive manufacturing, propeller/ignition systems; Avio Aero is the Italian MoD's propulsion partner and contributes to the Eurofighter's EJ200 engine and the new GCAP program).

Moat, as described/implied by the company: The ~50,000-unit commercial engine installed base drives a highly recurring, long-duration (decades-long) aftermarket services annuity — engines sold today generate services revenue for 20-30+ years. Sole-source engine positions on certain Boeing platforms provide structural pricing power. The CFM International JV with Safran gives GE access to the narrowbody market (the largest commercial segment) without bearing full platform risk alone. FLIGHT DECK lean operating model is positioned as a continuous execution/cost advantage.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, "Strategic Risks" listed first, alongside explicit MD&A commentary on current operational strain):
1. Global macro-environment / geopolitical risk — significant revenue from outside the U.S.; exposure to tariffs, export controls (including restrictions on rare earth minerals), sanctions, and broader trade-policy disruption.
2. Supply chain constraints — explicitly and currently live in the MD&A (not just boilerplate): "Global material availability and supplier delivery performance continue to cause disruptions and have impacted our production and delivery of equipment and services to our customers." Company states it is investing in manufacturing/overhaul facilities and its supply chain to improve yield and delivery, and expects supply chain constraints and inflation to continue affecting the business.
3. Cybersecurity — increased cybersecurity requirements, vulnerabilities, threats, and more sophisticated/targeted computer crime pose risk to systems, networks, products, and data; Audit Committee has board-level oversight.
4. Competitive/demand risk tied to commercial aviation cycle dynamics and government defense spending/programs — both segments are directly exposed to macro cyclicality in their respective end markets.
