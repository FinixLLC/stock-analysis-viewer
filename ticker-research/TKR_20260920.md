## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company operates as a global designer and manufacturer of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, supported by related services. Its business model relies on leveraging deep technical expertise in metallurgy, friction management, and industrial motion to serve a diverse customer base across 44 countries. Timken generates revenue through two primary segments: Engineered Bearings, which includes tapered, spherical, cylindrical, and ball bearings, and Industrial Motion, which encompasses linear motion solutions, industrial drives, automatic lubrication systems, belts, chains, couplings, and clutches. The company sells principally through internal sales organizations and authorized distributors, emphasizing a collaborative approach where sales engineers work closely with customers during product development and implementation. Timken also captures aftermarket demand by selling replacement parts and offering services such as bearing repair and power systems maintenance, creating value throughout the equipment lifecycle.

Timken’s competitive moat is built on its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, a position established by its founder’s patents in 1899. This foundational expertise allows the company to apply sophisticated engineering know-how, proprietary internal geometries, and high-precision tolerances across its entire portfolio, resulting in products with high load-carrying capacity and long service lives. The company differentiates itself through application engineering, product performance, and quality, competing against major global players like SKF Group and Schaeffler Group. Additionally, Timken benefits from a broad brand portfolio, including Timken, GGB, Philadelphia Gear, and Rollon, and maintains a significant global footprint with 116 manufacturing facilities and 29 technology centers. The company’s ability to serve demanding environments across diverse sectors—from aerospace and mining to renewable energy and agriculture—further solidifies its market position, as evidenced by its backlog of over $2.2 billion, approximately 92% of which is scheduled for delivery within the next 12 months.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three strategic elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. To achieve profitable growth, Timken aims to expand into new and existing markets by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and friction management, with a particular emphasis on highly fragmented sectors that value reliability and offer significant aftermarket demand. Operational excellence is pursued through a continuous improvement culture designed to increase efficiency, lower costs, eliminate waste, and enhance cash flow. Regarding capital deployment, the company prioritizes investing in the core business through capital expenditures and R&D, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. In 2025, Timken highlighted its commitment to sustainability by reporting a 42% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions intensity since 2018, targeting a 50% reduction by 2030.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on macroeconomic and operational factors. Timken faces exposure to commodity price volatility, particularly for special bar quality steel, which is subject to changes in supply, demand, and input costs such as energy and labor. The company mitigates this through surcharge mechanisms in customer contracts but remains vulnerable to cost increases. A significant recent risk factor is the impact of tariffs, which added $65 million to cost of products sold in 2025 and are expected to continue affecting earnings in 2026. Additionally, the company faces demand volatility, as evidenced by lower organic sales in 2025 driven by reduced demand in the Americas and Europe, particularly in auto/truck and heavy industries. Other flagged risks include foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, which impacted both revenue and costs, and pension-related expenses, where net actuarial losses in 2025 increased non-service pension expense significantly compared to 2024. The company also notes that while it has a substantial backlog, actual shipments depend on customers' ever-changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales.
