## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CVS_10-K_20260906.txt)

CVS Health operates as an integrated health solutions company that connects care across four reportable segments: Health Care Benefits, Health Services, Pharmacy Consumer Wellness, and Corporate/Other. As of December 31, 2025, the company’s scale includes approximately 9,000 retail locations, over 1,000 walk-in and primary care clinics, a pharmacy benefits manager (PBM) serving roughly 87 million plan members, and a health insurance division (Aetna) covering more than 37 million people. The business model relies on an integrated approach where the company leverages its PBM, retail pharmacy, and insurance assets to deliver personalized, technology-driven care, aiming to lower overall healthcare costs and improve outcomes. Revenue is generated through product sales, insurance premiums, and service fees, with the federal government accounting for approximately 20% of consolidated total revenues in 2025, largely driven by Medicare contracts.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from this vertical integration, which allows it to control the entire patient journey from insurance coverage and prescription management to direct care delivery. This structure enables CVS to share pharmaceutical rebates and discounts with clients, negotiate provider networks comprising approximately 2.0 million participating providers, and offer value-based care solutions through subsidiaries like Signify Health and Oak Street Health. Additionally, the company holds a leading position in standalone Medicare Part D prescription drug plans and maintains a broad network of commercial and government-sponsored health plans, creating a sticky ecosystem for both consumers and employer clients.

Management’s stated priorities focus on simplifying healthcare experiences, improving engagement, lowering costs, and delivering better health outcomes. The strategic ambition is to become America’s most trusted healthcare company by transforming consumer experiences and harnessing enterprise capabilities. Key operational initiatives include investing in artificial intelligence to automate and augment operational capabilities, expanding Medicare Advantage offerings, and exiting the individual public health insurance exchanges effective January 2026 to refocus resources. The company emphasizes "capital stewardship" and best-in-class execution as drivers for sustainable shareholder value.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and economic pressures. The company faces significant exposure to changes in Medicare and Medicaid funding and regulations, including potential reforms or surcharges. Legislative and regulatory activity seeking to regulate PBM activities in a majority of states and at the federal level poses a threat to the company’s ability to conduct business on commercially reasonable terms. Furthermore, the company notes that elevated healthcare utilization levels are expected to persist into 2026, potentially pressuring margins. Other material risks include increasing pressure from pharmaceutical manufacturers regarding rebate calculations, the inability to always achieve minimum pricing guarantees for clients, and macroeconomic factors such as inflation and tariffs that could disrupt supply chains and consumer demand. In 2025, these risks materialized in part through a $5.7 billion goodwill impairment charge related to the Health Care Delivery reporting unit and over $1.5 billion in litigation charges related to legacy business practices and opioid matters.
