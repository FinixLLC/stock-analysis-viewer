## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines operates as a major passenger carrier providing scheduled air transportation across the United States and near-international markets, utilizing a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft to serve 117 destinations. The company’s business model is built on a low-cost strategy that emphasizes cost discipline and competitive fares, supported by a point-to-point route structure that blends direct nonstop service with connectivity through core stations to offer reliable one-stop itineraries. This approach, combined with the use of secondary or downtown airports and a single aircraft type, allows for high asset utilization and simplified operations. In 2025, Southwest executed several transformational initiatives to modernize its customer experience and financial performance, including the introduction of assigned and extra legroom seating, a redesigned boarding model, and the launch of its first international airline partnerships to expand global connectivity. The company also introduced a new in-house vacation product, "Getaways by Southwest," and began operating 24-hour schedules with redeye flights to maximize aircraft utilization. Additionally, Southwest expanded its distribution channels to include online travel agencies and metasearch engines to attract new customers.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its unique route network, which enables frequent, conveniently timed flights and competitive pricing without the structural disruptions often associated with hub-and-spoke models. This network flexibility allows Southwest to adjust capacity quickly in response to market conditions, such as redeploying underperforming capacity from markets like Atlanta and Fort Lauderdale to more profitable routes like Nashville and San Diego. The use of a single aircraft type, the Boeing 737, further strengthens this moat by simplifying maintenance, training, and scheduling, thereby reducing unit costs. Southwest also maintains a strong brand identity centered on hospitality and customer-friendly policies, such as flexible cancellation terms for higher-tier fares and free checked bags for loyalty program members and specific fare classes.

Management’s stated priorities focus on elevating the customer experience, improving financial performance, and driving shareholder value through operational efficiency and cost discipline. Key initiatives include optimizing the route network to match capacity with demand, increasing employee productivity, and reducing unit costs through fleet modernization and process improvements. The company is also prioritizing the expansion of its network into new markets and the introduction of longer-haul flights to capture shifting travel demand. Furthermore, management is focused on enhancing connectivity through international partnerships and improving operational reliability and recoverability.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the volatility and unpredictability of fuel prices, which remain a significant portion of operating expenses, and the company’s decision to terminate its fuel hedging portfolio in 2025, leaving it exposed to market fluctuations. Labor costs constitute the largest operating expense category, and the company faces challenges from ratified contracts and rate inflation, although it has implemented workforce reductions to improve efficiency. Additionally, the company relies on Boeing for aircraft deliveries, and delays in the certification and delivery of the Boeing 737-7 could impact its fleet modernization plans and capacity growth. The filing also notes risks associated with the evolving travel market, including structural changes in demand for short-haul travel, and the need to continuously optimize its network to remain competitive.
