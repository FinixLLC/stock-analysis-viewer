## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ROST_10-K_20260906.txt)

Ross Stores, Inc. operates as a value-oriented off-price retailer through two primary banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model relies on purchasing broad-based assortments of apparel, home accents, and accessories from third-party sources, often buying excess inventory or overruns, and selling these items at significant discounts to regular retail prices. This approach is supported by a "packaway" inventory strategy, where merchandise is stored in warehouses for periods typically less than six months to maximize buying opportunities and align with seasonal demand. As of the end of fiscal 2025, the company operated 2,267 stores across the United States, including recent geographic expansions into Puerto Rico and the New York Metro area.

The company’s competitive advantage appears to stem from its ability to deliver "compelling values" and broad assortments, which management believes resonates with customers focused on value and convenience. The filing indicates that this model drives strong customer engagement, evidenced by a 5% increase in comparable store sales in fiscal 2025, driven by both traffic and basket growth. Additionally, the company maintains a strong balance sheet, with no borrowings outstanding under its $1.3 billion revolving credit facility as of January 31, 2026, and a history of repaying senior notes at maturity, which supports its ability to fund growth and return capital to shareholders.

Management’s stated priorities for fiscal 2026 and beyond focus on three key initiatives: merchandising, marketing, and store experience. Specifically, the company aims to deliver broad-based assortments timely with more brands at compelling values, advance marketing efforts to strengthen customer awareness and engagement, and make meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. The company also plans to accelerate store expansion, targeting approximately 110 new store openings in fiscal 2026, with a specific reacceleration of growth for the dd’s DISCOUNTS banner. Capital expenditures are projected to increase to approximately $1.1 billion in fiscal 2026, driven by investments in new and existing stores, supply chain infrastructure (including new distribution centers), and information technology systems.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to the broader macroeconomic environment and legal proceedings. Management explicitly cites uncertainty regarding tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions as factors that could impact results. In fiscal 2025, tariff-related costs negatively impacted merchandise margin by 20 basis points and reduced earnings per share by approximately $0.16. Additionally, the company faces ongoing class and representative action lawsuits, primarily in California, alleging violations of wage and hour laws, as well as other legal and regulatory proceedings involving commercial, product safety, and labor claims. While management believes the resolution of these pending matters will not have a material adverse effect on financial condition, they remain subject to significant uncertainties.
