## Business Profile

Ross Stores, Inc. operates as a leading off-price retailer in the United States, managing two distinct banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model relies on purchasing broad-based assortments of branded merchandise at compelling values, leveraging a "packaway" inventory strategy where goods are stored in warehouses for less than six months before being released to stores based on seasonality and product mix. With 2,267 total stores as of the end of fiscal 2025, the company generates significant scale, evidenced by $22.75 billion in sales and an 11.9% operating margin. The company maintains a strong balance sheet, characterized by high operating cash flow ($3.03 billion in fiscal 2025), minimal debt relative to its cash position, and a consistent history of returning capital to shareholders through dividends and substantial stock repurchases, including a newly approved $2.55 billion program.

The company’s competitive moat appears to be built on its ability to source high-quality, branded merchandise at deep discounts and its efficient supply chain infrastructure, which includes eight distribution centers. Management attributes its success to delivering broad assortments timely and offering more brands at compelling values, which drives customer traffic and basket size. The scale of its operations allows for significant leverage in occupancy and distribution costs, although recent expansion has temporarily impacted margins. The company continues to expand its footprint, planning to open approximately 110 new stores in 2026, including entry into new geographic markets like Puerto Rico and the New York Metro area, suggesting a strategy of geographic penetration and market share capture in the value retail segment.

Management’s stated priorities for the current period focus on three key initiatives: merchandising, marketing, and store experience. Specifically, the company aims to deliver broad-based assortments timely with more brands at compelling values, advance marketing efforts to strengthen customer awareness and engagement, and make meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. These initiatives are central to the company’s goal of driving sustainable, profitable growth. Management also emphasizes the importance of monitoring macroeconomic factors, including tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions, while continuing to leverage packaway inventory opportunities to maximize bargain offerings.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the broader macroeconomic environment, specifically citing tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions as sources of uncertainty. Tariff-related costs have already impacted the company, contributing to a 20 basis point decrease in merchandise margin and an estimated unfavorable impact of $0.16 per share in fiscal 2025. Additionally, the company faces legal risks, including pending class/representative action lawsuits in California alleging wage and hour law violations, as well as other commercial, product, and labor-related claims. While management believes these legal proceedings will not have a material adverse effect on financial condition, they remain subject to significant uncertainties. The company also notes that its performance is sensitive to changes in consumer spending habits and the availability of compelling merchandise in the marketplace.
