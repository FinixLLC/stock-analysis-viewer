## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates as a leading developer and supplier of integrated circuit products and wireless technology solutions, generating revenue through two primary segments: Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL). The QCT segment, which accounted for the majority of the company’s $44.3 billion in fiscal 2025 revenue, sells Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, modems, and other chipsets to customers in the handset, automotive, and IoT markets. The QTL segment generates high-margin licensing revenues by granting rights to use the company’s intellectual property portfolio, including patents essential to wireless products. This business model is characterized by significant seasonality, with revenues fluctuating based on consumer demand for wireless devices and the timing of customer device launches and innovation cycles.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its position as a leader in technology development, standardization, and intellectual property creation. Management explicitly states that sustaining long-term growth requires remaining a leading developer of integrated circuit products and a key licensor of IP. This is evidenced by the company’s ability to secure long-term licensing agreements with major global OEMs, including recent settlements with key Chinese manufacturers and Transsion, and its dominance in premium-tier Android devices where higher average selling prices and favorable mix drove QCT revenue growth. The high margin profile of the licensing segment, which maintained a 72% earnings before tax margin in fiscal 2025, further underscores the value of its IP portfolio.

Management’s stated priorities focus on leveraging on-device AI, high-performance low-power computing, and cellular technology to expand beyond mobile handsets into automotive and IoT industries. The company emphasizes the importance of continuing to lead in technology development and IP licensing to sustain its business. Additionally, management is actively monitoring global trade policies, including tariffs, to mitigate potential impacts on demand, pricing, and costs.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the impact of global trade policies and tariffs, which could negatively affect demand, pricing, and inventory levels. A significant financial risk emerged from the enactment of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB), which resulted in a $5.7 billion charge to establish a valuation allowance against federal deferred tax assets because the company no longer expects to realize them due to the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT). Other risks include the inherent uncertainty of legal and regulatory proceedings, the potential for material losses from pending litigation, and the volatility of revenue driven by the seasonality of consumer wireless device launches.
