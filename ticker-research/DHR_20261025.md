## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (DHR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Danaher operates as a diversified, multinational life sciences company with global operations, deriving approximately 59% of its sales from customers outside the United States. The company’s business model is built on geographic and industry diversity, which management states helps mitigate the impact of any single industry or national economy. Danaher serves three primary segments: Biotechnology, which provides equipment, consumables, software, and services for the research, development, and manufacture of biological medicines; Life Sciences, which offers instruments and consumables for studying the basic building blocks of life and for filtering contaminants; and Diagnostics, which focuses on clinical testing. The company generates revenue through a mix of product sales, services, and recurring consumables, with a significant portion of its growth strategy relying on organic expansion in high-growth geographies and market segments, as well as the identification, consummation, and integration of acquisitions and strategic partnerships.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its ability to develop innovative and differentiated new products and services with higher gross profit margins, coupled with a diversified global supply chain that sources parts and materials worldwide. Danaher emphasizes its capacity to expand its sales force and improve operating efficiency and quality through the ongoing application of its business system (DBS), which drives productivity improvements and cost efficiencies. Furthermore, the company’s scale allows it to make significant investments in manufacturing, R&D, and customer-facing resources to address rapid technological changes in computing, automation, AI, and digitization, positioning it to remain responsive to customers globally.

Management’s stated priorities for long-term shareholder value include expanding the business in high-growth geographies and higher-growth market segments, identifying and integrating appropriate acquisitions, and developing innovative products with higher margins. The company is also focused on expanding and improving the effectiveness of its sales force, reducing costs, and improving operating efficiency and quality. Additionally, management prioritizes effectively addressing the demands of an increasingly regulated global environment and a rapidly evolving trade environment, while making significant investments to address the rapid pace of technological change in its served markets.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of the rapidly evolving trade environment and new tariffs. Since early 2025, the U.S. government has implemented significant new tariffs on imports from a wide range of countries, prompting retaliatory actions, including export restrictions from China. As of December 31, 2025, the company incurred incremental tariff costs of less than $300 million, which it largely offset through manufacturing footprint changes, supply chain adjustments, surcharges, and productivity actions. However, management warns that if it is unable to continue offsetting these costs, or if additional tariffs are implemented, revenue and profitability could be adversely impacted. Other material risks include the impact of worldwide, regional, and industry-specific economic, political, and geopolitical factors; rapid technological development; consolidation of competitors; and increasing regulation. The company also notes that the full impact of U.S. government regulatory, policy, and personnel changes, including the elimination or downsizing of certain government agencies and the cancellation or delay of government contracts and research grants, remains uncertain.
