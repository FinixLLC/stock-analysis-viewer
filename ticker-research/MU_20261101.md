## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (MU_10-K_20260906.txt)

Micron Technology operates as a leading provider of memory and storage solutions, with a business model centered on the design, manufacturing, and sale of DRAM and NAND products. The company’s financial performance is highly cyclical, driven by industry-wide supply and demand dynamics, average selling prices, and bit shipment volumes. In fiscal year 2025, Micron benefited from a favorable industry environment characterized by AI-driven demand that is outpacing supply. The company strategically shifted a portion of its DRAM supply toward high-value data center and hyperscale cloud markets, specifically emphasizing High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) products. This pivot to higher-growth segments, combined with strong execution and constrained industry supply, resulted in substantial improvements in pricing, volumes, and margins, driving total revenue to $37.38 billion and net income to $8.54 billion.

Micron’s competitive position is reinforced by its ability to execute on advanced product and process technologies, which drive manufacturing cost reductions and margin expansion. The company’s focus on high-margin products like HBM and its alignment of NAND supply growth with projected demand environments demonstrate a strategic agility that allows it to capture value in high-growth areas while managing lower-margin legacy segments. The filing indicates that Micron’s profitability is increasingly weighted toward the Cloud and Data Center business units, which saw significant revenue and operating income growth in 2025 due to robust AI demand.

Management’s stated priorities include maintaining a prudent management of the NAND business to align supply with demand, continuing to invest in manufacturing technologies, facilities, and R&D to develop new product and process technology, and supporting future growth through significant capital expenditures, estimated at approximately $4.5 billion for the first quarter of 2026. The company also emphasizes the importance of leveraging government incentives to fund these capital projects, although these incentives are conditional and subject to clawbacks.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the inherent cyclicality of the memory industry, where cash generation is highly dependent on selling prices that can vary significantly. A specific geopolitical risk is the 2023 decision by China’s Cyberspace Administration (CAC) to deem Micron products a cybersecurity risk, restricting their purchase by critical information infrastructure operators in China, which has impacted the company’s domestic data center and networking markets in that region. Additionally, the company faces regulatory and tax risks, including the uncertain impact of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) and the implementation of OECD Pillar Two rules in jurisdictions like Singapore, which could materially affect its effective tax rate and financial statements.
