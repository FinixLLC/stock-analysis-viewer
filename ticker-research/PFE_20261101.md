## Business Profile

Pfizer Inc. operates as a research-based, global biopharmaceutical company that generates the majority of its revenue through the discovery, development, manufacture, marketing, and sale of biopharmaceutical products and vaccines. The company’s business model relies on a dual engine of commercial execution for existing brands and a robust research and development (R&D) pipeline to replenish product lines as they lose exclusivity. Pfizer actively manages its portfolio through business development activities, including acquisitions (such as Seagen and Metsera), collaborations, and licensing agreements, while also engaging in strategic divestitures to focus on core areas. The company’s commercial structure is organized into operating segments, with Biopharma being the primary reportable segment, and it is currently undergoing structural changes to optimize its cost base and manufacturing network.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its intellectual property portfolio and its ability to defend patent rights against infringement, which secures revenue during periods of exclusivity. However, the filing explicitly notes that this moat is subject to erosion through patent expirations, regulatory exclusivity losses, and the entry of generic and biosimilar competitors. Pfizer views its strong pipeline, advanced scientific capabilities, and platform technologies as critical to maintaining its competitive position and addressing unmet patient needs. Additionally, the company leverages its global scale and digital enablement, including automation and AI, to drive operational efficiency and productivity, which supports its ability to compete on cost and innovation.

Management has outlined four key priorities for 2026: maximizing the value of key transactions, delivering on critical R&D milestones, investing to maximize post-2028 growth, and scaling AI across the business. To support these goals, management emphasizes "digital enablement" to enhance efficiency and accelerate innovation. The company is executing multi-year cost realignment and manufacturing optimization programs to reduce costs of goods sold and selling, general, and administrative expenses, aiming to realign its cost structure with longer-term revenue expectations. Furthermore, management is focused on optimizing operating cash flows and maintaining liquidity to support capital allocation objectives, including debt management and potential future acquisitions.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the significant revenue impact from patent-based and regulatory exclusivity expirations, which is expected to accelerate over the next few years, with a specific $1.5 billion impact anticipated in 2026. The company faces substantial pricing and access pressures from global governments and U.S. payors, including the implementation of the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which has already negatively impacted 2025 revenues by approximately $1 billion due to Medicare Part D redesign and Maximum Fair Price provisions for products like Eliquis, Ibrance, Xtandi, and Xeljanz. Additionally, Pfizer faces legal and litigation risks, including product liability claims related to discontinued or divested products, and operational risks associated with the integration of recent acquisitions and the execution of its cost-reduction programs. The company also notes risks related to the volatility of equity securities and pension plan valuations, as well as the inherent uncertainties in clinical trial success and regulatory approvals.
