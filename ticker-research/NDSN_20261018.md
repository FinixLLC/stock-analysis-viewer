## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NDSN_10-K_20260827.txt)

Nordson is a global precision technology company that engineers, manufactures, and markets differentiated products and systems for the precision dispensing, application, and control of adhesives, coatings, polymers, sealants, and biomaterials, as well as for surface treatment, quality testing, and inspection. The company serves a broad range of end markets, including consumer non-durables, consumer durables, electronics, medical, and industrial sectors, with approximately 67% of its 2025 revenues generated outside the United States. Nordson’s business model relies on a direct global sales and service network in over 35 countries, supported by extensive application expertise. The company drives growth through organic innovation, leveraging existing technologies into new applications, and strategic acquisitions of companies with differentiated precision technology portfolios that complement existing capabilities and diversify into high-growth end markets.

Nordson’s competitive moat is derived from a combination of intellectual property and human capital. The company holds a global portfolio of more than 2,100 granted and pending patents and over 1,000 trademarks, which it actively enforces to protect its market position. However, management explicitly states that the company’s competitive advantage is largely attributable to the technical, marketing, and sales competence of its employees rather than any single patent or trademark. This is supported by a culture of innovation, a division-led organizational structure designed to foster an "Owner Mindset," and a data-based segmentation framework (NBS Next) that directs resources toward the most profitable growth opportunities. The company also benefits from a diversified customer base, with no single customer accounting for more than 10% of sales, and a broad product portfolio that spans from single-use components to complex automated systems.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on the "Ascend" strategy, launched in 2021, which aims to deliver top-tier revenue growth with leading margins and returns. This strategy is built on three pillars: the NBS Next growth framework for data-driven resource allocation, the Owner Mindset organizational structure, and a Winning Teams talent strategy focused on employee self-fulfillment and growth. Nordson emphasizes long-term value creation over short-term quarterly fluctuations, stating that it does not alter its foundational objectives to mitigate temporary swings in sales or earnings. The company prioritizes genuine customer satisfaction, employee development, and community engagement, having donated over $183 million to communities since 1989. Additionally, management focuses on maintaining high gross margins (55.2% in 2025) and improving segment EBITDA through favorable product mix, controlled spending, and the integration of acquisitions.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to macroeconomic conditions, supply chain volatility, and competitive pressures. Nordson operates in a competitive global marketplace against large, well-established manufacturers and faces risks from fluctuations in currency exchange rates, industry capacity changes, and economic conditions in the U.S. and abroad. The company notes that while global supply chains have stabilized, Asia-origin shipments remain volatile, and costs may be affected by raw material price changes and tariffs, particularly on imports from China. Additionally, the company faces risks associated with the integration of acquisitions, including the need to accurately estimate fair values for goodwill and intangible assets, which requires significant management judgment regarding future revenue growth, EBITDA margins, and discount rates. Although no goodwill impairment was recorded in 2025, management acknowledges that a sustained downturn in global economies could negatively impact estimated fair values. The company also faces risks related to its domestic pension plan, where changes in discount rates, expected returns on assets, and compensation increases can significantly affect reported expenses and obligations.
