## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates as a leading developer and supplier of integrated circuit products and intellectual property (IP) for wireless technologies, generating revenue through two primary segments: Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL). The QCT segment, which accounts for the majority of revenue, sells equipment and services including Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, modems, and chipsets for handsets, automotive, and IoT applications. The QTL segment generates high-margin licensing revenues by granting rights to use the company’s IP portfolio, including patents essential to wireless products. In fiscal 2025, total revenues reached $44.3 billion, driven by a 16% increase in QCT revenues from higher handset, IoT, and automotive sales, while QTL revenues remained approximately flat. The company’s business model is characterized by seasonality tied to consumer device launch cycles and innovation transitions, such as the shift to next-generation wireless technologies.

Qualcomm’s competitive moat is rooted in its position as a leader in technology development, standardization, and IP creation, particularly in on-device AI, high-performance low-power computing, and cellular technologies like 5G. The company emphasizes the importance of remaining a leading developer of integrated circuit products and a key licensor of IP to sustain long-term growth. Its IP portfolio includes patent rights essential to the manufacture, sale, or use of certain wireless products, creating a dependency for licensees that supports stable, high-margin licensing revenues. Additionally, the company’s ability to integrate advanced computing with cellular technology positions it favorably for expansion into non-mobile industries such as automotive and IoT, where it has seen significant revenue growth from new vehicle launches and edge networking products.

Management’s stated priorities focus on sustaining leadership in technology development, standardization, and IP licensing while expanding into growth areas like automotive and IoT. The company highlights the strategic importance of on-device AI and high-performance computing combined with cellular technology as key drivers of future adoption. Management also emphasizes the need to monitor and adapt to global trade policy changes, including tariffs, which could impact demand, pricing, and costs. Additionally, the company is focused on managing the financial impacts of recent tax legislation, specifically the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB), which has altered its tax provision and deferred tax asset realization.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the impact of global trade policies and tariffs, which may negatively affect demand, pricing, and costs, leading to potential inventory write-downs and reduced cash flows. The company also faces significant tax-related risks due to the OBBB, which has resulted in a $5.7 billion charge to establish a valuation allowance against federal deferred tax assets because the company no longer expects to realize substantially all of these assets due to the corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT). Other risks include the inherent uncertainty in legal and regulatory proceedings, which could result in material losses, and the challenges in estimating future customer demand, which may lead to inventory obsolescence. Additionally, the company notes that changes in tax laws, including CAMT rules, and the interpretation of complex tax regulations in various jurisdictions could materially impact its tax liabilities and financial results.
