Ross Stores, Inc. operates as a value-oriented off-price retailer through two distinct banners: Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. The company’s business model relies on purchasing broad-based assortments of apparel, accessories, and home goods from third-party sources, often taking advantage of "packaway" inventory opportunities to secure merchandise at compelling values. This inventory is stored in distribution centers and released to stores based on seasonality and product mix, allowing the company to offer a rotating selection of brands at prices significantly below traditional retail. As of the end of fiscal 2025, the company operated 2,267 stores across the United States, with a strategy focused on geographic expansion into new markets such as Puerto Rico and the New York Metro area, alongside a steady increase in store count driven by market penetration and demographic analysis.

The company’s competitive positioning is anchored in its ability to deliver value and convenience, supported by a supply chain that includes eight distribution centers and a significant packaway inventory model. While the filing does not explicitly define a traditional "moat," it highlights the company’s scale, with $22.7 billion in sales and a strong cash flow profile, as well as its ability to leverage overhead expenses through store expansion and distribution efficiencies. Management emphasizes that customers focus on value and convenience, which supports the company’s long-term growth strategy. The company maintains a strong financial position, with no borrowings outstanding under its $1.3 billion revolving credit facility and a commitment to returning capital to shareholders through dividends and stock repurchases, including a new $2.55 billion buyback program approved in March 2026.

Management’s stated priorities for fiscal 2026 and beyond center on three key initiatives: merchandising, marketing, and store experience. Specifically, the company aims to deliver broad-based assortments timely while offering more brands at compelling values. It seeks to advance marketing efforts to strengthen customer awareness and engagement and to make meaningful improvements to the in-store shopping experience. Additionally, management is focused on driving sustainable, profitable growth by monitoring macroeconomic factors such as tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical conditions, while continuing to expand its store footprint, with plans to open approximately 110 new stores in fiscal 2026.

Other risks include the uncertainty of the broader economic environment, including inflation and geopolitical conditions, as well as the operational challenges associated with expanding into new geographic markets and managing the timing of packaway inventory releases.
