## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ALL_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Allstate Corporation operates as a diversified insurance provider with two primary reportable segments: Allstate Protection and Protection Services. The Allstate Protection segment generates the majority of revenue by selling private passenger auto, homeowners, and other personal lines insurance to consumers through exclusive agents, independent agents, and direct channels (contact centers and online). The company’s business model relies on underwriting these policies to earn premiums, which are then invested to generate net investment income, while managing claims expenses and deferred acquisition costs. The Protection Services segment, which includes businesses like Protection Plans, Roadside, Dealer Services, and Identity Protection, operates on a subscription or service-fee basis, generating revenue from premiums written and other service fees. In 2025, the company also executed significant strategic dispositions, selling its employer voluntary benefits and group health businesses, which contributed substantial gains to net income but are no longer core reportable segments.

The excerpts do not explicitly define a traditional "competitive moat" such as network effects or high switching costs in the qualitative sense; however, they highlight the company’s scale and distribution reach, serving over 38 million policies in force in the Allstate Protection segment alone. The filing notes a strategy to offer "affordable, simple and connected protection products" and mentions a multi-year "Transformative Growth" initiative, suggesting that the company’s competitive advantage is derived from its brand recognition, broad distribution channels, and ability to implement rate changes to maintain profitability in competitive markets. The company also utilizes significant reinsurance and indemnification programs to manage aggregate exposure, which supports its financial stability and ability to underwrite large risks.

Management’s stated priorities, as inferred from the financial highlights and strategic notes, focus on driving premium growth through rate increases and policy count expansion, particularly in the Allstate Protection segment. The company emphasizes maintaining an acceptable combined ratio by balancing premium growth with claims cost management and expense control. Additionally, management prioritizes capital management and shareholder returns, evidenced by the significant increase in book value per share and return on equity, as well as the execution of share repurchases. The company also highlights its focus on international growth within the Protection Plans business and lead generation revenue at Arity (part of Protection Services) as key drivers of recent performance.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the inherent uncertainty in estimating claims reserves, particularly for long-tail liabilities such as asbestos and environmental claims, where reestimates can significantly impact net income and combined ratios. The company explicitly notes that reserve reestimates in 2025 were a major driver of financial results, with favorable releases boosting income, but this process is subject to variability based on claim activity and development factors. Another significant risk is exposure to catastrophe losses, which remain a substantial component of the loss ratio, and the company relies on catastrophe reinsurance programs to mitigate this risk. Additionally, the filing highlights macroeconomic risks, including the potential impact of U.S. tariffs on vehicle and building material costs, which could increase claims costs and affect demand. The company also faces credit risk from reinsurance recoverables, noting that adverse industry developments have led to a decline in the financial strength of some reinsurers, increasing the risk of uncollectible amounts. Finally, the company acknowledges risks related to global economic conditions, supply chain disruptions, and labor shortages that could impact operations and financial results.
