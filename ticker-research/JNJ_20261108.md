## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (JNJ_10-K_20260906.txt)

Johnson & Johnson operates as a global healthcare company with approximately 138,200 employees, organized into two primary business segments: Innovative Medicine and MedTech. The Innovative Medicine segment focuses on therapeutic areas including Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, and Cardiovascular and Metabolic, distributing prescription products directly to retailers, wholesalers, distributors, hospitals, and healthcare professionals. The MedTech segment provides products for Surgery, Orthopaedic, Cardiovascular, and Vision fields, distributed to wholesalers, hospitals, and retailers for use by physicians, nurses, and clinics. The company’s business model relies on internal research and development, as well as collaborations, acquisitions, joint ventures, and licensing arrangements to develop new products. In fiscal 2025, new products introduced within the past five years accounted for approximately 25% of sales. The company is heavily regulated, subject to FDA approval processes, and generates significant revenue internationally, with 43% of 2025 sales occurring outside the U.S.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its extensive intellectual property portfolio, including patents and proprietary rights essential to its products and manufacturing processes, although the filing notes that public policy has become increasingly unfavorable toward IP rights and competitors routinely challenge these protections. Additionally, the company leverages data science, machine learning, and AI across its business operations, though it acknowledges these technologies carry risks of obsolescence and regulatory uncertainty. The scale of its global operations and established distribution channels also contribute to its market position, despite facing challenges from illegal pharmaceutical imports and generic/biosimilar competition.

Management’s stated priorities center on innovation and the development of new, differentiated products and services to address evolving healthcare needs, which is critical for offsetting revenue losses from patent expirations and competition. The company is also actively pursuing portfolio optimization, having recently separated its Kenvue business and announced its intention to separate its Orthopaedics business within 18 to 24 months. Furthermore, management emphasizes the integration of emerging technologies like AI to drive business efficiency, while simultaneously managing significant compliance costs associated with global regulatory requirements.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the substantial litigation exposure arising from the use of talc-containing body powders, which could result in settlements or judgments exceeding current accruals and potentially having a material adverse effect on results of operations. The company does not purchase third-party product liability insurance, relying instead on a wholly owned captive insurance company subject to certain limits. Other significant risks include regulatory scrutiny and compliance costs related to FDA approvals, Good Manufacturing Practices, and anti-corruption laws such as the FCPA. The company also faces risks from changes in tax laws, intellectual property challenges that could lead to rapid revenue loss upon patent expiration, and financial risks associated with international operations, including foreign currency exchange fluctuations and high inflation in countries such as Argentina, Turkey, Venezuela, and Egypt.
