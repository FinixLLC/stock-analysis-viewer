## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

Snowflake operates as a cloud computing platform provider focused on data and artificial intelligence, delivering its "AI Data Cloud" through a consumption-based business model where customers pay for compute, storage, and data transfer resources used. The company’s cloud-native architecture consists of three independently scalable layers—storage, compute, and cloud services—deployed across three major public clouds in 53 global regions. This infrastructure allows organizations to consolidate structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data into a single source of truth, enabling analytics, data engineering, AI application development, and secure data sharing without moving underlying data. Snowflake’s competitive moat is driven by powerful network effects within the AI Data Cloud ecosystem; as more customers, partners, and data providers join, the value of data exchange and collaboration increases for all users. The company also leverages its multi-cloud, multi-region architecture to provide a consistent global experience while allowing customers to optimize for specific cloud provider features and regulatory requirements.

Management’s stated priorities center on advancing the AI Data Cloud through continuous innovation, acquiring new customers, and driving increased usage among existing ones. Specifically, the company aims to replace legacy data solutions, expand its global footprint in EMEA, APJ, and Latin America, and grow its partner network. Recent strategic moves include the acquisition of Crunchy Data to support its new Snowflake Postgres offering and Observe to enhance its AI-powered observability capabilities. Management emphasizes investing in research and development to strengthen technical leadership in areas such as Snowpark, Snowflake Intelligence, and AI agents, while also focusing on meeting the heightened security and compliance needs of regulated industries like financial services and the public sector.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on the company’s dependence on customer consumption patterns and macroeconomic conditions. Because revenue is recognized based on actual usage rather than ratably over contract terms, Snowflake lacks visibility into the timing of revenue recognition, making results susceptible to fluctuations if customers optimize their consumption or shorten contract durations due to budget rationalization. Additionally, the company faces risks related to its ability to attract and retain talent, maintain its reputation for security and trustworthiness, and compete effectively against established database and big data offerings. The filing also notes that improvements in storage compression and compute efficiency could lead to lower resource consumption for the same workloads, potentially reducing revenue if not offset by new workload adoption. Finally, the company highlights risks associated with geopolitical conflicts, trade wars, and the complexity of meeting data localization and privacy requirements in various global markets.
