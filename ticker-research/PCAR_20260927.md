## Business Profile

PACCAR operates as a global technology company with a diversified business model spanning three primary segments: Truck, Parts, and Financial Services. The Truck segment involves the design and manufacture of light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial vehicles, marketed under the Kenworth and Peterbilt nameplates in North America, DAF in Europe, and a mix of Kenworth and DAF in Mexico, Australia, and South America. The Parts segment distributes aftermarket components for these vehicles, while the Financial Services segment generates revenue by financing or leasing PACCAR products across North America, Europe, Australia, and South America. This integrated approach allows the company to capture value across the vehicle lifecycle, supported by a global financial services arm with operations in 26 countries and a loan and lease portfolio of $22.80 billion.

The company’s competitive moat appears to be built on brand strength, geographic diversification, and vertical integration. PACCAR maintains significant market share in key regions, holding 29.9% of the U.S. and Canada heavy-duty truck retail market and 13.5% of the European over-16-tonne market in 2025. Its financial services arm reinforces this position by increasing its finance market share of new PACCAR truck sales to 27.0% in 2025, creating a sticky ecosystem for dealers and customers. Furthermore, the company cites its "consistent focus on quality" and strong balance sheet as enablers of long-term profitability, having invested $9.2 billion in facilities and technology over the past decade. The local manufacturing footprint in the U.S., Canada, and Mexico is strategically positioned to mitigate tariff exposure, specifically under new Section 232 truck tariffs, by producing trucks locally for these markets.

Management’s stated priorities center on maintaining high returns on equity, investing in next-generation technologies, and navigating regulatory and trade headwinds. The company emphasizes its ability to deliver consistent profitability, noting 87 consecutive years of net income, with an adjusted return on equity of 15.1% in 2025. Strategic investments are directed toward clean diesel and alternative powertrains, autonomous and advanced driver assistance systems, integrated connected vehicle services, and the embedding of artificial intelligence across the business. Additionally, management is focused on enhancing manufacturing capabilities, evidenced by recent investments in robotic paint facilities, engine remanufacturing, and new parts distribution centers, while continuing to support its U.S.-based battery joint venture, Amplify Cell Technologies.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily driven by macroeconomic and regulatory factors. The most immediate threat is the impact of import tariffs imposed by the U.S. government and other countries, which have negatively affected truck order intake and profit margins since March 2025, despite mitigation efforts and local production advantages. The company also highlights exposure to changes in U.S. trade policy, including the pending Supreme Court ruling on International Emergency Economic Power Acts (IEEPA) tariffs. Other significant risks include the cyclical nature of the trucking industry, where lower retail demand in all major markets drove a 22% decrease in worldwide new truck deliveries in 2025. Additionally, the company faces potential credit losses and increased repossessions if freight transportation conditions decline due to a weaker economy, which would adversely affect the Financial Services segment. Finally, regulatory changes regarding emissions, such as EPA NOx limits and warranty requirements, present ongoing compliance and cost risks.
