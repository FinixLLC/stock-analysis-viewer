## Business Profile

**Source: locally cached 10-K text dump, `company_profile/financial_reports/AMG_10-K_20260708.txt`** (FY2025 Form 10-K, inline-XBRL HTML, Item 1 Business Overview and Item 1A/Item 7 sections read directly).

**What the company does:** AMG is not an asset manager in the traditional sense — it's a strategic holding company that takes minority equity (and some majority) stakes in independent, partner-owned investment management firms ("Affiliates") spanning alternatives (private markets, liquid alternatives) and differentiated long-only strategies (equities, multi-asset, fixed income). As of December 31, 2025, aggregate AUM across its Affiliates was approximately **$813 billion**. AMG does not run day-to-day investment operations at its Affiliates; Affiliate management retains autonomy and equity ownership, while AMG provides growth capital, product/distribution support (notably a "vertically-integrated U.S. wealth platform"), succession-planning solutions, and strategic advisory.

**Moat:** The moat is relational/structural rather than product-based — three-plus decades of partnership infrastructure that lets AMG source and structure minority-stake deals with high-quality independent managers who want to preserve autonomy (an alternative to outright M&A/consolidation), plus a distribution/capital-formation platform (especially U.S. wealth channel access) that individual boutique Affiliates could not easily build alone. Diversification across dozens of Affiliates, strategies, and geographies also smooths earnings versus a single-strategy manager.

**Management's stated priorities (from Item 7 MD&A, executive overview):** Continue rotating the portfolio toward alternatives (private markets and liquid alternatives) for better organic growth, earnings growth, and cash-flow stability; keep adding new partnerships in areas of "durable client demand"; expand the U.S. wealth distribution platform; and maintain a "disciplined capital allocation framework" — recycling proceeds from Affiliate sales into new investments and shareholder returns (buybacks/dividend).

Concrete 2025–early 2026 portfolio activity disclosed in the 10-K: new minority investments in NorthBridge Partners (industrial logistics real estate), Verition Fund Management (multi-strategy), Montefiore Investment (European PE, services sector), and Qualitas Energy (renewables/energy transition infrastructure); a strategic partnership with Brown Brothers Harriman for a minority stake in BBH Credit Partners (closed Jan 2026); an additional investment in existing Affiliate Garda Capital Partners; and a new stake in HighBrook Investors (real estate). On the exit side: sold its stake in Peppertree Capital Management as part of Peppertree's acquisition by TPG (gain $127.6M), sold Comvest Partners' private credit business to Manulife (gain $227.6M), and sold Montrusco Bolton Investments to Walter Global Asset Management (gain $16.2M).

**Key risks management flags (Item 1A Risk Factors):** The dominant, recurring risk theme is that AMG's results are a derivative of its Affiliates' AUM and fee levels, which are exposed to market declines/volatility, shifting investor preference toward lower-fee passive products, and product/asset-class concentration at individual Affiliates. Other material risk categories disclosed: reputational risk (AMG's and Affiliates'), heavy exposure to financial-services regulation (by far the most frequently discussed risk category in the filing), cybersecurity, key-person/succession risk at Affiliate firms (since equity and expertise are concentrated in relatively few principals per Affiliate), foreign-currency exposure from international Affiliates/clients, debt/leverage, tax exposure, and litigation.
