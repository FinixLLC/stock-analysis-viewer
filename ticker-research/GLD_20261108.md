## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (GLD_10-K_20260906.txt)

SPDR Gold Trust (GLD) is a passive investment trust established in 2004 under New York law, designed to provide investors with a cost-efficient and accessible means of gaining exposure to the price of gold bullion. Its business model is straightforward: the Trust holds allocated gold bullion and issues shares in blocks of 100,000 (Baskets) in exchange for deposits of gold, while distributing gold upon the redemption of Baskets. The Trust does not engage in active management, hold derivative securities, or generate income from cash holdings; instead, it sells small amounts of gold as needed to cover operational expenses. The primary value proposition for investors is the elimination of the logistical barriers associated with physical gold ownership, such as storage, insurance, and security, which are handled by custodians and built into the share price. Shares trade on the NYSE Arca and several international exchanges, allowing for easy access through traditional brokerage accounts.

The Trust’s competitive advantage stems from its status as a large, liquid, and transparent vehicle for gold exposure, backed by allocated London Good Delivery gold bars held in vaults in London, New York, and Zurich. The filing highlights that the Trust’s holdings are identified on the custodians' books as the property of the Trust, and the Trust does not hold unallocated gold. This structure, combined with the ability to trade on major exchanges and the use of the LBMA Gold Price for daily valuation, provides a reliable benchmark for investors seeking to implement strategic or tactical asset allocation strategies without the friction of physical bullion markets.

Management’s stated priorities, as reflected in the filing, focus on maintaining the Trust’s objective of reflecting the performance of gold bullion prices less expenses. The Trustee and Sponsor prioritize accurate daily valuation using the LBMA Gold Price PM and ensure that the Trust’s assets consist solely of allocated gold and cash used for expenses. The filing notes that the Trustee endeavors to sell the exact amount of gold needed to pay expenses to minimize non-gold asset holdings, thereby keeping the cash balance at zero and ensuring the Trust remains a pure play on gold prices.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are directly tied to the volatility of the gold price, as the Trust’s net income and asset value are entirely dependent on movements in the price of gold. The filing explicitly states that investing in the Shares does not insulate investors from price volatility. Additionally, there is a risk related to the valuation methodology; while the LBMA Gold Price is considered an appropriate indicator, the filing notes that other indicators could yield materially different fair value pricing, which could affect market adjustments or redemption values. The Trust also faces the inherent risk that the amount of gold per share will gradually decrease over time due to the sale of gold to pay expenses, a structural feature of the business model.
