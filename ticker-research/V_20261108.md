## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce and money movement through its proprietary VisaNet network. Its business model is based on the "four-party structure," where Visa provides transaction processing services—specifically authorization, clearing, and settlement—among consumers, issuing and acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. Crucially, Visa is not a financial institution; it does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk. Instead, it earns revenue through service fees, data processing fees, international transaction fees, and other value-added services, while explicitly separating its fee structure from interchange reimbursement fees (IRFs) and merchant discount rates (MDRs). The company has expanded its traditional card-based model to include digital banks, fintechs, and governments, utilizing a "Visa as a Service" stack that offers modular capabilities via APIs and its Model Context Protocol (MCP) server. In fiscal 2025, Visa processed 258 billion transactions with a total payments and cash volume of $17 trillion across more than 200 countries.

Visa’s competitive moat is derived from its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by nearly 5 billion payment credentials, access to approximately 12 billion cards, bank accounts, and digital wallets, and acceptance at over 175 million merchant locations. The company leverages this connectivity to offer a "network of networks" strategy, positioning itself as a single connection point for money movement regardless of the originating or completing network. Additionally, Visa maintains a technological edge through its proprietary tokenization technology (Visa Token Service), which has provisioned over 16 billion tokens, and its integration of artificial intelligence, including the recent acquisition of Featurespace for real-time AI payments protection. The company also differentiates itself through high-volume "Tap to Everything" capabilities, which accounted for 79% of global face-to-face transactions in fiscal 2025, and strong cross-border risk management and settlement capabilities.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, the goal is to strengthen card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion annual opportunity in underserved consumer spend. In CMS, the focus is on driving penetration in commercial payments and money movement for businesses and governments. In VAS, the priority is delivering innovative services to deepen client partnerships. Operationally, management is investing in next-generation technologies such as generative AI, stablecoins, and agentic commerce, while also executing capital return initiatives, including a $30 billion share repurchase program authorized in April 2025.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are primarily legal and regulatory in nature, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a U.S. litigation escrow account to address claims associated with this litigation. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 1, 2025. This litigation has a direct impact on the company’s capital structure, as the U.S. retrospective responsibility plan allows Visa to recover monetary liabilities through downward adjustments to the conversion rates of its Class B common stock into Class A common stock. While the filing notes that exchange rate movements did not have a material impact on revenue or expenses, the potential for further accruals related to the MDL and other uncovered legal matters represents a significant financial and structural risk.
