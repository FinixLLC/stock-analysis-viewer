Alliant Energy operates as a regulated investor-owned public utility holding company headquartered in Madison, Wisconsin, serving approximately 1.01 million electric and 435,000 natural gas customers in the Midwest through its two primary subsidiaries, Iowa Power & Light (IPL) and Wisconsin Power & Light (WPL). The company’s business model is centered on the generation, distribution, and transportation of electric and natural gas services within its service territories in Iowa and Wisconsin. While the core business is regulated retail utility service, the company also maintains a non-utility portfolio under Alliant Energy Financial (AEF), which includes minority interests in transmission companies, venture capital funds, a 50% stake in a non-utility wind farm, a leased natural gas-fired energy facility, and a supply chain solutions business. Additionally, the company benefits from significant barriers to entry associated with the capital-intensive nature of utility infrastructure, including generation facilities, transmission, and distribution networks. The filing highlights that IPL operates under non-exclusive franchises in Iowa and WPL operates under permits of indefinite duration in Wisconsin, creating stable, long-term revenue streams. Furthermore, the company leverages its existing infrastructure to offer value-added services, such as leasing underground conduit for fiber connectivity to data center customers and utilizing development-ready sites with transmission capabilities to attract industrial investment, thereby deepening its integration into the local economic ecosystem.

Management’s stated priorities focus on delivering affordable energy solutions, making customer-focused investments in reliability and resiliency, and growing customer demand. Specifically, the company aims to meet evolving customer expectations and deliver attractive returns for investors by advancing emerging technologies and generation capabilities. Management also prioritizes safety, employee engagement, and the retention of a qualified workforce, viewing these as critical to executing its purpose-driven strategy of serving customers and building stronger communities. The company is actively pursuing large-scale capital projects, including new natural gas resources, energy storage, and renewable generation, to address future load growth and resource adequacy requirements.

The company also faces risks associated with its capital-intensive business model, including the need for significant ongoing investment in generation, transmission, and distribution infrastructure. Additionally, the filing notes risks related to weather and temperature variations, which can significantly impact sales volumes and operating income. The company also highlights risks associated with its non-utility investments, including market volatility and the performance of its venture capital and transmission holdings.
