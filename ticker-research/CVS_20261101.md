## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CVS_10-K_20260906.txt)

CVS Health operates as an integrated health solutions company that connects care across four reportable segments: Health Care Benefits, Health Services, Pharmacy Consumer Wellness, and Corporate/Other. The company’s business model relies on a vertically integrated ecosystem that combines insurance, pharmacy benefits management (PBM), and direct care delivery. As of December 31, 2025, the company served approximately 37 million people through its Aetna insurance operations and managed benefits for approximately 87 million plan members through its CVS Caremark PBM. This integration allows CVS to dispense prescriptions in roughly 9,000 retail locations, operate over 1,000 walk-in and primary care clinics, and provide specialty pharmacy services. The company generates revenue from insurance premiums, product sales (primarily pharmaceuticals and general merchandise), and service fees, with the Health Care Benefits and Health Services segments representing the largest revenue contributors.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its scale and the synergies of its integrated model, which connects insurance, PBM, and care delivery assets. CVS leverages a nationwide provider network of approximately 2.0 million participating providers and utilizes its PBM position to negotiate pharmaceutical pricing and rebates. The company cites its ability to create new sources of value through personalized, technology-driven care delivery, including investments in artificial intelligence and digital platforms, as a key differentiator. Additionally, the company holds a leading position in the Medicare Part D prescription drug plan market and offers Medicare Advantage plans in 44 states and Washington, D.C., providing a significant foothold in the government-sponsored healthcare sector.

Management’s stated priorities focus on simplifying healthcare experiences, improving member engagement, lowering costs, and delivering better health outcomes. The company aims to be "America’s most trusted health care company" by transforming consumer experiences and harnessing enterprise capabilities. Strategic initiatives include expanding into personalized care delivery, increasing access to quality care, and leveraging innovation and capital stewardship to create sustainable shareholder value. Management emphasizes best-in-class execution and becoming the partner of choice for employers, individuals, and government entities.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to regulatory and market pressures. The company faces significant exposure to changes in Medicare and Medicaid funding and regulations, including potential reforms or surcharges. There is substantial legislative and regulatory activity seeking to regulate PBM activities, which could adversely affect the company’s ability to conduct business on commercially reasonable terms. Additionally, the company notes that elevated healthcare utilization levels may pressure its Health Care Benefits and Health Services segments. Other material risks include increasing pressure from pharmaceutical manufacturers regarding rebate calculations, the inability to always achieve minimum pricing guarantees for clients, and the impact of inflation, tariffs, and supply chain disruptions on costs and consumer demand. The company also exited the individual public health insurance exchanges effective January 2026, indicating a strategic shift in its commercial insurance distribution channels.
