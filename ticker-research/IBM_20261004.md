## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (IBM_10-K_20260906.txt)

IBM is a globally integrated enterprise that creates sustained value for clients by helping them leverage hybrid cloud and artificial intelligence to drive digital transformation. The company’s business model relies on a combination of software, consulting services, and mission-critical infrastructure, supported by a captive financing arm that facilitates client acquisitions of these products and services. IBM differentiates itself through deep incumbency in enterprise environments, a broad ecosystem of strategic partners—including hyperscalers like AWS and Microsoft, and software vendors like Salesforce and SAP—and one of the world’s leading research organizations. This integrated approach allows IBM to deliver end-to-end solutions that modernize applications and increase operational agility for clients.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its breadth of capabilities, deep industry expertise, and strong client relationships and trust. IBM positions itself as a leader in each of its business segments, leveraging technology innovation, performance, and quality to compete against a wide range of rivals, from large multinationals like Microsoft, Oracle, and Accenture to smaller, niche providers. In infrastructure, IBM differentiates through investments in security, scalability, and reliability for mission-critical and AI workloads, while in financing, it gains advantage through its ability to manage credit and residual value risk. The company also cites its talent, brand, and ability to deliver tangible business value as key methods of competition.

Management’s stated priorities center on executing a hybrid cloud and AI strategy to drive accelerated growth and prepare for future opportunities, such as quantum computing. To maintain leadership, IBM focuses on optimizing its portfolio through both organic and inorganic innovations and effective resource allocation. The company emphasizes bringing together different technologies to solve pressing business issues, leveraging its go-to-market reach and partner ecosystem to deliver solutions aligned with clients’ strategic priorities.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily stemming from a highly competitive environment where IBM faces hundreds of competitors worldwide and is regularly exposed to new entrants as it executes its strategy. Competition varies by segment, with software rivals including Alphabet, Amazon, and Broadcom; consulting competitors including Accenture and Capgemine; and infrastructure competitors including Dell, HPE, and Intel. Additionally, cloud service providers are leveraging innovation to compete with traditional providers, creating additional routes to market for server and storage systems. The company also notes that while it partners with many of these firms, they may be competitors in other areas, highlighting the complex nature of its market position.
