## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (BTSGU_10-K_20260906.txt)

BTSGU operates as a leading home and community-based healthcare services platform, delivering complementary pharmacy and provider services to medically complex Senior and Specialty patients, including those with behavioral health needs. The company’s business model is built on an integrated, scaled approach that addresses the high-touch, daily care requirements of patients who are often underserved by disjointed health systems. By serving over 465,000 patients daily across all 50 states through approximately 10,500 clinical providers and pharmacists, BTSGU leverages its national infrastructure to provide medication therapy management, infusion and specialty pharmacy services, home health, hospice, rehabilitation, and supportive care. This integrated model allows the company to manage longitudinal patient relationships, improve medication adherence, and reduce hospitalizations by delivering care in lower-cost, patient-preferred settings. The company serves Medicare, Medicaid, and commercially insured populations, with a significant portion of its revenue derived from patients with multiple chronic conditions who require both pharmacy and clinical interventions.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its market-leading scale, geographic density, and the complementary nature of its service offerings. BTSGU cites its ability to leverage economies of scale in purchasing, supplier contracting, technology, and payor relations as key differentiators. Its local, physical pharmacy network enables same-day service and in-person administration, which is critical for its target customer base and difficult for competitors to replicate. Furthermore, the integration of pharmacy and provider services creates a "white-glove" delivery model that supports value-based care strategies, such as home-based primary care and transitional care management. This integrated capability allows BTSGU to coordinate clinically integrated care across settings, bundle services for senior living communities and hospitals, and execute value-based contracts, including ACO shared savings arrangements. The company also highlights its quality outcomes, such as 99.99% order accuracy in pharmacy and hospitalization rates 35% lower than the national average in home-based primary care, as evidence of its operational excellence and differentiation.

Management’s stated priorities focus on expanding the integrated care model to drive optimized quality and reduced costs. Key strategic enablers include building out home-based primary care capabilities, customized transitional care management programs, and a clinical care coordination hub. The company aims to execute three primary strategies: coordinating clinically integrated care for patients receiving multiple services, providing bundled services to institutional customers like senior living communities and hospitals, and executing value-based care contracts both internally and with third-party payors. Additionally, management emphasizes the importance of maintaining operational excellence and leveraging scale to drive synergies post-acquisition, while continuing to grow in high-need, high-cost markets that represent a significant share of future healthcare expenditures.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the company’s reliance on a small number of large customers and payors, as well as its exposure to regulatory and reimbursement changes. The company notes that its business is heavily dependent on Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial insurance, and that changes in reimbursement rates or regulations could impact its financial performance. Additionally, the company faces risks associated with its acquisition strategy, including the costs and complexities of integrating acquired businesses, as evidenced by the substantial acquisition, integration, and transaction-related costs excluded from its non-GAAP measures. The filing also highlights legal and regulatory risks, referencing historical litigation matters and the need for ongoing compliance with healthcare regulations. Finally, the company acknowledges the competitive nature of the healthcare services industry and the risk that its integrated model may not be as effective as anticipated in driving value-based care outcomes.
