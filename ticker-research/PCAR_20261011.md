## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PCAR_10-K_20260827.txt)

PACCAR operates as a global technology company with a diversified business model spanning three primary segments: Truck, Parts, and Financial Services. The Truck segment designs and manufactures light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial vehicles, marketed under the Kenworth and Peterbilt nameplates in North America, DAF in Europe, and a mix of Kenworth and DAF in Mexico, Australia, and South America. The Parts segment distributes aftermarket components for these vehicles, while the Financial Services segment generates revenue by financing or leasing PACCAR products across four continents and 26 countries. This integrated structure allows the company to capture value across the vehicle lifecycle, with Financial Services supporting truck sales through a rigorous credit application process and a portfolio of loans and leases totaling $22.80 billion in assets.

The company’s competitive position is supported by its brand strength in premium trucking, evidenced by its market share of 29.9% in the U.S. and Canada heavy-duty market and 13.5% in the European over 16-tonne market in 2025. A significant structural advantage is its manufacturing footprint, which is described as optimally located to operate under new Section 232 truck tariffs; by producing trucks locally in Ohio, Texas, and Washington for the U.S., and in other regions for Canada and Mexico, PACCAR minimizes tariff exposure compared to competitors who may rely on cross-border imports. Additionally, the company maintains a strong financial profile, having earned net income for 87 consecutive years, and is investing heavily in next-generation technologies, including clean diesel, alternative powertrains, autonomous driving systems, and artificial intelligence, to sustain its competitive edge.

Management’s stated priorities focus on maintaining high-quality manufacturing, investing in innovation, and navigating regulatory and economic headwinds. The company is embedding artificial intelligence across its business to drive innovation and performance, while continuing to invest in its U.S.-based battery joint venture, Amplify Cell Technologies. Capital investment remains a key priority, with $728.5 million spent in 2025 on facilities such as a robotic chassis paint facility in Ohio and a new engine remanufacturing plant, alongside $445.5 million in research and development. Management also emphasizes the importance of the Financial Services segment in supporting market share growth, noting that PACCAR Financial Services increased its finance market share of new PACCAR truck sales to 27.0% in 2025.

The filing identifies several material risks, most notably the impact of U.S. import tariffs and trade policies, which have negatively affected truck order intake and profit margins since March 2025. The company explicitly flags the uncertainty surrounding the expected U.S. Supreme Court ruling on International Emergency Economic Power Acts (IEEPA) tariffs as a potential factor affecting results. Additionally, the company faces risks related to changing emissions regulations, specifically noting that the EPA’s reaffirmation of NOx limits could impact warranty and useful life requirements. Economic conditions remain a critical risk factor; management warns that if freight transportation conditions decline due to a weaker economy, the company could see increased past due accounts, truck repossessions, and credit losses, which would negatively impact the Financial Services segment. Finally, the company recorded a $264.5 million after-tax charge in 2025 related to civil litigation in Europe, highlighting the financial exposure associated with legal claims.
