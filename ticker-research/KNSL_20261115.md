## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KNSL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Kinsale Capital Group is a U.S.-based property and casualty insurer that operates exclusively within the excess and surplus lines (E&S) market, focusing on hard-to-place risks for small- to medium-sized commercial and personal accounts. The company’s business model relies on selling insurance products in all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands primarily through a network of independent brokers, while retaining full control over underwriting and claims management. In 2025, the company generated $2.0 billion in gross written premiums, with 97.0% derived from commercial lines and 3.0% from personal lines, achieving a combined ratio of 75.9% and a return on equity of 29.3%.

Kinsale’s competitive moat is built on four distinct pillars: exclusive focus on the E&S market, which historically offers higher margins than the standard admitted market; a proprietary technology platform that drives efficiency, reduces administrative errors, and allows for rapid response times to brokers; a significantly lower expense ratio (20.8% in 2025) compared to competitors, enabled by this technology and a culture of expense control; and a "contrarian" risk appetite that targets risks competitors may decline due to complexity or competition, thereby securing better pricing. Additionally, the company maintains a strong balance sheet with conservative reserves and investment practices, and it does not delegate underwriting authority to third parties, ensuring consistent pricing and coverage structure.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term stockholder value through consistent underwriting profits and prudent capital management. Key strategic objectives include expanding the company’s presence in the E&S market (currently holding approximately 1.5% market share), maintaining underwriting profitability regardless of market cycles, leveraging technology to drive efficiencies and scalability, and preserving a strong balance sheet to maintain confidence among policyholders, regulators, and investors. The management team, led by CEO Michael Kehoe, emphasizes an entrepreneurial approach and a track record of building high-performing specialty insurance companies.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the volatility of the insurance market and operational dependencies. Specifically, the company notes that its largest division, Commercial Property, experienced a 17.9% decline in gross written premiums in 2025 due to rate declines and an increasingly competitive environment, including encroachment by standard carriers. Other identified risks include exposure to catastrophe losses, which increased in 2025, and the inherent uncertainty in loss reserves, where 91.2% of total net loss reserves were for claims incurred but not reported. The company also acknowledges the need to continuously invest in and potentially replace its technology to maintain its competitive advantage and the impact of social inflation, wage inflation, and legal trends on loss costs.
