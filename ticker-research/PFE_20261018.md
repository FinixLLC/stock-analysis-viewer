## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PFE_10-K_20260827.txt)

Pfizer Inc. operates as a research-based, global biopharmaceutical company focused on the discovery, development, manufacture, marketing, and sale of biopharmaceutical products worldwide. Its business model relies heavily on generating revenue from the manufacture and sale of these medicines and vaccines, while simultaneously investing in a robust pipeline to replenish product lines that lose exclusivity or market share. The company manages its commercial operations through a global structure that, as of 2025, consisted of three operating segments—Biopharma, PC1, and Pfizer Ignite—with Biopharma being the only reportable segment. To support long-term growth, Pfizer actively pursues business development opportunities, including acquisitions such as Seagen and Metsera, and collaborates with healthcare providers and payors to expand patient access.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its intellectual property rights, including patents and regulatory exclusivities, which it vigorously defends against infringement and generic competition. Pfizer also leverages its scientific capabilities and platform technologies to address critical unmet patient needs, particularly in areas driven by an aging global population. Additionally, the company maintains a strong position through its extensive global manufacturing and distribution network, although it faces ongoing challenges related to the expiration of patent-based exclusivities, which it anticipates will significantly reduce revenues between 2026 and 2030.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 include maximizing the value of key transactions, delivering on critical research and development (R&D) milestones, investing to maximize post-2028 growth, and scaling artificial intelligence (AI) across the business. To achieve these goals, Pfizer is implementing digital enablement strategies, including automation and data-driven decision-making, to enhance productivity and accelerate innovation. The company is also executing multi-year cost realignment and manufacturing optimization programs to reduce costs of goods sold and align its cost base with longer-term revenue expectations, with a specific focus on improving productivity in selling, informational, and administrative (SIA) functions.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the loss of intellectual property rights and the expiration of patent-based or regulatory exclusivities, which are expected to cause a significant reduction in revenue over the next few years. Additionally, Pfizer faces substantial pricing and access pressures from governments and private payors globally, including the implementation of the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) in the U.S., which has already negatively impacted 2025 revenues by approximately $1 billion through the Medicare Part D redesign and the Maximum Fair Price program. The company also highlights risks related to the high degree of uncertainty in R&D, including the potential failure of clinical trials or commercial success, as well as the impact of legal matters, particularly product liability expenses related to discontinued or divested products.
