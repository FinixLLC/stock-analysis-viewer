## Business Profile

Source: live SEC EDGAR fetch — FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-27), Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) read and synthesized directly from source text; local cached 10-K text dump not present for TTD in this repo.

What the company does: The Trade Desk describes itself as "a global leader in advertising technology," operating a self-service, demand-side platform (DSP) that empowers ad buyers (advertising agencies, advertisers, and other service providers) to create, manage and optimize digital advertising campaigns across formats, channels and devices. Revenue is generated primarily as a platform fee based on a percentage of clients' total platform spend, plus value-added services and data. Clients enter ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) but retain the ability to choose spend levels and terminate with limited notice — a structural feature that makes client retention and spend growth an ongoing, not one-time, sales effort.

Industry positioning (from Item 1): TTD frames itself as an advocate for the buy side of a three-part digital-advertising ecosystem (buyers, sellers, marketplaces), contrasting its incentive alignment with participants who sit on the sell side or act as marketplace referees. The company cites total digital advertising spend of over $700B (more than 70% of total global ad-market spend), with total global advertising TAM having surpassed $1 trillion in 2024.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: TTD's stated strategic bets are (1) the generational shift from linear TV to connected TV (CTV), which it frames as a major structural tailwind as more ad dollars follow viewers to streaming, and (2) the continued shift of advertiser budgets from traditional to programmatic (algorithmically bought/targeted) advertising, and (3) AI-driven personalization and automation across the creative and campaign-execution lifecycle. The moat argument is essentially structural-tailwind-plus-execution: TTD isn't claiming a hard technical barrier to entry so much as being well-positioned inside multiple secular shifts (CTV, programmatic, AI) simultaneously — which makes the Q2 2026 growth deceleration (to just 3%) a meaningful data point against that thesis, since it suggests execution/demand issues are currently outweighing the structural tailwinds.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, discussed in flowing narrative form in this filing rather than a bulleted summary list):
1. Client retention and spend-growth risk — MSAs allow clients to choose spend amounts and terminate with limited notice; sustaining/growing revenue requires continuously adding new clients and expanding existing relationships, with an explicit acknowledgment that TTD could "reach a point of saturation" where internal client budget-allocation limits cap further growth from a given advertiser or agency.
2. Competitive/pricing pressure — if competitors introduce lower-cost or differentiated offerings, TTD's ability to sell to new or existing clients could be impaired; agencies/advertisers may react adversely to TTD's own pricing.
3. The filing's broader risk section (not fully captured in this read) covers additional categories typical of ad-tech platforms — data privacy/regulatory exposure, dependence on continued availability of third-party inventory and data partner integrations, and platform/technology execution risk — consistent with the "execution challenges" management itself cited on the Q2 2026 call.
