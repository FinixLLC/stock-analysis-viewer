## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K, `company_profile/financial_reports/AMD_10-K_20260708.txt` (inline-XBRL, converted and read directly), Item 1 (Business) and Item 7 (MD&A).

What the company does: AMD frames itself as driving "innovation in high performance and AI computing," offering full-stack AI-optimized CPUs, GPUs, networking and software across cloud/AI infrastructure, embedded systems, AI PCs and gaming. Three reportable segments (Client and Gaming merged into one segment starting Q1 FY2025):
- Data Center — EPYC server CPUs, Instinct GPUs (MI200 through MI350/MI355X), DPUs, AI NICs (Pensando "Pollara"/"Vulcano"), FPGAs/adaptive SoCs (post-Xilinx: Virtex, Versal, Zynq), and rack-scale "Helios" AI platform designs.
- Client and Gaming — Ryzen/Ryzen AI CPUs and APUs for desktops/notebooks (AI PC push with dedicated NPUs), Radeon discrete/professional GPUs, and semi-custom SoCs powering the Sony PlayStation 5, Microsoft Xbox Series S/X, and the new Valve Steam Machine.
- Embedded — embedded CPUs/APUs/FPGAs/SOMs for aerospace/defense, automotive, industrial, healthcare and communications infrastructure.

2025 strategic moves: Acquired ZT Group Int'l (ZT Systems) for data-center design capability, then sold the ZT manufacturing business to Sanmina (Oct 2025) while retaining the design IP/team. Entered a landmark 6-gigawatt, multiyear GPU product purchase agreement with OpenAI (Oct 2025), with the first gigawatt powered by Instinct MI450-series products. Announced (Aug 6, 2026) the acquisition of Taalas, a Toronto-based AI inference chip startup (founded 2023, ~$219M raised) that hardwires AI models directly into silicon — its HC1 demonstrator reportedly delivers ~17,000 tokens/sec/user on Llama 3.1 8B and claims 20x lower build cost and 10x lower power versus conventional HBM/advanced-packaging/liquid-cooled designs.

Moat / competitive positioning: PaoBull marks the moat intact. AMD's own filing emphasizes full-stack integration (compute + networking + systems + open ROCm software) as the differentiator versus point-product competitors, plus its position as the only company shipping x86 CPUs, discrete GPUs, FPGAs/adaptive SoCs and semi-custom silicon under one roof.

Risks (thematically drawn from the filing and current events): heavy reliance on TSMC for leading-edge (≤7nm) wafer production (single-source dependency); U.S. export-control exposure to China (the $440M MI308 charge already realized); customer concentration in a handful of hyperscale/AI-lab deals; intense competition from NVIDIA (GPUs), Intel (CPUs/APUs) and increasingly Huawei (AI compute, per EVCDM data below); and headline sensitivity to AI-customer-diversification news even when the underlying contracted demand (e.g., the OpenAI 6GW deal) is unaffected.
