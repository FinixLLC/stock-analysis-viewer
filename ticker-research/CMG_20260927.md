## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CMG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Chipotle Mexican Grill operates a fast-casual restaurant chain focused on serving responsibly sourced, classically cooked Mexican-inspired food, including burritos, bowls, and tacos, without artificial colors, flavors, or preservatives. As of year-end 2025, the company owned 3,938 U.S. restaurants and 104 international locations, with an additional 14 partner-operated international sites. The business model is primarily company-owned, generating revenue directly from restaurant sales, with digital channels representing 36.7% of food and beverage revenue. Chipotle manages its U.S. operations as a single reportable segment across 11 regions, relying on a supply chain of regional distribution centers and selected suppliers to maintain its "Food with Integrity" standards.

The company’s competitive position is built on a brand purpose centered on "Cultivating a Better World," differentiating itself through high-quality ingredients, animal welfare standards (such as "Responsibly Raised" meats), and a strong focus on food safety and quality assurance. Chipotle maintains a limited list of approved suppliers and utilizes advanced food safety technologies and third-party inspections to mitigate risk. Additionally, the company leverages its digital platform, including the Chipotle app and "Chipotlanes" (drive-through formats for digital pickup), to enhance guest experience and operational efficiency. While the filing notes that the fast-casual and quick-service segments are highly competitive regarding taste, price, and convenience, Chipotle distinguishes itself by combining higher-quality ingredients with rapid service and reasonable pricing, a combination it states is rare among competitors.

Management’s stated priorities, outlined in its "Recipe for Growth" strategy, focus on five key areas: protecting the core business through operational and culinary excellence; evolving brand messaging and menu innovation; modernizing the business model with industry-leading technology, including AI and a relaunched Rewards Program; expanding global reach through both company-owned and partner-operated markets; and cultivating top talent. The company emphasizes internal promotion, with nearly 90% of in-restaurant leadership roles filled internally in 2025, and invests heavily in employee development, mental health resources, and tuition assistance to maintain a competitive labor advantage. For 2026, management anticipates flat comparable restaurant sales and plans to open 350 to 370 new restaurants, with approximately 80% featuring a Chipotlane.

The filing identifies several material risks, including exposure to ingredient price volatility and supply shortages, particularly for key items like beef and chicken, which are sourced from a limited number of suppliers. The company notes that tariffs enacted in 2025 have impacted food costs, estimated to add about 15 basis points to ongoing costs. Labor costs have also risen as a percentage of revenue due to wage inflation and lower sales volumes. Additionally, Chipotle faces risks related to food safety incidents, which could damage its brand reputation, and competitive pressure from other restaurant formats and food delivery services. The company also highlights regulatory compliance costs and the potential impact of geopolitical crises, weather, and exchange rates on its supply chain and international operations.
