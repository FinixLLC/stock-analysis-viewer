## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/UNH_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2025-12-31 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text.

What the company does: UnitedHealth Group operates two complementary businesses. Optum is an information/technology-enabled health services business spanning three segments: Optum Health (patient-centered/value-based care, care management, wellness, and health financial services — serves ~95 million consumers and 100+ health payer partners, including Optum Financial/Optum Bank with ~26 million consumer accounts and $27B+ in AUM); Optum Insight (data, analytics, research, consulting, technology, and managed services); and Optum Rx (pharmacy care services). UnitedHealthcare offers health benefits through three lines: Employer & Individual, Medicare & Retirement, and Community & State (Medicaid). Effective 2026-01-01, the company realigned Optum Financial from Optum Health into Optum Insight (reportable segments unchanged).

Segment revenue mix: UnitedHealthcare grew faster than Optum in 2025 (+16% vs +7%), and CMS/Medicare premiums alone represent 44% of total consolidated revenue — underscoring how much of UNH's economics run through government-sponsored, risk-based programs whose cost trends the company doesn't fully control period-to-period.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: Scale across both the payer (UnitedHealthcare) and services/pharmacy/data (Optum) sides of the health system, integrated to "help build a modern, high-performing health system through improved access, affordability, outcomes and experiences." Optum Health's shift toward fully-accountable value-based arrangements (versus fee-for-service) is positioned as a structural differentiator versus pure insurers.

Management's stated priorities (from the filing and Q2 2026 commentary): stabilizing medical cost trends and margins via benefit-design and pricing actions; deliberately shrinking Medicare Advantage and Medicaid membership where funding/rates are insufficient rather than chasing volume; $1.5B AI investment to streamline operations; continued portfolio simplification (e.g., the South American operations exit).

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A, company's own priority ordering):
1. Medical cost estimation/pricing risk — explicitly the first-listed risk: "if we fail to estimate, price for and manage our medical costs... the profitability of our risk-based products... could decline." ~80% of consolidated revenue comes from risk-based products where premiums are fixed in advance but costs can (and in 2025 did) run above forecast. This is precisely what drove the 2025 earnings decline.
2. Regulatory/funding risk — Medicare Advantage funding pressure and Medicaid rate/eligibility mismatches, both explicitly disclosed as drivers of expected 2026 membership contraction.
3. Technology/data integrity risk — the ability to maintain, consolidate, and upgrade information systems, and to ensure technology products operate as intended, given the company's heavy reliance on IT across claims processing and care management.
4. Cybersecurity — the filing notes no cybersecurity incidents have materially affected the company to date (referencing the prior Change Healthcare cyberattack's lingering cash-flow effects being worked through as of 2025), but flags this as an ongoing risk category.

This aligns closely with the live news flow: the 2025 medical-cost shock and the company's 2026 remediation plan (benefit-design changes, deliberate membership contraction in Medicare Advantage/Medicaid, AI-driven cost efficiency) are the central through-line connecting the 10-K, the Q2 2026 print, and current analyst commentary.
