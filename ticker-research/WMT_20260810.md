## Business Profile

Source: freshly-fetched FY2026 10-K, Item 1 (Business) and Item 7 (MD&A), read and synthesized directly.

What the company does: Walmart describes itself as "a people-led, technology-powered omnichannel retailer" serving ~280 million customers weekly across more than 10,900 stores in 19 countries plus eCommerce. Three reportable segments:
- Walmart U.S. (largest, 68% of sales) — mass merchandiser under the "Walmart"/"Walmart Neighborhood Market" brands; three merchandise units (grocery, general merchandise, health & wellness); ~4,611 stores across all 50 states.
- Walmart International (19%) — 5,743 stores across 18 countries (Canada, Chile, China, Africa, India via majority-owned Flipkart/PhonePe, Mexico/Central America).
- Sam's Club U.S. (13%) — 601 membership warehouse clubs; membership income is a significant profit driver ($50/$110 annual tiers).

Strategy: "Make every day easier for busy families, operate with discipline, sharpen our culture and become more digital, and make trust a competitive advantage." Pricing philosophy centers on EDLP (everyday low price) backed by EDLC (everyday low cost discipline). Business is expanding into higher-margin adjacent offerings: digital advertising, marketplace/fulfillment services for third-party sellers, and financial services — explicitly framed as "mutually reinforcing pieces of our omnichannel model."

Moat, per the filing: Scale (192 U.S. + 179 international distribution facilities, ~2.1M associates), omnichannel integration (same-day pickup/delivery at 8,400+ locations), EDLP pricing discipline, and a growing ecosystem of higher-margin adjacent businesses (advertising, marketplace, Walmart+ membership). PaoBull independently marks the moat intact.

Management's stated priorities (from MD&A): Three financial priorities — Growth (omnichannel/membership/eCommerce expansion), Margin (productivity + mix shift toward higher-margin businesses like advertising), and Returns (ROI improvement via margin gains + disciplined capex). Increasing use of AI-powered tools across customer experience, associate productivity, and operations was repeatedly emphasized.

Risks management itself flags as most material:
- Failure to execute the omnichannel/AI/technology investment strategy, and the near-term cost drag of those investments
- Failure to predict changing consumer trends/preferences, including new risks from "emerging agentic platforms" and AI-driven search/discovery in retail
- Intense competition across brick-and-mortar, eCommerce, omnichannel and social-commerce formats, with rising competitive pressure from AI-enabled shopping tools developed by rivals
- Macro/tariff exposure: less than one-third of U.S.-sold merchandise is imported, with most imports from China, Mexico, Vietnam, India and Canada — an explicitly named tariff-sensitivity
- Supplier risk (financial instability, labor/safety compliance, transport disruption)
- Product quality/safety liability, including third-party marketplace liability exposure
- IT/cybersecurity — reliance on business-critical information systems processing high transaction volumes
- Natural disasters, climate change and geopolitical disruption to physical operations and supply chains
