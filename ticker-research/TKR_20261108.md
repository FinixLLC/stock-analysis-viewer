## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company is a global industrial manufacturer that designs, produces, and services engineered bearings and industrial motion products, leveraging over a century of expertise in metallurgy, friction management, and power transmission. Operating through two primary segments—Engineered Bearings and Industrial Motion—Timken serves a diverse customer base across sectors such as renewable energy, automotive, agriculture, aerospace, and heavy industry. The company’s business model relies on a combination of original equipment manufacturer (OEM) sales and a significant aftermarket presence, where it captures recurring revenue through equipment replacement cycles and service offerings like bearing repair and drivetrain maintenance. Timken differentiates itself through application engineering, product performance, and quality, utilizing a global footprint of 116 manufacturing facilities and 29 technology centers to support approximately 19,000 employees across 44 countries.

Timken’s competitive moat is anchored in its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, a position it has leveraged to expand into a broad portfolio of industry-leading products including linear motion solutions, industrial drives, and automatic lubrication systems. The company competes primarily on its total value proposition, which includes technical sales support, global delivery capabilities, and proprietary internal geometries that provide high load-carrying capacity and long service lives. While the industry is highly competitive, with major rivals including SKF Group and Schaeffler Group, Timken maintains its position by focusing on highly fragmented markets that demand high service levels and reliability, supported by strong brands such as Philadelphia Gear, Rollon, and Lovejoy.

Management’s stated strategic priorities focus on three core elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. The company aims to expand into attractive market sectors by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and friction management, with a particular emphasis on applications that offer significant aftermarket demand. Operational excellence is pursued through a continuous improvement culture designed to increase efficiency, lower costs, and build brand equity. Regarding capital allocation, Timken prioritizes investing in the core business through capital expenditures and R&D, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. In 2025, the company highlighted its commitment to sustainability, noting a 42% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions intensity from its 2018 baseline, and continued its streak of increasing annual dividends.

The filing identifies several material risks, with incremental tariff costs being a significant headwind that impacted 2025 results and is expected to continue affecting 2026. The company also faces risks related to lower demand across key market sectors, particularly in the Americas and Europe, which contributed to a decline in net income in 2025. Additionally, Timken is exposed to volatility in raw material costs, specifically special bar quality steel, which is subject to commodity price fluctuations for ferrous scrap, ore, and energy inputs. Other flagged risks include the impact of foreign currency exchange rate changes on sales and costs, as well as non-service pension and postretirement expenses, which saw significant mark-to-market losses in 2025. The company also notes that while it holds substantial backlog, actual shipments depend on customers' changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales.
