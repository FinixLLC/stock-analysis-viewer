## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ADP_10-K_20260906.txt)

ADP operates as a global leader in Human Capital Management (HCM), providing both software and outsourcing services designed to help clients manage their workforce through complex regulatory and business landscapes. The company’s business model is characterized by low capital intensity and significant cash flow generation, supported by a recurring revenue stream from over 1.1 million clients. A distinct feature of this model is the ability to earn interest income on client funds held for payroll and tax remittances, which contributed $1.35 billion to total revenues in fiscal 2026. The company is divided into two primary reportable segments: Employer Services, which offers payroll, tax, and HR solutions, and PEO Services, where ADP acts as a co-employer to provide benefits and administrative support.

ADP’s competitive moat is derived from its global scale, trusted brand, and deep domain expertise in a heavily regulated industry. Management highlights that the company leverages its data advantages to lead the industry’s AI transformation, embedding AI-powered agents into client workflows to improve accuracy and save time. The filing notes that ADP’s success depends on its ability to navigate a dynamic regulatory environment and maintain strong client relationships, evidenced by a 92.1% client revenue retention rate in Employer Services. Furthermore, the company’s position as a Certified PEO and its status as a co-employer create high barriers to entry, as these roles involve significant legal, tax, and ERISA obligations that require specialized compliance infrastructure.

Management’s stated strategic priorities focus on three key areas: leading with best-in-class HCM technology, providing unmatched expertise and outsourcing solutions, and leveraging global scale for client benefit. In fiscal 2026, the company emphasized its progress in integrating artificial intelligence, specifically launching ADP Assist agents across payroll, benefits, HR, and compliance solutions. Additionally, management prioritized driving enterprise sales momentum for its unified global payroll and HR solutions, such as ADP Lyric HCM and the ADP WorkForce Suite, while continuing to invest in research and development to enhance product offerings and internal productivity.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on legal and compliance obligations. ADP faces significant exposure to changes in U.S. and foreign laws, including tax regulations that could impact interest income from client funds or the viability of its Tax Credit Services business. The PEO business carries specific risks related to its co-employment status, including potential liabilities for wages and benefits if clients fail to remit payments, a risk that is magnified during financial disruptions. Additionally, the company highlights substantial risks associated with privacy, data protection, and cybersecurity, noting that non-compliance with regulations like the GDPR and the EU Artificial Intelligence Act could result in significant costs, penalties, and reputational damage. Finally, the filing flags risks related to anti-corruption, anti-money laundering, and sanctions compliance, particularly as the company expands globally and operates in developing economies where regulatory scrutiny is high.
