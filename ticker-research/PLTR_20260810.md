## Business Profile

Source: locally cached FY2025 10-K (`company_profile/financial_reports/PLTR_10-K_20260708.txt`), fiscal year ended 2025-12-31 — read and synthesized directly from Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors) source text.

What the company does: Palantir builds enterprise/government software that integrates organizations' data, decisions, and operations at scale, via four principal platforms: Gotham (defense/intelligence, situational awareness, mission planning — originally built for the U.S. intelligence community), Foundry (commercial data-operations "central operating system" — data pipelines, analytics, operational apps), Apollo (continuous-delivery/deployment infrastructure layer underlying the other platforms, cloud-agnostic), and AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform, launched 2023 — connects LLMs and other AI to an organization's data/operations via Palantir's "Ontology" data model, for building AI agents and automations). The company was founded in 2003, originally serving the U.S. intelligence community on counterterrorism work before expanding into commercial enterprise.

Segment/customer mix (FY2025): 954 total customers; 54% of $4.475B revenue from government, 46% from commercial. Top-20 customer average revenue nearly doubled YoY ($64.6M → $93.9M TTM), indicating strong account expansion (land-and-expand model) rather than just new-logo growth. Q2 2026 shows commercial — especially U.S. commercial — as the swing factor (+149% YoY).

Moat, as described in the company's own words:
- The company frames its core competitive threat as internal build efforts by potential customers, not just other vendors — i.e., Palantir competes primarily against the "we'll build it ourselves" instinct at large institutions, citing a real example of a U.S. military department spending over $1B on a failed internally-built enterprise resource-planning system.
- Deployment speed is positioned as the central differentiator — platforms "ready in days" versus internal builds taking months/years, reinforced by "AIP bootcamps" that deliver real workflows on live customer data in days.
- The Ontology (systematic mapping of data, logic, and actions into a unified representation of the organization) is described as the architectural core that differentiates Palantir from point-solution competitors.
- Deep, historically unique relationships with U.S. defense/intelligence agencies — the company explicitly states it believes it is "uniquely positioned" to provide commercially available software to the U.S. federal government and intends to capture a greater share of federal software spending.
- Privacy/governance engineering (dedicated Privacy and Civil Liberties team, granular access controls, AI governance tooling) is positioned as both a moat and a prerequisite for government/regulated-industry trust.

Management's stated priorities (from Item 1): continue building out AIP and Ontology-driven AI governance capabilities; expand U.S. commercial sales via direct sales force and "industry operating system" partnerships (airline, space, shipbuilding, insurance, healthcare, telecom, automotive); grow international and channel/cloud-partner relationships (e.g., Fujitsu joint venture in Japan); expand federal government market share.

Risks the company itself flags as most material (Item 1A summary, in the company's own ordering):
1. Revenue growth sustainability — explicit statement that past growth (now +56% FY2025, +93% Q2 2026) may not continue at current rates.
2. Long, unpredictable sales cycles and considerable sales-effort cost/time.
3. Customer concentration — a limited number of customers account for a substantial portion of revenue.
4. Contract terms — many customer contracts (notably government) can be terminated by the customer at any time for convenience.
5. Government dependency risk — explicitly flags "decline in U.S. and other government budgets, changes in spending or budgetary priorities, or delays in contract awards" as a named risk.
6. AI-specific risk — "issues raised by the use of AI... may result in reputational harm or liability," reflecting the centrality of AIP/LLM integration to the current growth story and its double-edged nature.
7. Governance/control structure — multi-class stock, Founder Voting Trust Agreement, and Founder Voting Agreement concentrate voting power with Stephen Cohen, Alexander Karp, and Peter Thiel — a named risk factor for minority shareholders.
8. Reputational/political scrutiny — explicitly flags that "news or social media coverage or other external scrutiny of Palantir or our leadership" could harm the business, an unusually direct acknowledgment given the company's public political profile.
