## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that builds integrated platforms to help organizations manage data, logic, and operations at scale. Originally developed for the U.S. intelligence community for counterterrorism, the company now serves both government and commercial sectors, generating $4.5 billion in revenue in 2025, with 54% derived from government customers and 46% from commercial entities. The business model relies on four principal software platforms: Gotham, which supports defense and intelligence missions; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform serving as a central operating system for enterprises; Apollo, a continuous delivery platform that enables secure software deployment in virtually any environment; and AIP, a generative AI platform that connects large language models to organizational data for decision-making. These platforms are anchored by the Palantir Ontology, a systematic mapping of data to meaningful context that integrates data, logic, and actions into a unified decision-centric architecture. Customers typically enter into contracts ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the term, and the company reports a dollar-weighted average contract duration of four years for contracts closed in 2025.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its vertically integrated platform architecture and its focus on high-complexity, large-scale opportunities that deter smaller competitors. Palantir targets institutions with complex data environments and high installation costs, areas where the company argues it has a distinct advantage due to its ability to deploy solutions in days rather than the months or years required for internal development or traditional vendor implementations. The filing highlights that the high failure risks and long sales cycles associated with these complex projects raise barriers to entry for competition. Additionally, the company leverages its "Ontology" to create deep integration into customer workflows, making the software "sticky" by enabling rapid experimentation and testing. Palantir also expands its reach through strategic partnerships, including cloud provider alliances that provide access to large enterprise customer bases, and industry-specific operating systems developed with partners in sectors such as healthcare, automotive, and government.

Management’s stated priorities center on expanding access to its platforms across a broader swath of the market while maintaining deep, mission-critical relationships with existing customers. The company emphasizes a strategy of "expanding access" through mechanisms like AIP bootcamps and Developer Tier offerings to lower entry barriers and accelerate adoption. A key priority is capturing a greater share of U.S. federal government software spending, leveraging its position as a provider of commercially available software to the public sector. Furthermore, management is focused on driving account growth by scaling contracts horizontally across divisions and vertically across user groups, as well as forming joint ventures and new business partnerships to embed Palantir’s capabilities into broader industry solutions. The company also notes a short-term focus on making its platforms available to increasingly broad markets while identifying component parts within those platforms that have potential as standalone commercial offerings.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the nature of its customer base and macroeconomic conditions. A significant risk is the dependence on government customers, which are subject to uncertainties regarding budgets, spending levels, and regulatory changes that can make contract awards unpredictable. Many contracts, particularly those with the U.S. federal government, contain termination for convenience provisions, and the government is prohibited from exercising contract options more than one year in advance, creating uncertainty around future revenue. The company also highlights risks associated with geopolitical tensions, such as the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, which could disrupt operations or the global economy, although it states these currently do not have a material impact on its financial position. Additionally, the filing notes exposure to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations and macroeconomic factors like interest rates and trade relations, which can affect both the company and its customers. Finally, there is inherent risk in the assumption that customers will exercise all contractual options and not terminate agreements, which underpins the company’s reported total remaining deal value of $11.2 billion as of December 31, 2025.
