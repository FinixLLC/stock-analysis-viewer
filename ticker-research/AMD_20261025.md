## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (AMD_10-K_20260906.txt)

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) operates as a high-performance computing and artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor company, delivering full-stack solutions that span cloud infrastructure, embedded systems, AI PCs, and gaming. The company’s business model is built on a broad portfolio of AI-optimized CPUs, GPUs, networking hardware, and software, which it sells through three reportable segments: Data Center, Client and Gaming, and Embedded. In 2025, AMD generated $34.6 billion in net revenue, a 34% year-over-year increase, driven primarily by strong demand for its 5th generation EPYC server processors and Instinct MI350 Series AI accelerators in the Data Center segment, as well as robust performance in the Client and Gaming segment. AMD differentiates itself by integrating dedicated neural processing units (NPUs) into x86 CPUs for AI PCs and by offering rack-scale platform designs that combine its CPUs, GPUs, and networking products to address large-scale AI workloads.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its ability to provide end-to-end AI leadership across the entire computing stack, rather than just individual chips. Management emphasizes that AMD is uniquely positioned to combine industry-leading compute engines with comprehensive software stacks, specifically the open-source ROCm platform, to support diverse AI ecosystems. This full-stack approach is reinforced by strategic acquisitions, such as ZT Systems, which allowed AMD to retain design intellectual property while outsourcing manufacturing to Sanmina, and other acquisitions aimed at enhancing software capabilities, co-packaged optics, and high-speed inference expertise. Furthermore, AMD has secured significant market validation through a multiyear strategic partnership with OpenAI, which includes a product purchase agreement for 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs and a warrant for up to 160 million shares, signaling deep integration into the leading AI infrastructure buildout.

Management’s stated priorities for 2025 centered on accelerating growth in the Data Center segment and executing a comprehensive AI strategy. Key initiatives included advancing the annual cadence of leadership for AMD Instinct AI GPUs, launching the 5th Gen AMD EPYC family, and previewing the Helios AI rack-scale platform. Beyond hardware, a major priority was strengthening the software ecosystem to improve performance for generative AI workloads and simplify the developer experience. The company also focused on expanding its presence in client computing by launching Ryzen AI processors with next-generation NPUs to capture the growing market for AI-enabled PCs, while continuing to invest in its embedded portfolio for automotive, industrial, and healthcare applications.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of U.S. government export controls on sales to China. In 2025, AMD recorded approximately $800 million in inventory and related charges due to new restrictions on its MI308 Data Center GPUs, although it reversed approximately $360 million of these charges in the fourth quarter after obtaining licenses to ship to certain customers. The company notes that U.S. officials have expressed an expectation that the government will receive 15% of revenue from licensed MI308 sales to China, a requirement that has not yet been formalized in regulation. Other significant risks include the concentration of revenue among a small number of customers, the need to maintain sufficient external financing, and the ongoing costs associated with enhancing information security controls. Additionally, the company faces risks related to the evolving legal and regulatory environment for emerging technologies like AI, as well as the uncertainty surrounding the integration of recent acquisitions and the achievement of their expected benefits.
