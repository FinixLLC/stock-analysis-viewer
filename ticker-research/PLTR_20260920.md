## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PLTR_10-K_20260906.txt)

Palantir Technologies is a software company founded in 2003 that provides integrated platforms to help organizations manage data, logic, and operations at scale. The company’s business model centers on four principal software platforms: Gotham, which serves defense and intelligence agencies; Foundry, a foundational data operations platform for commercial and government enterprises; Apollo, a continuous delivery infrastructure layer; and AIP, a generative artificial intelligence platform launched in 2023 that connects large language models to enterprise data and workflows. Palantir generates revenue primarily through long-term contracts, typically ranging from one to five years, with revenue recognized ratably over the contract term. As of 2025, the company had 954 customers, with 54% of revenue derived from government entities and 46% from commercial sectors, while 74% of total revenue originated from the United States.

The company’s competitive moat is built on its vertically integrated architecture, specifically the "Ontology," which maps data to meaningful context and integrates data, logic, and actions into a decision-centric system. This structure allows users of varying technical skills to collaborate and build interconnected workflows, creating a "central operating system" for institutions. Palantir targets large-scale, complex, and high-risk implementation opportunities that smaller competitors often avoid due to high installation costs and failure risks. The company leverages its ability to deploy solutions in days rather than months, often through "bootcamps" and direct embedding with customers, to establish deep trust and lock-in. Additionally, Palantir utilizes cloud partnerships and industry-specific operating systems to expand distribution and scale its reach across sectors such as healthcare, automotive, and government.

Management’s stated priorities include expanding access to its platforms across a broader swath of the market, both commercial and government, while continuing to deepen relationships with existing customers. The company is focused on capturing a greater share of U.S. federal government software spending and developing industry-specific operating systems to serve entire sectors. A key strategic initiative is the integration of AIP to enable "responsible AI-advantage," allowing customers to utilize LLMs within secure, audited, and legally compliant frameworks. Palantir also prioritizes building strategic partnerships, including joint ventures and cloud alliances, to extend its platform capabilities and distribution channels.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the public sector and macroeconomic conditions. A significant portion of revenue comes from government customers, which are subject to budget uncertainties, changes in spending priorities, and regulatory shifts that can impact contract timing and size. Many contracts contain termination for convenience provisions, and the U.S. federal government is prohibited from exercising contract options more than one year in advance, creating uncertainty in future revenue realization. Additionally, the company faces risks from geopolitical tensions, foreign currency fluctuations, and macroeconomic disruptions such as interest rate changes and trade relations. While management states that current geopolitical conflicts are not expected to have a material impact, ongoing instability could negatively affect operations and results.
