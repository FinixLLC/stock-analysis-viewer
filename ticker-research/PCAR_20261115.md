## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (PCAR_10-K_20260827.txt)

PACCAR operates as a global technology company with a diversified business model comprising three primary segments: Truck, Parts, and Financial Services. The Truck segment designs and manufactures light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial vehicles, marketed under the Kenworth and Peterbilt nameplates in North America, DAF in Europe, and a mix of Kenworth and DAF in Mexico, Australia, and South America. The Parts segment distributes aftermarket components for these vehicles, while the Financial Services segment generates revenue by financing and leasing PACCAR products across four continents and 26 countries. This integrated structure allows the company to capture value across the entire vehicle lifecycle, from initial sale through aftermarket support and financing.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its strong brand equity, evidenced by its consistent market share leadership in key regions, such as holding approximately 30% of the U.S. and Canada heavy-duty truck market and over 13% of the European over-16-tonne market. PACCAR differentiates itself through a consistent focus on quality, which supports premium pricing and strong customer loyalty, as well as through its vertically integrated Financial Services arm. This captive financing channel not only drives truck sales but also generates significant recurring revenue and interest income, supported by a rigorous credit application process that maintains a high-quality loan and lease portfolio. Additionally, the company’s local manufacturing footprint in North America and Europe minimizes tariff exposure and supply chain risks, providing a structural advantage over competitors reliant on imported components.

Management’s stated priorities center on maintaining long-term profitability, investing in innovation, and navigating macroeconomic headwinds. The company has committed to sustaining capital investments of $725 to $775 million and R&D spending of $450 to $500 million in 2026, focusing on next-generation clean diesel engines, alternative powertrains, autonomous driving technologies, and the integration of artificial intelligence across its business. PACCAR also prioritizes operational efficiency and customer value, as seen in recent facility expansions for robotic painting and engine remanufacturing. Furthermore, management is actively managing the impact of U.S. import tariffs by leveraging local production capabilities and adjusting pricing strategies, while continuing to grow its financial services portfolio through market share gains in new truck financing.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the adverse impact of U.S. import tariffs and trade policies on order intake and profit margins, which have already reduced truck revenues and increased costs. The company notes that while local production mitigates some exposure, the ongoing effects of tariffs remain unfavorable. Additionally, PACCAR faces significant regulatory and legal risks, highlighted by a $350 million pre-tax charge in 2025 related to civil litigation in Europe concerning EC-related claims. Economic cyclicality is another key risk, as the company’s performance is heavily tied to freight transportation conditions and general economic health; a decline in these areas could lead to increased credit losses, higher repossessions, and lower demand for both new trucks and parts. Finally, the company is exposed to changes in emissions regulations, such as EPA NOx limits, which could impact product design and warranty requirements.
