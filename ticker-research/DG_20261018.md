## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (DG_10-K_20260906.txt)

Dollar General is the largest discount retailer in the United States by store count, operating 20,959 locations across 48 U.S. states and Mexico as of February 2026. The company’s business model centers on providing everyday low prices, typically $10 or less, on a broad selection of consumables, seasonal items, home products, and apparel through convenient, small-box stores averaging approximately 7,500 square feet. Approximately 80% of these stores are located in towns with populations of 20,000 or fewer, serving a core customer base of low- and fixed-income households. The merchandise mix is heavily weighted toward consumables, which accounted for 82.0% of net sales in fiscal 2025, supplemented by private brand offerings that are priced at substantial discounts to national brands.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its "compelling value and convenience proposition," which combines highly competitive pricing with a high-density store network. Management notes that approximately 75% of the U.S. population lives within five miles of a Dollar General store, facilitating frequent trip frequency and customer loyalty in both rural and urban markets. This advantage is supported by a low-cost operating approach, a limited assortment per category to maintain strong purchasing power, and a simple store format that allows for attractive store economics and strong cash flow generation. While the company acknowledges intense competition from Walmart, Family Dollar, and Dollar Tree, it differentiates itself through its proximity to customers and its ability to serve markets with limited shopping alternatives.

Management’s stated long-term operating priorities are fourfold: driving profitable sales growth, capturing growth opportunities, enhancing the company’s position as a low-cost operator, and investing in the growth and development of its teams. To execute these priorities, the company is focusing on initiatives such as the "Project Elevate" and "Project Renovate" remodel programs to refresh store layouts, expanding digital tools including the Dollar General app and delivery services, and optimizing inventory shrink and damage reduction. In fiscal 2025, the company opened 589 new stores, remodeled over 4,000 stores, and closed 290 locations, while pausing new expansion of its pOpshelf format to evaluate its strategy.

The filing identifies several material risks, with significant emphasis on the macroeconomic environment affecting its core customers. Management highlights that customers are often among the first to be affected by negative economic conditions, citing pressures from inflation, wage growth, and changes in government assistance programs like SNAP. A particularly prominent risk is the uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policy and tariffs; while tariff rates did not materially impact financial results in 2025, management warns that current and potential future tariffs could significantly impact the business and customer budgets, especially following the Supreme Court’s invalidation of tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. Additionally, the company flags rising operating costs, including wage rates, occupancy costs, and new store opening expenses driven by inflation and interest rates, as well as the need to manage elevated inventory shrink and damage levels.
