## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (RVMD_10-K_20260827.txt)

Revolution Medicines (RVMD) is a clinical-stage precision oncology company focused on developing novel targeted therapies for RAS-addicted cancers. The company’s business model relies on its proprietary structure-based drug discovery platform, which utilizes deep chemical biology and cancer pharmacology know-how to create small molecules that bind to unconventional RAS binding sites. Unlike first-generation KRAS(OFF) inhibitors, RVMD’s pipeline consists of RAS(ON) Inhibitors designed to directly inhibit active RAS variants, including daraxonrasib (multi-selective), zoldonrasib (G12D-selective), elironrasib (G12C-selective), and RMC-5127 (G12V-selective). The company generates minimal revenue, having previously recognized collaboration revenue from a terminated Sanofi agreement, and currently operates at a loss with R&D expenses expected to increase as it advances multiple Phase 3 registrational trials in pancreatic cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, and colorectal cancer.

RVMD’s competitive moat is derived from its innovative mechanism of action, specifically the development of tri-complex inhibitors that target the RAS(ON) state rather than the RAS(OFF) state, allowing for broader selectivity across major RAS mutation hotspots (G12, G13, Q61). This approach is designed to overcome resistance mechanisms associated with earlier therapies and address unmet needs in cancers with high RAS mutation prevalence, such as pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), where over 90% of diagnoses involve RAS mutations. The company has secured several regulatory distinctions, including Breakthrough Therapy Designations for daraxonrasib, zoldonrasib, and elironrasib, as well as Orphan Drug Designation and a Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher for daraxonrasib in PDAC. Additionally, RVMD leverages strategic collaborations with major pharmaceutical companies, including Bristol Myers Squibb, Amgen, Summit Therapeutics, and Tango Therapeutics, to evaluate combination regimens and expand its clinical reach.

Management’s stated priorities center on advancing its deep pipeline of RAS(ON) Inhibitors through pivotal clinical trials, with a specific focus on achieving regulatory approval for daraxonrasib and zoldonrasib in pancreatic cancer and non-small cell lung cancer. The company is actively executing global, randomized Phase 3 registrational studies, such as RASolute 302, 303, 304, and 305 for PDAC, and RASolve 301 for NSCLC, with key data readouts expected in 2026. RVMD is also prioritizing the exploration of combination therapies to maximize clinical impact, particularly in colorectal cancer, and is investing in new classes of RAS(ON) Inhibitors to address drug resistance, with a first-in-human trial for this new class expected to initiate in late 2026. The company also emphasizes the importance of biomarker identification and AI-driven lead discovery through collaborations with Iambic Therapeutics and Break Through Cancer to enhance its development capabilities.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the inherent uncertainty and length of the regulatory approval process, with the company noting that it has not yet obtained approval for any product candidate and that failure to secure approval could substantially harm the business. Clinical trial risks are significant, including the potential for delays, failure to meet enrollment targets, and the possibility that interim or topline data may differ materially from final results. The company also highlights the risk of competition, where competitors may develop safer, more effective, or less expensive products, or obtain marketing approval more rapidly, thereby establishing a strong market position before RVMD can enter. Additionally, RVMD faces risks related to its reliance on third-party contractors for manufacturing and clinical trials, as well as the potential for regulatory changes, such as the implementation of the EU Clinical Trials Regulation, which could impact its development plans. The filing also notes the risk that even if approved, products may be priced at a premium over competitive products, reducing their competitiveness.
