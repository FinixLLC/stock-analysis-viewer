## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (KNSL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Kinsale Capital Group is a U.S.-domiciled property and casualty insurer that operates exclusively within the excess and surplus lines (E&S) market, focusing on hard-to-place risks for small- to medium-sized commercial accounts and personal lines. The company’s business model relies on selling insurance products in all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands primarily through a network of independent brokers, while retaining full control over underwriting and claims management. In 2025, the company generated $2.0 billion in gross written premiums, with a portfolio composition of 70.7% casualty and 29.3% property, and achieved a combined ratio of 75.9% and a return on equity of 29.3%.

Kinsale’s competitive moat is built on four distinct pillars: exclusive focus on the E&S market, which historically offers higher margins than the standard admitted market; a proprietary technology platform that drives efficiency, reduces administrative error, and enables rapid response times to brokers; a significantly lower expense ratio (20.8% in 2025) resulting from this technological efficiency and a culture of cost control; and a "contrarian" underwriting appetite that targets risks competitors may decline, thereby reducing competition and improving pricing power. The company emphasizes that it does not delegate underwriting authority to brokers and manages all claims in-house, leveraging a management team with an average of over 30 years of relevant experience.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering long-term stockholder value through consistent underwriting profits and prudent capital management. Key strategic objectives include expanding market share in the E&S sector (currently approximately 1.5% of the $129.8 billion market), maintaining a strong balance sheet with conservative reserves and investment portfolios, and leveraging technology to drive efficiencies and scalability. The company aims to grow its business by targeting classes where its underwriters have deep expertise, while maintaining adequate rate levels to ensure profitability regardless of market cycles.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the volatility of losses and loss adjustment expenses, which are influenced by claim frequency, severity, legal trends, wage inflation, social inflation, and medical costs. Specifically, the company notes that its largest division, Commercial Property, experienced a 17.9% decline in gross written premiums in 2025 due to rate declines and an increasingly competitive environment from standard carriers. Additionally, the company acknowledges exposure to catastrophe losses, which contributed to a 1.9% loss ratio component in 2025, and highlights the inherent uncertainty in estimating losses incurred but not reported, which constituted approximately 91.2% of total net loss reserves as of December 31, 2025.
