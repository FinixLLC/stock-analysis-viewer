## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (CRWD_10-K_20260826.txt)

CrowdStrike, founded in 2011, operates as a cloud-native cybersecurity provider that delivers its Falcon platform via a SaaS subscription model. The company’s business model is built on a "land-and-expand" strategy, where customers initially deploy a single lightweight sensor to collect data across endpoints, cloud workloads, and identities. This data is ingested once and reused across 33 integrated cloud modules, allowing customers to add functionality over time without deploying additional agents. Revenue is generated primarily through ratable subscription fees priced per endpoint and module, supplemented by professional services such as incident response, which the company views as a lead generator for new subscriptions.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its cloud-native architecture and the resulting network effects of its "Security Cloud." By aggregating trillions of cybersecurity events per week from a global customer base, CrowdStrike creates a massive, high-fidelity dataset that continuously trains its AI models. This "cloud-scale AI" allows for real-time threat detection and prevention that the company argues is superior to legacy on-premise or signature-based solutions, which it characterizes as siloed, slow, and ineffective against modern, malware-free attacks. The integration of this data with proprietary graph technologies (Threat, Intel, and Asset Graphs) enables the platform to correlate disparate signals into actionable intelligence, creating a barrier to entry for competitors who lack similar data volume and architectural integration.

Management’s stated priorities focus on maintaining high growth in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), which reached $5.3 billion in fiscal 2026, and sustaining strong dollar-based net retention rates, which stood at 115% as of January 31, 2026. The company emphasizes the need to continue investing in sales and marketing to expand its customer base globally, while also investing in research and development to rapidly deploy new cloud modules. Additionally, management highlights the importance of reducing customer complexity and total cost of ownership by consolidating fragmented legacy security tools into the unified Falcon platform.

The most material risks flagged in the filing relate to the aftermath of the July 19, 2024 incident, where a content update caused system crashes for certain Windows users. This event has resulted in ongoing lawsuits, increased legal and professional service expenses, and adverse effects on key business metrics. Specifically, the company reports delays in creating sales opportunities, longer sales cycles, and customer churn risks due to multi-year contract terms. To retain customers, CrowdStrike has offered commitment packages involving discounting and flexible terms, which have led to increased contraction and decreased upsell values. Furthermore, the company notes that its future success depends on the continued adoption of cloud-based security solutions over legacy on-premise products, a transition that remains difficult to predict due to existing customer investments in traditional infrastructure.
