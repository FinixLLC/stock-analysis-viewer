## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX Companies operates as the leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer in the United States and worldwide, managing over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four primary segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is built on "opportunistic buying," a strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise closer to the time of sale rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to source from a diverse universe of approximately 21,000 vendors globally, capturing closeouts, special productions, and order cancellations to offer quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers. By maintaining lean inventory levels and utilizing flexible store layouts without permanent department walls, TJX creates a "treasure hunt" shopping experience that drives frequent customer visits and rapid inventory turnover.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from this flexible supply chain and its ability to leverage substantial global buying power. TJX’s buyers have greater visibility into consumer trends and pricing when buying closer to need, which helps reduce markdown exposure. Additionally, the company maintains a low cost structure compared to traditional retailers, achieved through efficient distribution networks, minimal advertising spend focused on banners rather than products, and store designs that avoid heavy fixture investments. The filing notes that TJX’s willingness to purchase less-than-full assortments, pay promptly, and avoid typical retail concessions makes it an attractive channel for vendors, ensuring continued access to leading branded merchandise.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day and expanding the business through new store growth, which remains a key part of the global growth strategy. The company aims to maintain its pricing gap relative to traditional retailers while managing costs effectively. In fiscal 2026, management highlighted the importance of mitigating tariff pressures through its sourcing model and returned $4.3 billion to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends. The filing also notes a focus on enhancing the customer shopping experience through strategic store renovations and upgrades.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include significant uncertainty surrounding international trade relations and tariffs, particularly those imposed on imports from China and other countries. While management believes it can mitigate these pressures, the filing acknowledges that tariffs have led to significant global economic volatility and that the impact on direct imports, vendor pricing, and consumer demand remains uncertain. Additionally, the company faces risks related to foreign currency exchange rates, which affect operating results through translation, mark-to-market adjustments on inventory derivatives, and transactional gains or losses. The filing also highlights the competitive nature of the retail apparel and home fashion industry, where TJX competes on brand, price, quality, and shopping experience against a wide range of department, specialty, discount, and online retailers.
