## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ORCL_10-K_20260906.txt)

Oracle operates as a provider of enterprise IT frameworks, delivering products and services that build, run, and support business operations through a hybrid model of cloud, on-premise, and hybrid deployments. The company’s business model is anchored in three distinct segments: cloud and software, hardware, and services, with the cloud and software segment representing the vast majority of revenue at 87% in fiscal 2026. Oracle generates revenue through subscription-based cloud services (Oracle Cloud Applications and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure), perpetual software licenses, recurring software and hardware support contracts, and professional services. A central strategic pillar is the migration of its extensive installed base of on-premise customers to the Oracle Cloud, a transition that has driven cloud revenues to 51% of total company revenue in fiscal 2026, up from 37% in fiscal 2024. The company emphasizes flexibility and interoperability, allowing customers to choose deployment models that suit their specific needs, while integrating artificial intelligence capabilities into its applications and infrastructure to enhance automation and analytics.

Oracle’s competitive moat is derived from the comprehensiveness and breadth of its integrated portfolio, which management argues differentiates it from competitors offering more limited or specialized applications. The company leverages a deep understanding of how applications and infrastructure interact, using its own Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) to power its Oracle Cloud Applications (OCA), creating a tightly integrated ecosystem. This integration is supported by industry-leading infrastructure technologies, including the Oracle AI Database, MySQL, and Java, which are described as foundational building blocks for enterprise IT. Furthermore, Oracle maintains a significant advantage through its massive installed base of software licenses, where substantially all customers purchase support contracts, creating a recurring revenue stream and a pathway for migrating these workloads to the cloud. The company also cites its flexible deployment models, including multicloud options and Oracle Exadata Cloud@Customer, as key differentiators that better address customer needs for choice and interoperability relative to competitors.

Management’s stated priorities center on expanding the Oracle Cloud business by facilitating the migration of existing on-premise and cloud-based workloads to Oracle’s cloud environment. The company is actively investing in research and development, with expenditures increasing to $10.3 billion in fiscal 2026, to enhance its existing portfolio and develop new AI-driven technologies. Strategic focus areas include improving the performance, security, and cost-effectiveness of offerings, incorporating emerging technologies like generative AI to enable leaner business processes, and expanding the geographic footprint and capacity of its cloud operations. Additionally, management prioritizes maintaining a selective acquisition program to integrate complementary technologies and continues to emphasize direct sales through its worldwide sales force while also utilizing indirect channels for certain offerings.

The filing flags several material risks that could impact Oracle’s financial condition and results of operations. Key risks include the company’s ability to manage Oracle Cloud capacity requirements to meet existing and prospective customer demand, as well as the impact of general economic conditions, inflation, tariffs, and trade policies on both customer demand and supply chain costs. Management notes that margins may be adversely affected by increases in supply chain and energy costs and the inability to pass such costs on to customers. Other significant risks include governmental budgetary constraints, foreign currency rate fluctuations, and the competitive position of Oracle’s offerings relative to other cloud infrastructure providers. The company also highlights the uncertainty in quarterly hardware revenues due to the point-in-time nature of large transactions and the dependence on manufacturing partners’ ability to deliver cost-effectively. Finally, the filing identifies income taxes as a critical accounting estimate, noting that uncertainties in global tax calculations and the final tax outcome of various transactions could materially impact reported results.
