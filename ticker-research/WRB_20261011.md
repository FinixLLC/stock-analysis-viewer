## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (WRB_10-K_20260827.txt)

W. R. Berkley Corporation operates as a decentralized insurance holding company and one of the largest commercial lines writers in the United States, generating revenue through two primary segments: Insurance and Reinsurance Monoline Excess. The company’s business model relies on a network of 60 distinct businesses, 53 of which were developed internally, that specialize in niche markets requiring specialized knowledge. This structure allows individual units to respond quickly to local market conditions and specific customer needs while leveraging centralized capital, investment, reinsurance, and compliance support. The Insurance segment, which accounted for 88% of net premiums written in 2025, focuses on commercial and specialty personal lines, including excess and surplus lines, industry specialties, and regional products across the U.S. and international markets. The Reinsurance Monoline Excess segment provides facultative and treaty reinsurance and retains risk on an excess basis.

The company’s competitive position is supported by its ability to serve complex, hard-to-place risks that fall outside standard underwriting guidelines, as well as its strong financial standing. Thirty-three of its insurance subsidiaries hold A.M. Best financial strength ratings of A+ (Superior), while others hold AA- ratings from S&P and Fitch, and A1 from Moody’s. This high credit quality, combined with a decentralized structure that enables rapid adaptation to niche opportunities and a focus on maintaining a high-quality balance sheet, forms the basis of its competitive advantage.

Management’s stated priorities center on meeting customer needs, maintaining a high-quality balance sheet, and allocating capital to the best opportunities. A key strategic component is the formation of new businesses to capitalize on emerging opportunities in sectors such as healthcare, cyber security, energy, and agriculture, as well as in growing international markets like Asia-Pacific, South America, and Mexico. Management emphasizes that new ventures are initiated when specific opportunities are identified and the right talent is secured to lead them.

The most material risks flagged in the filing relate to the inherent uncertainty in estimating loss reserves and the adequacy of premium rates. Because premiums are set before claims are reported, profitability is heavily dependent on the ultimate severity and frequency of claims, which are influenced by factors beyond the company’s control, such as natural disasters, regulatory changes, judicial decisions, and economic or social inflation. The filing specifically highlights that reserves for long-tail lines, such as excess casualty and assumed reinsurance, involve greater uncertainty and variability due to extended claim settlement tails and reliance on data from ceding companies. Additionally, the company notes that its investment income is affected by interest rates, credit quality, and the volatility of alternative investments, such as private equity and investment funds.
