## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LLY_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eli Lilly and Company is a global pharmaceutical company that discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets human pharmaceutical products. Its business model focuses on creating innovative medicines across various therapeutic areas, including cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The company operates in approximately 90 countries, with manufacturing facilities in the U.S., Europe, and Asia. Key products include insulin analogs like Humalog and Jardiance for diabetes, anti-obesity medications such as Mounjaro and Zepbound, and cancer treatments like Cyramza and Verzenio.

Lilly's competitive moat is built on its ability to innovate and develop differentiated products that provide significant value to patients and payers. The company emphasizes research and development (R&D) to maintain a robust pipeline of new molecular entities and indications, which helps sustain growth despite generic and biosimilar competition. Lilly also leverages collaborations with other pharmaceutical companies to enhance its product portfolio.

Management's priorities include expanding access to medicines through digital platforms like LillyDirect, managing pricing agreements with governments, and navigating regulatory challenges. The company is focused on maintaining a strong R&D pipeline, particularly in cardiometabolic health, where products like Mounjaro and Zepbound have been critical drivers of revenue growth. Additionally, Lilly prioritizes addressing global healthcare cost containment efforts and ensuring compliance with evolving regulations.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include competition from generics and biosimilars, which can erode revenues for established products. Regulatory changes, such as government-set drug prices under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), pose significant financial and strategic challenges. Lilly also faces risks related to U.S. private sector dynamics, where payers and pharmacy benefit managers influence pricing and access. Furthermore, the company is exposed to market risks from counterfeit and misbranded medications, which can impact patient safety and reputation. Lastly, Lilly must navigate complex tax laws and increased scrutiny from tax authorities globally, which could affect its financial performance.
