## Business Profile

Source: FY2025 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-12), fetched live from SEC EDGAR and read directly (Item 1 Business).

What the company does: DexCom is a medical device company focused on the design, development, and commercialization of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems for diabetes and metabolic-health management. It received its first FDA product approval in 2006.

Product lineup:
- Dexcom G7 — the flagship integrated CGM (iCGM), FDA-cleared via 510(k) in December 2022, for ages 2+; classified as a Class II device, subject to FDA iCGM special controls.
- Dexcom G7 15 Day — launched late 2025, FDA-cleared April 2025 for adults 18+; extends wear to 15.5 days (vs. 10 for standard G7); an 8.0% Mean Absolute Relative Difference (MARD) accuracy score, which the company describes as the most accurate CGM cleared by the FDA to date, with 94.2% of readings within 20% of the comparator value.
- Stelo — launched August 2024, the first over-the-counter glucose biosensor in the U.S., targeting adults with prediabetes and non-insulin-using Type 2 diabetes — a deliberate expansion beyond the traditional insulin-dependent CGM user base.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: finger-stick elimination, continuous 5-minute glucose readings, broad ecosystem integration (insulin pumps, smart insulin pens, Apple Watch, Garmin, and other digital health apps), and — critically for the G7 15 Day specifically — best-in-class published accuracy metrics.

Manufacturing footprint: expanding international manufacturing sites in Ireland and Malaysia, which the company explicitly flags as introducing incremental foreign-currency exposure (previously a near-entirely U.S.-dollar-denominated sales/manufacturing base).

Competitive landscape (from the company's own risk factors): direct competitors include Abbott Laboratories' Diabetes Care division (Libre family — the most-cited direct rival), Medtronic plc's Diabetes Group / MiniMed (Guardian Connect, and a new disposable CGM called Simplera), Roche Diabetes Care, privately-held LifeScan, and Ascensia Diabetes Care. The company also explicitly flags an emerging, less traditional competitive threat: a January 2026 FDA guidance broadening what counts as a "general wellness device" (including consumer wearables reporting heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose), which could open the door to non-medical-device competitors from the consumer wearables space.

Risks the company itself flags as most material:
1. Medicare reimbursement risk (explicit, dated, structural): CMS extended the Durable Medical Equipment competitive bidding program to CGMs in late 2025, with contracting beginning 2027 and payment changes effective January 1, 2028. The company explicitly states it expects Medicare reimbursement for its CGM systems to decrease beginning in 2028 as a direct result of this program — a real, company-flagged multi-year headwind, not analyst speculation.
2. Competitive intensity from both traditional CGM rivals and emerging general-wellness-device entrants.
3. GLP-1 dynamic: the company frames GLP-1 obesity/diabetes drugs as complementary to CGM use today but acknowledges they "could potentially compete with our CGM systems and reduce sales" if usage patterns shift.
4. Cybersecurity/data privacy, given the company's cloud-based reporting infrastructure (Dexcom Clarity) and HIPAA-covered-entity status.
