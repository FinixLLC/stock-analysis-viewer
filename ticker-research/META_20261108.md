## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (META_10-K_20260906.txt)

Meta operates as a global technology company with a mission to build the future of human connection, generating substantially all of its revenue through advertising placements on its "Family of Apps" (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, and Threads). The company’s business model relies on a self-service ad platform that leverages proprietary data signals and artificial intelligence to deliver targeted marketing to a massive user base, which reached 3.58 billion daily active people in December 2025. While the core business is digital advertising, Meta is simultaneously investing heavily in its Reality Labs segment to develop next-generation computing platforms, including virtual reality headsets (Meta Quest), augmented reality glasses (Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta, and the Orion prototype), and neural interface technologies. This dual-segment structure allows Meta to fund long-term hardware and AI research through the high-margin cash flows of its social media and messaging applications.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its massive scale, which provides extensive data for training AI models and optimizing ad delivery, as well as its integrated ecosystem of communication tools that foster high user engagement and retention. Meta differentiates itself through significant investments in artificial intelligence, including the development of frontier models and a "discovery engine" that enhances content recommendation and ad targeting. Additionally, the company maintains a strong position in the emerging spatial computing market by owning the full stack of hardware, software, and content for VR and AR, while also leveraging its history of open-sourcing AI models like Llama to accelerate broader industry innovation and improve its own product capabilities.

Management’s stated priorities for 2026 focus on several key investment areas: advancing AI capabilities, expanding Reels and the discovery engine, developing wearables, improving product monetization, supporting youth engagement, ensuring platform integrity, and expanding infrastructure capacity. The company emphasizes a philosophy of continuous innovation in "social by design" products, aiming to deliver "personal superintelligence" to users. Strategically, Meta is shifting its focus beyond 2D screens toward immersive experiences, with approximately 70% of Reality Labs operating expenses expected to be directed toward wearables initiatives in 2026. The company also prioritizes operating efficiency while making significant capital expenditures, which totaled $72.22 billion in 2025, largely driven by the need for expanded data centers and technical infrastructure to support AI workloads.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on regulatory and competitive pressures that threaten the core advertising revenue stream. Meta explicitly flags that legislative and regulatory developments, such as the GDPR, Digital Markets Act, and U.S. state privacy laws, have limited its ability to use data signals for ad targeting and measurement, a trend it expects will continue to adversely impact revenue. Furthermore, changes by third-party mobile operating systems and browsers, particularly Apple’s iOS updates, have reduced the effectiveness of ad targeting, leading to lower marketer budgets. The company also notes significant competition from other products and services, especially among younger demographics, and acknowledges that while Reels is growing in usage, it monetizes at a lower rate than feed and stories products. Additionally, Meta highlights the financial risk of its Reality Labs segment, which operated at a loss of $19.19 billion in 2025, noting that its ability to sustain these long-term investments is dependent on generating sufficient profits from its other business areas. Finally, the filing cites exposure to macroeconomic volatility, geopolitical conditions, and the uncertainty of achieving its long-term vision for the metaverse and superintelligence.
