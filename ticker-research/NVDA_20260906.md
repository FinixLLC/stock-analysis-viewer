## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NVDA_10-K_20260906.txt)

NVIDIA is a leading technology company specializing in accelerated computing and AI infrastructure. The company's core business revolves around its GPU (Graphics Processing Unit) technology, which has evolved from enabling high-end graphics to driving advancements in artificial intelligence, data analytics, scientific computing, robotics, and more. NVIDIA's platform strategy integrates hardware, software, algorithms, and services to provide comprehensive solutions across multiple industries.

The company operates through two main segments: Compute Networking and Graphics. The Compute Networking segment focuses on data center infrastructure, AI solutions, and automotive technologies, while the Graphics segment serves the gaming and professional visualization markets. NVIDIA's competitive moat is bolstered by its extensive software ecosystem, including the CUDA platform, which enables developers to leverage GPU capabilities for a wide range of applications. This deep software stack, combined with hardware innovations like the Blackwell architecture, allows NVIDIA to maintain a significant performance advantage over competitors.

Management has prioritized expanding AI infrastructure, particularly in data centers, and accelerating product development cadences to meet growing demand. The company is also investing heavily in research and development, as well as partnerships and acquisitions to strengthen its ecosystem. For instance, the acquisition of Mellanox expanded NVIDIA's networking capabilities, while recent investments in startups aim to support early-stage AI model makers.

Key risks flagged in the filing include supply chain constraints, regulatory challenges (such as export restrictions impacting sales in China), macroeconomic factors like inflation and interest rate changes, and competitive pressures from open-source AI solutions. Additionally, inventory management and product transitions pose operational risks, potentially leading to revenue volatility or increased costs. Despite these challenges, NVIDIA's strong position in the AI and accelerated computing markets continues to drive growth across its segments.
