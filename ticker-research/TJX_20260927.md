## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TJX_10-K_20260906.txt)

TJX Companies operates as the leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer in the United States and globally, managing over 5,200 stores and six e-commerce sites across four primary segments: Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls), HomeGoods, TJX Canada, and TJX International. The company’s business model is defined by "opportunistic buying," a strategy that differentiates it from traditional retailers by acquiring merchandise closer to the time of sale rather than ordering far in advance. This approach allows TJX to source from a diverse universe of approximately 21,000 vendors across more than 100 countries, capturing closeouts, special productions, and overruns to offer quality, brand-name, and designer merchandise at prices generally 20% to 60% below full-price retailers. This model supports a "treasure hunt" shopping experience characterized by rapidly changing inventory, lean inventory levels, and flexible store layouts that can be easily adjusted to accommodate varying merchandise mixes.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its scale, buying power, and operational flexibility. TJX leverages its substantial global buying power to secure favorable terms, including the ability to purchase less-than-full assortments and small quantities, while maintaining a strong credit rating and prompt payment practices that make it an attractive channel for vendors. Its low-cost structure, supported by efficient distribution networks and minimal advertising spend relative to sales, allows the company to maintain its pricing gap and margins through various economic cycles. Furthermore, the synergistic nature of its operations enables the sharing of best practices and talent across its diverse retail banners, reinforcing its ability to deliver value across a broad range of customer income levels.

Management’s stated priorities center on delivering great value to customers every day and executing a flexible business model that adapts to changing market conditions. Key strategic initiatives include the continued expansion of store footprints, with long-term store growth potential estimated at 7,000 locations globally, and the strategic renovation of existing stores to enhance the shopping experience. The company also prioritizes supply chain efficiency, aiming to maintain low inventory levels while ensuring the right merchandise reaches the right stores at the right time. Additionally, management focuses on mitigating external pressures, such as tariffs, through its buying organization’s ability to adjust sourcing strategies, and it continues to return capital to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends.

The filing identifies several material risks, with the most significant being the volatility and uncertainty surrounding international trade relations and tariffs. While TJX believes it can mitigate tariff pressures through its sourcing model, the filing notes that tariffs have led to significant global economic volatility, and the ultimate impact on costs, vendor pricing, and consumer demand remains uncertain due to ongoing trade negotiations and legal developments, including recent U.S. Supreme Court rulings on tariff authority. Other flagged risks include the impact of foreign currency exchange rates on operating results, particularly for its Canadian and international segments, and the highly competitive nature of the retail apparel and home fashion industry, where TJX competes on brand, price, quality, and shopping experience against a wide array of traditional and online retailers.
