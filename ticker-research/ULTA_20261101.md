## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to bridge the historical separation between prestige, mass, and salon product channels. The company’s business model centers on a "beauty enthusiast" target consumer, offering a differentiated assortment of approximately 30,000 products from around 600 brands across all price points, alongside full-service salon services. This physical footprint, which includes over 1,500 U.S. stores typically featuring dedicated salon spaces, is integrated with an omnichannel strategy that leverages digital platforms, diverse fulfillment options like same-day delivery and buy-online-pick-up-in-store, and a robust loyalty program. Internationally, the company expands through its Space NK subsidiary in the U.K. and Ireland, a joint venture in Mexico, and a franchise in the Middle East.

The company’s competitive moat is built on several strategic differentiators, primarily its ability to aggregate prestige, mass, and salon brands in a single, welcoming environment, which competitors often cannot replicate due to channel exclusivity. A significant advantage is its best-in-class loyalty program, which has over 46 million members and accounts for approximately 95% of total sales, providing deep consumer data that drives personalization and marketing efficiency. Additionally, Ulta maintains a strong position in private label and exclusive third-party products, which contributed to roughly 11% of net sales in fiscal 2025, enhancing margins and brand differentiation. The company also cites its high-performing real estate portfolio, characterized by convenient, high-traffic locations, and its capacity to serve as a key launch and scaling partner for beauty brands as core elements of its defensibility.

Management’s stated priorities are outlined in the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan, which focuses on three areas: driving core business growth through operational excellence, digital acceleration, and merchandising innovation; scaling new, accretive businesses such as wellness products, the UB Marketplace, and international expansion; and aligning the foundation for future success by optimizing costs and culture. Specifically, the company aims to grow its U.S. store footprint to over 1,800 locations, enhance its digital capabilities to capture the higher spend of omnichannel guests, and expand into the wellness category to capture the convergence of beauty and self-care.

The filing flags several material risks, including the impact of persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures on consumer spending and profitability. The company notes that its variable-rate credit facilities expose it to increased interest costs in periods of elevated rates, a risk that materialized in fiscal 2025 when net interest expense turned positive due to increased borrowings. Additionally, the company highlights the inherent volatility of comparable sales, which can fluctuate based on general economic conditions, merchandise mix, and marketing effectiveness. There is also a noted risk regarding the execution of its strategic priorities, as the company acknowledges that maintaining its leadership position requires continued innovation and investment in a dynamic operating environment where guest expectations are constantly evolving.
