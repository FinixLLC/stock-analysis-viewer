## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to bridge the historical gap between prestige, mass, and salon product channels. The company’s business model centers on a "beauty enthusiast" demographic, offering a differentiated assortment of approximately 30,000 products from around 600 brands across all price points, alongside full-service salon capabilities. Ulta’s competitive moat is built on four key pillars: a broad and exclusive product mix that includes private-label and exclusive third-party brands; a convenient omnichannel footprint that integrates over 1,500 U.S. stores with digital platforms and diverse fulfillment options; a best-in-class loyalty program with over 46 million members that drives approximately 95% of sales and provides deep consumer data for personalization; and a focus on human connection through knowledgeable associates and welcoming store environments.

Management’s stated strategic priorities, outlined in the 2025 "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan, focus on three areas: driving core business growth through operational excellence, digital acceleration, and brand building; scaling new, accretive businesses by expanding into wellness, launching an online marketplace (UB Marketplace), and growing international presence via subsidiaries like Space NK; and aligning the company’s foundation for future success by optimizing workflows, streamlining costs, and fostering an associate-centered culture. The filing flags several material risks, including the impact of persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures on consumer spending and profitability, the company’s dependence on a small number of top brand partners (the top ten represented 51% of net sales in fiscal 2025), and the operational challenges associated with rapid store expansion and the integration of new business lines. Additionally, the company notes that its variable-rate debt facilities expose it to rising interest costs, and its performance remains sensitive to general economic conditions and the effectiveness of its marketing and merchandising strategies.
