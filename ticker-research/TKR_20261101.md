## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TKR_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Timken Company operates as a global designer and manufacturer of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, supported by related services. Its business model relies on leveraging deep expertise in metallurgy, friction management, and industrial motion to provide high-performance components that increase the reliability and efficiency of customer equipment. Timken serves a diverse customer base across sectors such as automotive, agriculture, construction, mining, and renewable energy, with no single customer accounting for more than 5% of net sales. The company differentiates itself through application engineering, product quality, and a strong aftermarket presence, selling primarily through internal sales organizations and authorized distributors. With approximately 19,000 employees and operations in 44 countries, Timken maintains a significant global footprint including 116 manufacturing facilities and 29 technology centers.

Timken’s competitive moat is anchored in its status as the world’s leading authority on tapered roller bearings, a position established by its founder’s patents in 1899. This foundational expertise allows the company to apply sophisticated engineering know-how, proprietary internal geometries, and high-precision tolerances across its entire portfolio, including spherical and cylindrical roller bearings, ball bearings, and housed units. The company competes against major players like SKF Group and Schaeffler Group based on a total value proposition that includes technical sales support, global delivery capabilities, and product performance. Additionally, Timken benefits from a broad, differentiated portfolio of industrial motion products—such as linear motion solutions, industrial drives, and automatic lubrication systems—acquired through brands like Rollon, Philadelphia Gear, and Cone Drive, which serve highly fragmented markets demanding high service levels and reliability.

Management’s stated priorities focus on three primary strategic elements: profitable growth, operational excellence, and capital deployment to drive shareholder value. For profitable growth, Timken aims to expand into attractive, fragmented market sectors by leveraging its knowledge of materials science and power transmission to create unique solutions for demanding applications, with a specific emphasis on capturing aftermarket demand throughout the equipment’s lifetime. Operational excellence involves a continuous improvement culture aimed at increasing efficiency, lowering costs, eliminating waste, and enhancing brand equity. Finally, the company’s capital allocation framework prioritizes investing in the core business through R&D and capital expenditures, pursuing strategic acquisitions to broaden its portfolio, returning capital to shareholders via dividends and share repurchases, and maintaining a strong balance sheet.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the impact of incremental tariff costs, which reduced operating income in 2025 by $65 million in cost of goods sold. The company also highlights exposure to raw material price variability, specifically for special bar quality (SBQ) steel, which is subject to changes in commodity prices, energy costs, and labor. While Timken uses surcharge mechanisms to partially recover these costs, it remains exposed to supply and demand fluctuations. Additionally, the company notes that actual shipments depend on customers' ever-changing production schedules, meaning backlog data does not reliably indicate future sales. Other flagged risks include lower demand across key market sectors such as auto/truck and heavy industries, as well as the financial impacts of restructuring charges and pension remeasurement losses.
