## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm (QCOM) operates as a leading developer and supplier of wireless technologies, integrated circuits, and software solutions for mobile devices, automotive systems, and IoT applications. The company's business model is centered around two primary segments: QCT (Qualcomm CDMA Technologies), which focuses on the design and sale of chipsets, modems, and other hardware components; and QTL (Qualcomm Technology Licensing), which generates revenue through licensing its extensive portfolio of patents essential to wireless communication technologies. Additionally, QSI (Qualcomm Strategic Initiatives) manages investments in early-stage companies and contributes to overall profitability through gains on marketable securities.

**Competitive Moat:**
QCOM's competitive advantage stems from its vast intellectual property portfolio, particularly its foundational patents in CDMA and subsequent advancements in 4G LTE and 5G technologies. This IP is critical for the functionality of modern wireless devices, giving QCOM significant leverage in licensing agreements with manufacturers. The company's Snapdragon platforms are widely recognized as industry-leading solutions, driving demand across multiple sectors including smartphones, automotive, and IoT.

**Management Priorities:**
Management emphasizes innovation and leadership in AI, high-performance computing, and cellular technologies like 5G. They prioritize expanding into growth areas such as automotive and IoT, while also focusing on maintaining strong licensing agreements with key OEMs. Additionally, the company is actively monitoring global trade policies and their potential impact on operations.

**Material Risks:**
The filing highlights several risks, including fluctuations in demand for wireless devices due to seasonality and innovation cycles, which can significantly affect QCT revenues. Licensing disputes and regulatory proceedings pose another material risk, as they can disrupt revenue streams and incur substantial legal costs. Furthermore, changes in tax laws, such as the OBBB Act, have led to significant adjustments in deferred tax assets, impacting profitability. Global trade policies, including tariffs and export restrictions, could also negatively affect demand, pricing, and inventory management, introducing additional financial risks.
