## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates through a dual-engine business model comprising the Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL) segments, supported by a smaller strategic investment arm (QSI). QCT generates the majority of revenue by selling integrated circuit products, including Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, modems, and RF components, to device manufacturers for use in handsets, automotive systems, and IoT devices. QTL generates high-margin revenue by licensing its intellectual property portfolio, specifically patent rights essential to wireless standards, to third-party licensees. In fiscal 2025, total revenues reached $44.3 billion, driven by a 16% increase in QCT equipment and services sales, while QTL licensing revenues remained approximately flat. The company’s competitive position relies on its leadership in on-device AI, high-performance low-power computing, and cellular technology standardization, with management explicitly prioritizing the expansion of these technologies into non-mobile verticals such as automotive and IoT to sustain long-term growth.

The filing identifies several material risks that could impact financial performance. First, the business is subject to significant seasonality and demand fluctuations tied to consumer device launch cycles and innovation transitions, which can cause revenue volatility. Second, global trade policy changes, including tariffs and related actions by the U.S., China, and other countries, pose uncertainties regarding demand, pricing, and costs, potentially leading to excess inventory or obsolete reserve charges. Third, the company faces legal and regulatory uncertainties, including ongoing disputes with licensees and governmental investigations, which may result in material losses or revenue impacts, as evidenced by the loss of Huawei royalties and the settlement of disputes with Chinese OEMs. Finally, a significant tax-related risk emerged from the enactment of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB), which subjects Qualcomm to the corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) starting in fiscal 2026. This change necessitated a $5.7 billion charge to establish a valuation allowance against federal deferred tax assets, as the company no longer expects to realize substantially all of these assets, fundamentally altering its effective tax rate and cash flow profile.
