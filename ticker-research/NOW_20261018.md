## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

ServiceNow operates as a cloud-based enterprise software provider, delivering a unified AI Platform designed to help public and private organizations govern, secure, and manage artificial intelligence while streamlining digital workflows. The company’s business model is primarily subscription-based, with revenues recognized ratably over contract terms, and it reports strong financial health, including a 98% renewal rate and $28.2 billion in remaining performance obligations as of December 31, 2025. The platform is structured around three core capabilities—AI, data, and workflows—integrated into four product areas: Technology, CRM and Industry, Core Business, and Creator and Other. ServiceNow positions itself not merely as an AI generator, but as the infrastructure layer that executes AI-generated insights by orchestrating actions across existing systems, enforcing governance, and managing complex cross-functional processes.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from two decades of deep institutional knowledge regarding how work flows across enterprise silos, which allows it to build cross-functional workflows that integrate with existing infrastructure rather than replacing it. Management argues that while competitors may offer point solutions for data analysis, ServiceNow possesses a distinct advantage in comprehensive integration, workflow orchestration, and enterprise-grade reliability. This is supported by a "one platform" architecture that connects people, processes, and data through a single data fabric, enabling the deployment of AI agents with human-in-the-loop governance. The filing notes that this ability to bridge the gap between AI’s analytical capabilities and the execution layer where business processes operate is difficult for new entrants to replicate, particularly given the need for regulatory compliance and operational continuity.

Management’s stated priorities focus on capitalizing on the accelerating adoption of AI by providing tools that allow organizations to operationalize AI strategies with speed, scale, and security. Key strategic initiatives include the continued development of the Now Assist AI suite, the integration of advanced enterprise search and virtual agent technology through the acquisition of Moveworks, Inc., and the expansion of AI governance tools such as the AI Control Tower. The company emphasizes its strategy of understanding evolving customer requirements through ongoing dialogue, aiming to deliver integrated solutions that convert AI insights into controlled, governed business outcomes while maintaining the reliability and security standards required for mission-critical functions.

Regarding risks, the filing explicitly flags the dynamic and rapidly evolving technology landscape as a primary concern, noting that competitive dynamics may shift as new approaches emerge and customer preferences evolve. Management acknowledges that while the AI era intensifies the need for unified platforms, it also presents uncertainties reminiscent of prior technological inflection points. Additionally, the company highlights macroeconomic risks, including global conflicts, interest rates, inflation, and tariffs, which could materially impact business operations if they lead to greater global economic disruptions. Although management states they do not currently believe these events will have a material impact, they note that persistent or worsening conditions could alter this outlook. The filing also references foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations as a factor that may cause variability in remaining performance obligations, given the increasing percentage of contracts denominated in foreign currencies such as the Euro and British Pound Sterling.
