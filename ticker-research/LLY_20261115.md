## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LLY_10-K_20260906.txt)

Eli Lilly and Company operates as a single-segment human pharmaceutical business that discovers, develops, manufactures, and markets innovative medicines across four primary therapeutic areas: cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The company’s business model relies on a continuous cycle of discovering or acquiring new molecular entities to replace products that lose intellectual property protection or face competitive displacement, supported by a global manufacturing footprint in the U.S., Europe, and Asia. Lilly distributes its products in approximately 90 countries, utilizing a mix of direct sales forces, third-party distributors, and digital platforms like LillyDirect to reach healthcare providers and patients. In 2025, the company generated $65.2 billion in revenue, with 56% of total revenue derived from its incretin-based products Mounjaro and Zepbound, which are central to its current growth trajectory.

Lilly’s competitive moat is anchored in its deep pipeline of next-generation incretin therapies and its ability to secure early market entry and rapid patient access. The company cites its long-term success as dependent on its capacity to innovate at the pace of global scientific advancement and to access innovation through strategic partnerships, particularly in response to intensifying competition from China and other markets with accelerated R&D capabilities. Key differentiators include the breadth of its cardiometabolic portfolio, which includes approved products like Mounjaro and Zepbound, as well as advanced-stage candidates such as orforglipron, which received a U.S. Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher for expedited review. Additionally, Lilly leverages its scale to undertake significant manufacturing expansion initiatives to support anticipated demand, aiming to maintain a competitive position as the therapeutic landscape evolves.

Management’s stated priorities focus on sustaining growth in the cardiometabolic health sector while navigating complex regulatory and pricing environments. A key strategic priority is executing preliminary voluntary agreements with the U.S. government to lower Medicaid and certain other drug prices in exchange for expanded access to obesity medicines for Medicare beneficiaries and state Medicaid programs. The company is also prioritizing the commercialization of new indications and products, such as the recent approvals for Mounjaro in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and pediatric type 2 diabetes, and the ongoing Phase 3 trials for retatrutide and orforglipron. Furthermore, management is focused on mitigating risks related to supply chain integrity, specifically addressing the production and sale of counterfeit or mass-compounded incretins, and managing the financial impact of global cost containment measures, including the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) and potential tariff impacts.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably the significant concentration of revenue in incretin medicines, which exposes the company to volume fluctuations and pricing pressures. The company highlights the uncertainty surrounding the definitive terms of its U.S. government pricing agreements, warning that expanded access may not generate sufficient revenue to offset pricing concessions. Regulatory risks are prominent, including the IRA’s mandate for government-set prices on selected drugs like Jardiance, Trulicity, and Verzenio, which could significantly impact future revenue. Additionally, the filing cites risks related to generic and biosimilar competition, which can lead to rapid revenue erosion upon loss of exclusivity, as well as operational risks from potential IT system breaches, data protection violations, and regulatory compliance issues regarding manufacturing quality and safety. Finally, the company notes that global trade disputes, tariffs, and international tensions could lead to supply disruptions or increased costs that it may not be able to pass on to customers.
