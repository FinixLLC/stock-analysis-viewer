## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

ServiceNow operates as a cloud-based enterprise software provider that delivers the "ServiceNow AI Platform," a unified architecture designed to govern, secure, and manage artificial intelligence while streamlining digital workflows. The company’s business model is primarily subscription-based, generating revenue from non-cancellable contracts that provide customers with access to AI-powered applications, data integration, and workflow orchestration tools. These solutions are categorized into four product areas: Technology, CRM and Industry, Core Business, and Creator and Other, allowing organizations to automate processes, integrate disparate systems, and manage AI agents with human oversight and governance guardrails.

The company’s competitive moat is rooted in its two decades of institutional knowledge regarding how work flows across enterprise silos, which allows it to build cross-functional workflows that integrate with existing infrastructure rather than replacing it. ServiceNow differentiates itself by providing the execution layer that translates AI-generated insights into actionable business outcomes, a capability it argues is lacking in point-solution competitors focused solely on data analysis. This advantage is reinforced by its single data fabric architecture, which connects people, processes, and data in real-time, and its operational expertise in maintaining enterprise-grade reliability, security, and performance at scale.

Management’s stated priorities focus on helping customers operationalize AI strategies by bridging the gap between analytical capabilities and the execution layer where business processes operate. The company emphasizes the importance of governance, security, and measurable returns on investment, positioning its platform as the necessary infrastructure for orchestrating action, enforcing compliance, and managing complex workflows in an era of accelerating AI adoption. Additionally, management highlights the strategic value of its deep customer relationships and the flexibility of its platform to adapt to evolving technological transitions, such as the shift from on-premises to cloud computing.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily related to the dynamic and rapidly evolving technology landscape, where competitive dynamics may shift as new approaches emerge and customer preferences evolve. Management acknowledges that while the AI era intensifies the need for unified platforms, it also presents uncertainties reminiscent of prior technological inflection points. Furthermore, the company notes exposure to macroeconomic events, including global conflicts, interest rates, inflation, and tariffs, which could materially impact business operations if they lead to greater global economic disruptions. Although the company states it has not experienced material impact from these events to date, it continues to monitor their direct and indirect effects on liquidity and financial results.
