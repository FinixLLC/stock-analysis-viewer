## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (QCOM_10-K_20260906.txt)

Qualcomm operates as a leading developer and supplier of integrated circuit products and wireless technologies, generating revenue through two primary segments: Qualcomm Technologies (QCT) and Qualcomm Licensing (QTL). The QCT segment, which accounted for the majority of the company’s $44.3 billion in fiscal 2025 revenue, sells equipment and services such as Snapdragon and Dragonwing platforms, modems, and radio frequency products to handset OEMs, automotive manufacturers, and IoT device makers. The QTL segment generates high-margin licensing revenues by granting rights to use its intellectual property portfolio, including patents essential to wireless products, with royalties recognized based on licensee sales. A third segment, Qualcomm Strategic Investments (QSI), holds equity investments in early-stage companies, contributing to earnings through capital gains rather than product sales.

The company’s competitive position is anchored in its leadership in technology development, standardization, and intellectual property creation, particularly in on-device AI, high-performance low-power computing, and cellular technologies like 5G. Management asserts that sustaining this leadership in integrated circuit products and IP licensing is critical to long-term growth. The business model benefits from a diversified customer base across handsets, automotive, and IoT, with QCT revenues driven by higher average selling prices and favorable mix in premium-tier Android devices, while QTL maintains a stable, high-margin revenue stream (72% EBT margin) through long-term licensing agreements, including recent renewals with key Chinese OEMs and new agreements with Transsion.

Management’s stated priorities focus on maintaining leadership in on-device AI and cellular technology to drive adoption beyond mobile handsets into automotive and IoT sectors. They emphasize the importance of remaining a leading developer of integrated circuit products and IP to sustain business growth. Additionally, management is actively monitoring global trade policies, including tariffs, to mitigate potential impacts on demand, pricing, and costs, while also managing the financial implications of recent U.S. tax reforms.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the impact of global trade policies and tariffs, which could negatively affect demand, pricing, and inventory levels, leading to potential write-downs. A significant financial risk emerged from the enactment of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB) in July 2025, which resulted in a $5.7 billion charge to establish a valuation allowance against federal deferred tax assets because the company no longer expects to realize them due to the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT). Other risks include seasonality in consumer wireless device demand, fluctuations in QCT revenues tied to device launch timing, and uncertainties in legal and regulatory proceedings, including licensing disputes and governmental investigations, which could result in material losses.
