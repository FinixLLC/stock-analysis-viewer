## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (SNOW_10-K_20260827.txt)

Snowflake operates as a cloud-native data platform provider, delivering an "AI Data Cloud" that allows organizations to consolidate structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data into a single source of truth. The company’s business model is consumption-based, generating the substantial majority of its revenue from fees for compute, storage, and data transfer resources rather than fixed licenses. This architecture, built on three major public clouds across 53 regional deployments, separates storage and compute to enable independent scalability, while a cloud services layer facilitates secure data sharing and AI application development. Snowflake’s competitive moat is driven by powerful network effects within its ecosystem; as more customers, partners, and data providers join the platform, the value of data exchange and collaboration increases for all users. This is evidenced by strong customer retention, with a net revenue retention rate of 125% as of January 31, 2026, and a growing base of 13,328 customers, including 790 Forbes Global 2000 companies that contribute approximately 43% of revenue.

Management’s stated priorities focus on advancing the AI Data Cloud through continuous innovation, such as the launch of Snowflake Intelligence and Snowflake Postgres, and expanding the platform’s utility in AI and data engineering. The company is also prioritizing the acquisition of new large enterprise customers, particularly in regulated markets like financial services and the public sector, while driving increased consumption among existing users by migrating more workloads to the platform. Additionally, Snowflake is investing in global expansion across EMEA, APJ, and Latin America, and strengthening its partner network and native application ecosystem to foster broader data collaboration.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily centered on the uncertainty of customer consumption patterns. Because revenue is recognized based on actual usage rather than ratably over contract terms, Snowflake lacks visibility into the timing of revenue recognition, making results susceptible to fluctuations if customers optimize their consumption or rationalize budgets due to macroeconomic pressures such as inflation and high interest rates. The company also notes that technological improvements in storage compression and compute efficiency could lead to lower resource consumption for the same workloads, potentially reducing revenue if not offset by new usage. Furthermore, the filing highlights risks related to the difficulty of predicting adoption rates for AI technologies due to evolving regulatory standards and compliance reviews, as well as the competitive challenge of displacing legacy database and big data solutions in which customers have already invested significant resources.
