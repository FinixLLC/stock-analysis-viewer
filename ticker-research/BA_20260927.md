## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (BA_10-K_20260906.txt)

Boeing operates as a global aerospace leader organized into three reportable segments: Commercial Airplanes (BCA), Defense, Space & Security (BDS), and Global Services (BGS). The company’s business model centers on the design, development, manufacture, sale, and support of commercial jetliners, military aircraft, satellites, missile defense systems, and human space flight capabilities. As one of only two major manufacturers of 100+ seat commercial airplanes, Boeing competes in a duopoly for the worldwide airline industry while also serving as one of the largest U.S. defense contractors. Its Global Services segment provides a full spectrum of aftermarket support, including maintenance, upgrades, spare parts, training, and digital solutions for both commercial and defense customers. The company relies on an extensive network of U.S. and non-U.S. partners, suppliers, and subcontractors to execute its production and service obligations.

The filing identifies Boeing’s competitive moat as its status as a leading producer of commercial aircraft and a major defense contractor, supported by a large portfolio of intellectual property, including patents, trademarks, trade secrets, and unpatented research, development, and engineering skills. While the company notes that it does not believe its business would be materially affected by the expiration of any single patent or license, the aggregate intellectual property and engineering expertise are described as important to the operation of its businesses. Additionally, the company leverages its scale and integration across defense, intelligence, communications, security, and space to deliver capability-driven solutions, aiming to capture next-generation programs and expand into adjacent and international markets.

Management’s stated priorities are centered on successful execution within these healthy core businesses. For Commercial Airplanes, the focus is on delivering superior design, safety, quality, efficiency, and value. Following the January 2024 737-9 door plug accident, a critical priority has been improving quality and safety culture, which included slowing production rates to reduce traveled work, investing in workforce training, simplifying processes, and eliminating defects. In Defense, Space & Security, the strategy involves leveraging core businesses to secure next-generation programs while expanding internationally. Global Services aims to provide innovative, cost-competitive support solutions. Recent strategic actions reflecting these priorities include the divestiture of the Digital Aviation Solutions business for $10.55 billion and the acquisition of Spirit AeroSystems for approximately $4.7 billion in stock to bring fuselage and major structure production in-house.

The filing flags several material risks, most notably labor instability and production disruptions. In 2024 and 2025, significant work stoppages by IAM District 751 (53 days) and IAM District 837 (101 days) halted or disrupted production of key commercial and defense programs, materially adversely impacting financial position and results. The company warns that future work stoppages could have similar material effects. Additionally, the 737-9 accident and subsequent FAA-imposed restrictions on production rates significantly impacted cash flows and earnings. Other material risks include supply chain inflationary pressures, technical and schedule challenges on fixed-price defense development programs that have resulted in significant earnings charges, and legal liabilities, evidenced by earnings charges related to agreements with the U.S. Department of Justice. The company also notes ongoing vulnerabilities to exogenous factors such as fuel price spikes, tariffs, geopolitical conflicts, and regulatory changes.
