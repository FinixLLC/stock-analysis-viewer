What the company does: Applied Digital is "a United States designer, developer, and operator of next-generation data center infrastructure across North America," providing data-center infrastructure solutions to the high-performance computing (HPC) and artificial intelligence (AI) industries. As of the FY2025 10-K, the company operated two continuing-operations segments: Data Center Hosting (energized infrastructure for crypto-mining customers, renting space based on power requirements) and HPC Hosting (purpose-built data centers for high-power-density AI/HPC workloads, i.e. GPU-dense facilities). A third business, Cloud Services (renting GPU compute/cloud access to AI/ML developers via third-party co-location), was in the process of being divested (held for sale) as of the filing.

Business model transition (the central narrative): the FY2025 10-K captures Applied Digital mid-pivot — its Data Center Hosting (crypto) business generated effectively 100% of continuing-operations revenue as of fiscal year-end May 2025, serving a single remaining crypto-mining customer, while the company was simultaneously constructing its first AI/HPC-focused facilities (Polaris Forge 1) with the explicit expectation that the HPC segment would only begin generating "meaningful revenues" once those buildings became operational later in calendar 2025. The subsequently reported FY2026 full-year revenue of $611M (+407% YoY) indicates this pivot has now materialized at real scale — the company has evidently transitioned from a crypto-hosting-dominated revenue base to a substantially AI/HPC-hosting-driven one within about a year, though this research pass did not independently verify the exact current revenue split between the two business lines from a more recent filing.

Moat, as described in the company's own words: primarily an infrastructure-development and power-procurement capability — securing large blocks of electrical power (hundreds of megawatts per site) and building purpose-built, high-power-density facilities for GPU-dense AI workloads. This is fundamentally a capital-intensive, execution-and-permitting-driven moat (land, power interconnection agreements, construction execution) rather than a technology or IP moat.

6. Tariff/trade-policy risk — changes in U.S. trade policy could materially affect the business (relevant given the company's reliance on imported data-center hardware/components).
