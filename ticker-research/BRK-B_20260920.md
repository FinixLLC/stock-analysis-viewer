## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (BRK-B_10-K_20260906.txt)

Berkshire Hathaway operates as a diversified conglomerate that generates earnings through a combination of insurance underwriting, investment income, and a portfolio of operating businesses spanning rail (BNSF), energy (Berkshire Hathaway Energy), and manufacturing, service, and retailing sectors. The company’s business model relies heavily on its insurance subsidiaries—GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group, and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group—to generate underwriting profits and float, which is then deployed into investments in U.S. Treasury bills, equity securities, and other assets. While the filing notes that investment gains and losses are volatile and often "meaningless" for evaluating periodic economic performance, they remain a significant component of reported net earnings, alongside the steady cash flows from its operating subsidiaries.

The company’s competitive moat is anchored in its insurance operations, which the filing describes as having generated "exceptional" underwriting results over the past three years compared to longer historical periods. GEICO, a primary writer of private passenger automobile insurance in all 50 states, maintains a low expense ratio (12.4% in 2025) and utilizes direct response methods to acquire customers, while the reinsurance arm (BHRG) provides global excess-of-loss and quota-share coverages. This structure allows Berkshire to leverage time-value-of-money concepts in premium pricing and maintain a large pool of investment capital. However, the filing explicitly notes that future underwriting earnings may decline due to ongoing industry competition and rising claim cost trends, suggesting that the moat is subject to market pressures rather than being static.

Management’s stated priorities, as inferred from the MD&A, focus on generating long-term pre-tax underwriting earnings in all business categories, with specific exceptions for retroactive reinsurance and periodic payment annuity businesses where time-value-of-money considerations dictate different pricing strategies. The company emphasizes operational efficiency, as seen in BNSF’s earnings increase driven by lower operating expenses and improved efficiencies, and in GEICO’s management of advertising and policy acquisition costs. Additionally, management prioritizes the management of catastrophe losses and claim cost trends, actively adjusting underwriting standards—such as GUARD’s exit from unprofitable lines—to protect long-term profitability.

The most material risks flagged in the filing include the significant volatility in periodic earnings caused by investment gains and losses, which are driven by market price changes and foreign currency exchange rates. The company also highlights the substantial uncertainty surrounding macroeconomic and geopolitical conflicts, including international trade policies and tariffs, which could adversely affect product availability, supply chain costs, and customer demand. Specific operational risks include the timing and magnitude of property catastrophe loss events, such as the Southern California wildfires in 2025 and Hurricanes Helene and Milton in 2024, which can cause significant after-tax losses. Furthermore, the filing notes that claim costs for liability coverages are negatively impacted by unfavorable social inflation trends, including jury awards and litigation costs, and that changes in estimates for unpaid losses can significantly affect underwriting results.
