## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines operates as a major passenger carrier providing scheduled air transportation across the United States and near-international markets, utilizing a fleet of 803 Boeing 737 aircraft to serve 117 destinations. The company’s business model is built on a low-cost strategy that leverages a point-to-point route structure, high-frequency short-haul service, and service to secondary or downtown airports to minimize congestion and maximize asset utilization. This approach, combined with the use of a single aircraft type, simplifies maintenance, training, and scheduling, allowing for competitive fares and high employee productivity. In 2025, Southwest began a significant transformation of its customer experience and revenue model by introducing assigned seating, extra legroom options, a redesigned boarding process, and new fare products such as the Basic fare, while also launching international airline partnerships and an in-house vacation product called Getaways.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its unique route network, which blends point-to-point nonstops with intentional connectivity around core stations to offer frequent, conveniently timed flights and competitive pricing. This structure allows Southwest to capture nonstop demand while providing reliable one-stop options, a flexibility that enables the company to adjust capacity and prune less profitable flights without structurally disrupting its core markets. Additionally, the single-fleet strategy and focus on secondary airports contribute to lower unit costs and higher asset utilization compared to traditional hub-and-spoke competitors.

Management’s stated priorities center on elevating the customer experience, improving financial performance, and driving shareholder value through modernization initiatives. Key strategic actions include optimizing the route network by shifting capacity from short-haul to longer-haul flights, introducing 24-hour operations with redeye flights to maximize aircraft utilization, and expanding distribution channels to attract new customers. The company is also focused on cost discipline, including reducing corporate headcount by 15% in 2025, improving operational efficiency through digital processes, and managing fuel costs, although it terminated its fuel hedging portfolio in 2025 due to high premium costs.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily related to fuel price volatility, which remains the second largest operating cost category, and labor costs, which constitute the largest operating expense at approximately 47% of total operating expenses. The company notes that increased labor costs from ratified contracts and rate inflation negatively impact its cost structure. Additionally, Southwest faces risks associated with aircraft delivery delays from Boeing, which have necessitated amendments to its order book and delivery schedules, as well as uncertainty regarding the timing of FAA certifications for new aircraft models. The company also highlights the risk of market demand shifts, noting that structural changes have reduced demand for short-haul travel, forcing a strategic redeployment of capacity to more profitable longer-haul markets.
