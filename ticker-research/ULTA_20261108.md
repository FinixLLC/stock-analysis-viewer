## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ULTA_10-K_20260827.txt)

Ulta Beauty, Inc. operates as the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, founded in 1990 to bridge the historical separation between prestige, mass, and salon product channels. The company’s business model centers on a "beauty enthusiast" consumer, offering a differentiated assortment of approximately 30,000 products from roughly 600 brands across all price points, alongside full-service salon capabilities. Ulta’s competitive moat is built on several key pillars: a dominant omnichannel footprint with over 1,500 U.S. stores that serve as both retail locations and fulfillment hubs; a best-in-class loyalty program with over 46 million members, which drives approximately 95% of total sales and provides deep consumer data for personalization; and a strong brand partnership ecosystem where the top ten partners account for roughly half of net sales, supplemented by exclusive private-label and third-party brands that contribute to margin and differentiation.

Management’s stated priorities are outlined in the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed" plan, which focuses on three strategic areas: driving core business growth through operational excellence, digital acceleration, and merchandising innovation; scaling new, accretive businesses such as wellness products, the UB Marketplace, and international expansion via subsidiaries like Space NK; and aligning the company’s foundation for future success by optimizing cost structures and fostering an associate-centered culture. The filing flags several material risks, including the impact of persistent inflationary and macroeconomic pressures on consumer spending and profitability, the operational complexities of rapid store expansion and digital transformation, and the inherent volatility of comparable sales driven by general economic conditions, competition, and the company’s ability to adapt to shifting consumer preferences. Additionally, the company notes that its variable-rate credit facilities expose it to increased interest costs in periods of elevated interest rates, and it faces execution risks in integrating new acquisitions and scaling new business lines.
