## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (ANET_10-K_20260906.txt)

Arista Networks operates as a provider of data-driven, client-to-cloud networking-as-a-service, positioning itself as an industry leader in high-speed Ethernet switching. The company’s business model is anchored by its "Centers of Data" strategy, which unifies four primary domains—AI Centers, Data Centers, Campus Centers, and WAN Centers—through a single software platform. This platform is built on the state-oriented Extensible Operating System (EOS) and the Network Data Lake (NetDL), allowing Arista to deliver a consolidated networking experience that replaces legacy siloed architectures. Arista differentiates itself through a focus on open, standards-based technology that avoids vendor lock-in, robust quality assurance, and intelligent automation that reduces manual operational workloads. Revenue is derived from both hardware (switching and routing products) and services (support contracts), with the company serving three primary customer segments: Cloud and AI Titans, AI and Specialty Providers, and Enterprise customers.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its broad, differentiated portfolio and its software-driven architecture. By utilizing best-of-breed merchant silicon and maintaining a single operating system, data lake, and management solution (CloudVision), Arista achieves feature consistency and operational simplicity across its entire product line. This modular, software-driven approach allows for rapid evolution and close partnership with customers, leading to lower total network operations costs. Additionally, Arista cites its award-winning support system, which includes 24/7/365 technical assistance and an industry-leading Net Promoter Score, as a key strength that fosters deep customer trust. The company also emphasizes its ability to deliver uncompromising reliability through automated diagnostics and real-time telemetry.

Management’s stated priorities center on sustaining leadership in the rapidly evolving cloud and AI networking markets. Key strategic goals include increasing sales in cloud, AI, and enterprise data center Ethernet switching and routing, as well as expanding into campus workspace markets. To achieve this, management prioritizes maintaining agility and increasing investment in research and development to deliver market-leading features and new software applications. Furthermore, the company is focused on expanding its global sales force and deepening channel partnerships to reach new customers and increase penetration among existing ones. Management also highlights the importance of adapting to the expansion of generative and agentic AI, which is driving demand for high-bandwidth, scalable, and lossless networks based on open standards.

The filing identifies several material risks, primarily centered on customer concentration and supply chain volatility. Two customers accounted for 26% and 16% of total revenue in 2025, respectively, and the company notes that sales to these high-volume customers are subject to unpredictability in timing due to the complexity of large-scale orders, fluctuations in demand, and internal cost-reduction initiatives. This concentration creates variability in revenue and can pressure gross margins due to pricing discounts required for large orders. Additionally, the company faces significant supply chain risks, including inflation, material scarcity (such as in the memory market), and geopolitical pressures like tariff uncertainty, which can impact product costs and gross margins. The filing also flags risks related to new product introductions in the AI Ethernet market, where customer trials and acceptance periods can lead to volatility in deferred revenue balances and potential inventory write-downs if requirements are not met.
