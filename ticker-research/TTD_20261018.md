## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (TTD_10-K_20260906.txt)

The Trade Desk (TTD) operates as a global leader in advertising technology, specifically focusing on the buy side of the digital advertising ecosystem. The company empowers advertising agencies, advertisers, and service providers to create, manage, and optimize digital campaigns across various formats, channels, and devices through a self-service platform and enterprise APIs. TTD’s business model is built on charging clients a platform fee, generally calculated as a percentage of the client’s total spend on the platform, along with revenue from value-added services and data. Unlike competitors who may serve both buyers and sellers, TTD maintains a strict buy-side focus to avoid conflicts of interest, positioning itself as an objective referee in a market where supply exceeds demand. This approach allows the company to build long-term trust, evidenced by a customer retention rate exceeding 95% for over a decade, and to leverage ongoing master services agreements (MSAs) rather than episodic orders.

TTD’s competitive moat is derived from its independent, buy-side objectivity, which fosters deep client relationships and trust in handling proprietary first-party data. The company differentiates itself through a "data-driven" philosophy, having built a data-management platform before its ad-buying technology, which enables precise targeting and closed-loop measurement. Additionally, TTD leverages an open platform architecture that allows clients to customize functionality via APIs and integrates a rich ecosystem of inventory, publisher, and data partners. The company highlights its "expressiveness"—the ability to target audiences with extreme precision through bid-factor-based architecture—and its AI capabilities, such as the "Koa" co-pilot, which automates optimization and decisioning. This combination of objectivity, data depth, and technological sophistication creates a barrier to entry for competitors who may offer lower prices but lack the same level of transparency and alignment with buyer interests.

Management’s stated priorities center on increasing the share of existing clients’ digital advertising spend, particularly as budgets shift toward programmatic channels and connected television (CTV). The company is actively pursuing global expansion, with specific focus on markets in Europe and Asia, including the U.K., Germany, France, China, Japan, India, and Australia. TTD is also prioritizing the continuous innovation of its technology, data, and measurement capabilities, including the recent "Kokai" platform upgrade and the development of AI-driven features like Audience Unlimited. Furthermore, management emphasizes the importance of expanding omnichannel reach, integrating more premium inventory sources, and driving the adoption of third-party and first-party data to enhance campaign performance.

The filing flags several material risks, primarily the dependence on the overall growth of the programmatic advertising market and the ability to retain existing client spend in a highly competitive environment. TTD notes that its future growth is contingent on advertisers continuing to shift budgets to programmatic channels and on the company’s ability to differentiate its value against competitors who may offer artificially low prices enabled by conflicts of interest. Macroeconomic uncertainty, including changes in interest rates, inflation, and geopolitical developments, poses a risk to client demand and operational costs, particularly regarding the rising prices of hosting infrastructure components. Additionally, the company highlights the challenge of managing increased costs associated with developing and hosting its AI-rich platform, as well as the risks associated with expanding into international markets where adoption may vary. Seasonality is also noted as a factor, with revenue typically peaking in the fourth quarter and dipping in the first.
