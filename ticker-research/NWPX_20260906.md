## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (NWPX_10-K_20260906.txt)

NWPX Infrastructure, Inc., formerly known as Northwest Pipe Company, is a leading manufacturer of water-related infrastructure products operating in two segments: Water Transmission Systems (WTS) and Precast Infrastructure and Engineered Systems. The company specializes in producing engineered water transmission systems, including steel casing pipe, bar-wrapped concrete cylinder pipe, and pipeline system joints and fittings. It also offers solution-based products such as high-quality reinforced precast concrete products, lined precast sanitary sewer system structures, water distribution and management equipment, pump lift stations, wastewater pretreatment, and stormwater quality products. NWPX has expanded its manufacturing footprint by introducing lined and engineered precast products at additional facilities, enhancing capacity and regional availability.

The company's competitive moat is bolstered by its position as the largest manufacturer of engineered water transmission systems in North America, a broad geographic footprint with 13 manufacturing facilities across North America, and strategic expansion through acquisitions, such as the purchase of Boughton’s Precast, Inc. This acquisition enhances its capabilities in stormwater infrastructure and sanitary sewer products.

Management's priorities include expanding the company's geographic reach, improving operational efficiency, and leveraging long-term demand for water infrastructure projects driven by urban growth and infrastructure upgrades. The company also focuses on innovation and quality while maintaining core values of accountability, commitment, and teamwork.

Key risks identified in the filing include economic uncertainties such as housing market softness, elevated federal funds rates, inflationary pressures, potential recession risks, and disruptions in steel markets, which significantly impact costs. Additionally, delays in federal funding for infrastructure projects under the IIJA and the Inflation Reduction Act could affect future project bids and revenue. The company also faces challenges related to volatile steel prices, which can lead to mismatches between fixed-price contracts and fluctuating material costs.
