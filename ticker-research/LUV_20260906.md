## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (LUV_10-K_20260827.txt)

Southwest Airlines Co., operating as Southwest Airlines, is a major U.S. passenger airline offering scheduled air transportation within the United States and near-international markets. The company, which began operations in 1971 with three Boeing 737 aircraft, has since expanded to serve 117 destinations across 42 states, Puerto Rico, and ten near-international countries. Southwest's business model is centered around a unique point-to-point route network, competitive fares, and customer service known as "Hospitality." This approach allows the airline to provide frequent, conveniently timed flights without relying on hub-and-spoke operations.

The company's competitive moat stems from its cost-effective strategies, including operating a single aircraft type (Boeing 737), which simplifies maintenance and training. Southwest also leverages secondary airports to minimize ground time and enhance asset utilization. Additionally, the airline has introduced initiatives such as assigned seating, expanded international partnerships, and 24-hour operations with redeye flights to improve customer experience and operational efficiency.

Management's priorities include enhancing customer experience through new fare products and services like Getaways by Southwest, optimizing its route network for profitability, and controlling costs through fleet modernization and workforce adjustments. The company has also focused on expanding its presence in key markets and improving connectivity while reducing less profitable routes.

Key risks flagged in the filing include volatile fuel prices, which significantly impact operating expenses, labor cost increases due to inflation and contractual obligations, and the challenges of maintaining operational efficiency amid evolving customer preferences and market conditions. Southwest also faces risks related to aircraft delivery delays and potential disruptions from global economic factors.
