## Business Profile
Source: 10-K (V_10-K_20260827.txt)

Visa operates as a global payments technology company that facilitates secure, reliable, and efficient commerce through a proprietary transaction processing network called VisaNet. The company’s business model is based on the "four-party model," where Visa provides authorization, clearing, and settlement services among consumers, issuing financial institutions, acquiring financial institutions, and sellers. Visa explicitly states that it is not a financial institution; it does not issue cards, extend credit, or bear credit risk. Instead, it earns revenue through service fees, data processing fees, international transaction fees, and other value-added services, while maintaining an open partnership approach that allows clients to integrate via APIs and other digital interfaces.

The company’s competitive moat is derived from its massive global scale and network effects, evidenced by processing 329 billion transactions in fiscal 2025, connecting nearly 14,500 financial institutions, and supporting nearly 5 billion payment credentials across more than 175 million merchant locations in over 200 countries. Visa leverages this infrastructure to offer a "network of networks" strategy, positioning itself as a single connection point for money movement regardless of the underlying network. Additionally, the company cites its proprietary technology stack, including Visa Token Service for security and fraud mitigation, and its "Visa as a Service" modular architecture, as key differentiators that allow clients of any size to access global payment capabilities.

Management’s stated priorities focus on accelerating revenue growth through three main pillars: Consumer Payments (CP), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and Value-Added Services (VAS). In CP, the company aims to strengthen card-based payments while expanding into non-card-based forms such as real-time payments and account-to-account transfers, targeting an estimated $20 trillion in underserved consumer spend. In CMS, the focus is on driving penetration in commercial and B2B payments. In VAS, the goal is to deepen client partnerships through innovative services. The company is also heavily investing in next-generation technologies, including generative AI, stablecoins, agentic commerce, and expanding its "Tap to Everything" ecosystem, which accounted for 79% of face-to-face transactions globally in fiscal 2025.

The most material risks flagged in the filing are primarily legal and regulatory, specifically the interchange multidistrict litigation (MDL). During fiscal 2025, Visa recorded additional accruals of $2.2 billion and made $875 million in deposits into a litigation escrow account to address these claims. The estimated interchange reimbursement fees at issue in unresolved claims were approximately $39.4 billion as of October 2025. This litigation is significant enough that it impacts the company’s capital structure, as the resolution of these claims is tied to the conversion rates of Class B common stock into Class A common stock under the U.S. retrospective responsibility plan. Other financial risks include the volatility of operating expenses, which increased 30% year-over-year in fiscal 2025 primarily due to higher litigation provisions and personnel costs, and the reliance on client incentives to drive volume, which partially offset revenue growth.
