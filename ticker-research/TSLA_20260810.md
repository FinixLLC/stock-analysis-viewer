## Business Profile

Source: Tesla FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 (Business) and Item 1A (Risk Factors), locally cached at `company_profile/financial_reports/TSLA_10-K_20260708.txt` (filed ~July 2026, covers fiscal year ended Dec 31, 2025). Read and synthesized directly from the filing text.

### What the company does
Tesla describes its mission as "bringing artificial intelligence into the real world," leveraging its existing EV and energy businesses (vehicle design/manufacturing/sales/leasing, energy generation and storage) as the substrate for AI-driven products: Full Self-Driving (Supervised), Robotaxi (launched June 2025), and Optimus (humanoid robot, "Bots"). It operates two reportable segments:
1. Automotive — design, manufacture, sale and lease of EVs (Model 3, Y, S, X, Cybertruck, and the Tesla Semi in early production), plus automotive regulatory credit sales, used-vehicle sales, non-warranty service/collision, paid Supercharging, an insurance business, parts and merchandise.
2. Energy generation and storage — Powerwall (residential/small commercial storage), Megapack (utility/commercial-scale storage, increasingly marketed toward AI-datacenter grid demand), solar generation systems, and related software (Autobidder, Powerhub).

Robotaxi currently runs on Model Y vehicles but Tesla states it will transition toward Cybercab, a purpose-built autonomous vehicle, over time.

### Competitive moat (as management frames it)
Tesla cites its "engineering expertise, advancements in real-world AI, vertically integrated business model, and focus on user experience" as differentiators. In automotive, it positions Cybertruck against pickups, Model S/X against premium sedans/SUVs, and Model 3/Y against mass-market sedans/compact SUVs — competing against both legacy ICE makers and a growing field of EV entrants. In energy storage, Tesla claims competitive advantage from product specs, brand strength, and modular/scalable design against rivals like Huawei, Sungrow, SMA Solar, and Enphase. In energy generation (solar), its main competitors are traditional utilities and other solar installers/financiers.

### Management's stated priorities (FY2025/2026 outlook, MD&A)
- Profitable growth via a "differentiated and efficiently managed product portfolio" leveraging existing factories.
- Advancing FSD (Supervised) toward autonomy, scaling Robotaxi/Cybercab.
- Cost reduction, higher utilized capacity and delivery throughput.
- Vertically integrating and localizing the supply chain (notably battery cell manufacturing).
- Expanding global service/charging infrastructure.
- Ramping energy storage production/market penetration (46.7 GWh deployed in 2025) and further vertically integrating that supply chain too.
- Management explicitly flags that "rapidly evolving trade and fiscal policy" and tariff uncertainty pose risk to global supply chain, cost structure, and demand — with energy storage/generation more exposed to the current tariff regime than automotive.

### Risks management itself flags as most material (Item 1A excerpts)
- Execution/ramp risk: repeated caution about delays in launching/ramping new products (Cybercab, Optimus, energy storage, Solar Roof) and the difficulty of hitting cost/quality/output targets simultaneously.
- Supply chain / tariffs: "thousands of parts purchased globally from hundreds of suppliers, including single-source direct suppliers"; explicit callout that 2025 U.S. tariff policy changes and retaliatory measures have already impacted supply chain costs.
- Key-person dependence: Tesla states plainly it is "highly dependent on the services of Elon Musk, Technoking of Tesla and our Chief Executive Officer," and that none of its key employees is bound by a fixed-term employment agreement.
- Policy/subsidy risk: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, enacted July 4, 2025) repealed individual consumer EV tax credits and curtailed residential energy-property credits that were previously available under the IRA — residential energy storage/solar credits expired Dec 31, 2025; automotive regulatory-credit revenue (a real, if declining, profit contributor) is also being constrained by policy shifts. This is a direct, filed-and-disclosed headwind to both segments.
- AI compute/infrastructure risk: company notes rapidly growing need for compute, memory, energy and thermal resources to support AI ambitions, which it flags as a resource-availability risk in itself.

## Bottom Line

Tesla is running two increasingly divergent stories at once. The core auto/energy business reaccelerated top-line growth in Q2 2026 (record revenue and deliveries) but did so at the cost of profitability — operating margin cratered to 1.4% and free cash flow went negative as capex surged, largely to fund the AI/compute buildout (Terafab, Robotaxi, Optimus) that is the real basis of the bull case. That buildout is happening against a backdrop of lost EV subsidy tailwinds (OBBBA repeal of consumer tax credits, expired residential energy credits) and intensifying competition, both from legacy/Chinese EV makers on the vehicle side and from Huawei/Enphase/SolarEdge-type rivals on the energy-storage side — all independently corroborated by the EVCDM industry graph, which also flags CATL as a full "critical dependency" for battery cells (confidence 1.0), Tesla's single most concentrated supply-chain risk on record. Robotaxi, the centerpiece of the AI-optionality thesis, is expanding geographically but its usage metrics (paid miles) were flat quarter-over-quarter, undercutting the pace of scaling previously signaled by management.
