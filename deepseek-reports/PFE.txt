Analysis of Pfizer Inc.'s Financial Health

1. Balance Sheet Overview:
- Cash and Cash Equivalents: Increased from $1,043 million to $1,343 million, indicating improved liquidity.
- Short-Term Investments: Decreased significantly from $19,434 million to $13,641 million, possibly due to strategic investments elsewhere or share buybacks.
- Trade Accounts Receivable and Inventories: Both increased, suggesting higher future cash flow and preparedness for sales growth.
- Current Assets: Total current assets decreased, which may indicate a need to monitor liquidity despite the cash increase.
- Non-Current Assets: Mixed trends with property investments up and intangible assets down, raising concerns about key asset expiration or divestiture.

2. Liabilities:
- Short-Term Borrowings: Decreased from $6,946 million to $4,303 million, improving short-term financial stability.
- Trade Accounts Payable: Decreased, enhancing cash flow by reducing obligations.

3. Equity Analysis:
- Additional Paid-in Capital and Retained Earnings: Both increased, reflecting strong equity positions and profit retention.
- Treasury Stock: Decrease in value, possibly indicating confidence through share buybacks.

4. Limitations:
- The absence of income statement and cash flow data significantly hinders a comprehensive analysis, leaving key performance indicators unaddressed.

5. External Considerations:
- Potential impacts from regulatory changes, competition (e.g., generic drugs), and market conditions should be considered for a holistic view.

Conclusion:
Pfizer's balance sheet shows positive trends in equity and short-term stability but raises concerns regarding current asset levels and intangible assets. Further analysis with complete financial statements is essential to fully assess financial health.
